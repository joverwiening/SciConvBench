Treat the flow as viscous (not inviscid), using μ = 0.1 Pa·s.
Use simple Couette flow as the assumed configuration (one plate moving, one stationary), as it is the most standard baseline assumption for viscous flow between parallel plates.
Assume a plate separation (gap) of h = 0.01 m (1 cm) as a reasonable engineering value.
Assume the top plate moves at U = 1 m/s and the bottom plate is stationary, which is a standard Couette flow assumption.
The velocity profile for Couette flow is u(y) = U * (y/h), where y is measured from the stationary plate.
Compute the velocity gradient as du/dy = U/h.
Compute the wall shear stress using τ = μ * du/dy.
Apply this formula at the wall (the result is uniform across the gap for pure Couette flow).
Report the final wall shear stress value in Pascals (Pa).
