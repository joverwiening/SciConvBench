Solve a Venturi meter flow rate problem using ideal Bernoulli equation with discharge coefficient Cd = 1.
Make reasonable best-practice assumptions for all parameters, clearly stating each assumed value before using it.
Assume a typical pipe (inlet) diameter of 100 mm (0.1 m) and a throat diameter of 50 mm (0.05 m), giving a beta ratio of 0.5.
Assume a differential pressure of 50 kPa across the Venturi meter.
Assume the flowing fluid is water at 20°C with a density of 998 kg/m³.
Express the final flow rate in m³/s and also convert to L/s for practical reference.
Apply the ideal Venturi flow equation: Q = A_throat * sqrt(2*Δp / (ρ*(1-(D_throat/D_pipe)^4))).
Note that Cd = 1 means irreversible losses in the throat and diffuser are ignored in the flow rate calculation, even though the problem acknowledges they exist physically.
State clearly that using Cd = 1 overestimates the actual flow rate compared to a real meter with Cd typically around 0.95–0.98.
