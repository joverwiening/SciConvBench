Solve the steady-state 1D heat conduction problem in a slab.
The governing equation is the steady form of the heat equation: d2T/dx2 = 0 (no time dependence).
Assume a slab of length L = 1 meter as a reasonable standard choice.
Apply Dirichlet boundary conditions: T(0) = 100°C at the left end and T(L) = 0°C at the right end, as a standard best-practice example.
Thermal diffusivity alpha is not needed for the steady-state solution since the time-dependent term vanishes, but assume the material is steel (alpha ≈ 1.172e-5 m²/s) if material context is needed.
Solve the boundary value problem analytically to obtain the temperature distribution T(x) across the slab.
Present the solution clearly, showing the derivation steps and the final linear temperature profile.
