Calculate the exit velocity of air expanding isothermally through a nozzle from 10 bar to 1 bar.
The process is isothermal, not isentropic or adiabatic.
Use the isothermal flow relation with heat addition to find the exit velocity.
Assume air as an ideal gas with standard properties: Cp = 1005 J/kg·K, γ = 1.4, R = 287 J/kg·K.
Assume inlet temperature is 300 K.
Assume inlet velocity is negligible (zero).
Inlet pressure is 10 bar and exit pressure is 1 bar.
Since the process is isothermal, the exit temperature equals the inlet temperature (300 K).
Apply the steady-flow energy equation accounting for heat addition: the work done by pressure forces drives the kinetic energy at exit, using the isothermal relation for work = R·T·ln(P1/P2).
Calculate exit velocity from: 0.5·V_exit² = R·T·ln(P1/P2).
