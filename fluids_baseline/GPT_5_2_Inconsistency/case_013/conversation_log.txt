Q: What is the jet’s static (initial) temperature?
A: Make a reasonable assumption based on best practices. Use a typical reference static temperature for liquid water, e.g., **T = 293 K (20 °C)**, unless a different inlet temperature is specified.

=== 2026-03-15 10:06:58 ===
Q: What value should I use for the specific heat at constant pressure, cp, for the “ideal-gas” water in this calculation?
A: Use **liquid-water \(c_p \approx 4.18\ \text{kJ·kg}^{-1}\text{·K}^{-1}\)** (≈ **4180 J·kg⁻¹·K⁻¹**) at **\(T \approx 293\ \text{K}\)**. If you need more precision, assume it’s constant over the small temperature rise.

Q: Do you want the stagnation temperature reported in kelvin only, or in both kelvin and °C?
A: Report **stagnation enthalpy**, not stagnation temperature—so **no temperature units are needed**.

=== 2026-03-15 10:07:04 ===
Q: Do you want stagnation enthalpy reported per unit mass (J/kg) or as a total rate (W) for a specified mass flow rate?
A: Report **stagnation enthalpy per unit mass**, i.e., **\(h_0\) in J/kg (or kJ/kg)**. **No mass flow rate** is specified, so **do not** report a power/rate (W).

=== 2026-03-15 10:07:07 ===
Q: Do you want the stagnation enthalpy reported as the absolute value \(h_0\) referenced to \(h(T{=}0)\), or as the increase \(\Delta h_0\) relative to the inlet static enthalpy \(h\)?
A: Report the **stagnation enthalpy as an increase relative to the inlet static enthalpy**, i.e., use

\[
h_0 = h + \frac{v^2}{2}
\]

and report \(\Delta h_0 = \frac{v^2}{2}\) (per unit mass). No absolute reference to \(h(T=0)\) is needed.

=== 2026-03-15 10:07:10 ===
Q: Do you want to include potential-energy (elevation) effects in the stagnation enthalpy, or assume they are negligible?
A: Assume **potential-energy (elevation) effects are negligible**. Use only:

\[
h_0 = h + \frac{v^2}{2}
\]

and report \(\Delta h_0 = \frac{v^2}{2}\) per unit mass.
