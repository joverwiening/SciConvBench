Use the ideal-gas law to compute the density of liquid water at 20 C and 1 atm.
Do not use the ideal-gas-law density in any Bernoulli calculation.
Assume a standard Bernoulli setup: steady, incompressible, inviscid flow from a large reservoir with free surface at 1 atm and negligible surface velocity through a small sharp-edged orifice discharging to atmosphere with exit pressure 1 atm.
Use liquid-water density \(\rho = 998\ \text{kg/m}^3\) at 20 °C in the Bernoulli/energy equation.
Assume a 1.0 m elevation drop (head) from the reservoir free surface to the orifice centerline.
Assume a sharp-edged orifice discharge coefficient \(C_d = 0.62\).
Assume an orifice diameter of 10 mm (0.010 m).
Compute and report the ideal (Torricelli) exit speed from the 1.0 m head.
Compute and report the volumetric flow rate using the 10 mm orifice with \(C_d = 0.62\).
Use gravitational acceleration \(g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\).
