Q: What kinematic viscosity value for water (ν) should I use to compute the Reynolds number?
A: Use water properties at 20°C: \( \rho = 998\ \text{kg/m}^3 \) and \( \mu = 1.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{Pa·s} \).  
So the kinematic viscosity is:

\[
\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho} = \frac{1.0\times10^{-3}}{998} \approx 1.00\times10^{-6}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}.
\]

Q: Do you want me to use your stated hydraulic diameter value exactly as given (Dh = 0.05 m), even though the hydraulic diameter for a full circular pipe is typically equal to the pipe diameter (0.10 m)?
A: Use the standard definition \(D_h = 4A/P\). For a full circular pipe, \(D_h = D = 0.10\ \text{m}\) (not 0.05 m).

=== 2026-03-15 10:10:49 ===
Q: Do you want the Reynolds number computed using \(Re = \rho V D_h/\mu\) or using \(Re = V D_h/\nu\)?
A: Use \(Re = \dfrac{\rho V D_h}{\mu}\) with \(\rho = 998\ \text{kg/m}^3\), \(\mu = 1.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{Pa·s}\), and \(D_h = D = 0.10\ \text{m}\). (Using \(Re = \dfrac{V D_h}{\nu}\) is equivalent if you take \(\nu=\mu/\rho\).)
