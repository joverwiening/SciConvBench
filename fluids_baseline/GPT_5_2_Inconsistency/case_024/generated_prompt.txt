Treat the 10 bar and 1 bar pressures as absolute pressures when computing density from the ideal-gas law.  
Assume a circular duct with 0.20 m internal diameter.  
Assume the average axial velocity is constant at 10 m/s along the duct length.  
Assume air temperature is constant at 293.15 K (20°C).  
Assume pressure varies linearly along the duct length from 10 bar to 1 bar.  
Compute mass flow rate using \(\dot m=\int_A \rho\,u\,dA\) with \(\rho\) varying along the duct.  
Compute \(\rho\) from an equation of state (use the ideal-gas law) as pressure drops from 10 bar to 1 bar.  
Use \(Q=A\cdot V\) only as needed to estimate local volumetric flow/velocity, but do not treat density as constant at 1.225 kg/m³ or base the calculation on standard-conditions volumetric flow.
