Calculate the pressure drop in a horizontal pipe that is 100 m long with an internal diameter of 50 mm. 
The pipe carries water at a flow rate of 2 L/s (0.002 m³/s), which gives an average velocity of about 1.0 m/s. 
Use water properties at 20 °C: density 998 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 1 × 10⁻³ Pa·s. 
Assume the pipe’s internal roughness is 0.15 mm (galvanized iron). 
Determine the Reynolds number, select the appropriate friction factor (e.g., using the Colebrook‑White equation), and compute the pressure drop using the Darcy‑Weisbach formula.
