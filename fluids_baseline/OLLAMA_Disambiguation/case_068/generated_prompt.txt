Calculate the boundary layer work done by an ideal gas expanding isothermally from an initial volume of 0.356 m³ to a final volume of 1.78 m³ in a piston‑cylinder device.  
Assume the gas mass is 2 kg and the specific gas constant is 0.2968 kJ/(kg·K).  
Assume the temperature remains constant at 300 K throughout the expansion.  
Assume the initial pressure is 500 kPa and the final pressure is 100 kPa.  
Assume the piston cross‑sectional area is 0.01 m² (approximately 11.3 cm diameter).  
Assume the expansion duration is 5 seconds, giving an average piston speed of 0.284 m s⁻¹.  
Assume the gas viscosity corresponds to standard air at 300 K, μ = 1.85 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s.  
Use the average piston speed and piston area to calculate the Reynolds number for the flow over the piston surface.  
Estimate the boundary‑layer thickness using an appropriate laminar or turbulent flat‑plate correlation based on the Reynolds number.  
Calculate the shear stress on the piston wall as τ = μ · (du/dy) using the estimated boundary‑layer thickness.  
Compute the boundary‑layer work as the time integral of shear stress multiplied by the piston displacement over the 5‑second expansion.  
Report the resulting boundary‑layer work in kilojoules.
