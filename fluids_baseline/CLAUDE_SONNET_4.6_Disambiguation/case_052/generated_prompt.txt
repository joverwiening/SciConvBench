Calculate the dynamic viscosity of a fluid using a falling-ball viscometer with a spherical steel ball.
The ball diameter is 3 mm (radius = 1.5 mm = 0.0015 m).
The density of the steel ball is 7850 kg/m³.
The density of the fluid is 1000 kg/m³.
The terminal velocity of the falling ball is 5 cm/s (0.05 m/s).
Assume the tube diameter is large enough that wall effects are negligible (no wall correction factor needed).
Use Stokes' Law to derive the dynamic viscosity: η = (2r²(ρ_ball - ρ_fluid)g) / (9v), where r is the ball radius, g is gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s²), and v is the terminal velocity.
Report the final dynamic viscosity in Pascal-seconds (Pa·s) or millipascal-seconds (mPa·s).
