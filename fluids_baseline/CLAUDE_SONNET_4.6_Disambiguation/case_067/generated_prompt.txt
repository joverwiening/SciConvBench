Find the terminal velocity of a spherical raindrop falling through air.
The raindrop has a diameter of 2 mm (radius = 1 mm = 0.001 m).
Use water density of 1000 kg/m³.
Use air density of 1.2 kg/m³.
Use a constant drag coefficient (Cd) of 0.47 (sphere) — wait, the user specified Cd = 0.5.
Use a constant drag coefficient of 0.5.
Use gravitational acceleration of 9.81 m/s² (standard value unless otherwise specified).
Apply the terminal velocity formula derived by balancing gravitational force against aerodynamic drag: v = sqrt((4 * d * g * (rho_water - rho_air)) / (3 * Cd * rho_air)), where d is the diameter of the raindrop.
Dynamic viscosity of air is not needed; do not use Stokes' law.
