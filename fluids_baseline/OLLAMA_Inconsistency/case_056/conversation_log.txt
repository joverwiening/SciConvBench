Q: What fluid should be used for the calculation, and should I use standard property values (ν and α) for that fluid at the film temperature?
A: Use air as the fluid (the typical choice for natural‑convection plate problems). Evaluate ν and α at the film temperature \(T_f\) and use the standard property values for air at that temperature.

Q: What are the values of the kinematic viscosity (ν) and thermal diffusivity (α) of air at the film temperature of 50 °C?
A: The complete requirement does not list specific property values. Use standard air‑property data at the film temperature (≈ 50 °C). A typical choice is  

- Kinematic viscosity, ν ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s  
- Thermal diffusivity, α ≈ 2.2 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s  

(You may verify these values from an air property table for 50 °C.)

Q: Should the thermal expansion coefficient β be calculated using the film temperature in Kelvin (absolute temperature) or directly in Celsius?
A: The thermal expansion coefficient β should be evaluated as β ≈ 1/T₍abs₎ using the film temperature expressed in Kelvin (absolute temperature).

=== 2026-03-28 08:48:45 ===
Q: What film temperature (in °C) should be used for evaluating the thermal expansion coefficient β?
A: The film temperature is the average of the surface and ambient temperatures:

\[
T_f = \frac{T_s + T_\infty}{2} = \frac{80^\circ\text{C} + 20^\circ\text{C}}{2} = 50^\circ\text{C}.
\]

Q: Should the temperature difference ΔT be taken as 60 °C (which is equivalent to 60 K) for the Rayleigh number calculation?
A: Yes. Use ΔT = Ts − T∞ = 80 °C − 20 °C = 60 °C, which is numerically the same as 60 K for the Rayleigh‑number calculation.
