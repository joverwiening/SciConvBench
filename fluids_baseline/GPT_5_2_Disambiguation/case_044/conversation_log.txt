Q: What material (including its latent heat and specific heat) should be used to compute the Stefan number?
A: Use **ice (melting to water) at 0°C**. Take properties at ~0°C:

- **Latent heat of fusion**: \(L_f \approx 334\,\text{kJ/kg}\)  
- **Specific heat** (use liquid water for the sensible heating term): \(c_p \approx 4.18\,\text{kJ/(kg·K)}\)

If you prefer using ice’s \(c_p\) instead, make a reasonable assumption based on best practices.

Q: Should the 10°C temperature difference be interpreted as heating from 0°C to 10°C (i.e., ΔT = 10 K for the sensible term)?
A: Yes. Interpret it as the surface being at **10°C** while the melting interface is at **0°C**, so use **ΔT = 10 K** for the sensible heating term.

=== 2026-03-15 10:43:09 ===
Q: Do you want the Stefan number defined as Ste = c_p ΔT / L_f?
A: Yes—use \( \mathrm{Ste} = \dfrac{c_p\,\Delta T}{L_f} \), with \(c_p = 4.18\,\text{kJ/(kg·K)}\), \(\Delta T = 10\,\text{K}\), and \(L_f = 334\,\text{kJ/kg}\).
