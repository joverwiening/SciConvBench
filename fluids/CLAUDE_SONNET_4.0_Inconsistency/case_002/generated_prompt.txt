Calculate the pressure drop in a horizontal pipe with water flow using the Darcy-Weisbach equation, not Bernoulli's equation alone.
The pipe is 40 m long with 50 mm diameter.
Water flows at 20°C with density 998 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 1.0×10⁻³ Pa·s.
The mean velocity is 2.0 m/s.
The flow is fully developed and viscous.
Assume the pipe is smooth.
First calculate the Reynolds number to determine the flow regime.
Use the appropriate friction factor correlation for smooth pipes.
Apply the Darcy-Weisbach equation: ΔP = f × (L/D) × (ρv²/2) where f is the friction factor.
Note that Bernoulli's equation alone cannot account for viscous losses and requires the addition of head loss terms.
