The quarter-cylindrical gate has radius R = 2.0 m and width b = 1.0 m.
The gate is oriented with its curved surface facing downward (concave side up, like a quarter-pipe opening upward).
The top edge of the gate is exactly at the free surface.
Water is present only on the curved side of the gate (the concave interior contains air, not water).
The fluid is water with density rho = 1000 kg/m³.
The acceleration due to gravity is g = 9.81 m/s².

The horizontal hydrostatic force component shall be computed using the horizontal projection area of the curved surface.
The vertical projection of the curved surface is a rectangle with height R and width b, giving projected area A_x = R × b = 2.0 m × 1.0 m = 2.0 m².
The centroid of this vertical projection is located at depth R/2 = 1.0 m below the free surface.
The horizontal force is F_H = rho × g × h_c × A_x = 1000 kg/m³ × 9.81 m/s² × 1.0 m × 2.0 m² = 19620 N, acting perpendicular to the vertical projection (horizontally).

The vertical hydrostatic force component shall be computed using the vertical projection area of the curved surface.
The horizontal projection of the curved surface is a rectangle with width R and length b, giving projected area A_z = R × b = 2.0 m × 1.0 m = 2.0 m².
The vertical force equals the weight of the fluid in the volume directly above the curved surface up to the free surface.
The volume of water above the curved surface is the volume of the quarter-cylinder: V = (pi × R² / 4) × b = (pi × 4.0 m² / 4) × 1.0 m = pi m³ ≈ 3.1416 m³.
The vertical force is F_V = rho × g × V = 1000 kg/m³ × 9.81 m/s² × 3.1416 m³ = 30787 N, acting upward.

The horizontal force component is 19620 N directed horizontally.
The vertical force component is 30787 N directed upward.
