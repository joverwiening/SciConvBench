The fluid is water with density rho = 1000 kg/m^3.
The flow speed along the curved streamline is V = 10 m/s.
The radius of curvature of the streamline is R = 5 m.
The normal distance across the streamline is Δn = 0.10 m.
The pressure increases toward the center of curvature, meaning the pressure gradient in the normal direction is positive toward the center.
Apply the normal momentum equation for curved streamlines: ∂p/∂n = ρV²/R, where n is the coordinate directed toward the center of curvature.
Integrate across the normal distance Δn to find the pressure difference: Δp = ρV²Δn/R.
Substitute the given values: Δp = (1000 kg/m³)(10 m/s)²(0.10 m)/(5 m).
Compute the result: Δp = 2000 Pa.
The pressure on the inner side of the streamline (closer to center of curvature) is higher than the pressure on the outer side by 2000 Pa.
