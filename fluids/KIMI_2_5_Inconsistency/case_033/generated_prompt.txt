The converging nozzle discharges air from a tank at 500 kPa to an ambient pressure of 100 kPa.

The tank temperature is assumed to be 300 K, which is a standard reference temperature for room-temperature gas dynamics problems.

The nozzle exit diameter is assumed to be 0.01 m (10 mm), which is a typical small-scale nozzle size for laboratory demonstrations and basic calculations.

The specific heat ratio (gamma) of air is 1.4.

The exit pressure is specified as 100 kPa, which equals the ambient pressure.

The critical pressure ratio for a converging nozzle with gamma = 1.4 is 0.528.

The pressure ratio across the nozzle is 100/500 = 0.2, which is less than the critical pressure ratio of 0.528.

The flow is choked because the pressure ratio is below critical, meaning the Mach number at the throat/exit is 1.0.

The exit pressure of 100 kPa is below the critical pressure (264 kPa), so the actual exit pressure in the jet will be 100 kPa with expansion waves outside the nozzle, but the mass flow is determined by choked flow conditions at the throat.

The mass flow rate calculation uses isentropic flow relations for choked flow.

The stagnation temperature is 300 K.

The stagnation pressure is 500 kPa.

The exit area is π × (0.005)² = 7.854 × 10⁻⁵ m².
