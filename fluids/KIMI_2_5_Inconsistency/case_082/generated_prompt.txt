The task is to compute flow rate through a Venturi meter using a discharge coefficient to account for irreversible losses.

Use the standard Venturi flow rate equation: Q = Cd * A2 * sqrt(2 * Δp / (ρ * (1 - beta^4))).

Assume a pipe diameter d1 of 0.1 m, which is a common size for laboratory and industrial flow measurement applications.

Assume a throat diameter d2 of 0.05 m, giving a diameter ratio beta of 0.5, which is within the typical recommended range of 0.3 to 0.75 for Venturi meters.

Assume a pressure difference Δp of 5000 Pa, representing a moderate measurement signal suitable for accurate flow determination.

Assume a fluid density ρ of 1000 kg/m³, corresponding to water at standard conditions.

Assume a discharge coefficient Cd of 0.98, which is typical for well-designed Venturi meters with gradual converging sections and properly fabricated throats.

Calculate the throat area A2 as π * (d2/2)^2 = π * (0.025)^2 = 0.0019635 m².

Calculate beta^4 as (0.5)^4 = 0.0625.

Calculate the denominator term (1 - beta^4) as 1 - 0.0625 = 0.9375.

Calculate the term 2 * Δp / (ρ * (1 - beta^4)) as 2 * 5000 / (1000 * 0.9375) = 10000 / 937.5 = 10.667 m²/s².

Take the square root to obtain 3.266 m/s.

Multiply by Cd and A2 to obtain the flow rate: Q = 0.98 * 0.0019635 * 3.266 = 0.00628 m³/s, or approximately 6.28 liters per second.

Report the final flow rate as 0.0063 m³/s or 6.3 L/s, with all assumed values documented.
