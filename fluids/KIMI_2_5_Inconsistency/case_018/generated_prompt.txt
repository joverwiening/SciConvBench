The kinetic-energy correction factor alpha shall be computed for fully developed laminar flow in a circular pipe.

The pipe diameter D is 20 mm.

The mean velocity V is 1.0 m/s.

The velocity profile is assumed to be uniform (plug flow), meaning the velocity is constant across the entire cross-section and equal to the mean velocity V at every point.

For a uniform velocity profile, the actual kinetic energy flux equals the kinetic energy flux computed using the mean velocity.

The kinetic-energy correction factor alpha is defined as the ratio of the actual kinetic energy flux to the kinetic energy flux based on mean velocity.

For plug flow, the velocity distribution is u(r) = V for all radial positions r from 0 to R, where R is the pipe radius.

The correction factor alpha is computed as (1/A) * integral over A of (u/V)^3 dA.

Substituting u = V everywhere gives (u/V)^3 = 1 everywhere.

Therefore alpha = (1/A) * integral over A of 1 dA = (1/A) * A = 1.

The kinetic-energy correction factor alpha equals 1.0.
