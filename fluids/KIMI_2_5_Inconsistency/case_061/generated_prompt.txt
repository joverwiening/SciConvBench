The degree of reaction for an axial compressor rotor is defined as the ratio of static enthalpy rise in the rotor to the total enthalpy rise in the stage.

Given data:
- Inlet relative velocity W1 = 150 m/s at angle beta1 = 45 degrees
- Outlet relative velocity W2 = 100 m/s at angle beta2 = 30 degrees
- Axial velocity is constant through the rotor

The degree of reaction R is computed as follows:

First, determine the axial velocity component which is constant:
Ca = W1 * cos(beta1) = 150 * cos(45°) = 150 * 0.7071 = 106.07 m/s

Verify with outlet:
Ca = W2 * cos(beta2) = 100 * cos(30°) = 100 * 0.866 = 86.6 m/s

The axial velocities do not match (106.07 m/s vs 86.6 m/s), which contradicts the given assumption of constant axial velocity. This indicates the problem as stated contains inconsistent data.

However, proceeding with the standard formula for degree of reaction assuming the intent was to use the given angles and velocities:

The tangential components of relative velocity are:
W_theta1 = W1 * sin(beta1) = 150 * sin(45°) = 106.07 m/s
W_theta2 = W2 * sin(beta2) = 100 * sin(30°) = 50 m/s

For a normal stage with constant axial velocity (which requires consistent data), the degree of reaction is:
R = (Ca / (2U)) * (tan(beta1) + tan(beta2))

Where U is the blade speed. Without U specified, or with the data inconsistency noted, the problem cannot be solved as stated with physically consistent results.
