The user wants to calculate the temperature after compression in a Brayton cycle using isentropic compression relations.

The Brayton cycle consists of four processes: isentropic compression, constant pressure heat addition, isentropic expansion, and constant pressure heat rejection.

Use the isentropic temperature-pressure relation for compression: T2/T1 = (p2/p1)^((gamma-1)/gamma).

T1 is the inlet temperature to the compressor.

T2 is the outlet temperature from the compressor.

p1 is the inlet pressure to the compressor.

p2 is the outlet pressure from the compressor.

gamma is the specific heat ratio (cp/cv) of the working fluid.

The pressure ratio is defined as rp = p2/p1.

The temperature ratio can be rewritten as T2/T1 = (rp)^((gamma-1)/gamma).

To compute T2, rearrange to: T2 = T1 * (p2/p1)^((gamma-1)/gamma).

The user must provide numerical values for T1, p1, p2 (or the pressure ratio), and gamma to obtain a numerical answer.

If typical values are assumed for air standard analysis: gamma = 1.4, and example values might be T1 = 300 K, p1 = 100 kPa, p2 = 1000 kPa (pressure ratio of 10), yielding T2 = 300 * (10)^(0.4/1.4) = 300 * 10^0.2857 ≈ 579.2 K.
