The simulation computes wall shear stress for viscous Couette/Poiseuille flow between parallel plates.

The fluid has dynamic viscosity mu = 0.1 Pa·s.

The gap height between plates is assumed to be h = 0.01 m (1 cm), a standard value for microfluidic and lubrication applications.

The flow is driven by a pressure gradient dp/dx = -1000 Pa/m, representing a moderate pressure-driven flow.

The velocity profile for plane Poiseuille flow is u(y) = -(1/2mu)(dp/dx)y(h-y), where y is measured from the bottom plate.

The maximum velocity occurs at the centerline y = h/2 and equals u_max = -(h^2/8mu)(dp/dx) = 0.125 m/s.

The velocity gradient is du/dy = -(1/2mu)(dp/dx)(h-2y).

At the bottom wall (y = 0), the velocity gradient is du/dy|_wall = -(h/2mu)(dp/dx) = 50 s^-1.

At the top wall (y = h), the velocity gradient is du/dy|_wall = (h/2mu)(dp/dx) = -50 s^-1.

The wall shear stress is computed using tau = mu * du/dy.

The magnitude of wall shear stress at both walls is tau_wall = 0.1 Pa·s * 50 s^-1 = 5 Pa.

The direction of shear stress opposes the flow direction at each wall.
