The Francis turbine runner is installed at a height of 20 meters above the tailrace water level.

The atmospheric head Ha is given as 10.3 meters.

The vapor pressure head Hv is given as 0.3 meters.

The cavitation factor sigma is defined as sigma = (Ha - Hv - z) / H, where z is the elevation of the runner above tailrace and H is the net head.

Substituting the given values: sigma = (10.3 - 0.3 - 20) / H = (-10) / H.

Since H is always positive for any operating turbine, sigma = -10/H is always negative.

The statement to prove is incorrect. The cavitation factor sigma is negative, not positive, for any positive head H.

Cavitation is actually inevitable under these conditions because the runner is installed too high above the tailrace. The available atmospheric pressure head (10.3 m) minus vapor pressure head (0.3 m) equals 10 m of available suction head, but the runner is placed at 20 m elevation, exceeding this limit by 10 m.

For cavitation to be impossible, the runner elevation z must satisfy z <= Ha - Hv = 10 m. The actual elevation of 20 m violates this condition.
