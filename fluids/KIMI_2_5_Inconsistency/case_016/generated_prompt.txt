Use the hydrostatic pressure formula P = rho*g*h to calculate the pressure distribution inside a centrifuge spinning at 10,000 RPM.

The centrifuge rotor has a maximum radius of 0.15 meters (distance from rotation axis to bottom of tube).

The inner radius of the centrifuge rotor is 0.05 meters (distance from rotation axis to liquid surface at top of fluid column).

The density of the fluid is 1000 kg/m^3 (water, a common biological sample assumption).

The height of the fluid column is 0.10 meters (vertical distance from liquid surface to bottom of tube).

The centrifuge tube is oriented vertically with the bottom pointing outward (radial orientation), which is standard for fixed-angle and swinging-bucket rotors when at full speed.

The effective gravitational acceleration in the centrifuge is calculated as g_eff = omega^2 * r, where omega = (2 * pi * 10000) / 60 = 1047.2 rad/s.

The pressure at any radial position r is calculated as P(r) = rho * omega^2 * integral from r_inner to r of (r' * dr'), which simplifies to P(r) = 0.5 * rho * omega^2 * (r^2 - r_inner^2).

The pressure at the liquid surface (r = 0.05 m) is 0 Pa (gauge pressure).

The pressure at the bottom of the tube (r = 0.15 m) is 0.5 * 1000 * (1047.2)^2 * (0.15^2 - 0.05^2) = 0.5 * 1000 * 1096619.8 * (0.0225 - 0.0025) = 548309900 * 0.02 = 10,966,198 Pa or approximately 10.97 MPa (109.7 bar).

The pressure distribution increases quadratically with radius from the rotation axis.

The calculation assumes incompressible fluid, isothermal conditions, and negligible Coriolis effects.

The hydrostatic pressure formula P = rho*g*h is adapted for centrifugal force by replacing g with the position-dependent centrifugal acceleration omega^2*r and integrating over the radial path.
