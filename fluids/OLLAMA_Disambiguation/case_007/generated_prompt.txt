Assume a standard Schedule 40 steel pipe with a nominal 2‑inch (50.8 mm) bore, an outer diameter of 2.375 in (60.3 mm) and a wall thickness of 0.154 in (3.9 mm).  
The inner radius is therefore 0.0263 m and the outer radius is 0.0302 m.  
Use a thermal conductivity for steel of 45 W·m⁻¹·K⁻¹.  
Assume natural convection from the horizontal pipe to ambient air with a heat‑transfer coefficient h = 10 W·m⁻²·K⁻¹.  
The steam temperature is 150 °C and the surrounding air temperature is 20 °C, giving a temperature difference ΔT = 130 K.  
Calculate the conductive resistance of the pipe wall: R_cond = ln(r_o / r_i) / (2 π k L).  
Calculate the external convective resistance: R_conv = 1 / (h · 2 π r_o L).  
Add the resistances to obtain the total thermal resistance: R_total = R_cond + R_conv.  
Compute the heat loss per meter: Q = ΔT / R_total.  
Using the values above, R_cond ≈ 4.9 × 10⁻⁴ K·W⁻¹, R_conv ≈ 0.528 K·W⁻¹, R_total ≈ 0.528 K·W⁻¹, and the heat loss is Q ≈ 2.46 × 10² W per meter of pipe.  
State the final result: the heat loss from a 1‑meter length of the 2‑inch steel pipe carrying 150 °C steam in 20 °C ambient air is approximately 246 W.
