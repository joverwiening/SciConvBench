Calculate the pressure drop in a 100 m long horizontal pipe with a 50 mm internal diameter carrying water at 20 °C with a volumetric flow rate of 2 L/s and an internal roughness height of 0.15 mm.

Use the Darcy‑Weisbach equation:
Δp = f · (L/D) · (ρ V²/2)

Determine the fluid properties of water at 20 °C:
- Density ρ ≈ 998.2 kg/m³
- Dynamic viscosity μ ≈ 1.002 × 10⁻³ Pa·s
- Kinematic viscosity ν = μ/ρ ≈ 1.004 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s

Compute the cross‑sectional area:
A = π D²/4 = π (0.05 m)²/4 ≈ 1.9635 × 10⁻³ m²

Calculate the average velocity:
V = Q/A = 0.002 m³/s ÷ 1.9635 × 10⁻³ m² ≈ 1.019 m/s

Calculate the Reynolds number:
Re = V D/ν = 1.019 m/s · 0.05 m ÷ 1.004 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s ≈ 5.07 × 10⁴ (turbulent flow)

Determine the relative roughness:
ε/D = 0.15 mm ÷ 50 mm = 0.003

Compute the Darcy friction factor using the Swamee‑Jain explicit formula:
f = 0.25 / [log₁₀( ε/(3.7 D) + 5.74/Re⁰·⁹ )]²
= 0.25 / [log₁₀(0.00081 + 0.00033)]² ≈ 0.0289

Apply the Darcy‑Weisbach equation:
Δp = 0.0289 · (100 m ÷ 0.05 m) · (998.2 kg/m³ · (1.019 m/s)² / 2)
≈ 0.0289 · 2000 · 517.5 Pa
≈ 2.99 × 10⁴ Pa

Result:
The pressure drop across the pipe is approximately 30 kPa (≈ 29.9 kPa).
