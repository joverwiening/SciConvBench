Determine the torque measured by a Couette (concentric‑cylinder) rotational viscometer operating at 100 rpm for a fluid with dynamic viscosity 0.5 Pa·s, inner cylinder radius 0.02 m, outer cylinder radius 0.021 m, and axial length 0.05 m.  

Convert the rotational speed to angular velocity: ω = 100 rpm × 2π rad/rev ÷ 60 s/min = 10π/3 rad/s ≈ 10.472 rad/s.  

Use the Couette torque formula for a Newtonian fluid with no end effects:  
T = (4 π μ L ω R_i² R_o²) / (R_o² – R_i²).  

Substitute the values:  
R_i² = (0.02 m)² = 0.0004 m²,  
R_o² = (0.021 m)² = 0.000441 m²,  
R_i² R_o² = 1.764 × 10⁻⁷ m⁴,  
R_o² – R_i² = 4.1 × 10⁻⁵ m²,  
4 π μ L = 4 π × 0.5 Pa·s × 0.05 m = 0.1 π ≈ 0.31416.  

Compute the numerator: 0.31416 × ω × R_i² R_o² ≈ 0.31416 × 10.472 × 1.764 × 10⁻⁷ ≈ 5.8 × 10⁻⁷.  

Divide by the denominator: T ≈ (5.8 × 10⁻⁷) / (4.1 × 10⁻⁵) ≈ 1.4 × 10⁻² N·m.  

Therefore, the torque measured by the viscometer under the specified conditions is approximately 0.014 N·m.  

Assumptions: the fluid completely fills the gap, temperature effects on viscosity are negligible, and end‑effect corrections are not required.
