Assume the raindrop is a perfect sphere with a diameter of 2 mm (radius 1 mm).  
Assume standard atmospheric conditions at 20 °C and 101.3 kPa, giving an air density of 1.204 kg·m⁻³ and a dynamic viscosity of 1.81 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s.  
Assume the water density is 1000 kg·m⁻³ and gravitational acceleration is 9.81 m·s⁻².  
Use a constant drag coefficient Cd = 0.5 for the drag force.  

Compute the terminal velocity vₜ by equating the gravitational force on the drop to the drag force:  

Weight = (4/3) π r³ ρ_water g  

Drag = ½ Cd ρ_air A vₜ², where A = π r².  

Solve for vₜ:  

vₜ = √[ ( (8/3) r ρ_water g ) / (Cd ρ_air) ]  

Substitute r = 0.001 m, ρ_water = 1000 kg·m⁻³, g = 9.81 m·s⁻², Cd = 0.5, and ρ_air = 1.204 kg·m⁻³.  

vₜ = √[ ( (8/3) × 0.001 × 1000 × 9.81 ) / (0.5 × 1.204) ] ≈ 6.6 m·s⁻¹.  

Report the terminal velocity of the 2 mm diameter raindrop as approximately 6.6 m/s.
