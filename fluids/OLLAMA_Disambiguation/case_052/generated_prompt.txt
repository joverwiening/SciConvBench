Determine the viscosity of the fluid at a temperature of 20 °C (standard laboratory room temperature).  
Assume the inner diameter of the viscometer tube is 10 mm, which provides a tube‑to‑ball diameter ratio of about 3.3 and satisfies best‑practice recommendations for minimal wall effects.  
Use a steel ball with a nominal diameter of 3 mm and a density of 7850 kg/m³.  
Measure the time required for the ball to fall a known distance (e.g., 100 mm) after reaching terminal velocity, and calculate the terminal velocity as distance divided by measured time.  
Apply Stokes’ law corrected for the finite tube diameter:  

η = ( (ρ_ball − ρ_fluid) · g · d² ) / (18 · v) · [1 + 2.4 · (d/D)],  

where η is the dynamic viscosity, ρ_ball and ρ_fluid are the densities of the ball and fluid respectively, g = 9.81 m/s², d is the ball diameter, D is the tube inner diameter, and v is the terminal velocity.  

Report the calculated viscosity in Pa·s (or mPa·s) and include the measured fall time, calculated terminal velocity, and any correction factors used.
