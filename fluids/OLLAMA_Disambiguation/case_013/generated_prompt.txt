Assume the pipe outer radius is 0.05 m.  
Assume the external convective heat‑transfer coefficient is 10 W/(m²·K).  
Use the given insulation thermal conductivity k = 0.17 W/(m·K).  
Calculate the critical radius for insulation as r_crit = k / h = 0.17 / 10 = 0.017 m.  
Determine the critical insulation thickness as the difference between the critical radius and the pipe outer radius: t_crit = r_crit − r_pipe = 0.017 m − 0.05 m = ‑0.033 m.  
Since the result is negative, the pipe radius already exceeds the critical radius; therefore the critical insulation thickness is effectively zero (any added insulation will reduce heat loss).
