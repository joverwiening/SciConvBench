Determine the average Nusselt number for air flowing over a flat plate of length 1.5 m with a free‑stream velocity of 10 m/s, free‑stream temperature of 20 °C, and ambient pressure of 101 kPa (standard atmospheric pressure).  

Use the following procedure:  

1. Assume constant thermophysical properties of air at 20 °C and 101 kPa: density ≈ 1.204 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity ≈ 1.81 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s, thermal conductivity ≈ 0.0257 W/m·K, and Prandtl number ≈ 0.71.  

2. Compute the Reynolds number based on the plate length:  
   Re_L = ρ U L / μ.  

3. Identify the transition Reynolds number for a flat plate (Re_tr ≈ 5 × 10⁵).  
   - If Re_L < 5 × 10⁵, the flow is entirely laminar.  
   - If Re_L > 5 × 10⁵, the flow is laminar from the leading edge to x_tr = Re_tr μ / (ρ U) and turbulent from x_tr to L.  

4. For the laminar region, use the local Nusselt correlation for a constant‑temperature plate:  
   Nu_x(lam) = 0.332 Re_x^{1/2} Pr^{1/3}.  

5. For the turbulent region, use the local Nusselt correlation for a constant‑temperature plate:  
   Nu_x(turb) = 0.0296 Re_x^{4/5} Pr^{1/3}.  

6. Integrate the local Nusselt number over each region to obtain the average Nusselt number for that region:  
   - Laminar average: Nū_lam = (2 × 0.332 Pr^{1/3}) / L ∫₀^{x_tr} (Re_x)^{1/2} dx.  
   - Turbulent average: Nū_turb = (5 × 0.0296 Pr^{1/3}) / L ∫_{x_tr}^{L} (Re_x)^{4/5} dx.  

7. Sum the contributions weighted by their respective length fractions to obtain the overall average Nusselt number:  
   Nū_total = (Nū_lam × x_tr + Nū_turb × (L − x_tr)) / L.  

8. Perform the calculations with the given numerical values:  

   - Re_L ≈ 9.97 × 10⁵ (turbulent flow).  
   - Transition location x_tr ≈ 0.75 m.  
   - Compute Nū_lam and Nū_turb using the integrals, then combine.  

9. Report the final average Nusselt number (Nū_total) as a dimensionless value.
