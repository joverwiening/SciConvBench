Calculate the Taylor number for flow between two concentric rotating cylinders.

The inner cylinder has a radius of 5 cm (0.05 m).
The gap between the cylinders is 2 mm (0.002 m).
The outer cylinder radius is therefore 0.052 m (inner radius plus gap).
The inner cylinder rotates at 100 RPM; convert this to angular velocity: ω = 100 × 2π / 60 ≈ 10.472 rad/s.
The dynamic viscosity of the fluid is 0.01 Pa·s.
Assume the fluid density is 1000 kg/m³ (water, as a standard best-practice assumption for unspecified liquids).
Calculate kinematic viscosity as ν = dynamic viscosity / density = 0.01 / 1000 = 1 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s.
Use the Taylor number formula: Ta = (ω² × r_i × d³) / ν², where r_i is the inner cylinder radius and d is the gap width.
Substitute values: Ta = (10.472² × 0.05 × 0.002³) / (1 × 10⁻⁵)².
Compute the numerator: 10.472² = 109.66; 0.002³ = 8 × 10⁻⁹; numerator = 109.66 × 0.05 × 8 × 10⁻⁹ = 4.386 × 10⁻⁸.
Compute the denominator: (1 × 10⁻⁵)² = 1 × 10⁻¹⁰.
Divide to get Ta = 4.386 × 10⁻⁸ / 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ = 438.6.
Report the final Taylor number as approximately 438.6.
Note that the critical Taylor number for onset of Taylor vortex flow is typically around 1708; since Ta ≈ 438.6 is below this threshold, the flow remains in the stable Couette flow regime.
State all assumed values clearly in the output, including the assumed fluid density of 1000 kg/m³.
