Calculate the speed of sound in a gas mixture of 70 mol% Helium (He) and 30 mol% Argon (Ar) at a temperature of 300 K.
Assume the gas mixture is at standard atmospheric pressure (101325 Pa), as this is the standard best-practice assumption for such calculations.
Treat the gas mixture as an ideal gas.
Use the formula for the speed of sound: v = sqrt(gamma_mix * R * T / M_mix), where R is the universal gas constant (8.314 J/mol·K), T is the temperature in Kelvin, gamma_mix is the mixture's adiabatic index, and M_mix is the mixture's molar mass.
Calculate M_mix as the mole-fraction-weighted average of the molar masses of He (4.0026 g/mol) and Ar (39.948 g/mol): M_mix = 0.70 * 4.0026 + 0.30 * 39.948.
Calculate gamma_mix using the mole-fraction-weighted average of the molar heat capacities Cp and Cv for each component, then take their ratio.
He is monatomic with Cp = (5/2)R and Cv = (3/2)R.
Ar is monatomic with Cp = (5/2)R and Cv = (3/2)R.
Since both gases are monatomic, gamma_mix = 5/3 (no weighted averaging needed as both components have the same gamma).
Compute M_mix = 0.70 * 4.0026 + 0.30 * 39.948 = 2.80182 + 11.9844 = 14.786 g/mol = 0.014786 kg/mol.
Compute v = sqrt((5/3) * 8.314 * 300 / 0.014786).
Report the final speed of sound in meters per second (m/s).
