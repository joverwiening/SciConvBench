Calculate the pressure drop in a horizontal pipe using the Darcy-Weisbach equation.
The pipe is 100 meters long.
The pipe has an internal diameter of 50 mm (0.05 m).
The pipe is horizontal, so elevation head difference is zero.
The fluid is water at standard conditions (density = 1000 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity = 0.001 Pa·s).
The volumetric flow rate is 2 L/s (0.002 m³/s).
Compute the cross-sectional area of the pipe as A = π/4 × D².
Compute the flow velocity as V = Q / A.
Compute the Reynolds number as Re = (ρ × V × D) / μ.
The pipe material is galvanized iron with an absolute roughness ε = 0.15 mm.
Compute the relative roughness as ε/D = 0.15 / 50 = 0.003.
Use the Colebrook-White equation (or Moody chart) to determine the Darcy friction factor f, using Re and ε/D.
Apply the Darcy-Weisbach equation: ΔP = f × (L/D) × (ρ × V²) / 2.
Report the final pressure drop ΔP in Pascals (Pa) and also convert it to kPa.
Ignore minor losses (no fittings, bends, or valves are considered).
