Compute the drag coefficient of a sphere in creeping flow using the Stokes drag law.

The flow regime is creeping (Stokes) flow, defined by a very low Reynolds number (Re = 0.1).
Use the Stokes drag formula for a sphere: F_drag = 3 * π * μ * D * U, where μ is the dynamic viscosity, D is the sphere diameter, and U is the free-stream velocity.
The drag coefficient is defined as Cd = F_drag / (0.5 * ρ * U² * A), where A is the projected frontal area of the sphere A = π * D² / 4.
Substitute the Stokes drag force into the Cd definition to obtain the analytical expression: Cd = 24 / Re.
Apply the given Reynolds number Re = 0.1 directly into the formula.
Calculate: Cd = 24 / 0.1 = 240.
The drag coefficient of the sphere at Re = 0.1 in creeping flow is Cd = 240.
This result is valid because Re = 0.1 satisfies the creeping flow assumption (Re << 1), where viscous forces dominate and inertial effects are negligible.
No numerical simulation or iterative method is required; the Stokes analytical solution is exact for this regime.
