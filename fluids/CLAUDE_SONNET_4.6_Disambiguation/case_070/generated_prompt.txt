Compute the drag force acting on a sphere moving through glycerin using Stokes' Law or the general drag force equation, depending on the flow regime.

Given parameters:
Sphere diameter is 5 mm (0.005 m), so the radius is 0.0025 m.
Sphere velocity is 0.1 m/s.
Glycerin density is 1260 kg/m³.
Glycerin dynamic viscosity is 1.5 Pa·s.

Step 1: Compute the Reynolds number using Re = (density × velocity × diameter) / dynamic viscosity.
Re = (1260 × 0.1 × 0.005) / 1.5.
Re = 0.63 / 1.5 = 0.42.

Step 2: Since Re is much less than 1, the flow is in the Stokes (creeping flow) regime.
Apply Stokes' Law: F_drag = 3 × π × dynamic viscosity × diameter × velocity.
F_drag = 3 × π × 1.5 × 0.005 × 0.1.

Step 3: Calculate the result.
F_drag = 3 × 3.14159 × 1.5 × 0.005 × 0.1.
F_drag ≈ 0.007069 N (approximately 7.07 × 10⁻³ N).

Report the Reynolds number to confirm the Stokes regime assumption is valid.
Report the final drag force in Newtons.
If the Reynolds number had exceeded 1, use the general drag force formula F = 0.5 × Cd × density × velocity² × cross-sectional area with an appropriate drag coefficient Cd instead.
