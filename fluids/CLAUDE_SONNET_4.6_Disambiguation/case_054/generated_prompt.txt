Calculate the average Nusselt number for air flowing over a flat plate of length 1.5 m.
The fluid is air at a free-stream temperature of 20°C (293.15 K).
Assume a flat plate surface temperature of 60°C, which is a standard assumption for moderate forced convection problems when surface temperature is unspecified.
Evaluate all air thermophysical properties at the film temperature, which is the average of the surface and free-stream temperatures: T_film = (20 + 60) / 2 = 40°C (313.15 K).
Use the following air properties at 40°C: density ρ = 1.127 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity μ = 1.906 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s, thermal conductivity k = 0.02756 W/m·K, Prandtl number Pr = 0.7255, kinematic viscosity ν = 1.696 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s.
The free-stream air velocity is U = 10 m/s.
The flat plate length is L = 1.5 m.
Assume the plate is treated as a uniform surface temperature boundary condition.
Calculate the Reynolds number at the end of the plate using Re_L = U × L / ν.
Use the critical Reynolds number Re_cr = 5 × 10⁵ to define the laminar-to-turbulent transition point.
Since Re_L exceeds Re_cr, treat the boundary layer as mixed (laminar from leading edge to transition point, turbulent thereafter).
Apply the standard mixed boundary layer average Nusselt number correlation: Nu_avg = (0.037 × Re_L^0.8 − 871) × Pr^(1/3).
This correlation is valid for 5 × 10⁵ < Re_L < 10⁷ and 0.6 < Pr < 60.
Compute the average convective heat transfer coefficient using h = Nu_avg × k / L.
Report the final values of Re_L, Nu_avg, and h with appropriate units.
