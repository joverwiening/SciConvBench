Calculate the rate of heat transfer through a concrete wall using Fourier's Law of Heat Conduction: Q = k * A * ΔT / d.
The thermal conductivity (k) of the concrete is 1.4 W/(m·K).
The total surface area (A) of the wall is 15 m².
The temperature difference (ΔT) across the wall is 20°C (equivalent to 20 K).
The thickness (d) of the wall is 0.2 m.
Substitute the values into the formula: Q = 1.4 * 15 * 20 / 0.2.
Compute the numerator: 1.4 * 15 * 20 = 420 W·m.
Divide by the thickness: 420 / 0.2 = 2100 W.
The rate of heat transfer through the concrete wall is 2100 W (or 2.1 kW).
Assume steady-state, one-dimensional heat conduction with uniform material properties throughout the wall.
Assume no heat loss from the edges of the wall; all heat transfer occurs perpendicular to the wall surface.
