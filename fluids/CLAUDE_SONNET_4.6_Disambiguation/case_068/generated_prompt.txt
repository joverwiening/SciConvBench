Calculate the boundary layer (boundary) work done by 2 kg of nitrogen gas expanding isothermally in a piston-cylinder device.

The gas is nitrogen with a mass of 2 kg.
The specific gas constant R is 0.2968 kJ/kg·K.
The process is isothermal, so temperature T is constant at 300 K.
The initial pressure P1 is 500 kPa.
The final pressure P2 is 100 kPa.

Use the ideal gas relation to find the initial volume: V1 = mRT/P1 = (2 × 0.2968 × 300) / 500.
Compute V1 = 178.08 / 500 = 0.35616 m³.

Use the ideal gas relation to find the final volume: V2 = mRT/P2 = (2 × 0.2968 × 300) / 100.
Compute V2 = 178.08 / 100 = 1.7808 m³.

For an isothermal process with an ideal gas, the boundary work formula is W_b = mRT × ln(V2/V1).
Substitute the values: W_b = (2 × 0.2968 × 300) × ln(1.7808 / 0.35616).
Compute the volume ratio: V2/V1 = 1.7808 / 0.35616 = 5.0 (which equals P1/P2 = 500/100, as expected for an isothermal process).
Compute ln(5.0) ≈ 1.6094.
Compute W_b = 178.08 × 1.6094 ≈ 286.6 kJ.

The boundary work done by the gas during this isothermal expansion is approximately 286.6 kJ.
Since the gas expands (V2 > V1), the work is positive, meaning the gas does work on the surroundings.
