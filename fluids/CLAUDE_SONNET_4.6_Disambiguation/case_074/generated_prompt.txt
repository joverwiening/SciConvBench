Calculate the degree of reaction for an axial flow compressor stage.
The air enters the rotor with a relative velocity (W1) of 150 m/s at an angle of 45°.
The air leaves the rotor with a relative velocity (W2) of 100 m/s at an angle of 30°.
All angles are measured from the axial direction.
Use the standard definition of degree of reaction: R = (static enthalpy rise across rotor) / (stagnation enthalpy rise across stage).
Express the degree of reaction using velocity triangle relationships: R = 1 - (Vw1 + Vw2) / (2 * U), where Vw1 and Vw2 are the whirl components of absolute velocity at rotor inlet and outlet, and U is the blade speed.
Alternatively, use the rotor-relative form: R = (W1² - W2²) / (2 * (W1² - W2²) + ... ) based on the velocity triangles derived from the given relative velocities and angles.
Compute the axial component of velocity at rotor inlet: Va1 = W1 * cos(45°) = 150 * cos(45°).
Compute the axial component of velocity at rotor exit: Va2 = W2 * cos(30°) = 100 * cos(30°).
Compute the whirl component of relative velocity at rotor inlet: Vwr1 = W1 * sin(45°) = 150 * sin(45°).
Compute the whirl component of relative velocity at rotor exit: Vwr2 = W2 * sin(30°) = 100 * sin(30°).
Apply the degree of reaction formula based on relative velocities: R = (Vwr1 + Vwr2) / (2 * U), where U is the blade speed derived from the velocity triangles.
If blade speed U is not separately given, express the degree of reaction in terms of the given relative velocity components as: R = (W1² - W2²) / (2 * U * Va * (tan β1 + tan β2)) or the simplified form R = (tan β1 + tan β2) / (2 * tan β1 + 2 * tan β2) depending on assumptions of constant axial velocity.
Assume constant axial velocity through the stage if Va1 ≠ Va2 creates inconsistency, and note any such assumption clearly.
Present the final numerical value of the degree of reaction as a dimensionless number.
