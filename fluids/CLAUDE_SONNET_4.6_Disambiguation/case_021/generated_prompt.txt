Calculate the thermal stress in a fully constrained steel rod subjected to a temperature drop of 40°C.

Use Young's modulus E = 200 GPa (200 × 10⁹ Pa).
Use the coefficient of thermal expansion α = 12 × 10⁻⁶ /°C.
Use the temperature change ΔT = -40°C (negative because it is a drop).
Assume the rod is fully constrained at both ends, meaning zero thermal strain is allowed (no deformation).
Apply the thermal stress formula: σ = -E × α × ΔT.
Substitute the values: σ = -(200 × 10⁹) × (12 × 10⁻⁶) × (-40).
Compute the result: σ = 200 × 10⁹ × 12 × 10⁻⁶ × 40 = 96 × 10⁶ Pa = 96 MPa.
The resulting thermal stress is 96 MPa in tension.
The stress is tensile because a temperature drop causes the rod to want to contract, but the constraints prevent this, inducing a pulling (tensile) force along the rod's axis.
Report the final answer as σ = +96 MPa (tensile).
