Determine the dynamic viscosity of a fluid using a falling-ball viscometer method.
Use a steel ball with a diameter of 3 mm (radius = 0.0015 m).
Use a steel ball density of 7850 kg/m³.
Use a fluid density of 1000 kg/m³.
Use a terminal velocity of 0.05 m/s.
Apply Stokes' Law formula: η = (2 × r² × (ρ_ball - ρ_fluid) × g) / (9 × v).
Use gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².
Compute the density difference as 7850 - 1000 = 6850 kg/m³.
Compute r² as (0.0015)² = 2.25 × 10⁻⁶ m².
Compute the numerator as 2 × 2.25 × 10⁻⁶ × 6850 × 9.81 = 0.30280 (approximately).
Compute the denominator as 9 × 0.05 = 0.45.
Divide numerator by denominator to obtain the dynamic viscosity η.
Report the final dynamic viscosity value in units of Pascal-seconds (Pa·s).
Verify that the Reynolds number (Re = ρ_fluid × v × 2r / η) is less than 0.1 to confirm Stokes' Law is valid for this calculation.
