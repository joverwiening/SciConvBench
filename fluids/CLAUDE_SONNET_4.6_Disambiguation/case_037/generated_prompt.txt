Calculate the thermal efficiency of an ideal Rankine cycle using the following conditions and steps.

The working fluid is water.
The boiler pressure is 4 MPa.
The condenser pressure is 10 kPa.
The turbine inlet state is saturated vapor at 4 MPa (quality x = 1).
The cycle is ideal, meaning the turbine and pump are both isentropic.

Step 1: Determine the state at the turbine inlet (State 1).
Look up saturated steam properties at 4 MPa: find h1 (enthalpy of saturated vapor) and s1 (entropy of saturated vapor).

Step 2: Determine the state at the turbine exit (State 2).
Assume isentropic expansion: s2 = s1.
The turbine exhausts to the condenser at 10 kPa.
Look up saturated liquid and vapor entropies (sf, sfg) and enthalpies (hf, hfg) at 10 kPa.
Calculate the quality at State 2: x2 = (s2 - sf) / sfg.
Calculate h2 = hf + x2 * hfg.

Step 3: Determine the state at the condenser exit (State 3).
State 3 is saturated liquid at 10 kPa.
Look up hf at 10 kPa: this is h3.
Look up the specific volume of saturated liquid vf at 10 kPa: this is v3.

Step 4: Determine the state at the pump exit (State 4).
Assume isentropic pump operation.
Calculate pump work: w_pump = v3 * (P_boiler - P_condenser) = v3 * (4,000 - 10) kPa.
Calculate h4 = h3 + w_pump.

Step 5: Calculate the net work and heat input.
Turbine work: w_turbine = h1 - h2.
Net work: w_net = w_turbine - w_pump.
Heat input in boiler: q_in = h1 - h4.

Step 6: Calculate the thermal efficiency.
Thermal efficiency = w_net / q_in.
Express the result as a percentage.

Use standard steam tables (e.g., from Cengel & Boles thermodynamics textbook) for all property lookups.
