Calculate the torque required to rotate a disk near a wall in a viscous fluid using the viscous shear stress model.

The disk has a radius R = 0.2 m.
The fluid dynamic viscosity is mu = 0.1 Pa·s.
The gap between the disk and the wall is h = 0.001 m.
The rotational speed is 60 RPM, which converts to omega = 60 × (2π/60) = 2π rad/s ≈ 6.2832 rad/s.

Assume the gap h is small and uniform, so the velocity profile across the gap is linear (Couette flow assumption).
Assume the disk is a solid flat circular disk rotating about its central axis perpendicular to the wall.
Assume the flow is laminar and steady-state.
Ignore edge effects at the rim of the disk.

At a radial position r, the local tangential velocity of the disk surface is v(r) = omega × r.
The shear stress at radial position r is tau(r) = mu × v(r) / h = mu × omega × r / h.
The elemental torque contribution from an annular ring of width dr at radius r is dT = r × tau(r) × 2π × r × dr = 2π × mu × omega × r³ / h × dr.
Integrate dT from r = 0 to r = R to get total torque T = 2π × mu × omega × R⁴ / (4 × h).
Simplify to T = π × mu × omega × R⁴ / (2 × h).

Substitute values: T = π × 0.1 × 2π × (0.2)⁴ / (2 × 0.001).
Compute (0.2)⁴ = 0.0016 m⁴.
Compute numerator: π × 0.1 × 2π × 0.0016 = 2π² × 0.00016 ≈ 0.003159.
Compute denominator: 2 × 0.001 = 0.002.
The required torque is T ≈ 0.003159 / 0.002 ≈ 1.58 × 10⁻³ N·m (approximately 1.58 mN·m).
