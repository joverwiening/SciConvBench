Compute the convective heat transfer coefficient for air flowing over a flat plate using the following specification.

The flat plate is 1 meter long (L = 1 m).
Air flows over the plate at a free-stream velocity of 5 m/s.
Assume a free-stream air temperature of 25°C (T_infinity = 298.15 K) and a plate surface temperature of 75°C (T_s = 348.15 K), which are standard reference conditions in external forced convection problems.
Evaluate all air properties at the film temperature, T_film = (T_infinity + T_s) / 2 = 50°C (323.15 K).
Use air properties at 50°C: density ρ = 1.093 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity μ = 1.963 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s, thermal conductivity k = 0.02826 W/m·K, Prandtl number Pr = 0.7228, kinematic viscosity ν = 1.796 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s.
Compute the Reynolds number at the end of the plate: Re_L = (U × L) / ν = (5 × 1) / (1.796 × 10⁻⁵).
Determine whether the flow is laminar or turbulent by comparing Re_L to the critical Reynolds number Re_cr = 5 × 10⁵.
If Re_L < 5 × 10⁵, the flow is entirely laminar over the plate; use the laminar average Nusselt number correlation: Nu_avg = 0.664 × Re_L^(1/2) × Pr^(1/3).
If Re_L >= 5 × 10⁵, the flow is mixed (laminar-to-turbulent transition); use the mixed boundary layer correlation: Nu_avg = (0.037 × Re_L^(4/5) − 871) × Pr^(1/3).
Compute the average convective heat transfer coefficient using: h = Nu_avg × k / L.
Report the final value of h in units of W/m²·K.
Report the Reynolds number, the flow regime, the average Nusselt number, and the average heat transfer coefficient as outputs.
