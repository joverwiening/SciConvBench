Calculate the cavitation number (sigma) for water flowing at 20 m/s.
Use the standard cavitation number formula: sigma = (P - Pv) / (0.5 * rho * V^2).
The local pressure P is 50 kPa (50,000 Pa).
The vapor pressure Pv is 2.3 kPa (2,300 Pa).
The flow velocity V is 20 m/s.
Assume the density of water rho is 998 kg/m³ (standard value for liquid water at approximately 20°C).
Compute the dynamic pressure as 0.5 * 998 * (20)^2 = 199,600 Pa.
Compute the pressure difference as 50,000 - 2,300 = 47,700 Pa.
Divide the pressure difference by the dynamic pressure: sigma = 47,700 / 199,600.
Report the final cavitation number as approximately 0.239.
State whether cavitation is likely by noting that lower cavitation numbers indicate higher cavitation risk, and a value below 1 suggests the flow is susceptible to cavitation.
