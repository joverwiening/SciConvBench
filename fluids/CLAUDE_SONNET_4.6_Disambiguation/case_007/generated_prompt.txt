Calculate the heat loss per unit length from a horizontal insulated steam pipe using cylindrical heat transfer analysis.

The pipe carries steam at 150°C and is assumed to have negligible inner wall resistance, so the inner pipe surface is at 150°C.
The pipe is a 2-inch nominal steel pipe; use a standard outer diameter of 60.3 mm (0.0603 m).
The steel pipe wall thermal resistance is negligible compared to insulation and convection, so ignore the pipe wall conduction resistance.
The insulation is glass wool with a thickness of 25 mm and thermal conductivity k = 0.04 W/m·K.
The outer radius of the insulation is (0.0603/2 + 0.025) = 0.0552 m.
The inner radius of the insulation equals the pipe outer radius = 0.0603/2 = 0.03015 m.
The ambient air temperature is 20°C.
The external convective heat transfer coefficient is h = 10 W/m²·K.
Ignore radiation heat loss from the outer insulation surface.
Calculate the thermal resistance of the insulation per unit length using R_ins = ln(r_outer/r_inner) / (2π × k).
Calculate the thermal resistance of external convection per unit length using R_conv = 1 / (2π × r_outer × h).
Calculate total resistance per unit length as R_total = R_ins + R_conv.
Calculate heat loss per unit length as q = (T_steam - T_ambient) / R_total, expressed in W/m.
Report the final result as heat loss per unit length of pipe in W/m.
