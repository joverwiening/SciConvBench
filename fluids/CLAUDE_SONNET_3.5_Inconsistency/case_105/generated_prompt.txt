Sutherland's law is specifically designed for gases and describes how gas viscosity varies with temperature.
Liquid viscosity and gas viscosity are fundamentally different phenomena with different molecular mechanisms.
Liquid water viscosity is dominated by intermolecular forces and hydrogen bonding, while gas viscosity comes from molecular momentum transfer.
The correct viscosity of liquid water at 20°C is 1.002 mPa·s (millipascal-seconds) or 1.002 cP (centipoise).
This value should be obtained from experimental data tables for liquid water rather than using gas viscosity formulas.
For accurate liquid water viscosity at other temperatures, use water-specific empirical equations or interpolate from standard reference tables.
