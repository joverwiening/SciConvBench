Calculate the critical insulation thickness for a cylindrical pipe using the following parameters and assumptions.

The thermal conductivity of the insulation is 0.17 W/m·K.

The convective heat transfer coefficient on the outside surface of the insulation is 8 W/m²·K.

The outer radius of the bare pipe (radius to which insulation is added) is assumed to be 0.05 m, representing a typical medium-sized industrial pipe (approximately 4 inch nominal pipe size).

The thermal conductivity of the pipe material is assumed to be 45 W/m·K, representing carbon steel, which is common for industrial piping.

The inner temperature of the pipe is assumed to be 150°C, representing typical hot water or low-pressure steam applications.

The ambient temperature surrounding the insulated pipe is assumed to be 25°C, representing standard indoor or mild outdoor conditions.

The inner radius of the pipe is assumed to be 0.046 m, consistent with standard pipe dimensions where wall thickness creates the difference between inner and outer radii.

The critical radius of insulation for a cylinder is calculated as r_critical = k_insulation / h_convection.

The critical radius equals 0.17 W/m·K divided by 8 W/m²·K, which equals 0.02125 m.

The critical insulation thickness equals the critical radius minus the outer radius of the bare pipe.

The critical insulation thickness equals 0.02125 m minus 0.05 m, which equals -0.02875 m.

Since the critical radius (0.02125 m) is less than the outer pipe radius (0.05 m), there is no positive critical insulation thickness for this configuration.

Adding any amount of insulation will reduce heat loss rather than increase it, because the pipe radius already exceeds the critical radius.

Report that no critical insulation thickness exists for this pipe configuration, as the pipe outer radius is already beyond the critical radius.
