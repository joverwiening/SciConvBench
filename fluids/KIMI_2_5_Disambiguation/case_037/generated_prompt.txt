Calculate the thermal efficiency of an ideal Rankine cycle with the following parameters.

The boiler pressure is 4 MPa.

The condenser pressure is 10 kPa.

The turbine inlet temperature is 400 degrees Celsius, which is a typical superheat temperature for this pressure level based on standard power plant practice.

The turbine is ideal with 100% isentropic efficiency.

The pump is ideal with 100% isentropic efficiency.

Use steam tables to determine all thermodynamic properties at each state point.

State 1 is saturated liquid at the condenser pressure of 10 kPa.

State 2 is the compressed liquid leaving the pump at the boiler pressure of 4 MPa.

State 3 is superheated steam at 4 MPa and 400 degrees Celsius entering the turbine.

State 4 is the steam leaving the turbine at 10 kPa after isentropic expansion.

Calculate pump work as h2 minus h1, where h2 equals h1 plus v1 times (P2 minus P1), with v1 being the specific volume of saturated liquid at state 1.

Calculate turbine work as h3 minus h4, where s4 equals s3 for isentropic expansion.

Calculate heat input in the boiler as h3 minus h2.

Calculate thermal efficiency as net work output divided by heat input, or (turbine work minus pump work) divided by heat input.

Report the final efficiency as a percentage rounded to two decimal places.
