The drag force acting on a sphere moving through glycerin shall be computed using the following parameters and methodology.

The sphere diameter is 5 mm (0.005 m).

The sphere velocity is 0.1 m/s.

The glycerin temperature is assumed to be 20°C, which is a standard reference temperature for fluid property data and laboratory conditions.

The sphere material is assumed to be steel, which is a common material for spheres in fluid dynamics experiments and applications.

The sphere density is assumed to be 7850 kg/m³, which is the standard density of carbon steel.

The drag coefficient model is Stokes' law: Cd = 24/Re for Reynolds number less than 1.

The glycerin density is 1260 kg/m³.

The glycerin dynamic viscosity is 1.5 Pa·s.

The Reynolds number shall be calculated as Re = (ρ_fluid × v × d) / μ, where ρ_fluid is glycerin density, v is velocity, d is diameter, and μ is dynamic viscosity.

The drag force shall be calculated using F_d = 0.5 × ρ_fluid × v² × A × Cd, where A is the cross-sectional area πd²/4, or equivalently using Stokes' drag formula F_d = 3πμdv for the creeping flow regime.
