Calculate the pressure drop in a horizontal pipe using the Darcy-Weisbach equation.
The pipe length is 100 m.
The pipe diameter is 50 mm (0.05 m).
The pipe material is galvanized iron with a roughness of 0.15 mm (0.00015 m).
The fluid is water at 20°C.
The flow rate is 2 L/s (0.002 m³/s).
The kinematic viscosity of water is 1.003 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s.
The density of water is 998 kg/m³.
First, calculate the cross-sectional area of the pipe: A = π × (0.05 m)² / 4 = 0.0019635 m².
Calculate the flow velocity: v = Q / A = 0.002 m³/s / 0.0019635 m² = 1.019 m/s.
Calculate the Reynolds number: Re = v × D / ν = 1.019 m/s × 0.05 m / 1.003 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s = 50,797.
Since Re > 4000, the flow is turbulent.
Calculate the relative roughness: ε/D = 0.00015 m / 0.05 m = 0.003.
Use the Colebrook-White equation or an explicit approximation (e.g., Haaland equation) to find the friction factor.
Using the Haaland equation: 1/√f = -1.8 × log₁₀[(ε/D)/3.7)^1.11 + 6.9/Re].
Calculate the friction factor f ≈ 0.0286.
Calculate the head loss using Darcy-Weisbach: hL = f × (L/D) × (v²/2g) = 0.0286 × (100/0.05) × (1.019² / (2 × 9.81)) = 3.03 m.
Calculate the pressure drop: ΔP = ρ × g × hL = 998 kg/m³ × 9.81 m/s² × 3.03 m = 29,658 Pa ≈ 29.7 kPa.
Alternatively, express the result as a head loss of approximately 3.03 m of water column.
