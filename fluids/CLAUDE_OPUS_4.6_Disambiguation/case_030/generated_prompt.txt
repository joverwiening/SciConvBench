Calculate the Reynolds number of a water droplet falling through air at terminal velocity.
The droplet diameter is 1 mm (0.001 m).
The kinematic viscosity of air is given as ν = 1.5e-5 m²/s.
Use standard conditions (20°C, 1 atm) for all other fluid properties: density of air ≈ 1.204 kg/m³, density of water ≈ 998 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity of air ≈ 1.81e-5 Pa·s.
Determine the terminal velocity as part of the calculation by balancing the drag force against the net gravitational force (buoyancy-corrected weight) on the droplet.
Use an iterative approach with the standard drag curve (e.g., start with Stokes' law or an empirical drag correlation such as the Schiller-Naumann correlation) to find terminal velocity, since the Reynolds number may not be in the Stokes regime.
Verify that the final Reynolds number is consistent with the drag model or correlation used.
The Reynolds number is defined as Re = V·D / ν, where V is the terminal velocity, D is the droplet diameter, and ν is the kinematic viscosity of air.
