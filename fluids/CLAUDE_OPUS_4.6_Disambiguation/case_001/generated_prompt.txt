Calculate the pressure drop in a horizontal pipe using the Darcy-Weisbach equation.
The pipe length is 100 m.
The pipe internal diameter is 50 mm.
The pipe material is galvanized iron with an absolute roughness of 0.15 mm.
The fluid is water at 20°C.
The water density is 998 kg/m³.
The water dynamic viscosity is 1×10⁻³ Pa·s.
The volumetric flow rate is 2 L/s.
Determine the flow velocity from the flow rate and pipe cross-sectional area.
Calculate the Reynolds number to determine the flow regime.
Determine the Darcy friction factor using the Colebrook-White equation (or Moody chart) with the relative roughness (0.15 mm / 50 mm) and the calculated Reynolds number.
Compute the pressure drop using the Darcy-Weisbach equation: ΔP = f × (L/D) × (ρ × V²/2).
The pipe is horizontal, so no elevation head correction is needed.
Report the final pressure drop in Pascals and also convert to kPa for convenience.
