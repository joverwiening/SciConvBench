Calculate the Taylor number for flow between two concentric rotating cylinders (Taylor-Couette flow).
The inner cylinder has a radius of 5 cm (0.05 m).
The gap width between the inner and outer cylinders is 2 mm (0.002 m).
The outer cylinder radius is therefore 0.052 m.
The inner cylinder rotates at 100 rpm.
The outer cylinder is stationary.
The dynamic viscosity of the fluid is 0.01 Pa·s.
The fluid density is assumed to be 1000 kg/m³ (water).
The kinematic viscosity is calculated as dynamic viscosity divided by density, giving 1.0e-5 m²/s.
The angular velocity of the inner cylinder is converted from 100 rpm to rad/s as 100 × 2π / 60.
The Taylor number is calculated using the standard definition: Ta = (ω² × R_i × d³) / ν², where ω is the angular velocity of the inner cylinder, R_i is the inner cylinder radius, d is the gap width, and ν is the kinematic viscosity.
Report the resulting Taylor number as a dimensionless value.
State whether the computed Taylor number exceeds the critical Taylor number (approximately 1708) and therefore whether Taylor vortices are expected.
