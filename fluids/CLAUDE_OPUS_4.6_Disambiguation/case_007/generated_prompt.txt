Calculate the heat loss per meter of length from a 2-inch horizontal pipe carrying steam.
The steam temperature inside the pipe is 150°C.
The ambient air temperature surrounding the pipe is 20°C.
The pipe is insulated with 25 mm of glass wool insulation.
The thermal conductivity of the glass wool insulation is k = 0.04 W/(m·K).
The outer surface convective heat transfer coefficient is h = 10 W/(m²·K).
Radiation heat loss is not considered separately; the given h value accounts for the outer surface heat transfer.
The nominal pipe size is 2 inches, so use the standard outer diameter of a 2-inch pipe (approximately 60.3 mm or 0.0603 m).
Assume the thermal resistance of the steel pipe wall is negligible compared to the insulation and outer convection resistances.
Assume the inner convective resistance (steam side) is negligible due to the high heat transfer coefficient of condensing steam.
Use a radial (cylindrical) conduction model for heat transfer through the insulation.
The outer diameter of the insulation is the pipe outer diameter plus twice the insulation thickness.
Calculate the total thermal resistance per meter as the sum of the conduction resistance through the cylindrical insulation layer and the convection resistance at the outer surface.
Report the heat loss in watts per meter of pipe length.
