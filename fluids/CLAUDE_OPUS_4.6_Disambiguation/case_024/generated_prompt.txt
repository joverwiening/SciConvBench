Calculate the Rayleigh number for natural convection along a vertical wall.
The vertical wall height (characteristic length) is 0.5 m.
The wall surface temperature is 80°C.
The ambient air temperature is 25°C.
The surrounding air is at standard atmospheric pressure (1 atm).
Evaluate all air properties at the film temperature of 52.5°C, which is the average of the wall and ambient temperatures.
The Rayleigh number is defined as Ra = Gr × Pr = (g × β × ΔT × L³) / (ν × α), where g is gravitational acceleration, β is the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient, ΔT is the temperature difference (80°C − 25°C = 55°C), L is the wall height, ν is the kinematic viscosity, and α is the thermal diffusivity of air.
For air modeled as an ideal gas, use β = 1 / T_film where T_film is in absolute units (Kelvin), giving β = 1 / (52.5 + 273.15) K.
Use standard tabulated or interpolated thermophysical properties of air at 1 atm and 52.5°C for kinematic viscosity, thermal diffusivity, and Prandtl number.
Report the final Rayleigh number value along with the intermediate property values used in the calculation.
