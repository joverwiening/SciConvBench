The task is to calculate the pressure drop for water flowing through a horizontal pipe using the energy equation with major friction losses.

The pipe is horizontal, 40 m long, and has an inner diameter of 50 mm (0.05 m).
The flow is fully developed with a mean velocity of 2.0 m/s.
The fluid is water at 20°C with density rho = 998 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity mu = 1.0×10⁻³ Pa·s.
The pipe is smooth.
Minor losses are neglected; only major (friction) losses are considered.

First, calculate the Reynolds number using Re = rho · v · D / mu.
Use Re to determine whether the flow is laminar (Re < 2300) or turbulent (Re >= 2300).
If laminar, use the Darcy friction factor f_D = 64 / Re.
If turbulent, use the Colebrook equation (or Moody chart) for a smooth pipe (roughness = 0) to determine f_D.

Then calculate the pressure drop using the Darcy-Weisbach equation: delta_P = f_D · (L / D) · (rho · v² / 2).

L = 40 m.
D = 0.05 m.
v = 2.0 m/s.
rho = 998 kg/m³.
mu = 1.0×10⁻³ Pa·s.

Since v1 = v2 for constant-diameter fully developed flow, the kinetic energy terms cancel, and the entire pressure drop equals the friction loss term.

Report the Reynolds number, the flow regime (laminar or turbulent), the Darcy friction factor f_D, and the resulting pressure drop in Pascals (and optionally in kPa).
