The analysis considers two scenarios of steady, two-dimensional, incompressible flow over a flat plate with a uniform free-stream velocity U∞. All fluid properties are left in general symbolic form (density ρ, kinematic viscosity ν).

Scenario (a): Viscous fluid with the no-slip boundary condition (u = 0 at the plate surface, y = 0).

The governing equations are the Navier-Stokes equations simplified for boundary layer flow (Prandtl boundary layer equations).
The boundary conditions are u = 0 and v = 0 at y = 0 (no-slip and no-penetration), and u → U∞ as y → ∞.
The primary analysis uses the Blasius similarity solution.
Introduce the similarity variable η = y × sqrt(U∞ / (ν x)), where x is the streamwise distance from the leading edge and y is the wall-normal distance.
Define a dimensionless stream function f(η) such that u = U∞ f′(η).
The Blasius equation is f‴ + (1/2) f f″ = 0, subject to f(0) = 0, f′(0) = 0, and f′(η → ∞) = 1.
This is a nonlinear ODE solved numerically; the solution gives a smooth velocity profile that grows from u = 0 at the wall to u ≈ U∞ at the edge of the boundary layer.
The boundary layer thickness scales as δ(x) ≈ 5.0 × sqrt(ν x / U∞), growing with the square root of the downstream distance.
The wall shear stress is τ_w = μ (∂u/∂y) at y = 0, which is nonzero and proportional to 0.332 ρ U∞² / sqrt(Re_x), where Re_x = U∞ x / ν.
For completeness, an approximate polynomial profile from the von Kármán integral momentum method can also be used, for example u/U∞ ≈ (3/2)(y/δ) − (1/2)(y/δ)³ for 0 ≤ y ≤ δ, which satisfies the no-slip condition and matches the free stream at y = δ. This gives similar qualitative behavior and approximate quantitative results for boundary layer thickness and skin friction.

Scenario (b): Inviscid fluid with the slip boundary condition (no-penetration only, v = 0 at y = 0, but u is unconstrained at the surface).

The governing equations are the Euler equations for inviscid flow.
The only boundary condition at the plate surface (y = 0) is v = 0 (no penetration). There is no constraint on the tangential velocity u at the wall.
For a flat plate aligned with the free stream and with no pressure gradient, the solution is trivially u = U∞ everywhere in the entire flow domain, including at the wall surface y = 0.
There is no boundary layer; the velocity is uniform from the wall to infinity.
The wall shear stress is τ_w = 0 because there is no viscosity (μ = 0) and no velocity gradient at the wall.
The fluid slips freely along the plate surface at the full free-stream velocity.

Contrast between the two scenarios.

In the viscous case, the no-slip condition forces u = 0 at the wall, creating a thin boundary layer region where the velocity transitions from zero at the surface to U∞ at the outer edge. In the inviscid case, no such layer exists and the velocity is U∞ everywhere, including at the wall.
The boundary layer thickness in the viscous case grows as sqrt(ν x / U∞) downstream. In the inviscid case, there is no characteristic thickness because there is no boundary layer.
The viscous case produces a nonzero wall shear stress (skin friction drag) that depends on the Reynolds number. The inviscid case produces zero wall shear stress and zero friction drag, consistent with d'Alembert's paradox for inviscid flow over a body.
The viscous solution requires solving the nonlinear Blasius ODE (or using an approximate integral method). The inviscid solution is trivially uniform flow with no computation needed beyond applying the no-penetration condition.
The fundamental physical reason for the difference is the presence or absence of viscosity. Viscosity is the mechanism that enforces the no-slip condition and creates velocity gradients near the wall. Without viscosity, the fluid has no mechanism to "stick" to the surface, and slip occurs freely.
