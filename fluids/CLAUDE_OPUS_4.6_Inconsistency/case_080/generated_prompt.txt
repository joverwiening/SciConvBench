This specification computes fin heat transfer for a straight rectangular fin with an adiabatic tip using standard assumed parameters consistent with best engineering practices.

Assume the fin material is aluminum with a thermal conductivity k = 200 W/(m·K).
Assume the fin is a straight rectangular fin with length L = 0.05 m (50 mm), width W = 0.10 m (100 mm), and thickness t = 0.002 m (2 mm).
Assume the convection heat transfer coefficient on the fin surface is h = 25 W/(m²·K), representative of natural or mild forced convection in air.
Assume the base temperature is T_base = 100 °C and the surrounding fluid temperature is T_fluid = 25 °C, giving a temperature excess θ_b = 75 °C.

Compute the fin parameter m using the formula m = sqrt((h · P) / (k · A_c)), where P is the perimeter of the fin cross-section and A_c is the cross-sectional area.
For the rectangular cross-section, compute P = 2·(W + t) = 2·(0.10 + 0.002) = 0.204 m.
Compute A_c = W · t = 0.10 · 0.002 = 0.0002 m².
Compute m = sqrt((25 · 0.204) / (200 · 0.0002)) = sqrt(5.1 / 0.04) = sqrt(127.5).

Compute the dimensionless product mL = m · L.

Compute the fin efficiency using the adiabatic-tip formula: η_f = tanh(mL) / (mL).
Verify that the computed η_f falls in the range 0 < η_f ≤ 1. If it does not, flag an error.

Compute the total fin surface area A_f = 2·W·L + W·t = 2·(0.10)·(0.05) + (0.10)·(0.002). The tip area term (W·t) is excluded from heat transfer since the tip is adiabatic, so use A_f = 2·W·L = 0.01 m².

Compute the fin heat transfer rate using q_f = η_f · h · A_f · θ_b.

Report all intermediate values: m, mL, η_f, A_f, and the final fin heat transfer rate q_f in watts.
