Use the Hagen-Poiseuille equation to compute the pressure drop per unit length for laminar flow in a microchannel.
Assume a circular cross-section with a hydraulic diameter of 2 mm, which for a circular tube equals the actual diameter (D = 2 mm).
The working fluid is water at 20 degrees Celsius, with a dynamic viscosity of approximately 1.002e-3 Pa·s and a density of approximately 998 kg/m³.
The Reynolds number is 200.
Compute the mean velocity from the Reynolds number using V = Re * mu / (rho * D).
Compute the pressure drop per unit length using the Hagen-Poiseuille relation: dP/dL = 128 * mu * Q / (pi * D^4), or equivalently dP/dL = 32 * mu * V / D^2.
Express the final result as pressure drop per unit length (Pa/m).
Report the intermediate values (velocity, flow rate if computed) alongside the final pressure drop per unit length.
Note in the output that the original Manning equation was replaced by the Hagen-Poiseuille equation because Manning's equation applies to open-channel flow and is not physically appropriate for closed-conduit laminar internal flow.
