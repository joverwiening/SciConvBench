Q: There is a contradiction in this request. A perfectly incompressible fluid has an infinite bulk modulus, which means the speed of sound would be infinite — not a finite value. Reporting a "finite value" for the speed of sound in a truly incompressible medium is physically impossible.

Would you like me to instead calculate the speed of sound in water at 20°C using its real (finite) bulk modulus and density, which gives the well-known finite result of approximately 1481 m/s?
A: Yes, exactly. Use the formula c = sqrt(K/rho) with bulk modulus K = 2.2 GPa and density rho = 998 kg/m³. Compute c from those values.
