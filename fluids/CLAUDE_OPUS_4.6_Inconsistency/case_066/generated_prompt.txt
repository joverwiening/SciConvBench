The task is to compute the degree of reaction for an axial compressor rotor using the following conditions and procedure.

W1 = 150 m/s and beta1 = 45 degrees at the rotor inlet.
W2 = 100 m/s and beta2 = 30 degrees are originally stated at the rotor exit.
Axial velocity is constant through the rotor.
Use the axial velocity from the inlet conditions: Va = W1 cos(beta1) = 150 cos(45 degrees), which is approximately 106.07 m/s.
To maintain consistency with the constant axial velocity assumption, recalculate W2 as W2 = Va / cos(beta2) = 106.07 / cos(30 degrees), which is approximately 122.47 m/s.
Use this corrected W2 of approximately 122.47 m/s along with beta2 = 30 degrees for all subsequent calculations.
Disregard the originally stated W2 = 100 m/s.
Compute the tangential components of relative velocity: W_theta1 = W1 sin(beta1) and W_theta2 = W2_corrected sin(beta2).
Determine the blade speed U from the velocity triangle relationship: U = Va (tan(alpha1) + ... ) or equivalently from the change in whirl components as appropriate.
Compute the degree of reaction using the standard definition: R = (static enthalpy rise across the rotor) / (stagnation enthalpy rise across the stage).
For an axial compressor with constant axial velocity, this is equivalent to R = (W1^2 - W2^2) / (2 U Va (tan(beta1) - tan(beta2))) or equivalently R = 1 - (Va (tan(alpha1) + tan(alpha2))) / (2 U), using whichever consistent form of the degree of reaction formula applies with the corrected velocity triangle values.
Report the final numerical value of the degree of reaction.
