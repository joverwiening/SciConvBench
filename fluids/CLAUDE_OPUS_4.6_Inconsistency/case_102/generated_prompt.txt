The fluid is air, not water.
The upstream Mach number is M1 = 2.0.
The ratio of specific heats (gamma) is 1.4.
Use the standard ideal-gas normal-shock relations (Rankine-Hugoniot equations).
Express all downstream conditions as ratios relative to upstream values: p2/p1 (static pressure ratio), T2/T1 (static temperature ratio), rho2/rho1 (density ratio), and p02/p01 (total/stagnation pressure ratio).
Compute the downstream Mach number M2.
No specific upstream static pressure or temperature is required; results are reported as dimensionless ratios.
The standard ideal-gas normal-shock equations to use are:
p2/p1 = (2 * gamma * M1^2 - (gamma - 1)) / (gamma + 1).
T2/T1 = ((2 * gamma * M1^2 - (gamma - 1)) * ((gamma - 1) * M1^2 + 2)) / ((gamma + 1)^2 * M1^2).
rho2/rho1 = ((gamma + 1) * M1^2) / ((gamma - 1) * M1^2 + 2).
M2^2 = ((gamma - 1) * M1^2 + 2) / (2 * gamma * M1^2 - (gamma - 1)).
p02/p01 = (rho2/rho1)^(gamma/(gamma-1)) * (p1/p2)^(1/(gamma-1)), or equivalently computed from the standard stagnation pressure ratio formula across a normal shock.
