I need to clarify something important here. In a **counterflow** heat exchanger, the standard LMTD temperature differences are defined as:

- ΔT1 = Th,in - Tc,out
- ΔT2 = Th,out - Tc,in

You are asking me to use the **parallel-flow** definitions instead:

- ΔT1 = Th,in - Tc,in
- ΔT2 = Th,out - Tc,out

For a counterflow heat exchanger, using the parallel-flow LMTD expression with parallel-flow temperature difference definitions will yield an **incorrect** LMTD and therefore an incorrect area. The parallel-flow formula applied with parallel-flow ΔT definitions does not represent the actual temperature driving forces in a counterflow arrangement.

Would you like me to proceed with the physically correct counterflow LMTD formulation (ΔT1 = Th,in - Tc,out, ΔT2 = Th,out - Tc,in), or do you specifically want the parallel-flow definitions as stated, understanding that the result will not be physically accurate for a counterflow exchanger?
A: Use the correct counterflow definitions: ΔT1 = Th,in - Tc,out and ΔT2 = Th,out - Tc,in, then compute LMTD = (ΔT1 - ΔT2) / ln(ΔT1 / ΔT2).
