The simulation involves a converging nozzle discharging air from a large reservoir (tank) to the ambient environment.
The stagnation pressure in the tank (P0) is 500 kPa absolute.
The ambient pressure (Pambient) is 100 kPa absolute.
The stagnation temperature in the tank (T0) is assumed to be 300 K (approximately room temperature), as no value is specified.
The exit area (throat area) of the converging nozzle is assumed to be 10 cm^2 (0.001 m^2), as no value is specified.
Air is treated as an ideal gas with specific heat ratio gamma = 1.4 and gas constant R = 287 J/(kg·K).
The flow is assumed to be isentropic (no friction, no heat transfer) through the nozzle.
First, check for choking by computing the critical pressure ratio, which is 0.528 for air (gamma = 1.4).
Compute the critical back pressure as P_critical = 0.528 * P0 = 0.528 * 500 kPa = 264 kPa.
Compare the ambient pressure (100 kPa) to the critical back pressure (264 kPa).
Since 100 kPa is less than 264 kPa, the nozzle is choked.
Report that the nozzle is choked.
When choked, the exit Mach number is 1.0.
When choked, the exit pressure equals the critical back pressure (264 kPa), not the ambient pressure of 100 kPa.
Note explicitly that the problem statement says "assuming the exit pressure is 100 kPa," but since the nozzle is choked the actual exit pressure is 264 kPa; the calculation proceeds with the physically correct choked exit pressure of 264 kPa.
