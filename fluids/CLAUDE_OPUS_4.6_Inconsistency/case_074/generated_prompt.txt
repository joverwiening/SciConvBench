This is a compressible flow problem for air compressed from 1 bar to 10 bar in a duct.
Use mass continuity with variable density: rho_1 * A_1 * V_1 = rho_2 * A_2 * V_2.
Use the ideal gas equation of state to compute density at each station: rho = P / (R * T), where R = 287 J/(kg·K) for air.
Assume the compression process is isothermal at T = 288.15 K (standard atmosphere, 15 degrees Celsius) throughout the duct, as a reasonable default when no thermodynamic process is specified.
Inlet pressure is P_1 = 1 bar (100,000 Pa).
Outlet pressure is P_2 = 10 bar (1,000,000 Pa).
Assume an inlet velocity of V_1 = 50 m/s, a representative low-Mach subsonic value suitable for duct flow.
Assume a duct area ratio of A_2 / A_1 = 0.5, meaning the outlet area is half the inlet area, representing a converging duct.
Compute inlet density as rho_1 = P_1 / (R * T).
Compute outlet density as rho_2 = P_2 / (R * T).
Solve for outlet velocity using V_2 = (rho_1 * A_1 * V_1) / (rho_2 * A_2).
Report both inlet and outlet densities and both inlet and outlet velocities with units.
Note explicitly in the results that the incompressible (constant density) assumption is invalid for a 10:1 pressure ratio and that variable density via the ideal gas law is required.
