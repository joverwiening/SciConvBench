The task is a thermodynamics calculation for steam condensation at 100 C.
Use a reference mass of 1 kg to keep the calculation general, and also present results per unit mass.
The phase change is condensation of steam at 100 C to liquid water at 100 C.
Explain why applying q = m cp ΔT with cp = 2.0 kJ/(kg·K) and ΔT = 0 is incorrect for this process, since it yields q = 0 and fails to account for the latent heat released during the phase change.
Perform the correct calculation using q = m hfg, where hfg at 100 C is approximately 2257 kJ/kg from standard steam tables.
For m = 1 kg, compute q = 1 kg × 2257 kJ/kg = 2257 kJ.
State that the formula q = m cp ΔT applies only to sensible heating or cooling where temperature changes, not to phase change processes where energy is absorbed or released at constant temperature.
Note that if the liquid condensate is further subcooled below 100 C to some lower temperature T_final, the total heat removed would be q = m hfg + m cp_liquid (100 - T_final), where cp_liquid is the specific heat of liquid water (approximately 4.18 kJ/(kg·K)), combining the latent heat term with the sensible cooling term.
