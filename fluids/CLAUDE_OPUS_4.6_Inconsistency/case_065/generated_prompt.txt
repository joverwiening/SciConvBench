The task is to analyze the cavitation factor (Thoma's sigma) for a Francis turbine runner installed 20 m above the tailrace, and demonstrate whether cavitation is a concern.

The general Thoma cavitation factor formula is σ = (Ha - Hv - Hs - h_loss) / H.

Ha (atmospheric pressure head) = 10.3 m.

Hv (vapor pressure head) = 0.3 m.

Hs (suction head, height of runner above tailrace) = 20 m.

h_loss (friction and minor losses in the draft tube) is assumed to be 0 since no value is provided, but the general formula must still include it.

H is the net head on the turbine, left as a general variable.

Compute the numerator: Ha - Hv - Hs - h_loss = 10.3 - 0.3 - 20 - 0 = -10 m.

Therefore σ = -10 / H, which is negative for any positive net head H.

Explain that a negative σ means the pressure at the runner drops below the vapor pressure of the fluid, making cavitation a real and unavoidable risk at this installation height.

Note that the original problem statement claiming "cavitation is impossible" contains an error or is a trick question; the correct conclusion is the opposite.

State that to avoid cavitation (achieve σ > 0), the suction head Hs must satisfy Hs < Ha - Hv - h_loss, i.e., Hs < 10 m (with h_loss = 0), so the runner must be lowered to less than 10 m above the tailrace, or placed below the tailrace entirely.

Present the general design guideline: reducing Hs (lowering the runner closer to or below the tailrace) or increasing back-pressure from the tailwater are the standard remedies for cavitation risk in Francis turbines.
