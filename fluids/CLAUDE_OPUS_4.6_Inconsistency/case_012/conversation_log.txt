Q: I need to clarify something important about this request before proceeding.

Bernoulli's equation applies along a streamline in an inviscid (frictionless), steady, incompressible flow. Couette flow is fundamentally a viscous flow — the entire velocity profile exists because of viscosity transmitting momentum from the moving plate to the fluid. Applying Bernoulli's equation across streamlines (from the stationary plate to the moving plate) in a viscous flow is physically incorrect and would yield a misleading result.

Could you clarify your intent: are you looking for me to (a) demonstrate *why* Bernoulli's equation is inapplicable here and instead derive the correct pressure variation using the Navier-Stokes equations, (b) apply Bernoulli's equation anyway as a pedagogical exercise to show the (incorrect) result it gives and contrast it with the correct answer, or (c) something else entirely?
