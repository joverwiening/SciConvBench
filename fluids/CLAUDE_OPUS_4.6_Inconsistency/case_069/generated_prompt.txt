The fluid is water with a density of 998 kg/m³ (approximately 1000 kg/m³).
The acceleration due to gravity is 9.81 m/s².
The pipe connects an inlet (point 1) to an outlet (point 2), where the outlet is 15 m higher than the inlet.
The pipe has a constant cross-sectional area, so the velocity at the inlet equals the velocity at the outlet and the velocity terms cancel in Bernoulli's equation.
The flow is steady, incompressible, and inviscid with no friction or minor losses, since no loss data is provided.
Apply the Bernoulli equation between the inlet and outlet: P1 + (1/2) ρ v1² + ρ g z1 = P2 + (1/2) ρ v2² + ρ g z2.
Since v1 = v2, the kinetic energy terms cancel, reducing the equation to P1 - P2 = ρ g (z2 - z1).
Set z2 - z1 = 15 m.
Compute the pressure difference P1 - P2 = ρ g Δz = (1000 kg/m³)(9.81 m/s²)(15 m).
Report the result in Pascals and optionally convert to kilopascals.
The positive result for P1 - P2 indicates the inlet pressure is higher than the outlet pressure, as expected for upward flow.
