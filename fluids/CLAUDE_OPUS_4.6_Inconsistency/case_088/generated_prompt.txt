The task is to produce a general methodology document for computing volumetric flow rate through a Venturi meter with significant irreversible losses.
The flow is incompressible.
The governing formula is Q = Cd × A₂ × sqrt(2 Δp / (ρ (1 − β⁴))).
A₂ is the cross-sectional area of the throat, computed as A₂ = π d₂² / 4.
β is the diameter ratio, defined as β = d₂ / d₁, where d₂ is the throat diameter and d₁ is the upstream pipe diameter.
Δp is the measured differential pressure between the upstream tap and the throat tap.
ρ is the fluid density, assumed constant (incompressible flow).
Cd is the discharge coefficient and must be strictly less than 1 to account for significant irreversible losses in the throat and diffuser.
Cd must not be assumed equal to 1; it should be obtained from experimental calibration or from published empirical correlations for the specific Venturi geometry and flow conditions.
The document should explain that using Cd = 1 (ideal Bernoulli) would overestimate the actual flow rate because it ignores frictional and separation losses.
The document should note that Cd typically ranges from roughly 0.95 to 0.99 for standard Venturi meters, but can be lower when losses are significant.
The methodology should state the assumptions: steady-state flow, incompressible fluid, fully developed flow upstream, and pressure taps located at the conventional upstream and throat positions.
Optionally, a working calculator or program may be provided that accepts d₁, d₂, Δp, ρ, and Cd as inputs and returns the volumetric flow rate Q.
