Compute the drag force on a sphere falling through water using the standard drag equation with an empirical drag coefficient correlation.

The sphere diameter is 5 mm (0.005 m).
The sphere velocity is 0.5 m/s.
The fluid is water at 20°C with density ρ = 998.2 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity μ = 1.002 × 10⁻³ Pa·s.

Step 1: Compute the Reynolds number as Re = ρ V D / μ.

Step 2: Since Re is not much less than 1, do not use Stokes drag. Instead, use the Schiller-Naumann correlation to compute the drag coefficient: Cd = (24 / Re) × (1 + 0.15 × Re^0.687). This correlation is valid for Re up to approximately 1000–2000 and is a reasonable best-practice choice for this Reynolds number range. If the computed Re exceeds the reliable range of Schiller-Naumann, note this and still report the result, since it remains a reasonable engineering estimate in the low thousands.

Step 3: Compute the frontal (projected) area of the sphere as A = π D² / 4.

Step 4: Compute the drag force using the standard drag equation Fd = 0.5 × ρ × V² × Cd × A.

Report the Reynolds number, the drag coefficient Cd, and the final drag force Fd with appropriate units.
