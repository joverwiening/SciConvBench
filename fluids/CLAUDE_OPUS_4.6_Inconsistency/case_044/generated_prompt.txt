The flow is steady, fully developed, viscous (laminar) flow of oil between two infinite parallel plates.
The dynamic viscosity of the oil is mu = 0.1 Pa·s.
The wall shear stress is computed using tau = mu * (du/dy).
Both Couette flow and Poiseuille flow cases are addressed.

For the Couette flow case, the top plate moves at a constant velocity U = 1 m/s, the bottom plate is stationary, and there is no applied pressure gradient.
The gap distance between the plates is h = 0.01 m (1 cm).
The velocity profile for Couette flow is u(y) = U * (y / h), where y is measured from the bottom plate.
The velocity gradient is du/dy = U / h, which is constant across the gap.
The wall shear stress for Couette flow is tau = mu * U / h.
Substituting values: tau = 0.1 * 1 / 0.01 = 10 Pa.
The wall shear stress is the same at both plates for Couette flow.

For the Poiseuille flow case, both plates are stationary and the flow is driven by a constant pressure gradient dP/dx = -12000 Pa/m (negative in the flow direction).
The gap distance between the plates is h = 0.01 m (1 cm).
The velocity profile for Poiseuille flow is u(y) = (1 / (2 * mu)) * (-dP/dx) * y * (h - y), where y is measured from the bottom plate.
The velocity gradient is du/dy = (1 / (2 * mu)) * (-dP/dx) * (h - 2y).
At the bottom wall (y = 0): du/dy = (-dP/dx) * h / (2 * mu).
At the top wall (y = h): du/dy = -(-dP/dx) * h / (2 * mu).
The wall shear stress magnitude for Poiseuille flow is tau_wall = (-dP/dx) * h / 2.
Substituting values: tau_wall = 12000 * 0.01 / 2 = 60 Pa.
The wall shear stress magnitude is the same at both plates for Poiseuille flow, but the directions are opposite (acting in the flow direction on both walls).

The symbolic expressions and numerical results are both provided for each case.
