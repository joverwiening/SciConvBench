The simulation calculates the mass flow rate of air through a duct where pressure drops from 10 bar to 1 bar.
The mass flow rate is computed by integrating ρ · u · dA across the duct cross-section at any given station.
Density is treated as variable and computed from the ideal gas law: ρ = P / (R · T), where R = 287 J/(kg·K) for dry air.
The flow is modeled as one-dimensional, meaning properties vary only along the duct length and are uniform across any given cross-section.
At any cross-section, the integral ρ · u · dA simplifies to ρ · u · A due to the one-dimensional assumption.
The duct is circular with a diameter of 0.1 m (a common reasonable assumption for industrial duct analysis).
The flow is assumed to be steady, compressible, and isentropic (adiabatic and frictionless) between the inlet and outlet.
The inlet stagnation pressure is 10 bar (1,000,000 Pa) and the outlet static pressure is 1 bar (100,000 Pa).
The inlet stagnation temperature is 293.15 K (standard ambient temperature, approximately 20°C).
For an isentropic process, temperature and pressure are related by T2/T1 = (P2/P1)^((γ-1)/γ), where γ = 1.4 for air.
The velocity at any station is derived from isentropic compressible flow relations, not prescribed independently.
The velocity profile across the cross-section is assumed uniform (plug flow) consistent with the one-dimensional assumption.
Conservation of mass (continuity) applies: the mass flow rate ṁ = ρ · u · A is constant along the duct.
The mass flow rate is determined by the isentropic flow conditions that satisfy the given inlet and outlet pressure boundary conditions.
If the pressure ratio (1 bar / 10 bar = 0.1) implies choked flow (critical pressure ratio for air is approximately 0.528), the flow is choked at the throat and the mass flow rate is limited by the choked condition.
Since 0.1 is less than 0.528, the flow will indeed be choked, and the mass flow rate is computed at the choked (sonic) condition using ṁ = A* · P0 · sqrt(γ / (R · T0)) · (2 / (γ+1))^((γ+1) / (2·(γ-1))), where A* is the throat area, P0 is stagnation pressure, and T0 is stagnation temperature.
The throat area equals the duct cross-sectional area (A = π · (0.05)^2 m²) assuming the duct itself acts as the minimum area.
The output is the computed mass flow rate in kg/s.
