The task is to determine whether an adiabatic or isothermal process is physically appropriate for gas flow through a nozzle, justify that choice, and then calculate the exit velocity of the gas.

The gas expands through a nozzle from an inlet pressure of 10 bar to an exit pressure of 1 bar.
The solver must argue whether the nozzle process is adiabatic or isothermal, providing physical justification for the choice.
After determining the appropriate process, the solver must use the corresponding method to calculate the exit velocity.
If the process is determined to be adiabatic, use conservation of stagnation enthalpy (constant total enthalpy) to find the exit velocity.
If the process is determined to be isothermal, use the appropriate isothermal relation with heat addition to find the exit velocity.
Assume the working gas is air with a ratio of specific heats γ = 1.4 and a specific gas constant R = 287 J/(kg·K).
Assume the gas behaves as an ideal gas throughout the calculation.
Assume a standard inlet temperature of 300 K.
Assume the gas is initially at rest at the inlet, so inlet conditions are stagnation conditions (zero inlet velocity).
Assume the specific heat at constant pressure for air is cp = 1005 J/(kg·K).
Present the final exit velocity in m/s.
Show all steps of the calculation clearly, including the justification for the process selection.
