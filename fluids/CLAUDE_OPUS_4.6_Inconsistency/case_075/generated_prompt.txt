Use the actual density of liquid water, ρ ≈ 998 kg/m³, treating water as an incompressible fluid.
Apply the steady-state, incompressible Bernoulli equation: P1 + ½ρv1² + ρgz1 = P2 + ½ρv2² + ρgz2.
Use a standard textbook demonstration scenario: Torricelli's problem, where a large open tank drains through a small orifice near the bottom.
The tank is open to the atmosphere at the top surface (Point 1) and the orifice is also open to the atmosphere (Point 2), so P1 = P2 = 1 atm.
The water surface (Point 1) is at height h above the orifice (Point 2), so z1 − z2 = h.
Assume the tank cross-section is much larger than the orifice, so the surface velocity v1 ≈ 0.
Set the elevation datum at the orifice, so z2 = 0 and z1 = h.
Use a representative water depth of h = 3 m above the orifice.
Solve for the exit velocity at the orifice, v2.
The simplified Bernoulli equation reduces to v2 = sqrt(2 g h), which is Torricelli's theorem.
Use g = 9.81 m/s².
Report the numerical exit velocity in m/s.
Show each algebraic step of the Bernoulli simplification clearly.
Note explicitly at the start that the ideal-gas law is not applicable to liquid water and that the correct liquid density ρ ≈ 998 kg/m³ is used instead.
