Q: There is a contradiction in your specification. You state that the wall is maintained at a fixed temperature of 400 K (which implies heat is being supplied to keep it at that temperature), but you also say to treat the wall as adiabatic (which means zero heat flux through the wall, i.e., no heat transfer at the surface). An adiabatic wall in convection would reach the fluid temperature (or the adiabatic wall temperature), not remain at a fixed temperature different from the fluid.

Which condition should I use for the calculation: the fixed wall temperature of 400 K (ignoring the adiabatic condition), or the adiabatic wall condition (ignoring the fixed temperature)?
A: Use the fixed wall temperature of 400 K. Ignore the adiabatic condition — that was an error in the problem statement. Simply calculate the convective heat flux as q'' = h (Ts - T∞).
