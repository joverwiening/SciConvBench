Calculate the capillary rise of water in a glass tube at room temperature (20°C).
The tube has an internal diameter of 0.5 mm.
Use water properties at 20°C: density = 998.2 kg/m³, surface tension = 0.072 N/m.
Use a contact angle of 0° between water and clean glass (complete wetting).
Apply the capillary rise equation: h = (2γ cos θ)/(ρgr), where γ is surface tension, θ is contact angle, ρ is density, g is gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s²), and r is tube radius.
The calculation should provide the equilibrium height of water rise in meters.
Assume the tube is perfectly clean and vertical.
Assume atmospheric pressure at sea level (101.325 kPa).
