Assume a steady, incompressible, laminar, two‑dimensional viscous flow over a flat plate aligned with the x‑axis, with the plate extending from x = 0 to x = 1 m.  
Assume the free‑stream velocity far from the plate is U∞ = 1 m s⁻¹ and the fluid kinematic viscosity is ν = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m² s⁻¹ (typical for air at room temperature).  
Apply the no‑slip boundary condition at the plate surface: u(x, y = 0) = 0 and v(x, y = 0) = 0.  
Use the Blasius similarity solution for the laminar boundary layer: define the similarity variable η = y √(U∞/(ν x)).  
Solve the Blasius ordinary differential equation f‴ + ½ f f″ = 0 with boundary conditions f(0) = 0, f′(0) = 0, and f′(∞) = 1 to obtain the dimensionless stream function f(η).  
Compute the velocity profile as u(x, y) = U∞ f′(η) and v(x, y) = ½ √(ν U∞/x) [η f′(η) – f(η)].  
Present the resulting u‑velocity distribution as a function of y for selected x‑positions (e.g., x = 0.1 m, 0.5 m, 1.0 m) to illustrate the growth of the boundary layer.  
State that the boundary‑layer thickness δ(x) can be approximated by δ ≈ 5 √(ν x/U∞).
