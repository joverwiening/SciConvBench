Solve the steady one‑dimensional heat conduction problem in a homogeneous slab of thickness L = 0.05 m.  
Assume the material is aluminum with thermal diffusivity α = 9.7 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s and thermal conductivity k = 237 W/(m·K).  
Apply a fixed temperature T₁ = 100 °C at the left surface (x = 0).  
Apply a convective boundary condition at the right surface (x = L) with ambient temperature T∞ = 25 °C and convection coefficient h = 10 W/(m²·K).  
Because the problem is steady, set ∂T/∂t = 0, which reduces the governing equation to d²T/dx² = 0.  
Integrate the governing equation twice and use the two boundary conditions to obtain the linear temperature distribution T(x) = T₁ – (T₁ – T∞)·(x/L)·[k/(k + h·L)].  
Compute the heat flux at the left surface as q₀ = –k·(dT/dx) evaluated at x = 0, which simplifies to q₀ = (k·h·(T₁ – T∞)) / (k + h·L).  
Report the temperature profile T(x) for 0 ≤ x ≤ L and the heat flux q₀.  
Validate the solution by confirming that the temperature gradient is constant and that the heat flux leaving the right surface equals q₀.
