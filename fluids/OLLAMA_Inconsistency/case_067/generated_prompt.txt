Use a superficial air velocity of 0.5 m s⁻¹ (reasonable best‑practice assumption for packed‑bed air flow).  
Assume standard air properties at 20 °C and 1 atm: dynamic viscosity μ = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s and density ρ = 1.2 kg m⁻³.  
Apply the Ergun equation with porosity ε = 0.40, particle diameter dₚ = 5 m (as instructed), and the assumed velocity U = 0.5 m s⁻¹:  

ΔP/L = [150 (1 − ε)² μ U] / [ε³ dₚ²] + [1.75 (1 − ε) ρ U²] / [ε³ dₚ]  

Compute the first term:  
150 (0.6)² = 54; ε³ = 0.064; dₚ² = 25 m².  
Term₁ = 54 × 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s × 0.5 m s⁻¹ / (0.064 × 25) ≈ 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ Pa m⁻¹.  

Compute the second term:  
1.75 (0.6) = 1.05; ε³ = 0.064; dₚ = 5 m.  
Term₂ = 1.05 × 1.2 kg m⁻³ × (0.5 m s⁻¹)² / (0.064 × 5) ≈ 0.98 Pa m⁻¹.  

Add the terms:  
ΔP/L ≈ 0.98 Pa m⁻¹ + 0.0003 Pa m⁻¹ ≈ 0.98 Pa m⁻¹.  

Thus, the pressure drop per unit length of the packed bed, using the specified substitution, is approximately 0.98 Pa per meter.
