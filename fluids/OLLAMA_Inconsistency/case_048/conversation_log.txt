Q: What is the thickness of the wall and the thermal condition (temperature, convection, etc.) at the opposite surface (x = L)?
A: The complete requirement does not specify a particular wall thickness \(L\); you may treat \(L\) as an arbitrary length (or make a reasonable assumption based on typical wall dimensions).  

At the opposite surface \(x = L\) the thermal condition is **insulated**, i.e., a zero‑heat‑flux condition:

\[
\left.\frac{dT}{dx}\right|_{x=L}=0 .
\]

Q: Should the boundary at x = 0 be treated as a prescribed temperature of 350 K (Dirichlet) or as an adiabatic (zero‑heat‑flux) condition?
A: Treat the boundary at x = 0 as a prescribed temperature T(0)=350 K (Dirichlet).
