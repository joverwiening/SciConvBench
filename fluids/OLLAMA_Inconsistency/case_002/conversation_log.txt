Q: Should the pressure drop be calculated by adding a friction loss term to Bernoulli’s equation (e.g., P₁ + 0.5 ρ v₁² = P₂ + 0.5 ρ v₂² + ΔP_f), or should it be assumed that the viscous effects are negligible and the pressure drop is zero?
A: The pressure drop must be calculated by including the friction‑loss term (Δp = f_D · L/D · ρ v²/2) in the energy equation; it is not assumed to be zero.

Q: What Darcy friction factor (f_D) should be used for the pipe flow?
A: The Reynolds number is  

\[
\mathrm{Re}= \frac{\rho v D}{\mu}= \frac{998\;\text{kg/m}^3 \times 2.0\;\text{m/s} \times 0.05\;\text{m}}{1.0\times10^{-3}\;\text{Pa·s}} \approx 9.98\times10^{4}
\]

Since Re ≈ 1 × 10⁵ > 4 000, the flow is turbulent.  
For a smooth pipe in this Reynolds‑number range the Blasius correlation is appropriate:

\[
f_D = 0.3164\,\mathrm{Re}^{-0.25}
\]

\[
f_D = 0.3164\,(9.98\times10^{4})^{-0.25} \approx 0.018
\]

**Use a Darcy friction factor f_D ≈ 0.018** (rounded to two significant figures).

Q: Calculate the pressure drop using the Darcy–Weisbach friction‑loss term:

Δp = f_D · (L/D) · (ρ v² / 2)

where  
L = 40 m, D = 0.05 m, v = 2.0 m s⁻¹, ρ = 998 kg m⁻³, f_D = 0.018.

Compute L/D = 40 / 0.05 = 800.  
Compute ρ v² /2 = 998 · (2.0)² / 2 = 1996 Pa.  
Compute f_D·(L/D) = 0.018 · 800 = 14.4.

Thus Δp = 14.4 · 1996 Pa ≈ 2.87 × 10⁴ Pa ≈ 2.9 × 10⁴ Pa (≈ 0.29 bar).
A: The pressure drop is
