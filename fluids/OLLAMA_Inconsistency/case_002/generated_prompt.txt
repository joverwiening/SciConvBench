Use a water density of 998 kg/m³.  
The pipe length is 40 m and the inner diameter is 0.05 m.  
The mean flow velocity is 2.0 m/s.  
Calculate the Reynolds number: Re = (ρ v D)/μ = (998 × 2.0 × 0.05)/(1.0 × 10⁻³) ≈ 9.98 × 10⁴.  
Since Re > 4 000, the flow is turbulent.  
For a smooth pipe in this Reynolds‑number range, use the Blasius correlation: f_D = 0.3164 Re⁻⁰·²⁵, giving f_D ≈ 0.018 (two significant figures).  
Compute the Darcy–Weisbach pressure drop: Δp = f_D · (L/D) · (ρ v²/2).  
L/D = 40 / 0.05 = 800.  
ρ v²/2 = 998 · (2.0)² / 2 = 1996 Pa.  
f_D · (L/D) = 0.018 · 800 = 14.4.  
Thus Δp = 14.4 · 1996 Pa ≈ 2.87 × 10⁴ Pa ≈ 2.9 × 10⁴ Pa (≈ 0.29 bar).
