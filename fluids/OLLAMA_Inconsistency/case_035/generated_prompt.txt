Assume the water has a density of 1000 kg/m³.  
Assume the angular velocity of the forced vortex is ω = 2π rad/s (one full revolution per second).  
Use Bernoulli’s equation along a horizontal radial line (same elevation) between r₁ = 0.1 m and r₂ = 0.3 m, noting that the tangential velocity is vθ = ω r.  
Since elevation is constant, the pressure difference is Δp = ½ ρ ω² (r₂² − r₁²).  
Calculate Δp:  
r₂² − r₁² = 0.3² − 0.1² = 0.09 − 0.01 = 0.08 m².  
ω² = (2π)² ≈ 39.48 (rad/s)².  
Δp = 0.5 × 1000 kg/m³ × 39.48 × 0.08 ≈ 1.58 × 10³ Pa.  
Thus the pressure at r = 0.3 m is higher than at r = 0.1 m by approximately 1.6 kPa.
