Assume the sphere is made of aluminum with thermal conductivity k = 205 W/(m·K), density ρ = 2700 kg/m³ and specific heat c = 900 J/(kg·K).  
Assume the sphere radius R = 0.05 m.  
Assume the initial uniform temperature of the sphere T₀ = 100 °C.  
Assume the ambient fluid temperature T∞ = 25 °C.  

Compute the characteristic length for a sphere: L = V/A = R/3 = 0.0167 m.  
Use the given Biot number Bi = 0.05 to obtain the convective heat‑transfer coefficient:  
h = Bi·k / L = 0.05 × 205 / 0.0167 ≈ 615 W/(m²·K).  

Calculate the sphere surface area A = 4πR² and volume V = 4/3 πR³.  
Determine the thermal time constant: τ = (ρ c V) / (h A).  

The lumped‑capacitance transient cooling curve is then:  
T(t) = T∞ + (T₀ – T∞) · exp(–t/τ).  

Provide the numerical values:  
A = 0.0314 m², V = 5.24 × 10⁻⁴ m³, τ ≈ (2700 × 900 × 5.24 × 10⁻⁴) / (615 × 0.0314) ≈ 68 s.  

Thus the final expression for the temperature as a function of time is:  
T(t) = 25 °C + 75 °C · exp(–t / 68 s).  

Use this equation to compute the transient cooling curve for any desired time points.
