Assume the hot stream is water entering at 80 °C and leaving at 40 °C, with a mass flow rate of 1.0 kg s⁻¹.  
Assume the cold stream is water entering at 20 °C and leaving at 60 °C, with a mass flow rate of 1.2 kg s⁻¹.  
Use the specific heat capacity of water, cp = 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, for both streams.  
Calculate the heat duty from the hot side: Q = ṁ_hot · cp · (Th,in − Th,out) = 1.0 · 4180 · (80 − 40) = 167 kW.  
Adopt a typical overall heat‑transfer coefficient for a well‑designed counterflow shell‑and‑tube exchanger: U = 500 W m⁻² K⁻¹.  
Compute the temperature differences for the LMTD method:  
ΔT1 = Th,in − Tc,in = 80 − 20 = 60 °C.  
ΔT2 = Th,out − Tc,out = 40 − 60 = −20 °C (use the absolute value 20 °C for the LMTD formula).  
Calculate the log‑mean temperature difference:  
LMTD = (ΔT1 − |ΔT2|) / ln(ΔT1 / |ΔT2|) = (60 − 20) / ln(60 / 20) ≈ 36.4 °C.  
Determine the required heat‑transfer area:  
A = Q / (U · LMTD) = 167 000 W / (500 W m⁻² K⁻¹ · 36.4 K) ≈ 9.2 m².  
Specify that the counterflow heat exchanger must provide at least 9.2 m² of heat‑transfer surface area to meet the assumed operating conditions.
