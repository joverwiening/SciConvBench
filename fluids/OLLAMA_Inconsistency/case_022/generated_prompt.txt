The pressure gradient normal to a curved streamline is given by dp/dn = ρ V² / R, where ρ is the fluid density, V is the local speed, and R is the radius of curvature.  
Compute the pressure difference across a normal distance Δn by multiplying the gradient by Δn: Δp = (ρ V² / R) · Δn.  
Substitute the given values: ρ = 1000 kg/m³, V = 10 m/s, R = 5 m, Δn = 0.10 m.  
Calculate ρ V² = 1000 × (10)² = 100 000 Pa·m, then ρ V² / R = 100 000 / 5 = 20 000 Pa/m.  
Finally, Δp = 20 000 Pa/m × 0.10 m = 2 000 Pa.  
Thus, the pressure on the inner side of the streamline is higher than on the outer side by 2 000 Pa.
