Assume the hot fluid is water entering at 150 °C and leaving at 90 °C.  
Assume the cold fluid is water entering at 30 °C and leaving at 110 °C.  
Assume a mass flow rate of 1 kg s⁻¹ for the hot side and a specific heat capacity of 4.18 kJ kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.  
Compute the heat duty: Q = ṁ · Cp · (Th,in − Th,out) = 1 kg s⁻¹ · 4.18 kJ kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ · (150 °C − 90 °C) = 292.6 kW.  
Use the counter‑flow LMTD expression:  
ΔT₁ = Th,in − Tc,out = 150 °C − 110 °C = 40 °C.  
ΔT₂ = Th,out − Tc,in = 90 °C − 30 °C = 60 °C.  
LMTD = (ΔT₂ − ΔT₁) / ln(ΔT₂/ΔT₁) = (60 °C − 40 °C) / ln(60/40) ≈ 49.35 °C.  
Assume an overall heat‑transfer coefficient U of 500 W m⁻² K⁻¹.  
Compute the required heat‑exchanger area: A = Q / (U · LMTD) = 292 600 W / (500 W m⁻² K⁻¹ · 49.35 K) ≈ 11.9 m².
