Assume a pressure drop of 1 kPa across a microchannel length of 1 cm (pressure gradient = 1 × 10⁵ Pa · m⁻¹).  
Use the continuum Navier–Stokes equations with no‑slip walls and treat the microchannel as a circular tube with hydraulic diameter Dh = 50 µm (radius r = Dh/2 = 25 µm).  
Take nitrogen properties at 300 K: dynamic viscosity μ ≈ 1.78 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s, density ρ ≈ 1.165 kg·m⁻³.  

Compute the volumetric flow rate with the Hagen–Poiseuille relation:  
Q = (π r⁴ ΔP) / (8 μ L).  

Substitute r = 25 × 10⁻⁶ m, ΔP = 1 × 10³ Pa, μ = 1.78 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s, L = 0.01 m:  
Q ≈ 8.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³·s⁻¹.  

Compute the mass flow rate:  
ṁ = ρ Q ≈ 1.165 kg·m⁻³ × 8.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³·s⁻¹ ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻⁹ kg·s⁻¹.  

Thus, under the stated assumptions, the pressure‑driven mass flow rate of nitrogen through the microchannel is approximately 1 × 10⁻⁹ kg · s⁻¹.
