Assume the fluid is water at 20 °C with a density of 998 kg/m³.  
Assume the upstream (inlet) pipe diameter is 0.10 m and the throat diameter is 0.05 m (a 2:1 diameter ratio, typical for Venturi meters).  
Assume the measured pressure differential across the Venturi meter (upstream minus throat) is 10 kPa.  

Use the ideal Bernoulli equation with a discharge coefficient Cd = 1 (no losses) to compute the volumetric flow rate Q:  

1. Compute the cross‑sectional areas: A₁ = π D₁²/4 and A₂ = π D₂²/4.  
2. Apply continuity: V₁ = (Q/A₁) and V₂ = (Q/A₂).  
3. Apply Bernoulli between the upstream section and the throat: Δp = (ρ/2)(V₂² − V₁²).  
4. Substitute V₁ = (Q/A₁) and V₂ = (Q/A₂) into the Bernoulli equation and solve for Q:  
   Q = A₂ √[ 2 Δp / ρ · 1 / (1 − (A₂/A₁)²) ].  
5. Insert the assumed values (Δp = 10 000 Pa, ρ = 998 kg/m³, A₁ and A₂ from the diameters) to obtain the numerical flow rate.  

Report the calculated flow rate in cubic meters per second (m³/s) and, if desired, convert to liters per minute (L/min).
