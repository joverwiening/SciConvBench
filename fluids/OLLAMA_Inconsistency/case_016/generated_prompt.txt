Use a fluid density of 1000 kg/m³ (typical for water).  
Assume the centrifuge rotor radius is 0.15 m, which is a common laboratory centrifuge size.  
Convert the rotation speed from RPM to angular velocity: ω = 2π × (10 000 RPM) / 60 ≈ 1 047 rad/s.  
Apply the hydrostatic pressure formula for a rotating fluid: P(r) = ½ ρ ω² r² + P₀, where P₀ is the reference pressure at the rotation axis (use atmospheric pressure, 101 325 Pa).  
Calculate the pressure at the assumed radius:  
P(0.15 m) = ½ × 1000 kg/m³ × (1 047 rad/s)² × (0.15 m)² + 101 325 Pa ≈ 1.17 × 10⁸ Pa (≈ 117 MPa) above atmospheric pressure.  
Report the pressure distribution as the function P(r) = ½ ρ ω² r² + 101 325 Pa for 0 ≤ r ≤ 0.15 m.
