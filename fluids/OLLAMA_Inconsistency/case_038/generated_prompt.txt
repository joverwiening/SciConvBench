Assume the density of water is 1000 kg/m³, which is the standard value used in most engineering calculations.  
Apply the steady‑flow Bernoulli equation between a point just upstream of the valve (section 1) and a point just downstream of the valve (section 2) at the instant the valve is fully closed.  
Because the pipe is horizontal and elevation changes are negligible, the Bernoulli equation reduces to:  

P₁ + ½ ρ v₁² = P₂ + ½ ρ v₂²  

Before closure the water velocity is v₁ = 2 m/s and the pressure is P₁ (reference).  
When the valve is fully closed the velocity downstream is v₂ = 0 m/s.  

Rearrange to find the pressure rise ΔP = P₂ − P₁:  

ΔP = ½ ρ (v₁² − v₂²)  

Substitute the values:  

ΔP = ½ (1000 kg/m³) (2 m/s)² = 0.5 × 1000 × 4 = 2000 Pa  

Therefore, the pressure increase caused by the valve closure is 2000 Pa (≈ 0.02 bar).  
Note: The given closure time (0.05 s) is not required for a steady‑flow Bernoulli calculation, which assumes an instantaneous change in velocity.
