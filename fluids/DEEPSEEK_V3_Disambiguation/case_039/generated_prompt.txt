The task is to calculate the torque required to rotate a disk in a viscous fluid near a wall.
The disk is a solid circular plate with a radius of 0.2 meters.
The fluid has a dynamic viscosity (mu) of 0.1 Pa·s.
The disk rotates at a constant speed of 60 revolutions per minute.
The distance between the disk face and the stationary wall is 1 millimeter, which defines the fluid film thickness.
Assume the fluid is a Newtonian oil, such as a light machine oil, with a density of 900 kg/m³.
Assume the fluid temperature is a standard 20°C (293 K) for calculation.
Assume both the disk and wall surfaces are smooth, rigid, and made of a material like polished steel.
Assume the surfaces are hydraulically smooth, meaning surface roughness is negligible compared to the fluid film gap.
Model the flow in the thin gap as Couette flow between two parallel plates, where one plate (the disk surface) moves tangentially.
The tangential velocity at any radial position r on the disk is given by v_theta = omega * r, where omega is the angular velocity in rad/s.
Convert the rotational speed from 60 rpm to an angular velocity: omega = (60 * 2 * pi) / 60 = 2 * pi rad/s.
The shear stress tau on the disk surface at a radius r is given by tau = mu * (dv/dy), where dv is the velocity difference and dy is the gap.
For Couette flow with a stationary wall, the velocity gradient is linear: dv/dy = (v_theta - 0) / h, where h is the 1 mm gap.
Therefore, shear stress is tau(r) = mu * (omega * r) / h.
The differential torque dT on an annular element of the disk at radius r with width dr is dT = r * tau(r) * dA, where dA = 2 * pi * r * dr.
Integrate dT from r=0 to r=R to find the total torque: T = ∫ from 0 to R of [ r * (mu * omega * r / h) * 2 * pi * r ] dr.
Simplify the integral: T = (2 * pi * mu * omega / h) * ∫ from 0 to R of r³ dr.
Perform the integration: T = (2 * pi * mu * omega / h) * (R^4 / 4).
The final formula for torque is T = (pi * mu * omega * R^4) / (2 * h).
Substitute the given values: mu = 0.1 Pa·s, omega = 2 * pi rad/s, R = 0.2 m, h = 0.001 m.
Calculate the numerical result: T = (pi * 0.1 * 2 * pi * (0.2)^4) / (2 * 0.001) N·m.
Report the torque value in Newton-meters (N·m).
