Calculate the steady-state pressure drop due to friction in a 100 m long horizontal circular pipe carrying water.
Use pipe internal diameter D = 0.05 m.
Use pipe length L = 100 m.
Use volumetric flow rate Q = 0.002 m³/s.
Use water properties at 20°C: density ρ = 998 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity μ = 1.0×10⁻³ Pa·s.
Use pipe absolute roughness ε = 1.5×10⁻⁴ m (galvanized iron).
Assume single-phase, incompressible flow with no elevation change.
Neglect minor losses (entrance, exit, fittings) unless explicitly added later.
Compute average velocity V = Q/(πD²/4).
Compute Reynolds number Re = ρVD/μ.
Compute relative roughness ε/D.
Determine Darcy friction factor f using the Colebrook-White equation (or an explicit approximation such as Swamee-Jain) consistent with Re and ε/D.
Compute pressure drop using Darcy–Weisbach: ΔP = f (L/D) (ρV²/2).
Report ΔP in Pa and kPa.
