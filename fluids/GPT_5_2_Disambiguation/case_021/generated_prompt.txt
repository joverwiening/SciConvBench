Compute thermal stress in a straight, prismatic steel rod subjected to a uniform temperature decrease of 40°C.
Assume the rod is fully fixed at both ends so the net axial strain is zero and the change in length is zero.
Use Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa.
Use coefficient of thermal expansion α = 12 × 10^-6 /°C.
Use linear thermoelastic relation: total strain ε_total = ε_mechanical + ε_thermal.
Set ε_total = 0 and ε_thermal = αΔT with ΔT = -40°C.
Solve for mechanical strain ε_mechanical = -αΔT.
Compute stress from Hooke’s law σ = E ε_mechanical = -E α ΔT.
Calculate σ = -(200×10^9 Pa)(12×10^-6 /°C)(-40°C) = +96×10^6 Pa.
Report thermal stress magnitude as 96 MPa.
Report stress sign as tensile (positive) because cooling under full restraint induces tension.
