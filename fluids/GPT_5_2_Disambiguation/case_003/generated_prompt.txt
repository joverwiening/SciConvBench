Compute the terminal settling velocity of a spherical particle of diameter 2 mm falling under gravity in oil.
Use particle density 7800 kg/m³ (steel) and oil density 890 kg/m³.
Use oil dynamic viscosity 0.29 Pa·s and assume these properties apply at room temperature (25 °C).
Assume gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².
Assume the particle is rigid, smooth, and remains spherical with no deformation.
Assume the oil is Newtonian, incompressible, and quiescent (no background flow).
Assume the particle falls far from walls so wall effects are negligible.
Determine the terminal velocity by balancing buoyancy-corrected weight with drag.
Start with Stokes’ law as an initial estimate and compute the particle Reynolds number Re = ρ_oil v d / μ.
If Re is not within the Stokes regime, iterate using an appropriate sphere drag correlation (e.g., Schiller–Naumann) until convergence of v.
Report the final terminal velocity in m/s and the corresponding Reynolds number.
State which drag model/correlation was used and whether the final solution is in the creeping-flow regime.
