Model a centrifuge spinning at 10,000 RPM filled with water (density ρ = 1000 kg/m³), which is the standard fluid used in centrifuge analysis.
Convert the rotational speed from RPM to radians per second using ω = 10,000 × (2π / 60), giving ω ≈ 1047.2 rad/s.
Assume the centrifuge has an inner radius r1 = 0.0 m (at the axis of rotation) and an outer radius r2 = 0.1 m (10 cm), which is a typical laboratory centrifuge rotor size.
Apply the rotating fluid equilibrium equation dP/dr = ρ × r × ω².
Integrate this equation from r1 to r to obtain P(r) = P0 + ½ × ρ × ω² × (r² − r1²), where P0 is the reference pressure at r1.
Set the reference pressure P0 at r1 = 0.0 m to atmospheric pressure, P0 = 101,325 Pa (absolute pressure boundary condition).
Evaluate P(r) at several radial positions: r = 0.0 m, r = 0.025 m, r = 0.05 m, r = 0.075 m, and r = 0.1 m.
At r = 0.0 m, P = 101,325 Pa (the reference boundary condition).
At r = 0.025 m, compute P = 101,325 + 0.5 × 1000 × (1047.2)² × (0.025)² ≈ 101,325 + 342,936 ≈ 444,261 Pa.
At r = 0.05 m, compute P = 101,325 + 0.5 × 1000 × (1047.2)² × (0.05)² ≈ 101,325 + 1,371,742 ≈ 1,473,067 Pa.
At r = 0.075 m, compute P = 101,325 + 0.5 × 1000 × (1047.2)² × (0.075)² ≈ 101,325 + 3,086,420 ≈ 3,187,745 Pa.
At r = 0.1 m, compute P = 101,325 + 0.5 × 1000 × (1047.2)² × (0.1)² ≈ 101,325 + 5,487,858 ≈ 5,589,183 Pa.
Express the final pressure distribution as the closed-form equation P(r) = 101,325 + 548,785,800 × r² (in Pascals, with r in meters).
Note that the pressure increases quadratically with radial distance, reaching approximately 5.59 MPa (about 55 atmospheres) at the outer wall.
Report all pressures in Pascals (Pa) as absolute pressures.
