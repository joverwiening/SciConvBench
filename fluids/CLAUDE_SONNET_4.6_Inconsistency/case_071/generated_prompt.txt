Compute the capillary rise (or depression) of mercury in a vertical glass tube using the Jurin's Law formula.

Use the capillary rise formula: h = (2 * sigma * cos(theta)) / (rho * g * r).

Use surface tension sigma = 0.48 N/m.

Use contact angle theta = 140 degrees, because mercury on glass has a well-established contact angle of approximately 140 degrees, not 0 degrees; 0 degrees is physically incorrect for mercury on glass and would imply complete wetting, which contradicts known mercury behavior.

Use density of mercury rho = 13,600 kg/m³.

Use gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².

Assume a standard capillary tube inner radius r = 1 mm = 0.001 m, as this is a commonly used reference radius in capillarity demonstrations and textbook problems.

Compute cos(140°) = -0.7660.

Compute h = (2 * 0.48 * (-0.7660)) / (13600 * 9.81 * 0.001).

Compute the numerator: 2 * 0.48 * (-0.7660) = -0.7354 N/m.

Compute the denominator: 13600 * 9.81 * 0.001 = 133.416 N/m³ * m² effectively giving units of N/m².

Compute h = -0.7354 / 133.416 = -0.005512 m ≈ -5.51 mm.

The negative sign indicates capillary depression, meaning mercury is depressed approximately 5.51 mm below the free surface level.

Report the final result as a capillary depression of approximately 5.5 mm for a 1 mm radius glass tube.
