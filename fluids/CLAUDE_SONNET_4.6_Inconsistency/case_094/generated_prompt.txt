Compute the capillary rise of water in a vertical glass tube using the capillary rise formula h = (2 * sigma * cos(theta)) / (rho * g * r).

Use surface tension sigma = 0.072 N/m.
Use contact angle theta = 0 degrees, so cos(theta) = 1.
Use water density rho = 1000 kg/m³.
Use gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².
Use tube inner radius r = 0.5 mm (0.0005 m), a standard representative value used in capillary rise demonstrations and textbook problems.

Substitute all values into the formula: h = (2 * 0.072 * 1) / (1000 * 9.81 * 0.0005).
Compute the numerator: 2 * 0.072 = 0.144 N/m.
Compute the denominator: 1000 * 9.81 * 0.0005 = 4.905 N/m².
Compute h = 0.144 / 4.905 ≈ 0.02936 m.
Report the capillary rise height as approximately 29.4 mm (about 2.94 cm).
State the units of the result clearly as meters and also convert to millimeters for practical reference.
Note that a smaller tube radius produces a greater capillary rise, and a larger radius produces a smaller rise, in inverse proportion.
