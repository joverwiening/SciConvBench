Use a rectangular straight fin with an adiabatic tip.

Assume the following reasonable baseline parameters based on best practices for an air-cooled fin system:
- Heat transfer coefficient: h = 25 W/(m²·K) (natural/forced convection in air)
- Fin thermal conductivity: k = 200 W/(m·K) (aluminum fin)
- Fin length: L = 0.05 m (50 mm)
- Fin thickness: t = 0.002 m (2 mm), so the fin width is w (unit width, w = 1 m for per-unit-width analysis)
- Fin cross-sectional area: A_c = t × w = 0.002 × 1 = 0.002 m²
- Fin perimeter: P = 2 × (t + w) ≈ 2 × 1 = 2 m (since w >> t, P ≈ 2w = 2 m)

Compute the fin parameter m using the formula m = sqrt(h·P / (k·A_c)).
m = sqrt(25 × 2 / (200 × 0.002)) = sqrt(50 / 0.4) = sqrt(125) ≈ 11.18 m⁻¹

Compute mL = 11.18 × 0.05 = 0.559

Compute fin efficiency using eta_f = tanh(mL) / (mL).
tanh(0.559) ≈ 0.507
eta_f = 0.507 / 0.559 ≈ 0.907

The fin efficiency is eta_f ≈ 0.907, which correctly satisfies 0 < eta_f ≤ 1.

Compute the maximum possible fin heat transfer (if the entire fin were at base temperature T_b):
q_max = h × P × L × (T_b - T_inf) = 25 × 2 × 0.05 × (T_b - T_inf) = 2.5 × (T_b - T_inf) W

Compute actual fin heat transfer using q_f = eta_f × q_max.
q_f = 0.907 × 2.5 × (T_b - T_inf) = 2.267 × (T_b - T_inf) W

The final fin heat transfer is q_f ≈ 2.27 × (T_b - T_inf) watts, where (T_b - T_inf) is the temperature difference in Kelvin between the fin base and the surrounding fluid.
