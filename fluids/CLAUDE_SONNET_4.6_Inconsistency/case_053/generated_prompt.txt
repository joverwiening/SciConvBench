Simulate the transient cooling of a solid sphere using the one-term series solution (Heisler chart method) for Bi = 5.

Define the characteristic length L as the radius R of the sphere (L = R).

Use the Biot number Bi = hR/k = 5.

Solve for the first eigenvalue zeta_1 by finding the first positive root of the transcendental equation: 1 - zeta_1 * cot(zeta_1) = Bi, which gives zeta_1 approximately 2.5704 for Bi = 5.

Compute the one-term series coefficient C_1 = 4 * (sin(zeta_1) - zeta_1 * cos(zeta_1)) / (2 * zeta_1 - sin(2 * zeta_1)).

Define the dimensionless temperature theta as (T - T_inf) / (T_i - T_inf), where T_i is the initial uniform temperature and T_inf is the fluid temperature.

Report the dimensionless temperature at three radial positions: the center (r/R = 0), the midpoint (r/R = 0.5), and the surface (r/R = 1.0).

At each radial position r, use the one-term series formula: theta(r, Fo) = C_1 * exp(-zeta_1^2 * Fo) * sin(zeta_1 * r/R) / (zeta_1 * r/R).

At the center (r/R = 0), apply the limit: sin(zeta_1 * r/R) / (zeta_1 * r/R) approaches 1 as r approaches 0.

Cover the Fourier number range from Fo = 0 to Fo = 1.2, which is sufficient for the sphere to cool to below 5 percent of its initial temperature difference for Bi = 5.

Use at least 100 evenly spaced Fourier number steps from 0 to 1.2.

Note that the one-term series approximation is valid for Fo >= 0.2; for Fo < 0.2, note on the plot that more terms would be needed for full accuracy.

Plot all three curves (center, midpoint, surface) on a single graph of theta versus Fo.
