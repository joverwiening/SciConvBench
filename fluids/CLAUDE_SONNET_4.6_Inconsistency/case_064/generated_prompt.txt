The pipe connects two points with an elevation difference of 15 m; the outlet is 15 m higher than the inlet.
The pipe is not horizontal; ignore the earlier "horizontal" description.
Apply the Bernoulli equation between inlet (point 1) and outlet (point 2) for steady, incompressible flow.
Assume constant pipe diameter so the velocity at inlet equals the velocity at outlet; the velocity terms (½ρv²) cancel from both sides.
Assume the fluid is water with a density of 1000 kg/m³ (standard best-practice assumption when no fluid is specified).
Use gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².
The simplified Bernoulli equation reduces to: P1 = P2 + ρgh, where h = 15 m.
Calculate the pressure difference as: ΔP = P1 − P2 = ρgh = 1000 × 9.81 × 15.
The result is ΔP = 147,150 Pa (approximately 147.15 kPa).
This means the inlet pressure must be 147.15 kPa higher than the outlet pressure to sustain the flow against the 15 m elevation rise.
No pipe friction losses, no pump work, and no minor losses are included in this calculation.
