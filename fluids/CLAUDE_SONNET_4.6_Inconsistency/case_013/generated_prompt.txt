Calculate the stagnation temperature of a liquid water jet moving at 20 m/s, treating water as an incompressible liquid.

Assume the static temperature of the water is 25°C (298.15 K), which is a standard ambient condition commonly used in engineering calculations.

Use the stagnation enthalpy relation: h0 = h + v²/2, where v = 20 m/s.

For an incompressible liquid, the enthalpy rise due to kinetic energy is related to temperature by: delta_T = v² / (2 * Cp).

Use the specific heat of liquid water at 25°C: Cp = 4182 J/(kg·K).

Calculate the kinetic energy term: v²/2 = (20)²/2 = 200 J/kg.

Calculate the temperature rise: delta_T = 200 / 4182 ≈ 0.04785 K.

Calculate the stagnation temperature: T0 = T_static + delta_T = 298.15 + 0.04785 ≈ 298.198 K (or approximately 25.048°C).

Do not apply ideal gas relations such as P = ρRT or the ideal gas stagnation formula T0 = T(1 + (γ-1)/2 * M²).

Report the final stagnation temperature as approximately 298.20 K (25.05°C).
