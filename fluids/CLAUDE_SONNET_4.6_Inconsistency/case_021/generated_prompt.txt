The gas is air with standard properties: molecular weight M = 29 g/mol, specific heat ratio γ = 1.4, and specific heat at constant pressure Cp = 1005 J/(kg·K).
The process is isothermal expansion through a nozzle from an inlet pressure of 10 bar to an exit pressure of 1 bar.
The inlet temperature is assumed to be standard ambient temperature: T = 298.15 K (25°C).
The inlet velocity is assumed negligible, so the inlet kinetic energy term is set to zero.
The governing equation is the steady-flow energy equation applied to the nozzle: h_inlet + V_inlet²/2 = h_exit + V_exit²/2.
Since inlet velocity is negligible, simplify to: V_exit = sqrt(2 * (h_inlet - h_exit)).
Because the process is isothermal and air is treated as an ideal gas, enthalpy depends only on temperature: h = Cp * T.
Since temperature is constant (isothermal), h_inlet equals h_exit for an ideal gas, making the enthalpy difference zero.
Recognize and resolve the physical contradiction: a truly isothermal ideal gas expansion through a nozzle produces zero enthalpy drop and thus zero exit velocity under the constant-enthalpy (isenthalpic) assumption alone.
To produce a meaningful exit velocity, apply the correct physical model: use the isentropic nozzle flow equations, which are standard practice for nozzle calculations, rather than the isothermal-plus-isenthalpic combination (which is self-contradictory for an ideal gas).
Use the isentropic relations with T_inlet = 298.15 K, P_inlet = 10 bar, P_exit = 1 bar, and γ = 1.4.
Calculate the exit temperature using: T_exit = T_inlet * (P_exit / P_inlet)^((γ-1)/γ).
Compute T_exit = 298.15 * (1/10)^(0.4/1.4) = 298.15 * (0.1)^(0.2857) ≈ 298.15 * 0.5179 ≈ 154.4 K.
Calculate the enthalpy drop: Δh = Cp * (T_inlet - T_exit) = 1005 * (298.15 - 154.4) = 1005 * 143.75 ≈ 144,469 J/kg.
Calculate exit velocity: V_exit = sqrt(2 * Δh) = sqrt(2 * 144,469) = sqrt(288,938) ≈ 537.5 m/s.
Report the final result as V_exit ≈ 537.5 m/s under isentropic nozzle flow assumptions for air expanding from 10 bar to 1 bar at an inlet temperature of 298.15 K.
