Q: There is a direct contradiction in your request.

An inviscid fluid (governed by Euler's equations) cannot satisfy the no-slip boundary condition — inviscid flow by definition has zero viscosity, which means viscous forces are absent and the fluid slips freely at the wall. Applying u=0 at the surface is a condition from viscous (Navier-Stokes) flow theory.

Which of these did you actually intend to model?

Option A: A **viscous** fluid over a flat plate (Blasius boundary layer), where the no-slip condition u=0 is physically valid.
Option B: An **inviscid** fluid over a flat plate (potential flow), where the correct wall condition is the no-penetration condition (v=0, meaning zero normal velocity at the surface).

Which option do you want to proceed with?
A: Option A: A **viscous** fluid over a flat plate with the no-slip condition u=0, giving the boundary layer (Blasius) profile.
