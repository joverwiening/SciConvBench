The working fluid is air, treated as an ideal gas.
Use a specific heat ratio (gamma) of 1.4 for air.
Use a specific gas constant R = 287 J/(kg·K) for air.
The stagnation (tank) pressure P0 is 500 kPa.
The ambient (back) pressure Pb is 100 kPa.
The exit pressure Pe is given as 100 kPa (unchoked assumption as stated by the user).
Check whether the nozzle is choked by computing the critical pressure ratio: P_critical = P0 * (2/(gamma+1))^(gamma/(gamma-1)).
Compute P_critical = 500 * (2/2.4)^(1.4/0.4) = 500 * (0.8333)^3.5.
Evaluate (0.8333)^3.5 ≈ 0.5283, so P_critical ≈ 264.15 kPa.
Since Pe = 100 kPa < P_critical = 264.15 kPa, the nozzle is actually choked.
Despite the problem statement assuming Pe = 100 kPa, the physically correct exit pressure for a choked converging nozzle is Pe = P_critical ≈ 264.15 kPa.
Use Pe = 264.15 kPa and Mach number Me = 1 at the exit for the mass flow calculation.
The stagnation temperature T0 is not explicitly given; assume T0 = 293 K (20°C, standard room temperature) unless otherwise specified.
Compute the exit temperature Te = T0 * (2/(gamma+1)) = 293 * (2/2.4) = 293 * 0.8333 ≈ 244.2 K.
Compute the exit speed of sound ae = sqrt(gamma * R * Te) = sqrt(1.4 * 287 * 244.2) ≈ sqrt(98,163) ≈ 313.3 m/s.
Since Me = 1, the exit velocity Ve = ae ≈ 313.3 m/s.
Compute the exit density rho_e = Pe / (R * Te) = 264,150 / (287 * 244.2) ≈ 264,150 / 70,085 ≈ 3.769 kg/m³.
