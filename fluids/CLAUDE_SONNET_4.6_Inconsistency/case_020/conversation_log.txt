Q: The Rayleigh-Pitot formula (also known as the Rayleigh Pitot tube formula) is typically applied to **supersonic** flows to relate the stagnation pressure measured behind a normal shock to the freestream conditions.

For a **subsonic** flow (M = 0.5), the standard isentropic relations are used instead — no shock exists, so the Rayleigh-Pitot formula does not apply in the traditional sense.

Could you clarify your intent: do you want to apply the **isentropic stagnation pressure relation** for M = 0.5 (the correct subsonic approach), or do you specifically want to use the **Rayleigh-Pitot formula** and perhaps the Mach number you intended is supersonic (e.g., M > 1)?
A: Use the isentropic flow relations (Saint-Venant) for M = 0.5. The Rayleigh-Pitot formula is for supersonic flow with a detached shock and does not apply here.
