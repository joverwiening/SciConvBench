Use liquid water at 20°C and 1 atm as an incompressible fluid with density rho = 998 kg/m³.
Do not apply the ideal-gas law at any point in the calculation.
Model a tank-and-orifice (Torricelli) scenario as the default Bernoulli application, as this is the most standard textbook demonstration of Bernoulli's equation with an incompressible liquid.
Assume a tank open to the atmosphere at the top with a small orifice at the bottom.
Let the water surface height above the orifice be h = 1.0 m as a representative value.
Apply Bernoulli's equation between the free surface (Point 1) and the orifice exit (Point 2): P1 + 0.5*rho*v1² + rho*g*h1 = P2 + 0.5*rho*v2² + rho*g*h2.
Set P1 = P2 = 1 atm = 101325 Pa, since both points are exposed to atmospheric pressure.
Assume the tank cross-sectional area is much larger than the orifice, so v1 ≈ 0 m/s.
Set h2 = 0 as the reference elevation and h1 = h = 1.0 m.
Use g = 9.81 m/s².
Solve for the exit velocity v2 using Torricelli's theorem: v2 = sqrt(2 * g * h).
Compute v2 = sqrt(2 * 9.81 * 1.0) = sqrt(19.62) ≈ 4.43 m/s.
Report rho = 998 kg/m³, h = 1.0 m, and v2 ≈ 4.43 m/s as the final results.
State explicitly that the ideal-gas law was not used and that the result is physically valid for liquid water under these conditions.
