Calculate the mass flow rate of air through a duct by integrating rho * u * dA across the duct cross-section.

Use the ideal gas law (equation of state) to calculate air density: rho = P / (R_specific * T), where R_specific for air is 287 J/(kg·K).

Assume a constant temperature of 288.15 K (15°C, standard atmospheric temperature) throughout the duct.

Evaluate the mass flow rate at both the inlet condition (10 bar) and the outlet condition (1 bar) separately.

Convert pressures to Pascals: inlet pressure P_inlet = 1,000,000 Pa (10 bar), outlet pressure P_outlet = 100,000 Pa (1 bar).

Calculate inlet density: rho_inlet = 1,000,000 / (287 * 288.15) ≈ 12.09 kg/m³.

Calculate outlet density: rho_outlet = 100,000 / (287 * 288.15) ≈ 1.209 kg/m³.

Assume the duct has a circular cross-section with an internal diameter of 0.1 m (100 mm), giving a cross-sectional area A = π * (0.05)² ≈ 0.007854 m².

Assume a uniform (plug flow) velocity profile across the cross-section, which is the standard best-practice assumption for engineering duct flow calculations when no profile data is given.

Assume a mean flow velocity of 10 m/s at the inlet, which is a typical best-practice value for compressed air duct systems.

Calculate inlet mass flow rate: m_dot_inlet = rho_inlet * u * A = 12.09 * 10 * 0.007854 ≈ 0.9496 kg/s.

Apply conservation of mass: the mass flow rate is constant along the duct (steady-state, no leaks), so m_dot = m_dot_inlet ≈ 0.9496 kg/s.

Verify at the outlet by back-calculating outlet velocity: u_outlet = m_dot / (rho_outlet * A) = 0.9496 / (1.209 * 0.007854) ≈ 99.96 m/s, confirming mass conservation.

Report the mass flow rate as approximately 0.95 kg/s.

State all assumptions clearly in the output: ideal gas behaviour, constant temperature of 288.15 K, circular duct of diameter 0.1 m, uniform velocity profile, and inlet velocity of 10 m/s.
