The task is to compute the heat removed when steam at 100°C condenses to liquid water at 100°C in a condenser.

Use the latent heat formula q = m · hfg, not q = m · cp · ΔT, because this is a phase change at constant temperature and ΔT = 0 gives a physically incorrect result of zero.

Use the latent heat of vaporization at 100°C from steam tables: hfg = 2257 kJ/kg.

Assume a mass of m = 1 kg of steam as the standard unit basis, which is common practice in thermodynamics problems when no specific mass is given.

Compute q = 1 kg × 2257 kJ/kg = 2257 kJ.

Report the heat removed as q = 2257 kJ per kilogram of steam condensed.

State clearly that this heat is removed from the steam (released to the cooling medium in the condenser).

Note in the solution that the cp · ΔT formula is inapplicable here because the process is an isothermal phase change, not sensible heating or cooling.
