Simulate a Diesel cycle thermal efficiency calculation with a step-by-step breakdown and a final result.

Use a compression ratio (r) of 18, which is a typical best-practice value for a Diesel cycle.
Use a cutoff ratio (r_c) of 2, which is a typical best-practice value representing moderate load.
Use a specific heat ratio (gamma) of 1.4, corresponding to air as an ideal diatomic gas.

Apply the Diesel cycle efficiency formula: eta = 1 - (1 / r^(gamma - 1)) * ((r_c^gamma - 1) / (gamma * (r_c - 1))).

Present the calculation in the following sequential steps:
Step 1: State the given values: r, r_c, and gamma.
Step 2: Compute r^(gamma - 1).
Step 3: Compute the cutoff ratio term numerator: r_c^gamma - 1.
Step 4: Compute the cutoff ratio term denominator: gamma * (r_c - 1).
Step 5: Compute the cutoff ratio correction factor by dividing the result of Step 3 by the result of Step 4.
Step 6: Compute the full subtracted term by dividing the result of Step 5 by the result of Step 2.
Step 7: Compute the final efficiency: eta = 1 minus the result of Step 6.

Express the final efficiency as both a decimal (rounded to four decimal places) and a percentage (rounded to two decimal places).
Note that this is the thermal efficiency of an ideal air-standard Diesel cycle and does not account for real-world losses.
