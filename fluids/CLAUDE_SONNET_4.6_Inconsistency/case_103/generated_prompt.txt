Simulate a Francis turbine cavitation analysis using the Thoma cavitation coefficient (sigma).

The working fluid is water at 100 degrees Celsius.

Use the given vapor pressure head Hv = 0.3 m.

Use the given atmospheric pressure head Ha = 10.3 m.

Assume a net head H = 100 m, which is a typical mid-range design head for Francis turbines and consistent with best engineering practice.

Compute the suction head Hs using the relation Hs = Ha - Hv, which gives Hs = 10.3 - 0.3 = 10.0 m.

Compute the Thoma cavitation coefficient sigma using the formula sigma = Hs / H.

Substitute the values: sigma = 10.0 / 100 = 0.10.

Report the final result as sigma = 0.10.

State that this value indicates the turbine's susceptibility to cavitation; a lower sigma means higher cavitation risk.

Note that the assumed net head H = 100 m should be replaced with the actual design head if known, as sigma scales inversely with H.
