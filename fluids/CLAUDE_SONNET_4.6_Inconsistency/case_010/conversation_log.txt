Q: The wall surface is maintained at a fixed temperature Ts = 400 K, which means it is **not** adiabatic — an adiabatic wall means zero heat flux (insulated, no heat transfer). These two conditions directly contradict each other.

Which condition should actually apply to the wall?

Option A: The wall is at a fixed temperature Ts = 400 K (non-adiabatic), and we calculate the convective heat flux using Newton's law of cooling.
Option B: The wall is truly adiabatic (zero heat flux), meaning the surface temperature equals the fluid temperature and convective heat flux is zero.

Which one did you intend?
A: Option A. Calculate the convective heat flux using q'' = h(Ts - T∞).
