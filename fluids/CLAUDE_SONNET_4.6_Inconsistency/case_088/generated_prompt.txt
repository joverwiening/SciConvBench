Simulate a Venturi meter flow rate calculation using the ideal Bernoulli equation with a discharge coefficient Cd = 1.

Use an inlet diameter D1 = 100 mm (0.1 m) as a standard pipe size.
Use a throat diameter D2 = 50 mm (0.05 m), giving a diameter ratio beta = D2/D1 = 0.5, which is a typical best-practice value for Venturi meters.
Use a differential pressure Δp = 50,000 Pa (50 kPa) as a representative measured pressure drop.
Assume the flowing fluid is water at 20°C with a density ρ = 998 kg/m³.
Assume the meter is installed horizontally so elevation terms cancel in the Bernoulli equation.

Compute the throat cross-sectional area A2 = π/4 × D2².
Compute the inlet cross-sectional area A1 = π/4 × D1².
Apply the continuity equation: A1 × V1 = A2 × V2, so V1 = V2 × (A2/A1).
Apply the ideal Bernoulli equation between inlet and throat: p1 + 0.5 × ρ × V1² = p2 + 0.5 × ρ × V2².
Substitute Δp = p1 - p2 and the continuity relation to solve for V2.
The formula for V2 is: V2 = sqrt(2 × Δp / (ρ × (1 - (A2/A1)²))).
Compute the volumetric flow rate Q = Cd × A2 × V2, where Cd = 1.
Compute the mass flow rate as ṁ = ρ × Q.
Report the results: throat velocity V2 in m/s, volumetric flow rate Q in m³/s and in liters per second, and mass flow rate ṁ in kg/s.
Note explicitly in the output that Cd = 1 assumes no irreversible losses, meaning the computed Q is the theoretical maximum and will overestimate the actual flow rate compared to a real Venturi meter where Cd is typically 0.95 to 0.99.
