The pipe is inclined, not horizontal.
The outlet is 15 m higher than the inlet (elevation difference z2 - z1 = 15 m).
Assume the fluid is water at standard conditions, with a density of 1000 kg/m³.
Assume the pipe has a constant diameter throughout, so the flow velocity is the same at both ends.
Apply Bernoulli's equation between inlet (point 1) and outlet (point 2): P1/ρg + v1²/2g + z1 = P2/ρg + v2²/2g + z2.
Since the pipe diameter is constant, v1 = v2, so the velocity head terms cancel.
The equation simplifies to: P1 - P2 = ρg(z2 - z1).
Substitute values: ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s², z2 - z1 = 15 m.
Calculate: P1 - P2 = 1000 × 9.81 × 15 = 147,150 Pa.
The pressure difference is 147,150 Pa (approximately 147.15 kPa), meaning the inlet pressure is higher than the outlet pressure by this amount.
This result reflects only the hydrostatic effect of the elevation change; friction losses are neglected as Bernoulli's equation assumes ideal (inviscid) flow.
