Compute the drag force on a sphere falling in water using the physically correct approach for the given conditions.

Given parameters are: sphere diameter D = 5 mm (0.005 m), velocity V = 0.5 m/s, fluid is water at standard conditions with dynamic viscosity mu = 1.002e-3 Pa·s and density rho = 998 kg/m³.

Compute the Reynolds number as Re = rho * V * D / mu.

Since Re will be much greater than 1, do not use Stokes drag.

Use the standard drag force formula Fd = 0.5 * rho * V² * Cd * A, where A is the projected frontal area of the sphere A = pi * D² / 4.

Determine the drag coefficient Cd using the appropriate correlation for a sphere based on the computed Re value.

For Re in the range 1000 to 2e5 (Newton's law regime), use Cd = 0.44.

If Re falls in the intermediate range 1 < Re < 1000, use the Schiller-Naumann correlation: Cd = (24 / Re) * (1 + 0.15 * Re^0.687).

Compute Re numerically, select the correct Cd from the appropriate regime, then compute and report the final drag force Fd in Newtons.
