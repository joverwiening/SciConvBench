Q: The assumption of perfect incompressibility leads to a direct physical contradiction: in an incompressible medium, the bulk modulus is infinite, which makes the speed of sound infinite (not a finite value), since the speed of sound is defined as the square root of the bulk modulus divided by density.

Do you want to instead use the real bulk modulus of water at 20°C (approximately 2.2 GPa) to calculate a physically accurate, finite speed of sound?
A: Yes, use K = 2.2 GPa and density 998 kg/m³ to compute c = sqrt(K/rho).
