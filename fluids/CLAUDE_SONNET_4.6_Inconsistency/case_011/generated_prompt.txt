The pipe system involves a sudden expansion from diameter D1 = 50 mm to diameter D2 = 100 mm.
The volumetric flow rate is Q = 0.010 m³/s.
The fluid is water with density ρ = 1000 kg/m³.
Flow is steady, incompressible, and turbulent through the sudden expansion.
Calculate the cross-sectional areas: A1 = π(D1/2)² and A2 = π(D2/2)².
Calculate the velocities: V1 = Q/A1 and V2 = Q/A2.
Apply the continuity equation to confirm Q = V1·A1 = V2·A2.
Calculate the Borda-Carnot head loss using h_L = (V1 - V2)² / (2g), where g = 9.81 m/s².
Apply the energy equation between inlet (section 1) and outlet (section 2): P1/(ρg) + V1²/(2g) = P2/(ρg) + V2²/(2g) + h_L.
Rearrange to find the pressure rise: ΔP = P2 - P1 = ρg · (V1²/(2g) - V2²/(2g) - h_L).
Simplify to: ΔP = ρ · (V1² - V2²)/2 - ρ · (V1 - V2)²/2.
Report the final pressure rise ΔP in Pascals.
This result represents the actual pressure recovery accounting for energy loss due to flow separation at the sudden expansion.
