Use the density of liquid water at 20°C and 1 atm, which is 998 kg/m³.
Treat the water as an incompressible fluid throughout the calculation.
Do not apply the ideal-gas law at any point.
Assume the flow scenario is steady, inviscid, incompressible flow from a large open tank discharging through a small orifice at its base, as this is the most common textbook application of Bernoulli's equation.
Apply Bernoulli's equation along a streamline from the free surface of the tank (point 1) to the center of the orifice exit (point 2).
At point 1, set the pressure equal to atmospheric pressure (101325 Pa), the velocity approximately equal to zero (large tank assumption), and the elevation equal to h (the height of the water surface above the orifice).
At point 2, set the pressure equal to atmospheric pressure (101325 Pa), the velocity equal to v (the exit velocity to be solved), and the elevation equal to zero (reference datum).
Write Bernoulli's equation as: P1 + 0.5 * rho * v1² + rho * g * z1 = P2 + 0.5 * rho * v2² + rho * g * z2.
Substitute the known values to simplify the equation to: rho * g * h = 0.5 * rho * v².
Cancel the density rho from both sides, confirming that the result is independent of density for this configuration.
Solve for the exit velocity: v = sqrt(2 * g * h), which is Torricelli's theorem.
Use g = 9.81 m/s².
Report the exit velocity v as a function of h, and provide a sample numerical result for a representative tank height of h = 1.0 m.
For h = 1.0 m, compute v = sqrt(2 * 9.81 * 1.0) = sqrt(19.62) ≈ 4.43 m/s.
