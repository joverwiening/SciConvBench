Calculate the terminal velocity of a spherical raindrop falling through air using a full drag-weight-buoyancy force balance.

Given values are: diameter D = 2 mm = 0.002 m, density of water rho_w = 1000 kg/m³, density of air rho_air = 1.225 kg/m³, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s², drag coefficient C_d = 0.5.

Compute the volume of the sphere as V = (pi D³) / 6.

Compute the buoyancy-corrected weight as F_net = (rho_w - rho_air) * g * V.

Compute the drag force reference area as A = (pi D²) / 4.

Set the drag force equal to the net weight: (1/2) * rho_air * V_t² * C_d * A = (rho_w - rho_air) * g * V.

Solve for terminal velocity V_t: V_t = sqrt( (4 * g * D * (rho_w - rho_air)) / (3 * C_d * rho_air) ).

Substitute all values into the formula and compute V_t numerically.

Report the final answer in meters per second, rounded to two decimal places.
