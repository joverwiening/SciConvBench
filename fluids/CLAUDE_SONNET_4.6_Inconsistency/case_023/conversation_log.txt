Q: The problem states "drag force is negligible compared to its weight," but terminal velocity is defined as the point where drag force **equals** weight (net force = 0). These two conditions directly contradict each other.

Which interpretation do you want to use?

1. **True terminal velocity** — drag equals weight (drag is NOT negligible), so we solve for the velocity where the forces balance.
2. **Free-fall approximation** — drag IS negligible, meaning the drop accelerates indefinitely (no terminal velocity exists in this case, only a speed at a given time or height).
A: Use option 1 — true terminal velocity where drag balances weight (minus buoyancy). The incomplete requirement's wording is misleading; ignore it. Use the full force balance: (rho_w - rho_air) g (pi D^3/6) = (1/2) rho_air V_t^2 C_d (pi D^2/4), with C_d = 0.5.
