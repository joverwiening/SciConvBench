Compute the momentum flux for fully developed laminar flow in a circular pipe using the momentum correction factor beta = 1.

Use a mean velocity V = 1 m/s as a reasonable representative value for laminar pipe flow.
Use a pipe diameter D = 0.05 m (50 mm), giving a radius R = 0.025 m, which is a common benchmark size for laminar flow problems.
Use fluid density ρ = 1000 kg/m³ (water at approximately 20°C), which is the standard reference fluid in fluid mechanics.

Verify the flow is laminar by checking the Reynolds number: Re = ρVD/μ, using dynamic viscosity μ = 0.001 Pa·s for water, giving Re = 1000 × 1 × 0.05 / 0.001 = 50000. Adjust note: use μ = 0.001 Pa·s and confirm Re = 50, which is well within the laminar regime (Re < 2300).
Correct the Reynolds number: Re = (1000 × 1 × 0.05) / 0.001 = 50000 is incorrect; recompute as Re = ρVD/μ = (1000)(1)(0.05)/(0.001) = 50000. Use V = 0.01 m/s instead so Re = (1000)(0.01)(0.05)/(0.001) = 500, confirming laminar flow.
Use mean velocity V = 0.01 m/s to ensure laminar conditions (Re = 500 < 2300).

Compute the cross-sectional area of the pipe: A = π(D/2)² = π(0.025)² = 1.9635 × 10⁻³ m².
Apply the momentum flux formula: Momentum Flux = beta × ρ × V² × A.
Substitute beta = 1, ρ = 1000 kg/m³, V = 0.01 m/s, A = 1.9635 × 10⁻³ m².
Compute: Momentum Flux = 1 × 1000 × (0.01)² × 1.9635 × 10⁻³ = 1000 × 0.0001 × 1.9635 × 10⁻³ = 1.9635 × 10⁻⁴ N.
Report the momentum flux as approximately 1.96 × 10⁻⁴ N (Newtons), since momentum flux has units of kg·m/s² = N.
Note that using beta = 1 (instead of the theoretical beta = 4/3 for fully developed laminar flow) treats the velocity profile as uniform, which is the specified condition for this computation.
