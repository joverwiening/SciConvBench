The microchannel has a hydraulic diameter of 2 mm (D_h = 0.002 m).
The flow is laminar with Reynolds number Re = 200.
The pressure drop is computed per unit length of the channel (ΔP/L, in Pa/m).
Assume a circular cross-section, as it is the standard assumption when only hydraulic diameter is given and no other shape is specified.
Assume the fluid is water at 20°C, with dynamic viscosity μ = 0.001002 Pa·s and density ρ = 998 kg/m³, as this is the standard reference fluid in microchannel analysis.
Do not use the Manning equation; it is invalid for closed-conduit pressure-driven laminar flow.
Use the Darcy-Weisbach equation combined with the laminar friction factor to compute pressure drop per unit length.
The laminar friction factor is f = 64 / Re = 64 / 200 = 0.32.
Compute the mean flow velocity from the Reynolds number definition: Re = ρ·V·D_h / μ, so V = Re·μ / (ρ·D_h) = (200 × 0.001002) / (998 × 0.002) = 0.2004 / 1.996 ≈ 0.1004 m/s.
Apply the Darcy-Weisbach equation per unit length: ΔP/L = f · (ρ · V²) / (2 · D_h) = 0.32 × (998 × 0.1004²) / (2 × 0.002).
Compute ρ·V² = 998 × 0.010080 ≈ 10.060 Pa.
Compute ΔP/L = 0.32 × 10.060 / 0.004 = 0.32 × 2515 ≈ 804.8 Pa/m.
Verify using the equivalent Hagen-Poiseuille form: ΔP/L = 32·μ·V / D_h² = 32 × 0.001002 × 0.1004 / (0.002²) = 0.003222 / 0.000004 ≈ 805.5 Pa/m, confirming consistency.
Report the final result as ΔP/L ≈ 805 Pa/m.
State all assumptions clearly in the output: circular cross-section, water at 20°C, Re = 200, D_h = 2 mm.
