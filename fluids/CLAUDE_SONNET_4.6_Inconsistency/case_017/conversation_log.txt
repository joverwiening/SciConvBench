Q: The formula Cd = 24/Re is Stokes' Law, which is only valid for creeping flow at very low Reynolds numbers (Re << 1). Applying it at Re = 10^5 is physically incorrect and will give a meaningless result (Cd ≈ 0.00024), far from the actual value of ~0.4–0.5 for a sphere at that Reynolds number.

Which would you like to do: use the correct empirical correlation for Cd at Re = 10^5 (such as the Schiller-Naumann or experimental data), or do you still want to apply Stokes' Law despite it being outside its valid range?
A: Use the experimental drag curves, where Cd is approximately 0.5 before the drag crisis.
