The stagnation-point pressure coefficient for incompressible inviscid flow is Cp = 1.0, not 1.5.
The pressure coefficient is defined as Cp = (p - p_inf) / (0.5 * rho * V_inf^2).
At a stagnation point, the local velocity is zero by definition.
Apply Bernoulli's equation along a streamline from the freestream to the stagnation point: p_inf + 0.5 * rho * V_inf^2 = p_stag + 0.5 * rho * (0)^2.
Rearrange to get the stagnation pressure rise: p_stag - p_inf = 0.5 * rho * V_inf^2.
Substitute into the Cp definition: Cp = (0.5 * rho * V_inf^2) / (0.5 * rho * V_inf^2) = 1.0.
The maximum possible Cp under incompressible Bernoulli is 1.0, occurring only at a stagnation point.
Any value of Cp greater than 1.0 in an incompressible flow analysis indicates one of the following: an error in the pressure coefficient definition, compressibility effects not accounted for, or measurement or calibration errors.
For compressible flow, the stagnation pressure coefficient can exceed 1.0 because the dynamic pressure term must account for density changes, and the isentropic or Rayleigh Pitot relations replace Bernoulli.
The value Cp = 1.5 stated in the original problem is therefore incorrect for incompressible inviscid flow and should be rejected.
