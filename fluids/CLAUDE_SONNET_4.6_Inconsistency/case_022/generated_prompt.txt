The flow follows a curved streamline with speed V = 10 m/s and radius of curvature R = 5 m.
The fluid is water with density rho = 1000 kg/m^3.
The normal distance across the streamline is delta_n = 0.10 m.
Use the centripetal pressure gradient condition for curved streamline flow: dP/dn = rho * V^2 / R, where n points toward the center of curvature.
This means pressure increases in the direction toward the center of curvature.
Compute the pressure gradient magnitude: dP/dn = 1000 * (10)^2 / 5 = 1000 * 100 / 5 = 20000 Pa/m.
Approximate the pressure difference over the normal distance delta_n = 0.10 m as delta_P = (dP/dn) * delta_n.
Compute delta_P = 20000 Pa/m * 0.10 m = 2000 Pa.
The pressure on the outer side of the curved streamline is higher than on the inner side by 2000 Pa.
Report the final result: the pressure difference between the outer side and the inner side across delta_n = 0.10 m is delta_P = 2000 Pa, with the outer side at higher pressure.
