The problem involves steady, incompressible flow in a constant-area pipe.
The inlet volumetric flow rate Q_in is 0.01 m³/s.
Apply conservation of mass (continuity equation) for steady incompressible flow: Q_in = Q_out.
Show that Q_out must equal Q_in = 0.01 m³/s.
Assume a circular pipe cross-section with an internal diameter of 0.1 m (a common nominal pipe size in engineering practice).
Calculate the cross-sectional area as A = π × (D/2)² = π × (0.05)² ≈ 0.007854 m².
Calculate the velocity at the inlet as V_in = Q_in / A = 0.01 / 0.007854 ≈ 1.27 m/s.
Because the pipe has constant area and the flow is steady and incompressible, the outlet velocity equals the inlet velocity: V_out = Q_out / A = 0.01 / 0.007854 ≈ 1.27 m/s.
State clearly that the purpose of the exercise is to demonstrate that mass conservation forces Q_out = Q_in = 0.01 m³/s for these conditions, and therefore V_in = V_out.
