The task is to compute the thermal stress in a steel rod under fully constrained conditions using the formula sigma = E * alpha * delta_T.

Use the formula: sigma = E * alpha * delta_T.

The rod is fixed at both ends, meaning no thermal expansion is permitted (fully constrained).

Use standard material properties for steel: Young's modulus E = 200 GPa (200 × 10^9 Pa), and coefficient of thermal expansion alpha = 12 × 10^-6 per degree Kelvin.

The temperature change delta_T = 50 K.

Substitute the values: sigma = (200 × 10^9 Pa) × (12 × 10^-6 /K) × (50 K).

Compute step by step: alpha × delta_T = 12 × 10^-6 × 50 = 600 × 10^-6 = 6 × 10^-4 (dimensionless strain).

Then sigma = 200 × 10^9 × 6 × 10^-4 = 120 × 10^6 Pa = 120 MPa.

The resulting thermal stress is 120 MPa (compressive), because the rod is prevented from expanding and the internal stress acts to compress the rod.

Report the final answer as sigma = 120 MPa (compressive).
