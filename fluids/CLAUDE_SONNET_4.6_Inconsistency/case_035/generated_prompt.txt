Model a forced vortex in a rotating cylindrical tank of water with tangential velocity v_theta = omega * r.
Assume omega = 10 rad/s as a reasonable engineering value for a laboratory-scale rotating tank.
Use water density rho = 1000 kg/m³.
Apply the radial form of the Euler/Bernoulli equation for a rotating flow: dP/dr = rho * omega² * r.
Integrate this equation from r1 = 0.1 m to r2 = 0.3 m along a radial line at constant elevation (ignore gravity effects in the radial direction).
The pressure difference is given by: P2 - P1 = (1/2) * rho * omega² * (r2² - r1²).
Substitute values: P2 - P1 = 0.5 * 1000 * 10² * (0.3² - 0.1²).
Compute r2² = 0.09 m², r1² = 0.01 m², so r2² - r1² = 0.08 m².
Compute P2 - P1 = 0.5 * 1000 * 100 * 0.08 = 4000 Pa.
Report the final pressure difference as P2 - P1 = 4000 Pa (4 kPa), with the higher pressure at the outer radius r = 0.3 m.
State clearly that this result assumes steady, incompressible, inviscid flow with no axial velocity component and no change in elevation along the radial line.
