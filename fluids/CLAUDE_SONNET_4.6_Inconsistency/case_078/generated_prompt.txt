The task is to compute the thermal efficiency of a Diesel cycle using standard engineering best-practice values.

The cycle is a Diesel cycle, not an Otto cycle.
The heat addition process in the Diesel cycle occurs at constant pressure.
The heat rejection process occurs at constant volume.
Use a compression ratio (r) of 18, which is a typical best-practice value for a Diesel cycle.
Use a cutoff ratio (r_c) of 2.5, which is a typical best-practice value representing the ratio of volume after to before heat addition.
Use a specific heat ratio (gamma) of 1.4, corresponding to air as the working fluid (air-standard analysis).
Apply the Diesel cycle thermal efficiency formula: eta = 1 - (1 / r^(gamma - 1)) * ((r_c^gamma - 1) / (gamma * (r_c - 1))).
Substitute r = 18, r_c = 2.5, and gamma = 1.4 into the formula.
Compute each term step by step: first compute r^(gamma - 1), then compute r_c^gamma, then compute the cutoff ratio correction factor, then compute the final efficiency.
State clearly that the cycle being analyzed is the Diesel cycle.
Report the final thermal efficiency as a percentage rounded to two decimal places.
Do not use the Otto cycle efficiency formula eta = 1 - 1/r^(gamma-1) anywhere in the solution.
