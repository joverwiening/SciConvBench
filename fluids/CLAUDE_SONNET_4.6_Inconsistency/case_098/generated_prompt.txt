Use thin airfoil theory to compute the lift coefficient of a NACA 4412 airfoil.
Use an angle of attack of 5 degrees.
Convert 5 degrees to radians before applying the formula: alpha = 5 × (π/180) ≈ 0.08727 radians.
Apply the thin airfoil theory lift formula: Cl = 2π × alpha.
Compute Cl = 2π × 0.08727 ≈ 0.5484.
Account for the NACA 4412 zero-lift angle of attack, which is approximately -4 degrees (-0.06981 radians), by using the effective angle: alpha_eff = alpha - alpha_L0 = 0.08727 - (-0.06981) = 0.15708 radians.
Compute the corrected Cl = 2π × 0.15708 ≈ 0.9870.
Report both results: the uncorrected Cl using geometric angle only (≈ 0.5484) and the corrected Cl accounting for camber (≈ 0.9870).
Note that thin airfoil theory with Cl = 2π alpha is valid only for small angles of attack before stall, which is satisfied at 5 degrees.
Note that the NACA 4412 is a cambered airfoil, so ignoring the zero-lift angle of attack underestimates the lift coefficient.
