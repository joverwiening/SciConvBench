Use the Ergun equation to compute the pressure drop per unit length of a packed bed.
The bed porosity (void fraction) is epsilon = 0.4.
The particle diameter is dp = 5 mm = 0.005 m.
Assume perfectly spherical particles with sphericity phi_s = 1.
Assume the fluid is air at standard conditions (20°C, 1 atm): density rho = 1.204 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity mu = 1.825e-5 Pa·s.
Assume a superficial velocity of u = 0.1 m/s, which is a typical value for gas flow through packed beds in industrial practice.
Apply the Ergun equation: dP/L = (150 * mu * u * (1 - epsilon)²) / (phi_s² * dp² * epsilon³) + (1.75 * rho * u² * (1 - epsilon)) / (phi_s * dp * epsilon³).
Compute each term separately (viscous/Kozeny-Carman term and inertial/Burke-Plummer term).
Report the pressure drop per unit length dP/L in units of Pa/m.
Report the individual contributions of the viscous term and the inertial term to show their relative importance.
