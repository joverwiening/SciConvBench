Simulate steady, viscous, incompressible flow of oil between two infinite parallel plates (Couette-Poiseuille or plane Poiseuille flow).

Use dynamic viscosity mu = 0.1 Pa·s for the oil.

Assume plate separation h = 0.01 m (1 cm) as a standard engineering value.

Treat the flow as pressure-driven (plane Poiseuille flow) with the upper plate stationary and the lower plate stationary (no moving wall).

Assume a pressure gradient dP/dx = -100 Pa/m as a reasonable representative value for viscous flow between plates.

The velocity profile for plane Poiseuille flow is u(y) = (1 / (2*mu)) * (-dP/dx) * y * (h - y), where y ranges from 0 to h.

Compute the velocity gradient du/dy = (1 / (2*mu)) * (-dP/dx) * (h - 2*y).

Evaluate du/dy at the wall: at y = 0, du/dy = (-dP/dx) * h / (2*mu); at y = h, du/dy = -(-dP/dx) * h / (2*mu).

Compute wall shear stress using tau_wall = mu * (du/dy) evaluated at y = 0.

Substitute values: tau_wall = mu * (-dP/dx) * h / (2*mu) = (-dP/dx) * h / 2.

Calculate: tau_wall = 100 * 0.01 / 2 = 0.5 Pa.

Report the wall shear stress as tau_wall = 0.5 Pa.

State all assumptions clearly in the output: viscous flow, mu = 0.1 Pa·s, h = 0.01 m, dP/dx = -100 Pa/m, plane Poiseuille flow, no-slip condition at both walls.
