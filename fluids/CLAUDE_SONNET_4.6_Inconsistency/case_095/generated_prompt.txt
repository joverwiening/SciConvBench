Compute the momentum flux for fully developed laminar flow in a circular pipe using a momentum correction factor beta = 1.

Use a mean velocity V = 1 m/s as a reasonable representative value for laminar pipe flow.
Use a pipe diameter D = 0.05 m (50 mm), giving a radius R = 0.025 m, which is a common benchmark size for laminar flow problems.
Use fluid density ρ = 1000 kg/m³ (water at approximately 20°C) as the standard reference fluid.
Compute the cross-sectional area of the pipe as A = π R² = π (0.025)² m².
The momentum flux is defined as M = beta × ρ × V² × A.
Substitute beta = 1, ρ = 1000 kg/m³, V = 1 m/s, and A = π (0.025)² m² into the formula.
Calculate A = π × 0.000625 = 0.0019635 m².
Calculate momentum flux M = 1 × 1000 × (1)² × 0.0019635 = 1.9635 N.
Report the final momentum flux as M ≈ 1.96 N.
Note that setting beta = 1 treats the velocity profile as uniform (plug flow assumption for momentum purposes), even though the actual parabolic velocity profile for laminar flow would give beta = 4/3 ≈ 1.333.
State clearly that the result M ≈ 1.96 N represents the momentum flux under the given assumption of beta = 1.
