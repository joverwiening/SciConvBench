The fin is a straight rectangular fin with thermal conductivity k = 200 W/m·K.
The fin length is L = 30 mm (0.030 m).
The fin thickness is t = 2 mm (0.002 m).
The fin width is w = 20 mm (0.020 m).
The convection coefficient is h = 25 W/m²·K.
Assume a base temperature T_b = 100°C and ambient temperature T_∞ = 25°C, giving an excess temperature θ_b = T_b - T_∞ = 75°C. This is a standard textbook assumption.
Use the convective-tip fin formula, which accounts for convection at the tip by replacing the actual length L with a corrected length L_c = L + t/2.
Compute L_c = 0.030 + 0.002/2 = 0.031 m.
Compute the fin cross-sectional area A_c = t × w = 0.002 × 0.020 = 4 × 10⁻⁵ m².
Compute the fin perimeter P = 2 × (t + w) = 2 × (0.002 + 0.020) = 0.044 m.
Compute the fin parameter m = sqrt(h × P / (k × A_c)) = sqrt(25 × 0.044 / (200 × 4 × 10⁻⁵)).
Evaluate the numerator: 25 × 0.044 = 1.1.
Evaluate the denominator: 200 × 4 × 10⁻⁵ = 0.008.
Compute m = sqrt(1.1 / 0.008) = sqrt(137.5) ≈ 11.726 m⁻¹.
Compute m × L_c = 11.726 × 0.031 ≈ 0.3635.
Compute the maximum fin heat transfer M = sqrt(h × P × k × A_c) × θ_b.
Evaluate h × P × k × A_c = 25 × 0.044 × 200 × 4 × 10⁻⁵ = 0.0088.
Compute M = sqrt(0.0088) × 75 = 0.09381 × 75 ≈ 7.036 W.
Apply the corrected-length adiabatic-tip formula: Q_fin = M × tanh(m × L_c).
Compute tanh(0.3635) ≈ 0.3484.
Compute Q_fin = 7.036 × 0.3484 ≈ 2.45 W.
Report the fin heat transfer rate as approximately 2.45 W under the stated conditions and assumptions.
