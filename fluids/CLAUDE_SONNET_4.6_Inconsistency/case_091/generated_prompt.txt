Design a counterflow heat exchanger and compute the required heat transfer area using the LMTD method with counterflow terminal difference definitions.

Assume a water-to-water counterflow heat exchanger in an HVAC or general process heating application.

Use the following assumed operating conditions based on best practices:
Hot-side inlet temperature Th,in = 90°C.
Hot-side outlet temperature Th,out = 60°C.
Cold-side inlet temperature Tc,in = 20°C.
Cold-side outlet temperature Tc,out = 50°C.

Compute ΔT1 = Th,in - Tc,out = 90 - 50 = 40°C.
Compute ΔT2 = Th,out - Tc,in = 60 - 20 = 40°C.

Since ΔT1 equals ΔT2, the LMTD equals that constant value: LMTD = 40°C.
If ΔT1 and ΔT2 are not equal in a variant scenario, compute LMTD = (ΔT1 - ΔT2) / ln(ΔT1 / ΔT2).

Assume the overall heat transfer coefficient U = 2000 W/(m²·K), which is a typical value for liquid-to-liquid heat exchangers.

Assume a heat duty Q = 250,000 W (250 kW), representative of a medium-scale HVAC application.

Verify energy balance consistency: Q = m_dot_h * Cp_h * (Th,in - Th,out) = m_dot_c * Cp_c * (Tc,out - Tc,in).
Use Cp = 4186 J/(kg·K) for water on both sides.
Hot-side mass flow rate = Q / (Cp * (Th,in - Th,out)) = 250000 / (4186 * 30) ≈ 1.99 kg/s.
Cold-side mass flow rate = Q / (Cp * (Tc,out - Tc,in)) = 250000 / (4186 * 30) ≈ 1.99 kg/s.

Compute the required heat transfer area using A = Q / (U * LMTD).
A = 250000 / (2000 * 40) = 3.125 m².

Report the final required heat exchanger area as A ≈ 3.13 m².
