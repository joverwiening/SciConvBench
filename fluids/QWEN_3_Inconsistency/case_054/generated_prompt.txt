Assume the base temperature of the fin is 100°C.  
Assume the ambient temperature is 25°C.  
The fin is made of material with thermal conductivity k = 200 W/m·K.  
The fin has a rectangular cross-section with length L = 30 mm.  
The fin has a thickness t = 2 mm.  
The fin has a width w = 20 mm.  
The tip of the fin is not perfectly insulated; convection occurs at the tip with heat transfer coefficient h = 25 W/m²·K.  
Convection also occurs along the lateral surfaces of the fin with the same heat transfer coefficient h = 25 W/m²·K.  
The fin heat transfer calculation must use the formula that accounts for convection at the tip.  
The cross-sectional area for conduction is constant along the fin.  
The perimeter for convection is calculated as the sum of the four sides of the rectangular cross-section.  
The fin efficiency is not assumed to be 100%; the exact analytical solution for a convective tip is used.  
The heat transfer rate is calculated using the standard fin equation with hyperbolic functions, including the tip convection term.  
The base of the fin is at uniform temperature.  
Steady-state conditions are assumed.  
One-dimensional conduction along the length of the fin is assumed.
