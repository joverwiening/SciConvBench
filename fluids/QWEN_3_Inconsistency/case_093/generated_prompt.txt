Assume the flow is around a two-dimensional blunt body in incompressible, inviscid flow.  
Use Bernoulli’s equation to relate stagnation pressure to freestream conditions.  
The stagnation-point pressure coefficient is defined as $ C_p = \frac{p_{\text{stag}} - p_{\infty}}{\frac{1}{2} \rho V_{\infty}^2} $.  
At the stagnation point, velocity is zero for incompressible, inviscid flow.  
Apply Bernoulli’s equation between freestream and stagnation point: $ p_{\infty} + \frac{1}{2} \rho V_{\infty}^2 = p_{\text{stag}} $.  
Substitute into the pressure coefficient definition: $ C_p = \frac{(p_{\text{stag}} - p_{\infty})}{\frac{1}{2} \rho V_{\infty}^2} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \rho V_{\infty}^2}{\frac{1}{2} \rho V_{\infty}^2} = 1 $.  
The theoretical value of the stagnation-point pressure coefficient for incompressible, inviscid flow is 1.  
The given value of $ C_p = 1.5 $ exceeds the theoretical maximum for incompressible flow.  
Report that $ C_p = 1.5 $ is not physically possible under standard incompressible, inviscid assumptions.
