Q: Assume a horizontal pipe with a gradual contraction (venturi) where the inlet diameter is 0.1 m, the throat diameter is 0.05 m, and the inlet velocity is 2 m/s. The flow is steady, incompressible, and inviscid. Apply the continuity equation and Bernoulli’s equation along a streamline from the inlet to the throat. Use the density of water as 998 kg/m³. Neglect viscous effects and elevation changes. Compute the pressure difference between the inlet and the throat.
A: Use density ρ = 998 kg/m³ for water at 20°C. Assume steady, incompressible, inviscid flow in a horizontal venturi with no elevation change.

Given:
- Inlet diameter, d₁ = 0.1 m
- Throat diameter, d₂ = 0.05 m
- Inlet velocity, V₁ = 2 m/s
- Density, ρ = 998 kg/m³

Apply continuity equation:
A₁V₁ = A₂V₂  
A = πd²/4  
A₁ = π(0.1)²/4 = 0.007854 m²  
A₂ = π(0.05)²/4 = 0.0019635 m²  
V₂ = (A₁/A₂)V₁ = (0.007854 / 0.0019635) × 2 ≈ 8 m/s

Apply Bernoulli’s equation (horizontal, no elevation change):
P₁ + ½ρV₁² = P₂ + ½ρV₂²  
ΔP = P₁ - P₂ = ½ρ(V₂² - V₁²)  
ΔP = 0.5 × 998 × (8² - 2²) = 0.5 × 998 × (64 - 4) = 0.5 × 998 × 60  
ΔP = 29,940 Pa ≈ 29.94 kPa

The pressure difference between inlet and throat is approximately 29.94 kPa.
