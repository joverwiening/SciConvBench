Compute the drag force acting on a sphere moving through glycerin using the following specifications:
The sphere has a diameter of 5 mm (radius of 2.5 mm).
The sphere moves at a constant velocity of 0.1 m/s through the glycerin.
Use room temperature conditions (20°C) for the glycerin properties.
At 20°C, glycerin has a dynamic viscosity of approximately 1.412 Pa·s and a density of approximately 1260 kg/m³.
Calculate the Reynolds number to determine the appropriate drag model.
If the Reynolds number is less than 1, use Stokes' law for drag force calculation.
If the Reynolds number is between 1 and 1000, use the intermediate Reynolds number correlation for drag coefficient.
If the Reynolds number exceeds 1000, use the standard drag equation with appropriate drag coefficient for a sphere.
The drag force should be expressed in Newtons.
Include the Reynolds number value in the final result to justify the drag model used.
