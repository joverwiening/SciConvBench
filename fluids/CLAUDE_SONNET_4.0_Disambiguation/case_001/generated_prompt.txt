Calculate the pressure drop in a horizontal pipe using the Darcy-Weisbach equation.
The pipe is 100 meters long with an inner diameter of 50 millimeters.
The pipe is made of galvanized iron with a surface roughness of 0.15 millimeters.
Water flows through the pipe at a rate of 2 liters per second.
The water temperature is 20°C.
Use the water properties at 20°C: density of 998.2 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity of 1.002 × 10⁻³ Pa·s.
Calculate the Reynolds number to determine the flow regime.
Use the Colebrook-White equation or Moody chart to determine the friction factor.
Apply the Darcy-Weisbach equation: ΔP = f × (L/D) × (ρv²/2) where f is the friction factor, L is length, D is diameter, ρ is density, and v is velocity.
Express the final pressure drop in Pascals or other appropriate pressure units.
