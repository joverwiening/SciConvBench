I will assume room temperature, which is typically 20°C (293.15 K). At this temperature, water's kinematic viscosity is approximately 1.004 × 10^-6 m²/s.

Now I will calculate the Reynolds number to determine flow regime:

Reynolds Number (Re) = (Velocity × Diameter) ÷ Kinematic Viscosity
Re = (1.5 m/s × 0.04 m) ÷ (1.004 × 10^-6 m²/s)
Re = 60,000


The flow is turbulent.
The Reynolds number (60,000) is well above the critical Reynolds number of 2,300 for pipe flow.
The flow regime is definitively turbulent for water in this smooth pipe.
Turbulent flow is characterized by irregular fluctuations and mixing within the fluid.
The high Reynolds number indicates significant inertial forces compared to viscous forces.
