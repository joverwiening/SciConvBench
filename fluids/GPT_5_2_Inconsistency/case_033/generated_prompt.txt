Model air as a perfect gas with gamma = 1.4 and R = 287 J/(kg·K).
Use tank (stagnation) conditions P0 = 500 kPa and T0 = 300 K.
Use ambient/back pressure Pb = 100 kPa.
Assume a converging nozzle with isentropic, ideal flow and discharge coefficient Cd = 1.0.
Assume the nozzle exit pressure equals the ambient pressure, Pe = 100 kPa.
Compute the pressure ratio Pe/P0 = 0.2 and compare to the critical ratio (2/(gamma+1))^(gamma/(gamma-1)) to determine whether the nozzle is choked.
If choked, set the exit (throat) Mach number to 1 and use the choked mass flux formula G = (mdot/A) = P0*sqrt(gamma/(R*T0))*(2/(gamma+1))^((gamma+1)/(2*(gamma-1))).
Report the primary result as mass flux G in kg/(s·m^2).
If a mass flow rate is also desired, assume a representative nozzle area A = 1.0e-4 m^2 (equivalent to a circular diameter of about 11.3 mm) and compute mdot = G*A, clearly labeling this as an assumed-area example.
