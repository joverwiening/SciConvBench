Assume steady, 1D, frictionless (isentropic) flow of air with γ = 1.4 and constant stagnation temperature T0 through the converging nozzle.
Given inlet Mach number M1 = 2.0 and area ratio A2/A1 = 0.7.
Use the isentropic area–Mach relation A/A* = (1/M)·[(2/(γ+1))·(1+(γ−1)M^2/2)]^((γ+1)/(2(γ−1))) to determine the exit Mach number M2 on the supersonic branch consistent with a converging nozzle (area decrease implies M increases for supersonic flow).
Compute A1/A* from M1, then compute A2/A* = (A2/A1)(A1/A*), then solve for M2.
Use constant T0 to relate velocity to Mach: V = M·sqrt(γRT) with T = T0/(1+(γ−1)M^2/2), equivalently V = M·sqrt(γRT0/(1+(γ−1)M^2/2)).
Report the exit-to-inlet velocity ratio V2/V1 = [M2/sqrt(1+(γ−1)M2^2/2)] / [M1/sqrt(1+(γ−1)M1^2/2)].
Report the velocity increase in terms of T0: ΔV = sqrt(γRT0)·( M2/sqrt(1+(γ−1)M2^2/2) − M1/sqrt(1+(γ−1)M1^2/2) ).
For a numerical ΔV, assume inlet static temperature T1 = 288 K and use T0 = T1·(1+(γ−1)M1^2/2).
Use R = 287 J/(kg·K) for air for the numerical evaluation.
Compute V1 and V2 from the above relations and output ΔV = V2 − V1 in m/s, along with V2/V1 and the ΔV expression in terms of T0.
