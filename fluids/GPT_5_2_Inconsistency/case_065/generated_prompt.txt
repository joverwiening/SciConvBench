Use Thoma’s cavitation factor definition σ = (Ha − Hv − hs − hloss) / H.
Take Ha = 10.3 m of water head.
Take Hv = 0.3 m of water head.
Take the runner installation (suction head) hs = 20 m (runner centerline 20 m above tailrace).
Assume suction-side/draft-tube head loss hloss ≈ 1 m, and optionally check the sensitivity for hloss in the range 0 to 1 m.
Compute the numerator N = Ha − Hv − hs − hloss.
Show numerically that N = 10.3 − 0.3 − 20 − 1 = −11.0 m (and for hloss = 0, N = −10.0 m).
Conclude that σ = N / H is negative for any physically meaningful operating head H > 0.
State that because σ cannot be positive for any H > 0 under the given installation height and site heads, the requirement “show σ is positive and cavitation is impossible for any head” is contradictory to the provided data.
State the physically correct conclusion: with the runner 20 m above tailrace at Ha = 10.3 m and Hv = 0.3 m, the available suction head is insufficient and cavitation risk is unavoidable (σ < 0) for any positive operating head, unless the installation height is reduced and/or the local absolute pressure is increased.
