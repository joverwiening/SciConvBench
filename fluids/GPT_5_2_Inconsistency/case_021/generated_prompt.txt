Treat the gas as ideal air with R = 287 J/(kg·K), cp = 1004 J/(kg·K), and γ = 1.4.
Use inlet stagnation temperature T0 = 300 K.
Use inlet pressure p0 = 10 bar and exit static pressure pe = 1 bar.
Model the nozzle as steady, one-dimensional flow with negligible potential energy change.
For the isothermal case, enforce T = 300 K throughout the nozzle via heat transfer as needed.
For the isothermal case, use the ideal-gas relation v = RT/p to relate specific volume to pressure at T = 300 K.
For the isothermal case, use the steady-flow energy equation with the “constant enthalpy” assumption (h1 = h2) so that the exit velocity is obtained from Ve = sqrt(2(h1 − h2) + V1^2) which reduces to Ve = V1 under strict constant-h, and therefore report that the constant-enthalpy assumption is incompatible with a nonzero acceleration for an ideal gas at constant temperature.
For the adiabatic case, assume isentropic flow with constant stagnation enthalpy h0 and use T/T0 = (p/p0)^((γ−1)/γ) to find exit static temperature Te.
For the adiabatic case, compute exit velocity from Ve = sqrt(2 cp (T0 − Te)) assuming negligible inlet velocity (V1 ≈ 0).
Do not use an incompressible/constant-density model.
