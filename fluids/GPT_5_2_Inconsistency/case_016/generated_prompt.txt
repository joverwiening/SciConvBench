Assume a benchtop microcentrifuge fixed-angle rotor operating at 10,000 RPM with a representative water column spanning radii r0 = 0.020 m (inner edge, defined as 0 gauge pressure) to r1 = 0.080 m (outer edge, rotor radius).  
Assume liquid water at 20 °C with density ρ = 998 kg/m^3.  
Use the physically correct “hydrostatic” relation in a rotating frame, replacing g·h with the centrifugal acceleration field, so that dP/dr = ρ·ω^2·r.  
Convert speed to angular velocity using ω = 2π·(RPM/60) with RPM = 10,000.  
Compute the gauge pressure distribution relative to r0 as P(r) = (1/2)·ρ·ω^2·(r^2 − r0^2) for r in [r0, r1].  
Report P(r) in Pascals and optionally in bar (1 bar = 100,000 Pa).  
Compute and report the maximum gauge pressure at the outer edge as Pmax = P(r1).  
If absolute pressure is needed, add atmospheric pressure: Pabs(r) = Patm + P(r) with Patm = 101,325 Pa.  
Provide a tabulated pressure profile at a reasonable radial resolution (e.g., 1 mm or 5 mm increments) from r0 to r1.  
State that using P = ρ·g·h directly is not appropriate for a centrifuge; the correct analog uses ω^2·r in place of g and integrates over radius.
