Q: What units is the 20° angle of attack given in for use in the formula (degrees to be converted to radians, or already in radians)?
A: It’s given as **20 degrees**. For \(C_l = 2\pi \alpha\), \(\alpha\) must be in **radians**, so you’d convert degrees to radians—but per the complete requirement, you **shouldn’t apply thin airfoil theory at 20°**; use a small angle like **5°** (converted to radians) or a nonlinear/stall model/experimental data for 20°.
