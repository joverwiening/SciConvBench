Treat liquid water at 20 °C as incompressible.
Use density ρ = 998 kg/m^3 for water at 20 °C.
Do not use the ideal-gas law for liquid-water density.
Assume a standard Bernoulli scenario: a large reservoir/tank draining to atmosphere through a horizontal nozzle/pipe.
Define point 1 at the tank free surface and point 2 at the outlet jet just outside the nozzle/pipe.
Assume both point 1 and point 2 are at atmospheric pressure, so p1 = p2 = 1 atm and pressure terms cancel in Bernoulli.
Assume the elevation head difference is z1 − z2 = Δz = 1.0 m.
Assume the tank is large so the free-surface velocity is negligible, v1 ≈ 0.
Assume no pump or turbine is present, so hp = 0 and ht = 0.
Assume head loss is negligible unless explicitly needed, so hL = 0.
Apply Bernoulli/energy equation between points 1 and 2 using the above assumptions and ρ = 998 kg/m^3.
Compute the outlet speed as v2 = sqrt(2 g Δz) with g = 9.81 m/s^2.
Optionally report the corresponding dynamic pressure at the outlet as (1/2) ρ v2^2 using ρ = 998 kg/m^3.
