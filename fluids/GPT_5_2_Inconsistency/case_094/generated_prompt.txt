Compute capillary rise of water in a clean glass capillary using Jurin’s law.  
Use physically valid surface tension σ = +0.072 N/m.  
Use gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².  
Assume the capillary is a vertical, circular, uniform-bore glass tube.  
Assume the tube inner radius r = 0.0005 m (0.5 mm) as a reasonable representative capillary size.  
Assume water perfectly wets clean glass with contact angle θ = 0° so cosθ = 1.  
Assume temperature 20 °C and use water density ρ = 998 kg/m³.  
Neglect vapor pressure effects, dynamic effects, and meniscus shape corrections beyond Jurin’s law.  
Compute capillary rise height h from h = (2 σ cosθ) / (ρ g r).  
Substitute values to obtain h = (2·0.072·1)/(998·9.81·0.0005) ≈ 0.0294 m.  
Report the capillary rise as approximately 2.94 cm upward.
