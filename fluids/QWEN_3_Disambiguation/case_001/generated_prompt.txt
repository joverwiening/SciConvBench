Calculate the pressure drop in a 100 m long horizontal pipe with a 50 mm diameter carrying water at a flow rate of 2 L/s and a temperature of 20°C.
Use the Darcy-Weisbach equation to determine the pressure drop.
Assume the pipe is smooth unless roughness is specified.
Use standard fluid properties for water at 20°C.
The pipe is horizontal, so elevation change does not contribute to pressure drop.
Account for frictional losses only.
Use the Reynolds number to determine the flow regime (laminar or turbulent).
If the flow is turbulent, use the Colebrook equation or an appropriate friction factor correlation.
Deliver the pressure drop in Pascals (Pa).
