The pressure difference between the inner and outer side of the streamline is computed.
The normal component of Euler's equation for curved streamlines is applied, where the pressure gradient dP/dn equals rho multiplied by the square of the velocity V, divided by the radius of curvature R.
The distance n is measured towards the center of curvature.
Given values are fluid speed V = 10 m/s, radius of curvature R = 5 m, normal distance Δn = 0.10 m, and fluid density rho = 1000 kg/m^3.
The pressure gradient dP/dn is calculated as (1000 kg/m^3) * (10 m/s)^2 / (5 m), which equals 20000 Pa/m.
The pressure difference ΔP across the normal distance Δn is calculated as (20000 Pa/m) * (0.10 m), which equals 2000 Pa.
The pressure on the inner side of the streamline is 2000 Pa higher than the pressure on the outer side.
