Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for a homogeneous isotropic solid column.  
Use the given geometric data: length L = 2000 mm, diameter d = 25 mm (radius r = 12.5 mm).  
Material data: Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa, yield strength σ_y = 250 MPa.  
Applied axial compressive load: P = 25 000 N.  
End condition: effective‑length factor K = 0.7 (fixed‑pinned), so effective length L_e = K·L = 1400 mm.  

Compute cross‑sectional area:  
A = π r² = π·(12.5 mm)² ≈ 4.91 × 10² mm².  

Compute second moment of area for a solid circle:  
I = π d⁴/64 = π·(25 mm)⁴/64 ≈ 1.92 × 10⁴ mm⁴.  

Compute radius of gyration:  
r_g = √(I/A) ≈ √(1.92 × 10⁴ / 4.91 × 10²) ≈ 6.25 mm.  

Slenderness ratio:  
λ = L_e / r_g ≈ 1400 mm / 6.25 mm ≈ 224 (highly slender).  

Euler critical buckling load (pinned‑pinned formula with effective length):  
P_cr = π² E I / L_e²  
 ≈ π²·200 000 MPa·1.92 × 10⁴ mm⁴ / (1400 mm)²  
 ≈ 1.93 × 10⁴ N ≈ 19.3 kN.  

Yield load based on axial stress:  
P_yield = σ_y · A = 250 MPa·4.91 × 10² mm² ≈ 1.23 × 10⁵ N ≈ 123 kN.  

Comparison of applied load with limits:  
- Applied load P = 25 kN > P_cr ≈ 19.3 kN → buckling will occur before reaching the critical Euler load.  
- P = 25 kN << P_yield ≈ 123 kN → material yielding is not the governing failure mode.  

Therefore, for the specified column and loading, elastic buckling governs the response.  

Additional assumptions:  
- No initial geometric imperfections or residual stresses.  
- Load is perfectly axial and applied slowly (quasi‑static).  
- Temperature effects are negligible.  

Governing relations to be used in analysis:  
1. Axial stress σ = P/A.  
2. Euler critical load P_cr = π² E I / (K L)².  
3. Yield load P_yield = σ_y · A.  
4. Slenderness ratio λ = K L / √(I/A).  

These equations and computed values provide the required comparison of yielding versus Euler buckling and confirm that a buckling/instability check is essential for the given column.
