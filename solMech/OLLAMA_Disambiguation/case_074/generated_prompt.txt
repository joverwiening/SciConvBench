Assume a simply supported rectangular steel beam with a span length L = 6 m.  
Assume a uniformly distributed transverse load w = 10 kN/m acting over the entire span.  
Assume an additional mid‑span bending moment M₀ = 5 kNm (representing the “moment” mentioned).  
Assume the beam material is structural steel with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa and an allowable bending stress σ_allow = 250 MPa.  
Assume the beam width b = 200 mm (0.20 m).  

Governing relations:  
1. Maximum bending moment from the distributed load: M_w = w L² / 8.  
2. Total maximum bending moment: M_max = M_w + M₀.  
3. Section modulus of a rectangular section: Z = b h² / 6.  
4. Bending stress: σ = M_max / Z.  
5. Required height to satisfy σ ≤ σ_allow: h = √[6 M_max / (b σ_allow)].  

Calculations:  
M_w = 10 kN/m × (6 m)² / 8 = 45 kNm.  
M_max = 45 kNm + 5 kNm = 50 kNm = 50 × 10⁶ N·mm.  
h = √[6 × 50 × 10⁶ N·mm / (200 mm × 250 N/mm²)] ≈ 77.5 mm.  

Select a practical height h = 80 mm (round up to the nearest standard size).  

Result: A rectangular steel beam 200 mm wide and 80 mm deep will keep the bending stress below the allowable 250 MPa under the assumed loading and moment.
