Assume the bar is prismatic, homogeneous, isotropic, and obeys small‑deformation linear‑elastic behavior.  
Assume the cross‑section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm, giving an area A = b·h.  
Assume the loads are applied slowly so that static equilibrium applies and inertia effects are negligible.  
Assume the axial normal stress is uniformly distributed over the section (valid for pure tension/compression).  
Assume the shear stress is uniformly distributed over the section (valid for average shear stress calculation).  
Assume plane sections remain plane and transverse shear deformation is neglected for the average stress evaluation.  

Let N be the axial tensile (positive) or compressive (negative) force, V be the transverse shear force, and M be the bending moment caused by the transverse load about the centroidal axis.  

Average normal stress at the section:  
σ̄ = N / A  

Average shear stress at the section:  
τ̄ = V / A  

If the transverse load produces a bending moment M, the normal stress distribution is linear, but its average over the section is zero, so σ̄ remains N/A.  

The governing equilibrium relations are:  
∑F_x = N = constant along the bar,  
∑F_y = V = constant along the bar,  
∑M_z = M = constant along the bar (if needed for bending stress distribution).  

These relations together with the geometric definition of A = b·h provide the required average stress quantities for the rectangular bar.
