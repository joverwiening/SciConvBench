Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for an isotropic structural steel member.  
Take the material properties as Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30.  

Assume the member is thin enough that a plane‑stress condition applies (out‑of‑plane stress σzz = 0).  
Assume the applied loads produce a uniform average normal stress σ (tensile) in the x‑direction and a uniform average shear stress τxy = τ on the same element.  

Governing relations  

1. Plane‑stress stress tensor  
   \[ \boldsymbol{\sigma} = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma & \tau \\ \tau & 0 \end{bmatrix} \]  

2. Shear modulus  
   \[ G = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)} \]  

3. Hooke’s law for plane stress (stress‑strain relations)  
   \[ \varepsilon_x = \frac{1}{E}\big(\sigma - \nu \cdot 0\big) = \frac{\sigma}{E} \]  
   \[ \varepsilon_y = \frac{1}{E}\big(0 - \nu \sigma\big) = -\frac{\nu\sigma}{E} \]  
   \[ \gamma_{xy} = \frac{\tau}{G} = \frac{2(1+\nu)}{E}\,\tau \]  

4. Plane‑stress strain tensor  
   \[ \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_x & \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{xy} \\ \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{xy} & \varepsilon_y \end{bmatrix} \]  

5. Principal stresses (eigenvalues of the stress tensor)  
   \[ \sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigma}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)^{2} + \tau^{2}} \]  

6. Principal strains (eigenvalues of the strain tensor)  
   \[ \varepsilon_{1,2} = \frac{\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\varepsilon_x-\varepsilon_y}{2}\right)^{2} + \left(\frac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}\right)^{2}} \]  

Additional assumptions  

- The stress state is uniform over the element; stress gradients are neglected.  
- No body forces, temperature changes, or time‑dependent effects are considered.  
- Out‑of‑plane strain εz is obtained from the plane‑stress condition: εz = −ν(σ/E).  
- The member’s thickness is sufficiently small that σzz ≈ 0 throughout the thickness.  

These relations provide the required response quantities: the plane‑stress components, the corresponding strain components, and the principal stress and strain measures for the combined tension‑shear loading.
