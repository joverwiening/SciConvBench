Assume a homogeneous, isotropic structural steel with Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30.  
Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior and a plane‑stress state (σz = 0).  
Assume the member is subjected to a uniform average normal stress σ̄ acting in the x‑direction and a uniform average shear stress τ̄ acting on the xy‑plane. No other stress components are present (σy = 0, τxz = τyz = 0).  

Governing stress relations:  
- Stress tensor in the xy‑plane:  
  \[ \boldsymbol{\sigma} = \begin{bmatrix} \sigmā & \taū \\ \taū & 0 \end{bmatrix} \]  

Principal stresses:  
- \[ \sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigmā}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigmā}{2}\right)^{2} + \taū^{2}} \]  

Shear stress on the principal planes: zero by definition.  

Material shear modulus:  
- \[ G = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)} = \frac{210\ \text{GPa}}{2(1+0.30)} = 80.77\ \text{GPa} \]  

Plane‑stress Hooke’s law (stress‑strain relations):  
- \[ \varepsilon_{x} = \frac{1}{E}\big(\sigmā - \nu \cdot 0\big) = \frac{\sigmā}{E} \]  
- \[ \varepsilon_{y} = -\frac{\nu}{E}\,\sigmā \]  
- \[ \gamma_{xy} = \frac{\taū}{G} \]  

Strain tensor in the xy‑plane:  
  \[ \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{x} & \gamma_{xy}/2 \\ \gamma_{xy}/2 & \varepsilon_{y} \end{bmatrix} \]  

Principal strains (eigenvalues of the strain tensor):  
- \[ \varepsilon_{1,2} = \frac{\varepsilon_{x}+\varepsilon_{y}}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{x}-\varepsilon_{y}}{2}\right)^{2} + \left(\frac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}\right)^{2}} \]  

Additional assumptions:  
- The member’s thickness is uniform and does not affect the plane‑stress condition.  
- Temperature effects, residual stresses, and body forces are neglected.  
- The loading is static and applied slowly enough to avoid dynamic effects.  

These relations provide the complete set of response quantities—stress components, principal stresses, strain components, and principal strains—for the combined tension‑and‑shear loading under the stated assumptions.
