Assume a straight prismatic bar of constant cross‑sectional area A, made of an isotropic linear‑elastic material with Young’s modulus E, subjected to static axial point loads applied at known positions along its length.  
Additional assumptions: small deformations, no temperature effects, no body forces, the bar is perfectly straight, and the ends may be either fixed, free, or supported as specified by the boundary conditions.  

Relevant response quantities:  
- Axial internal force N(x) in the bar.  
- Axial stress σ(x) = N(x)/A.  
- Axial strain ε(x) = σ(x)/E.  
- Axial displacement u(x) measured from a chosen reference point.  
- Reaction forces at the supports or constraints.  

Governing relations:  
1. Equilibrium of forces in any segment: dN/dx + Σ P_i δ(x−x_i) = 0, where P_i are the applied point loads and δ is the Dirac delta.  
2. Constitutive law (Hooke’s law): σ(x) = E ε(x).  
3. Kinematic relation: ε(x) = du/dx.  
4. Compatibility: integrate ε(x) over the bar length to obtain displacement, applying the appropriate boundary conditions (e.g., u = 0 at a fixed end).  
5. Reaction forces are obtained from global equilibrium: Σ P_i + Σ R_j = 0, where R_j are the unknown support reactions.  

Using these equations, the internal force N(x) is piecewise constant between point loads, the stress and strain follow directly from N(x), and the displacement field u(x) is obtained by integrating the strain, ensuring continuity of displacement at each load point and satisfying the prescribed boundary conditions.
