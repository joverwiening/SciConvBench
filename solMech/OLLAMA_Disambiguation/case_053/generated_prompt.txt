Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, circular shaft of constant cross‑section, small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, no axial load, no bending, temperature effects neglected, and a transverse shear force of zero.  

Given data:  
- Shaft radius r = 10 mm (diameter d = 20 mm).  
- Shaft length L = 1200 mm.  
- Transmitted torque T = 3.98 × 10⁴ N·mm (≈ 39.8 N·m).  
- Shear modulus G = 25 939.8 MPa = 25 939.8 N/mm².  
- Rotational speed ω = 2π · (1200 rpm / 60) = 40π rad/s ≈ 125.66 rad/s.  

Governing relations and computed response quantities:  

1. Polar moment of inertia J = π r⁴ / 2 = (π/2)·(10 mm)⁴ = 1.5708·10⁴ mm⁴ ≈ 1.571 × 10⁴ mm⁴.  

2. Maximum torsional shear stress τ_max = T r / J = (3.98 × 10⁴ N·mm · 10 mm) / 1.571 × 10⁴ mm⁴ ≈ 25.3 N/mm² = 25.3 MPa.  

3. Angle of twist over the shaft length θ = T L / (G J) = (3.98 × 10⁴ N·mm · 1200 mm) / (25 939.8 N/mm² · 1.571 × 10⁴ mm⁴) ≈ 0.117 rad ≈ 6.7°.  

4. Torsional shear strain γ = τ_max / G = 25.3 MPa / 25 939.8 MPa ≈ 9.8 × 10⁻⁴ (dimensionless).  

5. Power–torque conversion check: P = T ω = 39.8 N·m · 125.66 rad/s ≈ 5.0 kW, confirming the specified motor power.  

6. Since the transverse shear force is assumed zero, no additional shear‑force induced stresses or deflections are considered.  

These relations and results constitute the complete torsional analysis required for power‑torque conversion and torsion checks of the specified shaft.
