Assume the beam undergoes small deformations and behaves as a linear‑elastic, isotropic material with constant cross‑section along its length.  
Neglect the self‑weight of the beam and any shear deformation; plane sections remain plane (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory).  
The cantilever is fixed at x = 0 and a vertical point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end x = L = 1500 mm.  
The material Young’s modulus is E = 200 000 MPa (200 000 N/mm²) and the second moment of area about the bending axis is I = 800 000 mm⁴.  
The bending moment distribution along the beam is M(x) = P·(L – x) for 0 ≤ x ≤ L.  
The strain energy stored in bending is U = ∫₀ᴸ M(x)²/(2EI) dx = P²L³/(6EI).  
By Castigliano’s theorem, the vertical deflection at the load point is δ = ∂U/∂P = PL³/(3EI).  
Substituting the given values yields δ = (500 N·(1500 mm)³)/(3·200 000 N/mm²·800 000 mm⁴) ≈ 3.52 mm.  
Thus the end deflection of the cantilever under the 500 N load is approximately 3.5 mm.
