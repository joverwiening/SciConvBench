Assume the shaft material is isotropic, homogeneous, and follows linear‑elastic behavior with a constant shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa; deformations are small and the shaft is straight with a constant circular hollow cross‑section along its length L = 1200 mm; the only loading is a pure torque T = 600 000 N·mm applied at the ends, with no axial force, bending, or thermal effects.  

Compute the polar moment of inertia of the hollow section as J = (π/2)·(r_o⁴ − r_i⁴), where r_o = 30 mm and r_i = 22 mm.  

Determine the torsional rigidity as G·J.  

Obtain the shear stress distribution across the wall as τ(r) = T·r / J for r_i ≤ r ≤ r_o.  

Find the maximum shear stress at the outer surface as τ_max = T·r_o / J.  

Calculate the angle of twist over the shaft length as φ = T·L / (G·J).  

Derive the twist per unit length (rate of twist) as θ' = T / (G·J).  

Express the shear strain at any radius as γ(r) = τ(r) / G.  

State that for a circular shaft the warping function is zero, so cross‑sections remain plane and perpendicular to the axis during torsion.
