Determine the bending stress distribution in a rectangular beam with width b = 50 mm, height h = 120 mm, subjected to a pure bending moment M = 4 000 000 N·mm.

Assume:
- Small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior.
- Material is homogeneous and isotropic.
- Plane sections remain plane (Bernoulli‑Euler beam theory).
- The bending moment is applied about the axis parallel to the width, producing curvature in the height direction.
- The neutral axis coincides with the centroid of the rectangular cross‑section, located at mid‑height.

Governing relations:
1. Second moment of area of the rectangular cross‑section about the neutral axis: I = (b · h³) / 12.
2. Distance y from the neutral axis to any fiber is measured positive upward from the centroid; the top surface has y = +h/2, the bottom surface y = –h/2.
3. Bending stress at any fiber location: σ(y) = (M · y) / I.
4. The maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fibers: σ_max = (M · (h/2)) / I.

Calculate:
- I = (50 mm · (120 mm)³) / 12 = 5.76 × 10⁶ mm⁴.
- σ_max = (4 000 000 N·mm · 60 mm) / 5.76 × 10⁶ mm⁴ = 41.67 N/mm² (tension on one side, compression on the opposite side).

Provide the stress distribution as the function σ(y) = (4 000 000 N·mm · y) / 5.76 × 10⁶ mm⁴, valid for –60 mm ≤ y ≤ +60 mm. This relation can be used to evaluate the bending stress at any arbitrary fiber location within the beam cross‑section.
