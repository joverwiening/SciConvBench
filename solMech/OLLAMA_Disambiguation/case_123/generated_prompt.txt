The rod is assumed to be prismatic, straight, and subjected to a uniform temperature increase of ΔT = 50 °C while its axial ends are fully restrained, preventing any axial deformation.  
Material properties: Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa, coefficient of thermal expansion α = 2 × 10⁻⁵ °C⁻¹.  
Cross‑section: radius = 10 mm, area A = π · (10 mm)² ≈ 314.16 mm².  

Thermal strain that would occur without restraint:  
ε_th = α ΔT = 2 × 10⁻⁵ °C⁻¹ · 50 °C = 1.0 × 10⁻³ (0.001).  

Because the rod is fully restrained, the total axial strain is zero:  
ε_total = 0 = ε_mech + ε_th → ε_mech = −ε_th = −1.0 × 10⁻³.  

The corresponding mechanical stress (compressive) is:  
σ = E · ε_mech = 200 000 MPa · (−1.0 × 10⁻³) = −200 MPa.  

Resulting axial force:  
F = σ · A = (−200 MPa) · 314.16 mm² ≈ −6.28 × 10⁴ N = −62.8 kN (compressive).  

Because the ends are restrained, the axial displacement of the rod is zero.  

Additional assumptions: small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior; temperature is uniform along the rod; no other mechanical loads or bending; material is isotropic; ends are perfectly fixed so that no axial movement occurs.  

Governing relations:  
ε_th = α ΔT.  
ε_total = 0 = ε_mech + ε_th.  
σ = E · ε_mech.  
F = σ · A.
