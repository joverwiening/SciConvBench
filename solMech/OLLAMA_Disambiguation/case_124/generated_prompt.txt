Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic rectangular beam under pure bending with small deformations.  
The beam’s cross‑section width is b = 40 mm and height is h = 100 mm.  
The applied sagging bending moment is M = 4 000 000 N·mm.  

Additional assumptions:  
- Plane sections remain plane after bending (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory).  
- No axial force, shear force, or torsion is present.  
- The neutral axis coincides with the centroid of the rectangular cross‑section.  

Governing relations:  
1. The second moment of area of the rectangular cross‑section about the neutral axis is I = b h³ / 12.  
2. The distance y from the neutral axis to any fiber is measured positive in the tension side (downward for sagging).  
3. The normal bending stress at that fiber is σ(y) = M · y / I.  
4. The extreme fiber stresses occur at y = ±h/2, giving σ_max = M · (h/2) / I.  

To obtain the bending stress at any arbitrary fiber location, substitute the desired y value into σ(y) = M y / I using the given b, h, and M.
