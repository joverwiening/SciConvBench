Assume a prismatic, homogeneous Euler‑Bernoulli cantilever beam of length L = 800 mm, Young’s modulus E = 69 000 MPa, second moment of area I = 1 600 000 mm⁴, subjected to a vertical point load P = 2500 N applied at the free end.  
Neglect beam self‑weight, axial effects, shear deformation, and temperature changes; use small‑deflection, linear‑elastic theory.  

Governing relations:  
The bending moment distribution is M(x) = –P (L – x) for 0 ≤ x ≤ L, where x is measured from the fixed support.  
The shear force is V(x) = –P (constant).  

The Euler‑Bernoulli beam equation is EI d²y/dx² = M(x).  

Integrating with the boundary conditions y(0)=0 and dy/dx|_{x=0}=0 (no deflection or rotation at the fixed support) gives:  
Slope at a distance x: θ(x) = dy/dx = –P (L x – x²/2) / (EI).  
Deflection at a distance x: y(x) = –P (L x²/2 – x³/6) / (EI).  

Relevant response quantities at the free end (x = L):  
Tip deflection δ = y(L) = –P L³ / (3 EI).  
Tip rotation θ_tip = θ(L) = –P L² / (2 EI).  

Numerical values:  
δ = –(2500 N)(800 mm)³ / [3 (69 000 MPa)(1 600 000 mm⁴)] = –0.018 mm (downward).  
θ_tip = –(2500 N)(800 mm)² / [2 (69 000 MPa)(1 600 000 mm⁴)] = –0.000072 rad.  

Maximum bending stress occurs at the fixed support: σ_max = M_max c / I, where M_max = P L and c is the distance from the neutral axis to the outermost fiber (must be supplied for a numerical value).  

These equations and results fully describe the cantilever beam’s elastic response under the specified end point load.
