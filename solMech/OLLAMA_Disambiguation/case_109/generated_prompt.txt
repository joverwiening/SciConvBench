Assume a prismatic, homogeneous, isotropic beam that follows Euler‑Bernoulli theory, experiences small deformations, and behaves as a linear‑elastic material with Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa.  
The beam is simply supported at its ends, has a span length L = 2500 mm, a second moment of area I = 3 000 000 mm⁴, and is loaded by a single vertical point load P = 1000 N applied at the mid‑span.  
Neglect axial effects, shear deformation, temperature changes, and any dynamic effects.  

The bending moment distribution is:  
M(x) = P x / 2 for 0 ≤ x ≤ L⁄2, and M(x) = P (L − x) / 2 for L⁄2 ≤ x ≤ L.  

The strain‑energy stored in the beam is  
U = ∫₀ᴸ [M(x)² / (2 E I)] dx.  

Apply Castigliano’s theorem: the mid‑span vertical deflection δ = ∂U / ∂P.  
Carrying out the integration gives the closed‑form expression  
δ = P L³ / (48 E I).  

Substituting the given values (using consistent units of N, mm, and N/mm²):  
δ = (1000 N)(2500 mm)³ / [48 (200 000 N/mm²)(3 000 000 mm⁴)] ≈ 0.54 mm.  

Thus the mid‑span deflection of the simply supported beam under the central point load is approximately 0.54 mm.
