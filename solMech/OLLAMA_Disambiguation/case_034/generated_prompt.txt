Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior and Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory; neglect shear deformation and axial effects.  
Consider a simply supported beam of length L = 3000 mm with flexural rigidity EI = 2.415 × 10¹¹ N·mm², subjected to a single vertical point load P = 4000 N applied at midspan.  

Use the Euler‑Bernoulli differential relation M(x) = –EI d²w/dx², where w(x) is the transverse deflection and M(x) is the bending moment distribution.  

For a simply supported beam with a central point load, the bending moment diagram is symmetric and the maximum deflection occurs at midspan.  

Midspan deflection:  
 δ_max = (P L³) / (48 EI)  

Support slope (rotation at either support):  
 θ_support = (P L²) / (16 EI)  

Compute the numerical values:  

 L³ = (3000 mm)³ = 2.7 × 10¹⁰ mm³  
 δ_max = (4000 N × 2.7 × 10¹⁰ mm³) / (48 × 2.415 × 10¹¹ N·mm²) ≈ 9.3 mm  

 L² = (3000 mm)² = 9.0 × 10⁶ mm²  
 θ_support = (4000 N × 9.0 × 10⁶ mm²) / (16 × 2.415 × 10¹¹ N·mm²) ≈ 9.3 × 10⁻³ rad (≈ 0.53°)  

State the results: the beam deflects approximately 9.3 mm at midspan, and the slope at each support is about 0.0093 rad.
