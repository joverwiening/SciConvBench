Assume a small‑deformation, linear‑elastic prismatic bar with a rectangular cross‑section of width b = 30 mm and height h = 100 mm (area A = b·h = 30 mm × 100 mm = 3000 mm²).  
Assume the bar length L = 1000 mm (1 m), which is a typical value for short bars in practice.  

Apply the following loads at the bar ends:  
- Axial tension force P = 50 000 N acting uniformly over the cross‑section.  
- A transverse point load at the free end of a cantilevered configuration, producing an internal shear force V = 5000 N at the section of interest. The resulting bending moment at that section is M = V·L = 5000 N × 1 m = 5000 N·m.  

Compute the average stress quantities at the chosen cross‑section:  

1. Average normal stress due to axial tension:  
   σ_avg,axial = P / A = 50 000 N / 3000 mm² = 16.67 MPa.  

2. Bending normal stress distribution (linear across the height). The extreme‑fiber bending stress is:  
   σ_max,bend = M·c / I, where c = h/2 = 50 mm, I = b·h³/12 = 30 mm·(100 mm)³/12 = 2.5 × 10⁶ mm⁴.  
   σ_max,bend = (5000 N·m × 10³ mm/m)·50 mm / 2.5 × 10⁶ mm⁴ = 0.10 MPa.  
   The average bending normal stress over the section is zero because the tensile and compressive halves cancel.  

3. Average shear stress due to transverse shear force:  
   τ_avg = V / A = 5000 N / 3000 mm² = 1.67 MPa.  

State the final response quantities:  
- Average normal stress at the section = 16.67 MPa (tension).  
- Average shear stress at the section = 1.67 MPa.  
- Extreme bending normal stress = ±0.10 MPa (tension on one face, compression on the opposite).  

All governing relations used are:  
σ = P / A for axial tension,  
τ = V / A for average shear,  
σ = M·c / I for extreme bending stress, with I = b·h³/12 and c = h/2.  

No additional material properties are required for stress evaluation under the stated assumptions.
