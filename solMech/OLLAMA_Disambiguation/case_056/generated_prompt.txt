Determine the axial response of a linearly tapered bar under a static axial load.

Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, constant temperature, and that the bar is fixed at x = 0 while the axial load P is applied at x = L.

Given data:
- Bar length L = 800 mm.
- Applied axial load P = 30 000 N.
- Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa (200 000 N/mm²).
- Cross‑sectional area varies linearly from A₁ = 250 mm² at x = 0 to A₂ = 450 mm² at x = L.

Governing relations:
1. Area distribution: A(x) = A₁ + (A₂ − A₁)·(x/L) = 250 + 0.25 x [mm²].
2. Axial stress: σ(x) = P / A(x) [N/mm²].
3. Axial strain: ε(x) = σ(x) / E [dimensionless].
4. Axial displacement at the loaded end: u(L) = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x) dx = (P/E) ∫₀ᴸ 1/A(x) dx.
   Evaluating the integral gives ∫₀ᴸ 1/A(x) dx = 4 ln(A₂/A₁) = 4 ln(1.8).
   Hence u(L) = (P/E)·4 ln(1.8) ≈ 0.15 mm × 2.3512 ≈ 0.353 mm.
5. Axial deformation distribution: du/dx = ε(x) = P / [E·A(x)].

Report:
- Provide σ(x) and ε(x) as functions of x using the expressions above.
- State the total axial elongation of the bar as u(L) ≈ 0.353 mm.
- Include any intermediate results (e.g., the integral value 4 ln(1.8) ≈ 2.351).
