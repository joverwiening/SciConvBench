Determine the Euler critical buckling load for a column with fixed–pinned end conditions, length L = 2000 mm, made of AISI 1020 steel (Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa, yield stress σ_y = 250 MPa).  

Assume a solid circular cross‑section with diameter d = 100 mm (cross‑sectional area A = πd²/4 = 7.85 × 10⁻³ m², second moment of area I = πd⁴/64 = 4.91 × 10⁻⁶ m⁴).  

Use the effective length factor K = 0.7 for fixed–pinned ends, so the effective length is K L = 0.7 × 2.0 m = 1.4 m.  

Compute the Euler critical load:  
P_cr = π² E I / (K L)² = π² × 200 GPa × 4.91 × 10⁻⁶ m⁴ / (1.4 m)² ≈ 4.94 MN.  

Perform a buckling/instability check:  
Yield load P_y = A σ_y = 7.85 × 10⁻³ m² × 250 MPa ≈ 1.96 MN.  

Since P_cr > P_y, the column would reach material yield before Euler buckling; therefore, an inelastic buckling assessment (e.g., Johnson’s parabolic formula) is required for design.  

State the governing relations used:  
- Euler critical load: P_cr = π² E I / (K L)².  
- Effective length factor K for fixed–pinned ends = 0.7.  
- Cross‑sectional area for a solid circle: A = πd²/4.  
- Second moment of area for a solid circle: I = πd⁴/64.  
- Yield load: P_y = A σ_y.  

Document the assumptions: circular solid cross‑section, diameter 100 mm, material properties as given, small‑deformation linear‑elastic behavior up to yield, and use of the effective length factor for fixed–pinned ends.
