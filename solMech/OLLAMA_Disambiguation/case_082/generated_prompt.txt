Assume the specimen is a homogeneous, isotropic solid with a uniform original cross‑sectional area A0 and an original gauge length L0.  
Assume the material behavior is small‑deformation, linear‑elastic, and temperature effects are negligible.  
Assume the tensile loading is applied quasi‑statically so that inertial effects can be ignored.  

Define the engineering (nominal) stress σ as the applied axial load P divided by the original cross‑sectional area A0: σ = P / A0.  
Define the engineering (nominal) strain ε as the change in gauge length ΔL divided by the original gauge length L0: ε = ΔL / L0.  

For the linear‑elastic region, relate stress and strain by Hooke’s law: σ = E ε, where E is the Young’s modulus (tensile modulus) of the material.  

The tensile modulus E is obtained from the slope of the initial linear portion of the engineering stress–strain curve: E = dσ/dε (constant for a linear‑elastic material).  

These governing relations provide the required response quantities for a standard tension test.
