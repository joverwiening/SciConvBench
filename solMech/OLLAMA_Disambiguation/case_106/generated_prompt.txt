Assume a prismatic, homogeneous beam that follows Euler‑Bernoulli theory, with small deformations and linear‑elastic material behavior.  
The beam is simply supported at its ends, has constant flexural rigidity \(EI = 7.0 \times 10^{11}\ \text{N·mm}^2\), length \(L = 2500\ \text{mm}\), and is loaded only by a single vertical point load \(P = 2500\ \text{N}\) applied at the mid‑span.  
No other transverse loads, axial loads, or moments act on the beam.  

Governing relation for bending of an Euler‑Bernoulli beam:  
\( \frac{d^2 v}{dx^2} = \frac{M(x)}{EI} \), where \(v(x)\) is the transverse deflection and \(M(x)\) is the bending moment distribution.  

For a simply supported beam with a central point load, the bending moment diagram is symmetric and given by  
\( M(x) = \frac{P L}{4} \, x \) for \(0 \le x \le L/2\) and \( M(x) = \frac{P L}{4} \, (L - x) \) for \(L/2 \le x \le L\).  

Integrating the moment‑curvature relation and applying the boundary conditions \(v(0)=v(L)=0\) and slope symmetry yields the standard closed‑form results:  

- Mid‑span deflection:  
\( \displaystyle \delta_{\text{mid}} = \frac{P L^{3}}{48\,EI} \).  

- Slope at each support (positive upward on the left support, negative on the right):  
\( \displaystyle \theta_{\text{support}} = \frac{P L^{2}}{16\,EI} \).  

Substituting the given numerical values:  

\( \delta_{\text{mid}} = \frac{2500\ \text{N} \times (2500\ \text{mm})^{3}}{48 \times 7.0 \times 10^{11}\ \text{N·mm}^2}
= 1.46 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{mm} \) (approximately).  

\( \theta_{\text{support}} = \frac{2500\ \text{N} \times (2500\ \text{mm})^{2}}{16 \times 7.0 \times 10^{11}\ \text{N·mm}^2}
= 3.52 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{rad} \) (approximately).  

Report the mid‑span deflection and support slope using the above formulas and numerical results.
