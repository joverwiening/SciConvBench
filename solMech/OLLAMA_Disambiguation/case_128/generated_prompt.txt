Assume the shaft material is isotropic, homogeneous, linear‑elastic, and deforms only slightly (small‑deformation theory).  
The shaft is a solid circular cylinder of diameter d = 50 mm (radius r = 25 mm) and length L = 600 mm.  
The applied torque is T = 500 000 N·mm.  
The shear modulus of the material is G = 77 519.4 MPa (N/mm²).  

Compute the polar moment of inertia:  
J = π d⁴ / 32 = π · (50 mm)⁴ / 32 ≈ 6.136 × 10⁵ mm⁴.  

Maximum shear stress in the shaft:  
τ_max = T · c / J, where c = r = 25 mm.  
τ_max = 500 000 N·mm · 25 mm / 6.136 × 10⁵ mm⁴ ≈ 20.4 MPa.  

Angle of twist over the length L:  
θ = T · L / (J · G).  
θ = 500 000 N·mm · 600 mm / (6.136 × 10⁵ mm⁴ · 77 519.4 N/mm²) ≈ 0.0063 rad ≈ 0.36°.  

State the governing relations used:  
τ(r) = T · r / J for any radius r, with the maximum at r = c.  
θ = T · L / (J · G).  

These relations and the computed values provide the required shear stress distribution and overall angle of twist for the given shaft under the specified torque.
