Q: Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic material behavior; constant shear modulus G; the torque is applied uniformly along the shaft length; ends are free to rotate so the angle of twist is given by the uniform‑torsion formula.

Given data  
Outer radius rₒ = 20 mm, inner radius rᵢ = 15 mm.  
Shear modulus G = 39179.1 MPa = 39179.1 N/mm².  
Applied torque T = 300 000 N·mm.  
Shaft length L = 1000 mm.

Compute the polar moment of inertia for a hollow circular section:  
J = (π/2)·(rₒ⁴ − rᵢ⁴) = (π/2)·(20⁴ − 15⁴) mm⁴ ≈ 1.718 × 10⁵ mm⁴.

Shear stress distribution: τ(r) = T·r / J.  
Maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface:  
τ_max = T·rₒ / J = 300 000·20 / 1.718 × 10⁵ ≈ 34.9 MPa.

Shear strain distribution: γ(r) = τ(r) / G.  
Maximum shear strain at the outer surface:  
γ_max = τ_max / G = 34.9 / 39179.1 ≈ 8.9 × 10⁻⁴ (dimensionless).

Torsional rigidity: K_t = J·G = 1.718 × 10⁵ mm⁴ · 39179.1 N/mm² ≈ 6.73 × 10⁹ N·mm².

Angle of twist over the length L:  
φ = T·L / (J·G) = 300 000·1000 / 6.73 × 10⁹ ≈ 0.0446 rad ≈ 2.56 degrees.

Twist per unit length (rate of twist):  
θ' = φ / L = 0.0446 rad / 1000 mm = 4.46 × 10⁻⁵ rad/mm.

- Polar moment of inertia J ≈ 1.718 × 10⁵ mm⁴.
