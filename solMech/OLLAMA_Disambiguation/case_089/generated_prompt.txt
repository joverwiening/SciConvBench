Assume a small‑deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic material under static axial tension with uniform temperature and no body forces.  
The bar consists of two cylindrical segments in series, each carrying the same axial load P.

Given data:  
Segment 1: length L₁ = 400 mm, diameter d₁ = 16 mm.  
Segment 2: length L₂ = 400 mm, diameter d₂ = 40 mm.  
Axial tensile load: P = 25 000 N.  
Young’s modulus: E = 200 000 MPa (N/mm²).

Governing relations:  
- Cross‑sectional area A = π d² / 4.  
- Axial stress σ = P / A.  
- Axial strain ε = σ / E.  
- Axial elongation of a segment ΔL = ε L = (P L) / (A E).  
- Total elongation ΔL_total = ΔL₁ + ΔL₂.

Calculations:  
A₁ = π (16 mm)² / 4 = 64 π mm² ≈ 201.06 mm².  
A₂ = π (40 mm)² / 4 = 400 π mm² ≈ 1 256.64 mm².  

σ₁ = 25 000 N / 201.06 mm² ≈ 124.3 MPa.  
σ₂ = 25 000 N / 1 256.64 mm² ≈ 19.9 MPa.  

ΔL₁ = (25 000 N × 400 mm) / (201.06 mm² × 200 000 N/mm²) ≈ 0.249 mm.  
ΔL₂ = (25 000 N × 400 mm) / (1 256.64 mm² × 200 000 N/mm²) ≈ 0.040 mm.  

ΔL_total = 0.249 mm + 0.040 mm ≈ 0.289 mm.

Resulting response quantities:  
- Axial stress in segment 1 is approximately 124.3 MPa.  
- Axial stress in segment 2 is approximately 19.9 MPa.  
- Total axial elongation of the stepped bar is approximately 0.289 mm.
