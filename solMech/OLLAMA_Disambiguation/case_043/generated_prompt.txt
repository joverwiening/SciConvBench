Assume the column is made of typical structural steel with Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa and yield strength σ_y ≈ 250 MPa.  
Assume the column ends are pinned‑pinned, giving an effective length factor K = 1.0.  
Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior and that the column is slender so Euler buckling governs.  

Geometric properties:  
Length L = 2000 mm.  
Diameter d = 20 mm, radius r = 10 mm.  
Cross‑sectional area A = π r² = 3.1416 × 10⁻⁴ m² = 314.16 mm².  
Second moment of area I = π r⁴/4 = 7.85 × 10⁻⁹ m⁴ = 7850 mm⁴.  

Governing relations:  
Euler critical buckling load P_cr = π² E I / (K L)².  
Allowable compressive load based on a factor of safety FS_buckling P_allow = P_cr / FS_buckling.  
Factor of safety with respect to buckling for a given applied load P_applied FS_buckling = P_cr / P_applied.  

Numerical evaluation:  
P_cr = π² (200 000 MPa)(7850 mm⁴) / (1 × 2000 mm)² ≈ 3.88 kN.  

Assume a reasonable design axial load P_applied = 1.0 kN (typical for a slender steel column of this size).  
Then FS_buckling = P_cr / P_applied ≈ 3.9.  

Select a design factor of safety FS_buckling = 2.0 (common practice for buckling).  
Allowable load P_allow = P_cr / 2.0 ≈ 1.94 kN.  

The required response quantities are:  
- Euler critical buckling load P_cr ≈ 3.88 kN.  
- Chosen factor of safety against buckling FS_buckling ≈ 3.9 for the assumed load, or the design FS = 2.0.  
- Allowable compressive load P_allow ≈ 1.94 kN.  

All governing equations, assumptions, and numerical results are stated explicitly.
