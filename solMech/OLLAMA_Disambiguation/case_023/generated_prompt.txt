The cantilever beam is assumed to undergo small deformations and to behave as a linear‑elastic material with Young’s modulus E = 200 000 N/mm² and second moment of area I = 1 000 000 mm⁴.  
The beam length is L = 2500 mm and it is subjected to a uniformly distributed transverse load w = 1.0 N/mm acting over the entire span.  

Assumptions  
- Plane sections remain plane (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory).  
- Shear deformation is neglected.  
- Material is isotropic and homogeneous.  
- Load is static and applied continuously along the beam.  

Governing relations for a cantilever under a uniform load w  

1. Bending moment distribution: M(x) = – w (L – x)² / 2, where x is measured from the fixed support (x = 0) toward the free end (x = L).  
2. Shear force distribution: V(x) = – w (L – x).  
3. Slope (rotation) along the beam: θ(x) = – w (L – x)³ / (6 E I).  
4. Deflection along the beam: v(x) = – w (L – x)⁴ / (24 E I).  

Relevant response quantities  

- Maximum deflection at the free end (x = L): δ_max = w L⁴ / (8 E I).  
- Slope at the free end: θ_max = w L³ / (6 E I).  
- Maximum bending moment at the fixed support: M_max = w L² / 2.  
- Maximum shear force at the fixed support: V_max = w L.  

These equations provide the complete description of the cantilever’s elastic response under the specified uniform transverse load.
