Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid with small deformations.  
Take the material constants as Young’s modulus \(E\) and Poisson’s ratio \(\nu\); the shear modulus is \(G = \dfrac{E}{2(1+\nu)}\).  

Given data:  
- Shaft diameter \(d = 30\; \text{mm}\) (radius \(r = 15\; \text{mm}\)).  
- Shaft length \(L = 300\; \text{mm}\).  
- Bending moment about the strong axis \(M = 4{,}000{,}000\; \text{N·mm}\).  
- Torsional moment (torque) \(T = 400{,}000\; \text{N·mm}\).  
- Axial force \(N = 0\).  

Assumptions:  
1. The shaft is prismatic and the loading is applied at the mid‑span, so the bending stress distribution is linear across the cross‑section.  
2. Pure torsion produces a uniform shear stress distribution over the cross‑section.  
3. No axial load, temperature effects, or residual stresses are present.  
4. Plane sections remain plane (Bernoulli‑Euler hypothesis).  

Geometric properties of the circular cross‑section:  
- Area moment of inertia \(I = \dfrac{\pi d^{4}}{64}\).  
- Polar moment of inertia \(J = \dfrac{\pi d^{4}}{32}\).  

Response quantities to be determined:  

1. **Maximum normal bending stress**  
\[
\sigma_{b} = \frac{M\,c}{I}, \qquad c = r = \frac{d}{2}.
\]

2. **Maximum shear stress due to torsion**  
\[
\tau_{t} = \frac{T\,r}{J}.
\]

3. **Combined stress state at the outer fiber** (where \(\sigma_{b}\) is maximum and \(\tau_{t}\) acts on the same point)  
\[
\sigma_{x} = \sigma_{b}, \qquad \sigma_{y}=0, \qquad \tau_{xy}= \tau_{t}.
\]

4. **Principal normal stresses**  
\[
\sigma_{1,2}= \frac{\sigma_{x}}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{x}}{2}\right)^{2}+ \tau_{t}^{2}}.
\]

5. **Principal strains** (using Hooke’s law for plane stress)  
\[
\varepsilon_{1,2}= \frac{1}{E}\Bigl[\sigma_{1,2} - \nu \sigma_{2,1}\Bigr].
\]

6. **Maximum shear stress (principal shear)**  
\[
\tau_{\max}= \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{x}}{2}\right)^{2}+ \tau_{t}^{2}}.
\]

7. **Von Mises equivalent stress** for yielding assessment  
\[
\sigma_{v}= \sqrt{\sigma_{b}^{2}+3\tau_{t}^{2}}.
\]

8. **Deflection due to bending** (optional)  
\[
\delta = \frac{M L^{2}}{2 E I}.
\]

9. **Angle of twist due to torsion** (optional)  
\[
\theta = \frac{T L}{G J}.
\]

All governing relations above are to be used to compute the requested response quantities for the specified shaft geometry and loading.
