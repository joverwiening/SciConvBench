Determine the required diameter of a solid circular 6061‑T6 aluminum column that will support an axial compressive load of 25 000 N over a length of 2200 mm with an effective‑length factor K = 2.0, while providing a minimum factor of safety against Euler buckling of n = 2.5.

Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, concentric loading, no initial imperfections, and neglect temperature effects.

Use the Euler buckling formula for a column with end condition factor K:  
Pcr = (π² E I) / (K L)², where I = π d⁴ / 64 for a solid circular cross‑section.

Require Pcr ≥ n · P, solve for the diameter d:  
d = [ (64 · n · P · (K L)²) / (π³ E) ]¹⁄⁴.

Insert the data (E = 69 000 N/mm², P = 25 000 N, L = 2200 mm, K = 2.0, n = 2.5) to obtain  
d ≈ 78 mm.

Compute the actual buckling capacity with d = 78 mm:  
I = π d⁴ / 64 ≈ 1.82 × 10⁶ mm⁴,  
Pcr = (π² E I) / (K L)² ≈ 64 000 N,  
Factor of safety FS = Pcr / P ≈ 2.56 (≥ 2.5).

Check compressive stress against material yield (σy ≈ 276 MPa):  
Cross‑sectional area A = π d² / 4 ≈ 4 780 mm²,  
σ = P / A ≈ 5.2 N/mm² << σy, so yielding is not a concern.

Provide the governing relations, the calculated diameter (≈ 78 mm), and the verified factor of safety (≈ 2.56).
