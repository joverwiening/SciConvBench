Determine the axial response of a linearly tapered bar of length L = 500 mm whose cross‑section varies as A(x)=200 mm² (1 + x/500), subjected to a constant tensile axial load P = 12 000 N. The material is linear‑elastic with Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa. Assume small deformations, constant temperature, no body forces, and that plane sections remain plane.

Governing relations  
1. Axial equilibrium: dN/dx = 0 ⇒ axial force N(x)=P (constant).  
2. Axial stress distribution: σ(x)=N/A(x)=P/[200 (1 + x/500)] N/mm² = 60 MPa / (1 + x/500).  
3. Axial strain distribution: ε(x)=σ(x)/E = [60 MPa / (1 + x/500)] / 200 000 MPa = 0.0003 / (1 + x/500).  
4. Axial displacement gradient: du/dx = ε(x).  
5. Axial displacement field: u(x)=∫₀ˣ ε(ξ) dξ = 0.0003 · 500 · ln(1 + x/500) mm = 0.15 ln(1 + x/500) mm.  
6. Total elongation of the bar: Δ = u(L) = 0.15 ln(2) mm ≈ 0.104 mm.

Response quantities to be reported  
- Axial stress σ(x) varying from 60 MPa at the left end (x=0) to 30 MPa at the right end (x=500 mm).  
- Axial strain ε(x) varying from 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ at x=0 to 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ at x=500 mm.  
- Axial displacement u(x) given by u(x)=0.15 ln(1 + x/500) mm.  
- Total axial deformation (elongation) Δ ≈ 0.104 mm.  

All quantities are derived from the stated assumptions and the provided material and geometric data.
