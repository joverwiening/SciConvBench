Assume a small‑deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic solid circular shaft made of a material with shear modulus G = 77 519.4 MPa.  
The shaft consists of two straight segments in series:  

- Segment AB: length L₁ = 400 mm, radius r₁ = 15 mm (diameter 30 mm).  
- Segment BC: length L₂ = 700 mm, radius r₂ = 15 mm (diameter 30 mm).  

A constant torque T = 600 000 N·mm (600 N·m) is applied at the free end, and the torque is transmitted uniformly through both segments. No axial loads, bending moments, or transverse shear forces are present.

**Governing relations**  
1. Polar moment of inertia for a solid circular cross‑section: J = π r⁴ / 2.  
2. Maximum shear stress in a circular shaft under torque: τ_max = T r / J.  
3. Angle of twist of a uniform shaft segment: θ = T L / (J G).  

**Calculations**  

- Polar moment of inertia (identical for both segments):  
  J = π (15 mm)⁴ / 2 = 7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴.  

- Shear stress (identical for both segments):  
  τ_max = (600 000 N·mm)(15 mm) / 7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴ ≈ 1.13 × 10² N/mm² = 113 MPa.  

- Angle of twist for segment AB:  
  θ₁ = (600 000 N·mm)(400 mm) / (7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴ · 77 519.4 N/mm²) ≈ 0.0389 rad = 2.23°.  

- Angle of twist for segment BC:  
  θ₂ = (600 000 N·mm)(700 mm) / (7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴ · 77 519.4 N/mm²) ≈ 0.0681 rad = 3.90°.  

- Total twist of the stepped shaft:  
  θ_total = θ₁ + θ₂ ≈ 0.107 rad = 6.13°.  

**Response quantities**  
- Maximum shear stress in the shaft: τ_max ≈ 113 MPa.  
- Total angle of twist over the length L₁ + L₂ = 1100 mm: θ_total ≈ 0.107 rad (≈ 6.13°).  

All assumptions and governing equations are stated explicitly for reproducibility.
