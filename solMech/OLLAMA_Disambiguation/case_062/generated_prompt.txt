Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for the AISI rod.  
Assume the rod is prismatic with constant cross‑sectional area A, length L, Young’s modulus E, and coefficient of thermal expansion α, and that the temperature change ΔT is uniform over the entire rod.  
Assume both ends of the rod are fully restrained so that the axial displacement is zero (total axial strain ε = 0).  
Assume the axial load P is applied uniformly and acts in the same direction as the thermal stress (sign convention: tensile positive).  

Governing relations:  
The total axial strain is the sum of mechanical strain and thermal strain: ε = σ/E + α ΔT.  
Full axial restraint imposes ε = 0, therefore the induced axial stress due to the temperature change alone is σ_th = –E α ΔT.  
If an external axial load P is also applied, the net axial stress in the rod is σ = σ_th + P/A = –E α ΔT + P/A.  
The reaction force at each restrained support equals the axial stress multiplied by the cross‑sectional area: R = σ A = –E α ΔT A + P.  

Relevant response quantities:  
- Thermal strain (free, without restraint): ε_free = α ΔT.  
- Constrained axial strain: ε = 0 (by definition).  
- Axial stress caused by thermal restraint: σ_th = –E α ΔT.  
- Net axial stress with external load: σ = –E α ΔT + P/A.  
- Reaction force at the restraints: R = –E α ΔT A + P.  
- Axial displacement of any point along the rod: u = 0 (full restraint).  

These equations fully describe the mechanical response of the rod under the specified thermal and axial loading conditions.
