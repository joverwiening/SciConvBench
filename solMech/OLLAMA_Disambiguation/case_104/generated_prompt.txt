Assume a straight, prismatic vertical bar of uniform cross‑section, made of a homogeneous linear‑elastic material, deformed under its own weight only; small deformations are considered.  
Take the gravitational acceleration as g = 9.81 m s⁻².  

The bar length is L = 3000 mm (3 m).  
Cross‑sectional area is A = 300 mm².  
Young’s modulus is E = 69 000 MPa (6.9 × 10¹⁰ N m⁻²).  
Material density is ρ = 8900 kg m⁻³.  
The top end is fixed, the bottom end is free.  

Governing relations:  
1. Axial force at a distance x measured from the fixed top:  
   N(x) = ρ A g (L − x).  
2. Axial stress distribution:  
   σ(x) = N(x)/A = ρ g (L − x).  
3. Linear‑elastic constitutive law (Hooke’s law):  
   ε(x) = σ(x)/E = ρ g (L − x)/E.  
4. Axial strain–displacement relation:  
   du/dx = ε(x).  
5. Total elongation of the bar due to self‑weight:  
   δ = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x) dx = (ρ g L²)/(2E).  

Numerical results:  
- Maximum axial stress at the fixed top: σ_max = ρ g L = 8900 × 9.81 × 3 ≈ 2.62 × 10⁵ Pa = 0.262 MPa.  
- Total elongation: δ = (8900 × 9.81 × 3²)/(2 × 6.9 × 10¹⁰) ≈ 5.7 × 10⁻⁶ m = 0.0057 mm.  

These equations and results fully describe the bar’s response to its self‑weight under the stated assumptions.
