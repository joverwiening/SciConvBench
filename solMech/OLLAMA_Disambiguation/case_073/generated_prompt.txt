Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid circular shaft under small deformation.  
Material shear modulus: G = 25 939.8 MPa (N mm⁻²).  
Shaft radius: r = 15 mm.  
Shaft length: L = 1000 mm.  
Applied torque: T = 450 000 N·mm.  

Governing relations for torsion of a solid circular shaft:  
1. Polar moment of inertia: J = π r⁴ / 2 = π d⁴ / 32.  
2. Torsional stiffness: kₜ = G J / L.  
3. Angle of twist (Castigliano’s theorem): θ = ∂U/∂T = T L / (G J).  
4. Strain‑energy stored: U = ½ T θ.  
5. Maximum shear stress: τ_max = T r / J.  
6. Shear strain: γ = τ_max / G.  

Numerical calculations (all in mm‑N units):  
- J = π (15 mm)⁴ / 2 = 7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴.  
- G J = 25 939.8 N mm⁻² × 7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴ = 2.0628 × 10⁹ N·mm².  
- Angle of twist: θ = (450 000 N·mm × 1000 mm) / (2.0628 × 10⁹ N·mm²) = 0.218 rad (≈ 12.5°).  
- Strain energy: U = ½ × 450 000 N·mm × 0.218 rad = 4.91 × 10⁴ N·mm = 49.1 J.  
- Maximum shear stress: τ_max = (450 000 N·mm × 15 mm) / 7.952 × 10⁴ mm⁴ = 84.9 N mm⁻² = 84.9 MPa.  
- Shear strain: γ = τ_max / G = 84.9 MPa / 25 939.8 MPa = 3.27 × 10⁻³.  

These relations and results fully define the torsional response of the shaft using the energy (Castigliano) method.
