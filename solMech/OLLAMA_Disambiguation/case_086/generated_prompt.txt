Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic and deforms under small strains.  
Use the three‑dimensional isotropic Hooke’s law in strain–stress form.

Governing relations  
- Shear modulus: \(G = \dfrac{E}{2(1+\nu)}\).  
- Normal strains: \(\displaystyle \varepsilon_{x}= \frac{1}{E}\bigl[\sigma_{x}-\nu(\sigma_{y}+\sigma_{z})\bigr]\)  
  \(\displaystyle \varepsilon_{y}= \frac{1}{E}\bigl[\sigma_{y}-\nu(\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{z})\bigr]\)  
  \(\displaystyle \varepsilon_{z}= \frac{1}{E}\bigl[\sigma_{z}-\nu(\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y})\bigr]\).  
- Tensorial shear strains: \(\displaystyle \varepsilon_{xy}= \frac{\tau_{xy}}{2G},\;
  \varepsilon_{xz}= \frac{\tau_{xz}}{2G},\;
  \varepsilon_{yz}= \frac{\tau_{yz}}{2G}\).

Material data  
\(E = 69\,000\ \text{MPa}\) , \(\nu = 0.33\).

Compute auxiliary property  
\(G = \dfrac{69\,000}{2(1+0.33)} = 2.5939\times10^{4}\ \text{MPa}\).

Compute strains  

\[
\varepsilon_{x}= \frac{1}{69\,000}\bigl[120 - 0.33(120+0)\bigr]
               = \frac{80.4}{69\,000}=1.164\times10^{-3}
\]

\[
\varepsilon_{y}= \varepsilon_{x}=1.164\times10^{-3}
\]

\[
\varepsilon_{z}= \frac{1}{69\,000}\bigl[0 - 0.33(120+120)\bigr]
               = \frac{-79.2}{69\,000}= -1.148\times10^{-3}
\]

\[
\varepsilon_{xy}= \frac{75}{2(2.5939\times10^{4})}=1.445\times10^{-3}
\]

\[
\varepsilon_{xz}=0,\qquad \varepsilon_{yz}=0
\]

Resulting strain tensor (tensorial components)

\[
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=
\begin{bmatrix}
1.164\times10^{-3} & 1.445\times10^{-3} & 0\\[2pt]
1.445\times10^{-3} & 1.164\times10^{-3} & 0\\[2pt]
0 & 0 & -1.148\times10^{-3}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
