Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, isotropic material, constant Young’s modulus E and second moment of area I along the beam, and a constant uniformly distributed transverse load w acting downward over the entire length L. Neglect beam self‑weight and any axial effects; plane sections remain plane.

Governing differential equation for bending of an Euler‑Bernoulli beam:  
EI d⁴y/dx⁴ = w, where y(x) is the transverse deflection and x measures distance from the fixed support (x = 0) toward the free end (x = L).

Integrating with the boundary conditions for a cantilever (y = 0, dy/dx = 0, d²y/dx² = 0, d³y/dx³ = 0 at x = 0) gives:

Shear force: V(x) = w (L − x)  
Bending moment: M(x) = ½ w (L − x)²  
Slope: θ(x) = dy/dx = [w x (L² − L x + x²/3)] / (6 E I)  
Deflection: y(x) = [w x² (6 L² − 4 L x + x²)] / (24 E I)

Maximum deflection occurs at the free end (x = L):

δ_max = y(L) = w L⁴ / (8 E I)

Using the provided data:  
L = 1500 mm  
w = 1.5 N/mm  
E = 200 000 MPa = 200 000 N/mm²  
I = 1 500 000 mm⁴  

Compute response quantities:

- Maximum deflection: δ_max = (1.5 × 1500⁴) / (8 × 200 000 × 1 500 000) ≈ 3.16 mm  
- Slope at free end: θ(L) = w L³ / (6 E I) ≈ 0.00281 rad ≈ 0.161°  
- Shear at fixed support: V(0) = w L = 1.5 × 1500 = 2250 N  
- Bending moment at fixed support: M(0) = ½ w L² = 0.5 × 1.5 × 1500² = 1 687 500 N·mm  

These equations and numerical results fully describe the cantilever’s response under the specified uniformly distributed transverse load.
