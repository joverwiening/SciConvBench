Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid circular shaft subjected only to a pure torque of 400 000 N·mm; no transverse shear force is applied.  
Assume small deformations, constant material properties along the shaft, and neglect temperature effects.  

Use the polar moment of inertia for a solid circular cross‑section:  
\(J = \dfrac{\pi r^{4}}{2}\) where \(r = 15\; \text{mm}\).  

Compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface:  
\(\tau_{\max} = \dfrac{T\,r}{J}\) with \(T = 400\,000\; \text{N·mm}\).  

Determine the angle of twist over the shaft length:  
\(\theta = \dfrac{T\,L}{J\,G}\) where \(L = 1200\; \text{mm}\) and \(G = 39\,179.1\; \text{MPa}\).  

Present the governing relations, the numerical values of \(J\), \(\tau_{\max}\), and \(\theta\), and state that the shear stress varies linearly from zero at the centre to \(\tau_{\max}\) at the surface.
