Determine the internal torque distribution along the shaft, the resulting shear stress distribution, the angle of twist at any section, and the reaction torque at the fixed support.

Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, a uniform circular cross‑section, constant shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa, and that plane sections remain plane (Saint‑Venant torsion theory). Neglect axial loads, bending, and temperature effects.

Use the polar moment of inertia J = π r⁴ / 2 with r = 17.5 mm.

Apply equilibrium of torques to obtain the internal torque T(x):
- For 0 ≤ x < 800 mm, T(x) = T_R (reaction torque at the fixed end).
- For 800 ≤ x < 1200 mm, T(x) = T_R − T₂ (subtract the torque applied at x = 800 mm, note T₂ is opposite in sense to T₁).
- For x = 1200 mm (free end), T(x) = T_R − T₂ − T₁ = 0, which yields the reaction torque T_R = T₁ + T₂.

Compute shear stress τ(x, r) = T(x) · r / J, and the maximum shear stress τ_max(x) at the outer surface (r = 17.5 mm).

Determine the angle of twist θ(x) by integrating the torsional deformation relation dθ/dx = T(x) / (G J):
θ(x) = ∫₀ˣ T(ξ) / (G J) dξ.
Provide the total twist at the free end θ(L) for L = 1200 mm.

Present the governing equations:
- Polar moment of inertia: J = π r⁴ / 2.
- Torsional shear stress: τ = T r / J.
- Torsional deformation: dθ/dx = T / (G J).
- Equilibrium of torques: ΣT = 0 for any cut section.

Report the numerical values for:
- Reaction torque T_R at x = 0.
- Internal torque T(x) in each segment.
- Maximum shear stress in each segment.
- Angle of twist at x = 800 mm, at x = 1200 mm, and the total twist θ(L).
