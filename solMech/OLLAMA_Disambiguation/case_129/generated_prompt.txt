Assume a prismatic, homogeneous beam with constant Young’s modulus E and second moment of area I along its length.  
Assume small deformations, linear‑elastic material behavior, and pure bending (no axial force or shear deformation).  
Assume plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the deformed axis (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory).  

The curvature κ of the beam due to a bending moment M is given by  
κ = M / (E I).  

The radius of curvature R is the reciprocal of curvature:  
R = 1 / κ.  

Using the provided data:  
M = 3 000 000 N·mm, E = 200 000 MPa = 200 000 N/mm², I = 1 200 000 mm⁴.  

Compute curvature:  
κ = 3 000 000 N·mm / (200 000 N/mm² × 1 200 000 mm⁴) = 1.25 × 10⁻⁵ mm⁻¹.  

Compute radius of curvature:  
R = 1 / κ = 8.0 × 10⁴ mm = 80 m.  

Thus the beam’s curvature is 1.25 × 10⁻⁵ mm⁻¹ and its radius of curvature is 80 m under the given bending moment.
