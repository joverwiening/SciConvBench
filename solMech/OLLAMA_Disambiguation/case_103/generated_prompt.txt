Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic behavior for a circular rod of initial diameter d₀ = 30 mm and length L = 500 mm subjected only to the given axial load P = 25 000 N (the lateral load is neglected for the Poisson‑effect analysis).  
Use the material properties Young’s modulus E = 105 000 MPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.34.  

Compute the response quantities as follows:  

1. Cross‑sectional area A = π d₀² / 4 = π · 30² / 4 ≈ 706.86 mm².  
2. Axial stress σ = P / A ≈ 25 000 N / 706.86 mm² ≈ 35.38 MPa.  
3. Axial strain ε_z = σ / E ≈ 35.38 MPa / 105 000 MPa ≈ 3.36 × 10⁻⁴ (dimensionless).  
4. Lateral (radial) strain due to Poisson effect ε_lat = −ν ε_z ≈ −0.34 · 3.36 × 10⁻⁴ ≈ −1.14 × 10⁻⁴.  
5. Change in length ΔL = ε_z · L ≈ 3.36 × 10⁻⁴ · 500 mm ≈ 0.168 mm.  
6. Change in diameter Δd = ε_lat · d₀ ≈ −1.14 × 10⁻⁴ · 30 mm ≈ −0.0034 mm.  

Present the governing relations:  

σ = P / A, ε_z = σ / E, ε_lat = −ν ε_z, ΔL = ε_z L, Δd = ε_lat d₀.  

State the additional assumptions: the rod remains straight, the load is applied uniformly, temperature effects are ignored, and the lateral load does not affect the axial deformation considered here.
