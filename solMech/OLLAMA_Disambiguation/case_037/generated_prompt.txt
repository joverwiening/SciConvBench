Assume a prismatic, homogeneous beam that follows Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory, with small deformations, linear‑elastic material behavior, constant flexural rigidity \(EI\), and simply supported end conditions.  
The beam is subjected to a uniformly distributed transverse load \(w = 0.3\ \text{N/mm}\) over its entire span \(L = 4500\ \text{mm}\).  
Material properties: Young’s modulus \(E = 200{,}000\ \text{N/mm}^2\).  
Geometric property: second moment of area \(I = 2{,}000{,}000\ \text{mm}^4\).  

Governing differential equation: \(EI\,\frac{d^2 v}{dx^2}=M(x)\), where the bending moment for a uniform load on a simply supported beam is \(M(x)=\frac{w\,x\,(L-x)}{2}\).  

Deflection shape (integration of the moment curvature relation with the boundary conditions \(v(0)=v(L)=0\)):  
\[
v(x)=\frac{w\,x\,(L^{3}-2Lx^{2}+x^{3})}{24\,EI}.
\]

Slope distribution (first derivative of the deflection):  
\[
\theta(x)=\frac{dv}{dx}= \frac{w\,(L^{3}-6Lx^{2}+4x^{3})}{24\,EI}.
\]

Maximum mid‑span deflection (at \(x=L/2\)):  
\[
\delta_{\max}= \frac{5\,w\,L^{4}}{384\,EI}= \frac{5(0.3)(4500)^{4}}{384(200{,}000)(2{,}000{,}000)}\approx 4.0\ \text{mm}.
\]

Slope at each support (at \(x=0\) and \(x=L\)):  
\[
\theta_{\text{support}}= \frac{w\,L^{3}}{24\,EI}= \frac{0.3(4500)^{3}}{24(200{,}000)(2{,}000{,}000)}\approx 2.85\times10^{-3}\ \text{rad}\;(\approx0.163^{\circ}).
\]

All required response quantities (deflection curve, slope distribution, maximum deflection, and support slopes) are obtained from the above relations using the given numerical data.
