Assume a prismatic, homogeneous, isotropic bar of constant cross‑sectional area A = 200 mm² and Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa, undergoing small‑deformation, linear‑elastic axial loading.  
The left end A is fixed (u_A = 0). The right end D is free and carries an external axial load of 20 kN acting to the right.  
Two additional point loads act on the bar:  
- At x = 700 mm from A (point B) a 18 kN load acting to the right.  
- At x = 1300 mm from A (point C) an 8 kN load acting to the left (i.e., –8 kN in the positive‑right sign convention).  

Additional assumptions:  
- No body forces or temperature effects.  
- The bar remains straight; shear deformations are neglected.  

Governing relations to be used:  
1. Axial equilibrium: dN/dx + Σ P_i δ(x–x_i) = 0, where N(x) is the internal axial force and P_i are the applied point loads.  
2. Constitutive law: σ(x) = N(x)/A = E ε(x).  
3. Strain–displacement compatibility: ε(x) = du/dx, where u(x) is the axial displacement.  
4. Boundary conditions: u(0) = 0 at A; the resultant axial force at the free end D equals the applied 20 kN.  

Required response quantities:  
- Reaction axial force at the fixed support A.  
- Axial force diagram N(x) along the bar, accounting for the three point loads.  
- Axial stress distribution σ(x) = N(x)/A.  
- Axial strain distribution ε(x) = σ(x)/E.  
- Axial displacement field u(x) obtained by integrating ε(x) and applying u(0)=0.  
- Total elongation of the bar at the free end D, Δ = u(L) where L = 1800 mm.  

Present the above quantities analytically (piecewise expressions for each segment between loads) and, if desired, compute their numerical values using the given data.
