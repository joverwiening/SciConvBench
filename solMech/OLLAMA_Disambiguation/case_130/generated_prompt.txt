Assume the material is isotropic, homogeneous, linear‑elastic, and follows small‑deformation theory.  
Adopt typical structural‑steel properties: Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30.  
Treat the member as a thin plate so that plane‑stress conditions apply (σz = τxz = τyz = 0).  

1. Define the in‑plane stress tensor using the given average stresses:  
   σ = [[σxx, τxy], [τxy, σyy]] = [[6.67 MPa, 2.67 MPa], [2.67 MPa, 0]].  

2. Compute the principal stresses from the eigenvalues of the stress tensor:  
   σ1,2 = (σxx + σyy)/2 ± √[((σxx – σyy)/2)² + τxy²].  

3. Calculate the corresponding principal stress directions (θp) using:  
   tan 2θp = 2τxy / (σxx – σyy).  

4. Obtain the plane‑stress strain components from Hooke’s law:  
   εxx = (1/E)[σxx – νσyy],  
   εyy = (1/E)[σyy – νσxx],  
   γxy = (1+ν)/E · τxy.  

5. Form the strain tensor:  
   ε = [[εxx, γxy/2], [γxy/2, εyy]].  

6. Determine the principal strains as the eigenvalues of ε:  
   ε1,2 = (εxx + εyy)/2 ± √[((εxx – εyy)/2)² + (γxy/2)²].  

7. State the additional assumptions made:  
   - Plane‑stress condition (out‑of‑plane stresses are zero).  
   - Uniform stress distribution over the cross‑section.  
   - No temperature effects or residual stresses.  
   - Linear superposition of normal and shear loading is valid.  

8. Present the governing relations clearly:  
   - Stress tensor definition (step 1).  
   - Principal stress formulas (step 2, 3).  
   - Plane‑stress Hooke’s law (step 4).  
   - Strain tensor construction (step 5).  
   - Principal strain formulas (step 6).  

These steps provide all required response quantities: combined plane‑stress state, principal stresses, principal strains, and the orientations of the principal axes.
