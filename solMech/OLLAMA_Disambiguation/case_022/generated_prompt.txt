Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid with small deformations.  
Adopt material properties of typical structural steel: Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30, giving a shear modulus G = E/[2(1+ν)] ≈ 80.8 GPa.  
Neglect temperature effects, residual stresses, and any transverse shear force; only the applied torque T = 450 000 N·mm (≈ 450 N·m) is considered.  
Assume the shaft is fixed at one end and the torque is applied at the opposite end, so the relative twist between the ends is the quantity of interest.  

Governing relations  
1. Polar moment of inertia for a circular cross‑section: J = π r⁴ / 2.  
2. Maximum shear stress in a solid circular shaft: τ_max = T r / J = 2 T / (π r³).  
3. Rate of twist per unit length: dθ/dx = T / (G J).  
4. Total twist over a segment of length L: θ = T L / (G J).  

Segment AB (r₁ = 12.5 mm, L₁ = 300 mm)  
- J₁ = π (12.5 mm)⁴ / 2 = 3.833 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴.  
- τ_max,AB = 2 T / (π r₁³) ≈ 1.467 × 10⁸ Pa = 146.7 MPa.  
- θ_AB = T L₁ / (G J₁) ≈ 0.0436 rad.  

Segment BC (r₂ = 15 mm, L₂ = 700 mm)  
- J₂ = π (15 mm)⁴ / 2 = 7.950 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴.  
- τ_max,BC = 2 T / (π r₂³) ≈ 8.49 × 10⁷ Pa = 84.9 MPa.  
- θ_BC = T L₂ / (G J₂) ≈ 0.0491 rad.  

Overall response  
- Maximum shear stress in the shaft is the larger of the two segment values: τ_max = 146.7 MPa occurring in segment AB.  
- Total twist of the stepped shaft: θ_total = θ_AB + θ_BC ≈ 0.0927 rad ≈ 5.3°.  

Present the above governing equations, numerical results, and the stated assumptions in the final report.
