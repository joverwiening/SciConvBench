Assume a prismatic, homogeneous, small‑deformation, linear‑elastic beam of length L = 3000 mm that is simply supported at its ends A (x = 0) and B (x = 3000 mm).  
The only external load is a vertical point load P = 12 000 N acting downward at x = 1500 mm (mid‑span). No axial tension load or applied moment is present.  

Additional assumptions:  
- The beam’s weight and any distributed loads are neglected.  
- Supports provide vertical reactions only; there is no horizontal reaction or restraint against rotation beyond the simple supports.  
- Plane sections remain plane (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory).  

Governing relations:  
1. Equilibrium of vertical forces: R_A + R_B − P = 0.  
2. Equilibrium of moments about support A: R_B·L − P·x_P = 0, where x_P = 1500 mm.  
3. Internal shear V(x) satisfies dM/dx = V(x).  

Computed support reactions:  
- R_A = R_B = P/2 = 6000 N (upward).  

Internal shear V(x):  
- For 0 ≤ x < 1500 mm: V(x) = +6000 N.  
- For 1500 mm < x ≤ 3000 mm: V(x) = −6000 N.  

Internal bending moment M(x) (taking M = 0 at both supports):  
- For 0 ≤ x ≤ 1500 mm: M(x) = R_A·x = 6000 · x [N·mm].  
- For 1500 mm ≤ x ≤ 3000 mm: M(x) = R_B·(L − x) = 6000 · (3000 − x) [N·mm].  

Maximum bending moment occurs at the load point (x = 1500 mm):  
M_max = 6000 · 1500 = 9 000 000 N·mm = 9 kN·m.  

These piecewise expressions for V(x) and M(x) fully describe the internal shear force and bending moment distribution along the beam under the given loading.
