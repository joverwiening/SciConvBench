Assume the vertical bar has a uniform rectangular (or circular) cross‑section of area A = 200 mm² along its entire length L = 3500 mm.  
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic with Young’s modulus E = 69 000 MPa and density ρ = 8900 kg/m³.  
Assume temperature effects are negligible and the bar is only subjected to its own weight; no external axial loads are applied.  
Use the standard gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².  

Compute the self‑weight force per unit length:  
\( w = \rho \, A \, g \)   (convert A to m² for consistency).  

Determine the axial stress distribution along the bar:  
\( \sigma(x) = w \, (L - x) \)   where x is measured from the top (free end) and \(0 \le x \le L\).  

Relate stress to strain using Hooke’s law:  
\( \varepsilon(x) = \sigma(x) / E \).  

Integrate the strain over the length to obtain total elongation due to self‑weight:  
\( \delta = \int_{0}^{L} \varepsilon(x) \, dx = \frac{w L^{2}}{2 E} \).  

Report the following response quantities:  
- Axial stress at the base (x = 0): \( \sigma_{base} = w L \).  
- Maximum axial strain at the base: \( \varepsilon_{base} = \sigma_{base} / E \).  
- Total elongation of the bar: \( \delta = \frac{\rho A g L^{2}}{2 E} \).  

State the numerical results in consistent units (e.g., stress in MPa, strain dimensionless, elongation in mm).
