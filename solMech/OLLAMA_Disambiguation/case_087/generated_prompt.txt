Assume the tensile specimen has an initial uniform cross‑section A0 and an initial gauge length L0 measured before loading.  
Assume the material behaves as a small‑deformation, linear‑elastic solid up to the proportional limit and then yields plastically until fracture, with isotropic properties and no strain‑rate effects.  
Assume the fracture occurs at a well‑defined neck so that the final gauge length Lf and the final cross‑section Af can be measured after the test.  
Define engineering stress as σ = P / A0, where P is the applied axial load.  
Define engineering strain as ε = (Lf – L0) / L0 for the elastic portion of the test.  
Apply Hooke’s law σ = E ε for the elastic region, where E is Young’s modulus.  
State the ductility measure percent elongation as %EL = ((Lf – L0) / L0) × 100 %.  
State the ductility measure percent reduction of area as %RA = ((A0 – Af) / A0) × 100 %.  
If needed, relate true strain at fracture to the measured quantities by ε_true = ln(Lf / L0) = ln(A0 / Af).  
Present these governing relations clearly and indicate that the two ductility measures are calculated from the measured final dimensions after fracture.
