Assume a prismatic, straight cantilever beam of constant cross‑section, made of a homogeneous isotropic material that behaves as a linear‑elastic solid under small deformations; plane sections remain plane (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory) and shear deformation is neglected.  
The beam length is L = 2000 mm, the uniformly distributed transverse load is w = 1.0 N/mm acting downward, the Young’s modulus is E = 200 000 MPa (200 000 N/mm²), and the second moment of area of the cross‑section is I = 1 500 000 mm⁴.  

The governing differential equation for the transverse deflection v(x) of an Euler‑Bernoulli beam under a distributed load w is  
E I d⁴v/dx⁴ = w, with x measured from the fixed support (x = 0) to the free end (x = L).  

Integrating with the cantilever boundary conditions (v = 0, dv/dx = 0 at x = 0) gives the deflection curve  
v(x) = (w x⁴)/(24 E I) – (w L x³)/(6 E I) + (w L² x²)/(4 E I).  

The tip deflection (at x = L) is therefore  
δ_tip = w L⁴ / (8 E I).  

Substituting the given values:  
L⁴ = (2000 mm)⁴ = 1.6 × 10¹³ mm⁴  
δ_tip = (1.0 N/mm · 1.6 × 10¹³ mm⁴) / (8 · 200 000 N/mm² · 1 500 000 mm⁴) ≈ 6.67 mm.  

The slope at the free end is  
θ_tip = w L³ / (6 E I) ≈ 0.00444 rad (≈ 0.254°).  

The maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed support:  
M_max = w L² / 2 = (1.0 N/mm · (2000 mm)²) / 2 = 2.0 × 10⁶ N·mm.  

If the distance from the neutral axis to the outermost fiber is c, the extreme‑fiber bending stress is  
σ_max = M_max c / I.  

These relations and numerical results constitute the complete specification for the cantilever deflection under the given uniform distributed load.
