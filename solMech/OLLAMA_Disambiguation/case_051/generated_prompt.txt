Determine the transverse shear stress distribution in the rectangular beam with width b = 50 mm and height h = 80 mm subjected to a shear force V = 20 000 N.

Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, isotropic material, and that plane sections remain plane after deformation. Neglect any axial or bending loads; only the transverse shear force is considered.

Use the standard shear stress formula for a rectangular cross‑section:
τ(y) = V · Q(y) / (I · b)

where:
- I = b h³ / 12 is the second moment of area about the neutral axis.
- Q(y) = (b / 2) · (h / 2 − y) · [ (h / 2) + y ] is the first moment of the area above the point at distance y from the neutral axis (y = 0 at the centroid, positive upward).
- b is the width of the beam.

From these relations the shear stress varies parabolically across the height and attains its maximum at the neutral axis (y = 0):
τ_max = 1.5 · V / (b h)

Calculate the numerical values:
- I = (0.050 m) · (0.080 m)³ / 12 = 2.13 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴
- τ_max = 1.5 · 20 000 N / (0.050 m · 0.080 m) = 7.5 × 10⁶ Pa = 7.5 MPa

Thus the transverse shear stress distribution is:
τ(y) = (3 · V) / (2 b h) · [1 − (2y / h)²]   (−h/2 ≤ y ≤ h/2)

and the maximum shear stress at the neutral axis is 7.5 MPa.

If shear strain or deflection is required, the shear modulus G = E / [2(1 + ν)] must be supplied; the shear strain is γ(y) = τ(y) / G. No further material properties are needed for the stress results.
