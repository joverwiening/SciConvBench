Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid circular shaft of constant diameter d = 20 mm (radius r = 10 mm) and length L = 1000 mm, subjected only to a steady torque T = 3.98 × 10⁴ N·mm (≈ 39.8 N·m) and no transverse shear force. Small‑deformation theory applies; temperature effects, residual stresses, and axial loads are neglected.

1. Compute the polar moment of inertia of the circular cross‑section:  
   J = π d⁴ / 32 = π (20 mm)⁴ / 32 = π × 1.6 × 10⁵ mm⁴ / 32 ≈ 1.57 × 10⁴ mm⁴.

2. Maximum shear stress in the shaft:  
   τ_max = T · r / J = (3.98 × 10⁴ N·mm) · 10 mm / (1.57 × 10⁴ mm⁴) ≈ 25.3 MPa.

3. Shear stress distribution over the radius:  
   τ(r') = T · r' / J, 0 ≤ r' ≤ r.

4. Angle of twist over the shaft length:  
   θ = T L / (G J) = (3.98 × 10⁴ N·mm) · 1000 mm / (7.69231 × 10⁴ MPa · 1.57 × 10⁴ mm⁴)  
   Convert G to N/mm²: G = 7.69231 × 10⁴ N/mm².  
   θ ≈ 0.033 rad ≈ 1.9°.

5. Power–torque conversion (steady state):  
   ω = 2π · (1200 rpm / 60) = 40π rad/s.  
   P = T · ω = 3.98 × 10⁴ N·mm · 40π rad/s ≈ 5.0 kW (consistent with the given motor power).

6. Torsional check: compare τ_max with the allowable shear stress of the material (if provided). If no allowable value is given, state that τ_max must be less than the material’s shear yield strength divided by a safety factor.

7. Since the transverse shear force is assumed zero, no bending stresses or shear deflection are considered.

8. Summary of required response quantities:  
   - Polar moment of inertia J.  
   - Maximum shear stress τ_max ≈ 25.3 MPa.  
   - Shear stress distribution τ(r').  
   - Angle of twist θ ≈ 0.033 rad (1.9°) over the 1 m length.  
   - Power transmitted P ≈ 5 kW, confirming the torque‑speed relationship.  

These governing relations and computed values fully describe the torsional response of the shaft under the specified loading.
