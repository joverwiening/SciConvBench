Assume a prismatic, straight cantilever beam of length L = 1500 mm made of a linear‑elastic, isotropic material with Young’s modulus E = 69 000 MPa and second moment of area I = 2 500 000 mm⁴. The beam is subjected to a single vertical point load P = 500 N applied at the free end, and all deformations are small so that Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory applies.

Additional assumptions:
- The beam cross‑section remains plane and perpendicular to the longitudinal axis during bending (Euler‑Bernoulli hypothesis).
- Shear deformation and axial effects are neglected.
- The material follows Hooke’s law (stress proportional to strain) throughout the loading.
- The load is static and applied slowly enough to avoid dynamic effects.

Governing relations:
1. Bending moment distribution along the beam: M(x) = P · (L − x), where x is measured from the fixed support (0 ≤ x ≤ L).
2. Curvature–moment relation: d²v/dx² = M(x)/(E I), where v(x) is the transverse deflection.
3. Strain energy stored in the beam due to bending:  
   U = ∫₀ᴸ M(x)²/(2 E I) dx = P² L³/(6 E I).
4. Castigliano’s theorem for the end deflection:  
   δ_end = ∂U/∂P = P L³/(3 E I).

Resulting end deflection:
δ_end = (500 N) · (1500 mm)³ / [3 · (69 000 N/mm²) · 2 500 000 mm⁴]  
 = 0.0216 mm (approximately).
