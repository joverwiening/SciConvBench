Determine the relevant response quantities for the impact loading of a dropped weight on an axially loaded rod.

Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior and use SI units throughout.

Additional assumptions  
- The rod is fixed at one end and free at the other where the weight impacts.  
- The impact is perfectly elastic; no energy is lost to plastic deformation, friction, or damping.  
- The weight impacts centrally on the rod axis and the rod remains straight during deformation.  
- Gravity acting on the rod after impact is neglected compared with the impact forces.  
- The material is homogeneous and isotropic with Young’s modulus \(E = 200\,000\ \text{MPa}\) and density \(\rho = 7\,850\ \text{kg/m}^3\).  

Given data  
- Rod length \(L = 1.200\ \text{m}\).  
- Rod diameter \(d = 30\ \text{mm}\) → cross‑sectional area \(A = \pi d^{2}/4 = 225\pi\ \text{mm}^2 = 7.07\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2\).  
- Axial static load \(P_{\text{static}} = 0\ \text{N}\).  
- Dropped weight mass \(m = 51\ \text{kg}\) (weight \(W = 500\ \text{N}\)).  
- Drop height \(h = 0.200\ \text{m}\).  

Rod mass  
\[
M = \rho A L = 7\,850\ \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}\times 7.07\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2 \times 1.200\ \text{m}
      \approx 6.66\ \text{kg}.
\]

Impact velocity (free‑fall)  
\[
v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2 \times 0.200\ \text{m}}
    \approx 1.98\ \text{m/s}.
\]

Axial stiffness of the rod (fixed–free)  
\[
k = \frac{E A}{L}.
\]

Maximum deformation \(\delta_{\max}\) from energy balance  
\[
\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} = \frac{1}{2} k \delta_{\max}^{2}
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
\delta_{\max} = v \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}.
\]

Peak impact force  
\[
F_{\text{peak}} = k \,\delta_{\max}
               = v \sqrt{k\,m}
               = \sqrt{E A \, \frac{m}{L}} \; v.
\]

Resulting axial stress in the rod at peak force  
\[
\sigma_{\max} = \frac{F_{\text{peak}}}{A}.
\]

Resulting axial strain at peak stress (Hooke’s law)  
\[
\varepsilon_{\max} = \frac{\sigma_{\max}}{E}.
\]

Summary of required response quantities  
1. Impact velocity \(v\).  
2. Rod axial stiffness \(k\).  
3. Peak impact force \(F_{\text{peak}}\).  
4. Maximum axial deformation \(\delta_{\max}\).  
5. Maximum axial stress \(\sigma_{\max}\).  
6. Maximum axial strain \(\varepsilon_{\max}\).  

All governing relations are provided above and can be evaluated directly with the given numerical data.
