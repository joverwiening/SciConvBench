Assume the beam undergoes small deformations and behaves as a linear‑elastic material with Young’s modulus E and Poisson’s ratio ν.  
The beam is prismatic with a constant rectangular cross‑section of width b and height h, length L, and is subjected to an axial force P (positive in tension, negative in compression) and a bending moment M about the strong axis.  

Additional assumptions:  
- Plane sections remain plane after deformation (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory).  
- Shear deformation is neglected.  
- The material is homogeneous and isotropic.  
- Loads are static and applied slowly so that inertial effects are absent.  

Governing relations:  
- Cross‑sectional area: A = b h.  
- Second moment of area about the neutral axis: I = (b h³)/12.  
- Axial stress due to the axial force: σₐ = P/A.  
- Bending stress at a distance y from the neutral axis: σ_b = M y/I.  
- Combined normal stress at any point in the cross‑section: σ = σₐ ± σ_b = P/A ± M y/I (tension positive, compression negative).  
- Curvature of the beam: κ = M/(E I).  
- Deflection of the beam under a bending moment distribution can be obtained by integrating the curvature: v''(x) = M(x)/(E I).  

These equations constitute the flexure formula and related response quantities for a rectangular prismatic beam under combined compression, tension, and bending loads.
