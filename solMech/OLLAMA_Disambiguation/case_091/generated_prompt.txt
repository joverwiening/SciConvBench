Assume a prismatic bar of constant cross‑sectional area A, length L, made of a homogeneous isotropic linear‑elastic material with Young’s modulus E.  
The bar’s longitudinal axis coincides with the x‑axis, with x = 0 at the left end and x = L at the right end.  
Multiple axial point loads Pi are applied at known positions xi measured from the left end, where 0 < xi < L.  

Additional assumptions:  
- Small deformations so that strain–displacement relations are linear.  
- No temperature changes, body forces, or shear deformations.  
- The cross‑section remains plane and perpendicular to the axis (Bernoulli‑Euler axial assumption).  
- Boundary conditions are specified (e.g., left end fixed, right end free, or both ends simply supported).  

Governing relations:  
1. The axial internal force N(x) at any section x equals the algebraic sum of all external forces acting to the left of that section, including support reactions.  
2. Axial stress σ(x) = N(x) / A.  
3. Axial strain ε(x) = σ(x) / E = N(x) / (E A).  
4. Axial displacement u(x) = ∫0^x ε(ξ) dξ = (1/(E A)) ∫0^x N(ξ) dξ.  
5. For a bar fixed at x = 0, the reaction R at the fixed support is R = Σ Pi.  
6. For a bar simply supported at both ends, the reactions R1 and R2 are obtained from equilibrium: R1 + R2 = Σ Pi and Σ Pi xi = R2 L.  
7. The axial deformation at the free end (or at any point) follows from the displacement expression in (4).  

These relations provide the axial force distribution, stress, strain, and displacement fields for any configuration of multiple point loads on the bar under the stated assumptions.
