Determine the axial stress in the bar using the static relation σ_static = P / A.  
Assume the bar is prismatic, homogeneous, isotropic, and obeys linear‑elastic small‑deformation behavior with Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa.  
Assume one end of the bar is rigidly fixed and the axial load P = 8000 N is applied suddenly (instantaneously) at the free end, with no external damping or resistance other than the fixed support.  
For a suddenly applied axial load, the maximum dynamic stress is σ_max = 2 σ_static, derived from equating the work done by the load to the strain energy stored in the bar at the instant of peak stress.  
Compute the static stress: σ_static = 8000 N / 300 mm² = 26.67 MPa.  
Compute the maximum stress: σ_max = 2 × 26.67 MPa = 53.33 MPa.  
State the governing relations:  
Work of the load: W = P · δ, where δ is the axial displacement at the loaded end.  
Strain energy in the bar: U = (σ² · V) / (2E), where V = A · L is the bar volume (L cancels in the energy balance for the sudden load case).  
Energy balance at peak stress: P · δ = U, leading to σ_max = 2 P / A.  
Report the static stress, the maximum dynamic stress, and note that the dynamic amplification factor is 2 under the stated assumptions.
