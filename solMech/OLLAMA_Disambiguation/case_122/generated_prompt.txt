Assume the material behaves as a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid with small deformations.  
Take the Young’s modulus E and Poisson’s ratio ν as material parameters to be supplied for quantitative strain or deflection results.  
The column cross‑section is square with width b = 100 mm and height h = 100 mm.  
The applied axial load is compressive with magnitude P = 90 000 N.  
The load acts with an eccentricity e = 15 mm measured from the centroid along the width direction.  

Compute the cross‑sectional area: A = b · h = 100 mm × 100 mm = 10 000 mm².  
Compute the second moment of area about the centroidal axis perpendicular to the eccentricity direction: I = b · h³ / 12 = 100 mm · (100 mm)³ / 12 = 8.33 × 10⁶ mm⁴.  
Form the eccentric bending moment: M = P · e = 90 000 N · 15 mm = 1.35 × 10⁶ N·mm.  

The normal stress distribution over the depth y from the neutral axis is given by the superposition of axial and bending stresses: σ(y) = P/A ± M·c/I, where c = h/2 = 50 mm is the distance from the centroid to the extreme fiber.  

Calculate the axial stress component: σₐ = P/A = 90 000 N / 10 000 mm² = 9 N/mm² (compressive).  
Calculate the bending stress component at the extreme fiber: σ_b = M·c/I = (1.35 × 10⁶ N·mm · 50 mm) / 8.33 × 10⁶ mm⁴ ≈ 8.1 N/mm² (compressive on the side toward which the load is eccentric, tensile on the opposite side).  

Determine the maximum and minimum normal stresses:  
σ_max = σₐ + σ_b ≈ 9 + 8.1 = 17.1 N/mm² (compressive) at the fiber nearest the load eccentricity.  
σ_min = σₐ – σ_b ≈ 9 – 8.1 = 0.9 N/mm² (compressive) at the opposite extreme fiber.  

If the material modulus E is known, the corresponding normal strains are ε_max = σ_max / E and ε_min = σ_min / E.  
The curvature of the column due to the eccentric load is κ = M / (E · I).  
If the column length L is specified, the lateral deflection at the mid‑height (or at the end for a cantilevered case) can be obtained from the curvature integration, e.g., for a simply supported column the lateral deflection at mid‑span is δ = (M · L) / (2 · E · I).
