Assume a prismatic bar of constant cross‑sectional area A, length L, and homogeneous linear‑elastic material with Young’s modulus E, subjected to a set of N axial point loads Pi applied at positions xi measured from the left end (0 < xi < L).  
State the additional assumptions: the bar is straight, the cross‑section is uniform, temperature effects are neglected, and the loads act only in the axial direction.  

Relevant response quantities:  
- Axial internal force N(x) along the bar.  
- Axial stress σ(x) = N(x)/A.  
- Axial strain ε(x) = σ(x)/E.  
- Axial displacement u(x) measured from the left end.  
- Reaction forces at the supports (if the bar is fixed or simply supported).  

Governing relations:  
1. Equilibrium of a differential element: dN/dx + Σ Pi δ(x‑xi) = 0, where δ is the Dirac delta function representing point loads.  
2. Constitutive law (linear elasticity): σ(x) = E ε(x).  
3. Kinematic compatibility: ε(x) = du/dx.  
4. Integration of equilibrium gives the piecewise constant internal force:  
   N(x) = Rleft – Σ_{xi < x} Pi, where Rleft is the reaction at the left support.  
5. Stress distribution: σ(x) = N(x)/A.  
6. Strain distribution: ε(x) = σ(x)/E.  
7. Displacement field obtained by integrating strain:  
   u(x) = u(0) + ∫₀ˣ ε(s) ds = u(0) + (1/(E A)) ∫₀ˣ N(s) ds.  
   For a bar fixed at the left end, u(0)=0; for a free left end, u(0) is determined from compatibility with the right support.  
8. Reaction forces are found from overall static equilibrium: Σ Pi + Rleft + Rright = 0 and Σ Pi xi + Rright L = 0 (sign conventions as appropriate).  

These equations fully define the axial stress, strain, internal force, displacement, and support reactions for any number of point loads applied to a prismatic, linearly elastic bar under small deformations.
