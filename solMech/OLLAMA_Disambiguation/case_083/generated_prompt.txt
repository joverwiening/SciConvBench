Assume a homogeneous, isotropic steel shaft with Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa, shear modulus G = 80 GPa, and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30; small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior; uniform circular cross‑section of diameter d = 25 mm (radius r = 12.5 mm); length is sufficiently long that end effects are negligible; temperature effects are ignored; plane sections remain plane; no stress concentrations are present.  
Compute the cross‑sectional area A = π r² = 4.909 × 10⁻⁴ m².  
Compute the axial normal stress σₐ = P/A = 25 000 N / 4.909 × 10⁻⁴ m² ≈ 5.09 × 10⁷ Pa = 50.9 MPa (tensile).  
Compute the polar moment of inertia J = π r⁴/2 = 3.835 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴.  
Convert the applied torque T = 300 000 N·mm = 300 N·m.  
Compute the maximum torsional shear stress τₜ = T r / J = 300 · 0.0125 / 3.835 × 10⁻⁸ ≈ 9.78 × 10⁷ Pa = 97.8 MPa, acting on the surface and varying linearly to zero at the centre.  
Form the combined stress state at the outer surface: normal stress σ = σₐ (axial) and shear stress τ = τₜ (torsional).  
Determine the principal stresses using σ₁,₂ = σ/2 ± √[(σ/2)² + τ²]:  
σ₁ ≈ 1.265 × 10⁸ Pa = 126.5 MPa (maximum principal stress),  
σ₂ ≈ ‑7.56 × 10⁷ Pa = ‑75.6 MPa (minimum principal stress).  
The third principal stress σ₃ ≈ 0 Pa (radial stress is negligible).  
Compute the shear strain γ = τ/G = 9.78 × 10⁷ Pa / 8.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa ≈ 1.22 × 10⁻³ (dimensionless).  
Compute the axial normal strain εₐ = σₐ/E ≈ 5.09 × 10⁷ Pa / 2.10 × 10¹¹ Pa ≈ 2.43 × 10⁻⁴.  
Obtain the principal strains from the strain transformation:  
ε₁,₂ = (σ/2E)(1 ‑ ν) ± √[(σ/2E)(1 + ν)]² + (γ/2)², yielding ε₁ ≈ 1.27 × 10⁻³ and ε₂ ≈ ‑7.56 × 10⁻⁴ (numerical values shown for reference).  
Present the governing relations:  
σₐ = P/A, τₜ = T r / J, J = π d⁴/32, A = π d²/4, σ₁,₂ = σ/2 ± √[(σ/2)² + τ²], γ = τ/G, εₐ = σₐ/E, ε₁,₂ = (σ/2E)(1 ‑ ν) ± √[(σ/2E)(1 + ν)]² + (γ/2)².  
Summarize the required response quantities: axial normal stress, torsional shear stress distribution, combined stress tensor at the outer surface, principal stresses, principal strains, and the associated strain components.
