Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic behavior for a prismatic circular rod under axial tension.  
Use the following governing relations:  
Average normal stress σ = P / A, where P is the axial tensile load and A = π d²/4 is the cross‑sectional area.  
Axial strain ε = σ / E, where E is Young’s modulus.  
Elongation ΔL = ε L₀, where L₀ is the original gage length.  
Factor of safety with respect to yielding FS = S_y / σ, where S_y is the material yield strength.  

Given:  
Diameter d = 30 mm → cross‑sectional area A = 706.86 mm².  
Axial tensile load P = 15 000 N.  
Young’s modulus E = 69 000 MPa.  
Yield strength S_y = 275 MPa.  
Original length L₀ = 800 mm.  

Computed response quantities:  
Average normal stress σ = 21.2 MPa.  
Average axial strain ε = 3.08 × 10⁻⁴ (unitless).  
Elongation ΔL = 0.246 mm.  
Factor of safety FS = 12.9 (≈13).  

Additional assumptions:  
The load is applied centrally and uniformly, producing a constant stress state along the rod; temperature effects are negligible; no residual stresses or geometric imperfections are present; buckling is not a concern for pure tension.
