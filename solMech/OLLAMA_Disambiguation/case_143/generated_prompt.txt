Assume a homogeneous, isotropic steel shaft with small deformations and linear‑elastic behavior.  
Given data: radius r = 12.5 mm, length L = 500 mm, axial load P = 15 000 N, applied torque T = 400 000 N·mm, Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa, Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30.  

Assumptions:  
- Plane sections remain plane; no warping.  
- No bending, only axial tension/compression and pure torsion.  
- Radial and circumferential normal stresses are zero (except shear from torsion).  

Governing relations:  
1. Cross‑sectional area A = π r².  
2. Polar moment of inertia J = π r⁴ / 2.  
3. Axial normal stress σₐ = P / A.  
4. Shear stress due to torsion τ = T r / J.  
5. Shear modulus G = E / [2(1+ν)].  
6. Axial strain εₐ = σₐ / E.  
7. Shear strain γ = τ / G.  
8. Principal normal stresses σ₁,₂ = σₐ/2 ± √[(σₐ/2)² + τ²].  
9. Principal strains ε₁,₂ = εₐ/2 ± √[(εₐ/2)² + (γ/2)²].  
10. Twist per unit length θ′ = T / (G J).  
11. Total twist θ = θ′ L.  
12. Axial elongation δ = P L / (A E).  

Numerical results:  
- A = π (12.5 mm)² = 490.9 mm².  
- J = π (12.5 mm)⁴ / 2 = 38 336.5 mm⁴.  
- σₐ = 15 000 N / 490.9 mm² = 30.6 MPa.  
- τ = 400 000 N·mm · 12.5 mm / 38 336.5 mm⁴ = 130.4 MPa.  
- G = 210 GPa / [2(1+0.30)] = 80.8 GPa.  
- εₐ = 30.6 MPa / 210 000 MPa = 1.46 × 10⁻⁴.  
- γ = 130.4 MPa / 80 769 MPa = 1.62 × 10⁻³.  
- σ₁ = 146.6 MPa, σ₂ = –116.0 MPa.  
- ε₁ = 8.83 × 10⁻⁴, ε₂ = –7.38 × 10⁻⁴.  
- θ′ = 400 000 N·mm / (80 769 N/mm² · 38 336.5 mm⁴) = 1.29 × 10⁻⁴ rad/mm.  
- θ = θ′ L = 1.29 × 10⁻⁴ rad/mm · 500 mm = 0.0645 rad = 3.70°.  
- δ = 15 000 N · 500 mm / (490.9 mm² · 210 000 N/mm²) = 0.0728 mm.  

These equations and computed quantities fully define the combined axial‑torsional response of the circular shaft, including principal stress and strain measures.
