Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for a homogeneous steel rod (E = 200 000 MPa, ρ = 7850 kg/m³).  
The rod has diameter d = 25 mm, cross‑sectional area A = 4.91 × 10⁻⁴ m², length L = 0.800 m, and is clamped at the top end only.  
A static axial load Pₛ = 1500 N is applied continuously.  
A weight of mass m = 153 kg is released from height h = 0.200 m onto the free lower end, producing impact loading.  

Additional assumptions:  
- One‑dimensional axial stress state; shear and bending are neglected.  
- No friction or energy loss at the impact interface; the impact is perfectly elastic.  
- Stress waves travel only along the rod axis; lateral inertia is ignored.  
- Damping is negligible; the rod behaves as an ideal elastic bar.  

Required response quantities and governing relations:  

1. Impact velocity of the dropped weight: v₀ = √(2 g h).  
2. Longitudinal wave speed in the rod: c = √(E/ρ).  
3. Characteristic acoustic impedance of the rod: Z = ρ c A.  
4. Peak impact force transmitted to the rod (elastic impact model): Fᵢ = (2 Z v₀) / (1 + Z/(m c)).  
5. Total axial force during impact: F_total = Pₛ + Fᵢ.  
6. Maximum axial stress in the rod: σ_max = F_total / A.  
7. Corresponding axial strain: ε_max = σ_max / E.  
8. Maximum axial displacement of the free end during impact: δ_max = (F_total L) / (E A).  
9. Duration of the impact pulse (half‑wave travel time): t_imp = L / c.  
10. Natural frequency of the clamped‑free rod (first mode): f₁ = (1.875² c) / (2 π L).  

Present the above quantities in the final analysis, using the given numerical values and the listed equations.
