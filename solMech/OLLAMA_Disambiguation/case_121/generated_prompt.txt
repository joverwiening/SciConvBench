Assume the column material is structural steel with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa.  
Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior and that plane sections remain plane.  
The rectangular cross‑section has width b = 120 mm and height h = 200 mm, giving an area A = b·h = 24 000 mm².  
The second moment of area about the centroidal axis parallel to the width (bending about the strong axis) is I = b·h³/12 = 120 mm·(200 mm)³/12 = 1.28 × 10⁸ mm⁴.  
The applied axial load is P = 40 000 N acting at an eccentricity e = 25 mm from the centroid, producing a bending moment M = P·e = 40 000 N·25 mm = 1.0 × 10⁶ N·mm.  
The axial stress due to the direct load is σₐ = P / A = 40 000 N / 24 000 mm² = 1.667 MPa.  
The bending stress distribution is σ_b(y) = M·y / I, where y is the distance from the neutral axis; the maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fiber y = h/2 = 100 mm, giving σ_b,max = M·(h/2) / I = 1.0 × 10⁶ N·mm·100 mm / 1.28 × 10⁸ mm⁴ = 0.781 MPa.  
The total normal stress at any fiber is σ(y) = σₐ + σ_b(y).  
The axial strain is εₐ = σₐ / E.  
The bending strain at a fiber distance y from the neutral axis is ε_b(y) = (M·y) / (E·I).  
The total normal strain at any fiber is ε(y) = εₐ + ε_b(y).  
Check that the combined stress does not exceed the material’s yield stress (assume typical structural steel yield ≈ 250 MPa) to verify elastic behavior.  
Report the following response quantities: cross‑sectional area A, second moment of area I, axial stress σₐ, maximum bending stress σ_b,max, combined maximum normal stress σ_max = σₐ + σ_b,max, axial strain εₐ, and maximum combined strain ε_max = εₐ + (M·(h/2))/(E·I).
