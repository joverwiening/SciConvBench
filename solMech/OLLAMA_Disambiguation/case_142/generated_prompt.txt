Assume small deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic material behavior.  
Consider a prismatic tension‑test specimen with uniform cross‑section area A and initial gauge length L₀.  
Apply a static axial load P uniformly at the specimen ends.  

Engineering stress is defined as σ = P / A.  
Engineering strain is defined as ε = ΔL / L₀, where ΔL is the change in length of the gauge section.  

For linear‑elastic response, the governing relation is σ = E ε, where E is Young’s modulus (the slope of the σ–ε curve in the elastic region).  

Additional assumptions:  
- The material properties (E, Poisson’s ratio) are constant and temperature effects are neglected.  
- Lateral contraction (Poisson effect) does not affect the axial stress‑strain calculation.  
- No body forces or dynamic effects are present; loading is quasi‑static.  
- The specimen remains within the elastic limit throughout the analysis.
