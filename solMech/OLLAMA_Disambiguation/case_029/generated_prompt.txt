Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, circular rod of constant diameter d₀ = 20 mm and length L = 1000 mm, subjected only to an axial tensile load P = 25 000 N; the lateral load is taken as zero.  
Adopt small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior with Young’s modulus E = 105 000 MPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.34.  
The cross‑sectional area is A = π d₀² / 4 = π·20² / 4 = 314.159 mm².  
The axial normal stress is σ = P / A = 25 000 N / 314.159 mm² ≈ 79.58 MPa.  
The axial strain is εₓ = σ / E ≈ 79.58 MPa / 105 000 MPa = 7.58 × 10⁻⁴ (dimensionless).  
The axial elongation is ΔL = εₓ · L ≈ 7.58 × 10⁻⁴ · 1000 mm = 0.758 mm, giving a final length L′ ≈ 1000.758 mm.  
The transverse (lateral) strain due to Poisson’s effect is ε_y = ε_z = −ν · εₓ ≈ −0.34 · 7.58 × 10⁻⁴ = −2.58 × 10⁻⁴.  
The change in diameter is Δd = ε_y · d₀ ≈ −2.58 × 10⁻⁴ · 20 mm = −0.0052 mm, giving a final diameter d′ ≈ 19.9948 mm.  
Summarize the governing relations:  
σ = P / A, εₓ = σ / E, ΔL = εₓ L, ε_y = ε_z = −ν εₓ, Δd = ε_y d₀.  
These quantities constitute the required numerical response for the Poisson effect under uniaxial tension.
