Assume a prismatic, homogeneous, isotropic steel shaft of circular cross‑section with diameter d = 35 mm (radius c = 17.5 mm).  
Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, no axial force, and that the shaft is sufficiently long for the applied bending moment M = 3 000 000 N·mm and torque T = 500 000 N·mm to be developed at the section of interest.  
Assume material properties of typical structural steel: Young’s modulus E = 210 000 MPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30.  
Compute shear modulus G = E/[2(1+ν)] = 80 769 MPa.  

Geometric properties:  
Second moment of area I = πd⁴/64 = 73 630 mm⁴.  
Polar moment of inertia J = πd⁴/32 = 147 261 mm⁴.  

Bending stress at the outer fiber:  
σ_b = M·c / I = (3 000 000 N·mm)(17.5 mm) / 73 630 mm⁴ ≈ 713.5 MPa (tensile on one side, compressive on the opposite side).  

Torsional shear stress at the outer surface:  
τ = T·c / J = (500 000 N·mm)(17.5 mm) / 147 261 mm⁴ ≈ 59.4 MPa.  

Combined stress state (superposition of σ_b and τ) at the outer fiber:  
Normal stress σ = σ_b, shear stress τ = 59.4 MPa.  

Principal stresses:  
σ₁ = σ_b/2 + √[(σ_b/2)² + τ²] ≈ 718.4 MPa (maximum tensile).  
σ₂ = σ_b/2 − √[(σ_b/2)² + τ²] ≈ −4.9 MPa (compressive).  
Maximum shear stress τ_max = √[(σ_b/2)² + τ²] ≈ 361.7 MPa.  

Normal strain due to bending: ε_b = σ_b / E ≈ 3.40 × 10⁻³.  
Shear strain due to torsion: γ = τ / G ≈ 7.36 × 10⁻⁴.  

Principal strains (plane‑stress relations):  
ε₁ = σ₁/E − ν·σ₂/E ≈ 3.43 × 10⁻³.  
ε₂ = σ₂/E − ν·σ₁/E ≈ −1.05 × 10⁻³.  

These governing relations and computed quantities fully describe the combined bending‑torsion response of the circular steel shaft and its principal stress/strain measures under the specified loading.
