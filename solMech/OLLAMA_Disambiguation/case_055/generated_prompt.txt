Determine the axial response of a linearly tapered bar under a static axial tensile load.

Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, constant temperature, no body forces, and that the left end (x = 0) is fixed while the tensile load P is applied at the right end (x = L).

Given data  
P = 20 000 N  
E = 69 000 MPa (69 000 N/mm²)  
L = 1 200 mm  
A₀ = 200 mm² at x = 0  
A(L) = 320 mm², i.e. the area varies linearly with slope factor k = 0.6.

Governing relations  

1. Area variation: A(x) = A₀ [1 + k x/L] for 0 ≤ x ≤ L.  
2. Axial equilibrium: axial force is constant, σ(x) A(x) = P.  
3. Constitutive law: σ(x) = E ε(x).  
4. Strain–displacement compatibility: ε(x) = du/dx, where u(x) is the axial displacement.  
5. Axial stress distribution: σ(x) = P / A(x) = P / [A₀ (1 + k x/L)].  
6. Strain distribution: ε(x) = σ(x)/E = P / [E A₀ (1 + k x/L)].  
7. Total axial deformation (displacement of the loaded end):  

 δ = u(L) = ∫₀ᴸ P / [E A(x)] dx  
  = (P / [E A₀]) · (L / k) · ln(1 + k)  

 Substituting the numerical values:  

 δ = (20 000 / [69 000 · 200]) · (1 200 / 0.6) · ln(1.6)  
 δ ≈ 1.36 mm.

8. Maximum and minimum stresses:  

 σ_max at x = 0: σ_max = P / A₀ = 20 000 / 200 = 100 MPa.  
 σ_min at x = L: σ_min = P / [A₀(1 + k)] = 20 000 / 320 = 62.5 MPa.

9. Corresponding strains:  

 ε_max = σ_max / E ≈ 100 / 69 000 = 1.45 × 10⁻³.  
 ε_min = σ_min / E ≈ 62.5 / 69 000 = 9.06 × 10⁻⁴.  

10. Average strain over the bar: ε_avg = δ / L ≈ 1.36 mm / 1 200 mm = 1.13 × 10⁻³.

Present the above governing equations, numerical results, and the stated assumptions as the complete specification for the axial deformation analysis of the tapered bar.
