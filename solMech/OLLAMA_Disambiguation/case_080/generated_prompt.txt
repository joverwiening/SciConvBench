Determine the required solid circular shaft diameter based on the angle‑of‑twist limit.

Assume:
- Small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior.
- Uniform, isotropic material with constant shear modulus G = 25 939.8 MPa.
- Pure torsion along the entire shaft length L = 1500 mm; no bending, axial, or transverse loads.
- The shaft is solid and circular with constant diameter d over its length.
- Temperature effects, residual stresses, and stress concentrations are neglected.
- Units are consistent (N·mm for torque, mm for length, MPa for G, radians for angle).

Governing relations:
1. Angle of twist for a solid circular shaft: φ = (T L) / (J G), where J = π d⁴ / 32.
2. Rearranged for diameter: d = [ (16 T L) / (π G φ) ]^(1/4).

Compute the required diameter:
- Convert allowable angle of twist to radians: φ_allow = 0.75° = 0.0131 rad.
- Substitute T = 1 000 000 N·mm, L = 1500 mm, G = 25 939.8 MPa (≈ 25 939.8 N/mm²), φ = 0.0131 rad into the diameter formula.
- Evaluate d and round up to a standard engineering size.

Present the final shaft diameter value and verify that the resulting angle of twist does not exceed φ_allow. Include a brief statement that the design satisfies the twist limitation under the given loading and material properties.
