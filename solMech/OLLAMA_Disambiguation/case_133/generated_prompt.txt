Assume a small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for the beam.  
Assume the beam is made of structural steel S355 with a yield stress of 355 MPa and adopt an allowable bending stress of 0.6 × yield = 213 MPa.  
Assume a simply supported span length L = 6 m.  
Assume a uniformly distributed vertical load w = 20 kN/m acting over the entire span.  
Assume an additional applied moment at mid‑span Mₐₚₚ = 6 kNm.  

Governing relations:  
- Maximum bending moment from the uniform load: M_w = w L² / 8.  
- Total maximum bending moment at mid‑span: M_max = M_w + Mₐₚₚ.  
- For a rectangular cross‑section of width b and height h, the second moment of area I = b h³ / 12 and the distance from the neutral axis to the extreme fiber c = h / 2.  
- Bending stress: σ = M_max c / I = 6 M_max / (b h²).  
- Design requirement: σ ≤ σ_allowable.  

Calculations:  
1. M_w = (20 kN/m × 6² m²) / 8 = 90 kNm.  
2. M_max = 90 kNm + 6 kNm = 96 kNm = 96 000 000 N·mm.  
3. Choose a practical width b = 200 mm.  
4. Solve for h from σ = 6 M_max / (b h²) ≤ 213 N/mm²:  
   h² ≥ 6 M_max / (b σ_allow) = (6 × 96 000 000) / (200 × 213) ≈ 13 521 mm².  
   h ≥ √13 521 ≈ 116 mm.  
5. Adopt the next standard height, h = 120 mm.  

Resulting design: a rectangular steel beam 200 mm wide and 120 mm deep satisfies the allowable bending stress under the assumed loading and span.  

State any additional assumptions: shear deformation is neglected, self‑weight of the beam is ignored, and the applied moment is assumed to act at mid‑span.
