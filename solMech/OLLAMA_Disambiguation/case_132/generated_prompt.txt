Assume a simply supported rectangular steel beam with a span length of 6 m, which is a common practice for medium‑scale structural members.  
Assume the beam material is structural steel (ASTM A36) with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa and yield strength σ_y = 250 MPa.  
Adopt an allowable bending stress σ_allow = 0.66 σ_y = 165 MPa, which follows typical steel design codes.  
Assume the beam width b is half of its height h (b = h/2), a conventional proportion for rectangular sections.  
The applied bending moment is M = 3 kNm (3 × 10⁶ N·mm).  
Use the linear‑elastic bending formula σ_max = M c/I, where c = h/2 and I = b h³/12.  
Substituting b = h/2 gives I = (h⁴)/24 and σ_max = 12 M/(h³).  
Set σ_max equal to the allowable stress and solve for h:  
h = (12 M/σ_allow)¹ᐟ³.  
Compute h using M = 3 × 10⁶ N·mm and σ_allow = 165 MPa (165 N/mm²):  
h = (12 × 3 × 10⁶ / 165)¹ᐟ³ ≈ 0.158 m ≈ 158 mm.  
Thus the required beam height is 158 mm, and the corresponding width is b = h/2 ≈ 79 mm.  
State that shear deformation and deflection are not considered in this design, as the focus is on allowable bending stress under small‑deformation, linear‑elastic assumptions.
