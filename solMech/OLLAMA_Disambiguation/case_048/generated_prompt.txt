Assume the shaft material is homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic, and deforms only slightly.  
Assume the applied torque is constant along the shaft length and acts about the longitudinal axis.  
Assume the shaft has a circular hollow cross‑section with outer radius rₒ = 30 mm and inner radius rᵢ = 24 mm.  

Compute the polar moment of inertia of the hollow section:  
J = (π/2)·(rₒ⁴ – rᵢ⁴).  

Compute the torsional rigidity:  
Kₜ = G·J.  

Determine the shear stress distribution over the radius r (rᵢ ≤ r ≤ rₒ):  
τ(r) = T·r / J.  

Obtain the maximum shear stress at the outer surface:  
τ_max = T·rₒ / J.  

Calculate the shear strain distribution:  
γ(r) = τ(r) / G.  

Determine the angle of twist over the shaft length L:  
θ = T·L / (G·J).  

Provide the numerical results using the given data (T = 450 000 N·mm, L = 800 mm, G = 76 923.1 MPa):  
J = (π/2)·(30⁴ – 24⁴) mm⁴ ≈ 7.51 × 10⁵ mm⁴.  
Kₜ = G·J ≈ 5.78 × 10⁷ N·mm².  
τ_max = (450 000 N·mm·30 mm) / J ≈ 1.80 × 10¹ N/mm² ≈ 18 MPa.  
θ = (450 000 N·mm·800 mm) / (G·J) ≈ 6.23 × 10⁻³ rad ≈ 0.357°.  

State that all governing relations above are valid for the specified small‑deformation, linear‑elastic torsion of a hollow circular shaft.
