Assume a prismatic, homogeneous beam of length L with constant flexural rigidity EI, made of a linear‑elastic material and undergoing small deformations.  
Adopt Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory (plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis).  
The beam is simply supported at x = 0 and x = L (pin at left, roller at right).  
A uniformly distributed transverse load of intensity w (force per unit length) acts over the entire span.  

Governing differential equation:  
EI d⁴v(x)/dx⁴ = w, where v(x) is the transverse deflection.  

Boundary conditions for a simply supported beam:  
v(0) = 0 (zero deflection at left support)  
v(L) = 0 (zero deflection at right support)  
d²v/dx² (0) = 0 (zero bending moment at left support)  
d²v/dx² (L) = 0 (zero bending moment at right support)  

Solution for deflection:  
v(x) = (w x (L³ – 2 L x² + x³)) / (24 EI)  

Slope (rotation) is the first derivative of deflection:  
θ(x) = dv/dx = (w (L³ – 2 L x² + x³)) / (24 EI) · (1 – 4x/L + 3x²/L²)  

Relevant response quantities:  
Maximum deflection occurs at mid‑span (x = L/2):  
v_max = (5 w L⁴) / (384 EI)  

Slope at the supports (x = 0 and x = L):  
θ(0) = –θ(L) = (w L³) / (24 EI)  

These expressions provide the required deflection and slope for the simply supported beam under a uniform load, given the beam’s material modulus E, moment of inertia I, length L, and load intensity w.
