Assume a prismatic Euler‑Bernoulli beam of length L = 3000 mm, simply supported at both ends, made of a linear‑elastic, isotropic material with constant flexural rigidity EI.  
Assume small deformations and that shear deformation is negligible.  

Treat the loading as the superposition of:  
1. A uniformly distributed transverse load w = 0.3 N/mm acting over the entire span.  
2. A concentrated downward point load P = 3000 N applied at the midspan (x = L/2).  

Use the governing differential equation for beam deflection:  
EI d⁴v/dx⁴ = q(x), where q(x) is the applied transverse load per unit length (positive downward).  

For the uniform load case, integrate the equation with the simply supported boundary conditions v(0)=v(L)=0 and bending moment M(0)=M(L)=0 to obtain the deflection curve:  
v_w(x) = (w x (L³ − 2L x² + x³)) / (24 EI).  

For the point load case, use the standard simply supported beam solution:  
v_P(x) = (P x (L² − x²)) / (16 EI L) for 0 ≤ x ≤ L/2, and  
v_P(x) = (P (L − x) (L² − (L − x)²)) / (16 EI L) for L/2 ≤ x ≤ L.  

Obtain the total deflection by superposition:  
v_total(x) = v_w(x) + v_P(x).  

Determine the following response quantities:  
- The reaction forces at the supports for each load case and for the combined loading.  
- The bending moment distribution M(x) for each load case and the combined case (M(x) = EI d²v/dx²).  
- The slope (rotation) θ(x) = dv/dx for each load case and the combined case.  
- The maximum deflection of the beam, which occurs at midspan (x = L/2); compute v_max = v_total(L/2).  
- The location and magnitude of the maximum bending moment.  

Present all expressions in closed form, clearly indicating the dependence on L, w, P, and EI.  

State that the results are valid only under the stated assumptions and that any variation in EI, cross‑section, or support conditions would require re‑derivation.
