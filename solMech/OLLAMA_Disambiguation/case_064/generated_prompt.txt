Determine the bending stress distribution in a rectangular beam with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm subjected to a bending moment M = 3 000 000 N·mm, assuming small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior.  
Assume the beam is prismatic, the material is homogeneous and isotropic, and plane sections remain plane after bending (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory).  
Take the origin of the y‑coordinate at the centroid of the cross‑section, positive upward, and the neutral axis coincident with the centroidal axis.  
Compute the second moment of area about the neutral axis: I = b h³ / 12 = (50 mm)(100 mm)³ / 12 = 4.1667 × 10⁶ mm⁴.  
For any fiber located at a distance y from the neutral axis, calculate the bending stress using σ(y) = M·y / I.  
State that tensile stress is positive for y > 0 (above the neutral axis) and compressive stress is negative for y < 0 (below the neutral axis).  
Provide the expression for the maximum bending stress at the extreme fibers (y = ±h/2): σ_max = M·(h/2) / I = 3 000 000 N·mm · 50 mm / 4.1667 × 10⁶ mm⁴ = 36 MPa.  
Summarize that the bending stress varies linearly with y across the depth of the beam, reaching ±36 MPa at the top and bottom surfaces.
