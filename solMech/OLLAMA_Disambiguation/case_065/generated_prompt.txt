Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid circular shaft of constant radius R and length L, subjected to a pure torque T (and optionally a transverse shear force V). Small deformations and Saint‑Venant’s principle apply.  
The material is characterized by its shear modulus G (modulus of rigidity).  
The polar moment of inertia of the circular cross‑section is J = π R⁴ / 2 and the cross‑sectional area is A = π R².  

The shear stress distribution due to the torque is τ(r) = T r / J, where r is the radial distance from the shaft axis (0 ≤ r ≤ R). The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface: τ_max = T R / J.  

The angle of twist per unit length is dθ/dx = T / (G J). For a shaft of length L the total angle of twist is θ = T L / (G J).  

If a transverse shear force V is also applied, the average shear stress from V is τ_V = 4 V / (3 A), and the corresponding shear deformation can be superposed with the torsional deformation.  

Neglect temperature effects, stress concentrations, dynamic effects, and any axial loads.
