Assume the beam material is homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic and obeys small‑deformation theory.  
The beam has a rectangular cross‑section of width b = 40 mm and height h = 80 mm.  
A vertical shear force V = 25 000 N is applied at the section of interest (e.g., mid‑span).  

Compute the cross‑sectional area: A = b h.  

Average transverse shear stress: τ_avg = V / A.  

For a rectangular section, the transverse shear stress distribution across the height is  
τ(y) = (3/2) τ_avg [1 − (2y/h)²], where y = 0 at the neutral axis and y = ±h/2 at the outer fibers.  

Maximum shear stress occurs at the neutral axis (y = 0):  
τ_max = (3/2) τ_avg = 1.5 V/(b h).  

Numerical evaluation:  
A = 40 mm × 80 mm = 3200 mm² = 3.2 × 10⁻³ m².  
τ_avg = 25 000 N / 3.2 × 10⁻³ m² = 7.8125 MPa.  
τ_max = 1.5 × 7.8125 MPa = 11.7188 MPa.  

State that shear stress is zero at the top and bottom surfaces (y = ±h/2) and varies parabolically between these points.  

If shear strain or deflection is later required, the shear modulus G (or Young’s modulus E and Poisson’s ratio ν) must be supplied; it is not needed for the stress calculation.
