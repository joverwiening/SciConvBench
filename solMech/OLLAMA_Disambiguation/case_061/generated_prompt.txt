Assume a homogeneous, isotropic structural steel column with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.3 (typical for steel).  
Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior and that the column is short enough that global buckling can be neglected; only axial and bending effects due to eccentricity are considered.  
Assume the eccentric load is applied in the width direction, i.e., the eccentricity e = 20 mm is measured from the centroid along the b‑axis.  

Given data:  
- Width of rectangular cross‑section b = 60 mm.  
- Height of rectangular cross‑section h = 120 mm.  
- Axial load magnitude P = 60 000 N.  
- Eccentricity e = 20 mm.  

Compute the following response quantities:  

1. Cross‑sectional area A = b · h.  
2. Second moment of area about the centroidal axis parallel to the height (bending about the width direction) I = (b · h³) / 12.  
3. Axial stress due to the direct load σₐ = P / A.  
4. Bending moment caused by the eccentricity M = P · e.  
5. Maximum distance from the neutral axis to the extreme fiber y_max = h / 2.  
6. Bending stress at the extreme fiber σ_b = M · y_max / I.  
7. Combined normal stress at the extreme fiber σ_max = σₐ + σ_b (tension side) and σ_min = σₐ – σ_b (compression side).  
8. Axial strain εₐ = σₐ / E.  
9. Bending strain at the extreme fiber ε_b = σ_b / E.  
10. Total normal strain at the extreme fiber ε_total = εₐ ± ε_b.  

Present the governing relations clearly:  

- A = b h.  
- I = (b h³) / 12.  
- σₐ = P / A.  
- M = P e.  
- σ_b = M y / I, with y = ±h/2 for the extreme fibers.  
- σ = σₐ + σ_b (tension side) or σ = σₐ – σ_b (compression side).  
- ε = σ / E.  

These equations provide all required response quantities for the eccentric axial loading of the rectangular column.
