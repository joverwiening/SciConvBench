Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid circular shaft subjected to pure torsion, with small deformations and constant cross‑section along its length. Neglect temperature effects, axial loads, and any stress concentrations.

Given:
- Diameter d = 35 mm, radius r = 17.5 mm.
- Applied torque T = 250 000 N·mm.
- Length L = 400 mm.
- Shear modulus G = 77 519.4 MPa.

Compute the polar moment of inertia:
J = π d⁴ / 32 = π (35 mm)⁴ / 32 = 1.18 × 10⁶ mm⁴.

Shear stress distribution (maximum at the outer surface):
τ_max = T · c / J, where c = r = 17.5 mm.
τ_max = 250 000 N·mm · 17.5 mm / 1.18 × 10⁶ mm⁴ = 3.71 MPa.

Angle of twist over the shaft length:
θ = T · L / (J · G).
θ = 250 000 N·mm · 400 mm / (1.18 × 10⁶ mm⁴ · 77 519.4 MPa) = 1.10 × 10⁻³ rad ≈ 0.063°.

Present the governing relations:
- Polar moment of inertia: J = π d⁴ / 32.
- Maximum shear stress: τ_max = T c / J.
- Angle of twist: θ = T L / (J G).

State that the results are valid only under the listed assumptions.
