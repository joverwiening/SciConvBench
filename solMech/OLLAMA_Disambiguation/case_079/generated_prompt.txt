Assume a uniform, solid, circular shaft made of AISI 1020 steel, operating at room temperature, with small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior, and no stress concentrations or residual stresses.  
Use the shear modulus G = 76 923.1 MPa ( = 76.9231 N·mm⁻²·10⁻³).  

Governing relations for a solid circular shaft:  

1. Polar moment of inertia: J = π d⁴ / 32.  
2. Angle of twist: φ = (T L) / (J G).  
3. Maximum shear stress: τ_max = T c / J = 16 T / (π d³), where c = d/2.  

Design equation from the allowable angle of twist φ_allow:  

φ_allow = (T L) / (J G) → substitute J → φ_allow = (16 T L) / (π G d⁴).  

Solve for the required shaft diameter d:  

d = [ (16 T L) / (π G φ_allow) ]^(1/4).  

Insert the given data (convert all to consistent units, N·mm, mm, MPa):  

- T = 600 000 N·mm  
- L = 800 mm  
- G = 76 923.1 MPa = 76 923.1 N·mm⁻²·10⁻³ = 76 923.1 N·mm⁻²·10⁻³ (use N·mm⁻² directly)  
- φ_allow = 0.0262 rad  

Compute:  

d = [ (16 × 600 000 N·mm × 800 mm) / (π × 76 923.1 N·mm⁻² × 0.0262) ]^(1/4)  

d ≈ 45.3 mm (rounded to the nearest 0.1 mm).  

Resulting response quantities:  

- Required shaft diameter: d ≈ 45.3 mm.  
- Polar moment of inertia: J = π d⁴ / 32 ≈ 2.86 × 10⁶ mm⁴.  
- Maximum shear stress under the applied torque: τ_max = 16 T / (π d³) ≈ 13.5 MPa.  
- Actual angle of twist with the designed diameter: φ = (T L) / (J G) ≈ 0.0262 rad = 1.5°, meeting the limit.  

These equations and the computed diameter satisfy the design requirement based on the specified angle‑of‑twist limit.
