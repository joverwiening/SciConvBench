Assume a homogeneous, isotropic, linear‑elastic solid with small deformations.  
The shaft is a hollow circular cylinder with outer radius \(r_o = 25\ \text{mm}\) and inner radius \(r_i = 21\ \text{mm}\).  
Applied torque magnitude \(T = 450{,}000\ \text{N·mm}\).  
Shear modulus \(G = 77{,}519.4\ \text{N/mm}^2\).  
Length of the shaft \(L = 1{,}200\ \text{mm}\).

Governing relations and computed response quantities:

1. Polar moment of inertia of a hollow circular shaft:  
   \(J = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\bigl(r_o^{4} - r_i^{4}\bigr)\).  
   Substituting the radii gives \(J = 3.08 \times 10^{5}\ \text{mm}^{4}\) (≈ \(3.08 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}^{4}\)).

2. Torsional rigidity:  
   \(GJ = G \times J = 7.7519 \times 10^{4}\ \text{N/mm}^{2} \times 3.08 \times 10^{5}\ \text{mm}^{4} = 2.39 \times 10^{10}\ \text{N·mm}^{2}\).

3. Shear stress distribution over the radius:  
   \(\tau(r) = \dfrac{T\,r}{J}\).  
   Maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface:  
   \(\tau_{\max} = \dfrac{T\,r_o}{J} = \dfrac{450{,}000\ \text{N·mm} \times 25\ \text{mm}}{3.08 \times 10^{5}\ \text{mm}^{4}} \approx 36.5\ \text{MPa}\).

4. Angle of twist over the shaft length:  
   \(\phi = \dfrac{T\,L}{GJ}\).  
   Substituting the values gives \(\phi = \dfrac{450{,}000 \times 1{,}200}{2.39 \times 10^{10}} \approx 0.0226\ \text{rad}\) (≈ 1.29°).

5. Shear strain at any radius:  
   \(\gamma(r) = \dfrac{\tau(r)}{G}\).  
   At the outer surface, \(\gamma_{\max} = \tau_{\max}/G \approx 36.5\ \text{MPa} / 77{,}519.4\ \text{MPa} = 4.71 \times 10^{-4}\) (dimensionless).

6. Torsional shear stress can also be expressed using the torsional constant \(J\) and torque:  
   \(\tau_{\max} = \dfrac{16\,T}{\pi\,(r_o^{4} - r_i^{4})}\), which yields the same numerical result as in (3).

These equations and numerical results fully describe the torsional response of the given hollow circular shaft under the specified loading.
