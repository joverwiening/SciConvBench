Assume the rod behaves as a small‑deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic solid until the yield stress is reached.  
Take the cross‑section to be uniform and the load to be applied axially and centrally, producing a pure axial stress state.  

Compute the cross‑sectional area:  
\(A = \pi d^{2}/4 = \pi (25\text{ mm})^{2}/4 = 490.9\text{ mm}^{2}\).  

Determine the average normal stress:  
\(\sigma = P/A = 20\,000\text{ N} / 490.9\text{ mm}^{2} = 40.7\text{ MPa}\).  

Obtain the axial strain using Hooke’s law:  
\(\varepsilon = \sigma / E = 40.7\text{ MPa} / 200\,000\text{ MPa} = 2.04\times10^{-4}\).  

Calculate the elongation over the gage length:  
\(\Delta L = \varepsilon L = 2.04\times10^{-4} \times 800\text{ mm} = 0.163\text{ mm}\).  

Evaluate the factor of safety with respect to yielding:  
\(FS = \sigma_{y} / \sigma = 250\text{ MPa} / 40.7\text{ MPa} = 6.14\).  

State additional assumptions:  
- Temperature effects are negligible.  
- No residual stresses are present.  
- The material remains homogeneous and isotropic throughout the rod.  
- End effects are ignored; the stress is uniform over the cross‑section.  

Present the governing relations used:  
\(A = \pi d^{2}/4\).  
\(\sigma = P/A\).  
\(\varepsilon = \sigma/E\).  
\(\Delta L = \varepsilon L\).  
\(FS = \sigma_{y}/\sigma\).
