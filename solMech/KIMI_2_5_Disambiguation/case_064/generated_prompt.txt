The beam is modeled using small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.

The material is assumed to be structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.

The applied bending moment is M = 3,000,000 N·mm (3 kN·m) in the sagging direction. The analysis is performed at a specific cross-section where this moment is known, consistent with standard section-based stress analysis in beam theory.

The coordinate system has the longitudinal beam axis as the x-axis. The transverse coordinate y is measured upward from the neutral axis (centroid), with y = 0 at the neutral axis. The top fiber is located at y = +50 mm and the bottom fiber at y = –50 mm.

The governing relation for bending stress is the flexure formula: σ_x(y) = –M·y / I, where I is the second moment of area about the neutral axis.

For the rectangular cross-section, I = b·h³ / 12 = (50 mm)·(100 mm)³ / 12 = 4,166,667 mm⁴.

The bending stress at arbitrary fiber location y is σ_x(y) = –(3,000,000 N·mm)·y / (4,166,667 mm⁴) = –0.72·y MPa (with y in mm).

At the top fiber (y = +50 mm): σ_x = –36 MPa (compressive).

At the bottom fiber (y = –50 mm): σ_x = +36 MPa (tensile).

The maximum bending stress magnitude is 36 MPa, occurring at the extreme fibers.

The neutral axis (y = 0) experiences zero bending stress.

The stress distribution is linear through the depth, varying from maximum compression at the top to maximum tension at the bottom.
