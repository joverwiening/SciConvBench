The column is made of structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The column length is L = 2000 mm.
The column diameter is d = 20 mm.
The column is assumed to be a solid circular cross-section.
The column is subjected to compressive axial loading.
The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The analysis assumes Euler buckling theory applies (slender column behavior).
The column is assumed to be pinned-pinned (effective length factor K = 1.0) unless specified otherwise.
The moment of inertia for a solid circular cross-section is I = πd^4 / 64.
The cross-sectional area is A = πd^2 / 4.
The radius of gyration is r = sqrt(I/A) = d/4.
The slenderness ratio is λ = KL / r.
The critical Euler buckling load is P_cr = π^2 E I / (KL)^2.
The allowable column load is P_allow = P_cr / FS, where FS is the factor of safety against buckling.
The factor of safety with respect to buckling is FS = P_cr / P_applied for a given applied load.
For design purposes, a minimum factor of safety of FS = 2.0 to 3.0 is typically required for buckling-critical members.
The governing Euler buckling relation is P_cr = π^2 E I / L_e^2, where L_e = KL is the effective length.
The critical buckling stress is σ_cr = P_cr / A = π^2 E / (KL/r)^2.
The column is considered slender if KL/r > sqrt(2π^2 E / σ_y), where σ_y is the yield strength.
