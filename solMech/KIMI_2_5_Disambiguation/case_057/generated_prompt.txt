The column is a fixed-fixed Euler buckling column with length L = 3000 mm, moment of inertia I = 2500000 mm^4, made of 6061-T6 aluminum with Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

Assume the column is prismatic with constant cross-section along its length.

Assume the applied compressive load is axial and concentric.

Assume no initial imperfections or residual stresses are present in the column.

Assume the fixed-fixed end conditions provide complete restraint against rotation and translation at both ends.

The effective length factor for fixed-fixed end conditions is K = 0.5.

The effective length of the column is Le = K × L = 0.5 × 3000 mm = 1500 mm.

The Euler critical buckling load is Pcr = π² × E × I / Le².

Substituting values: Pcr = π² × 69000 MPa × 2500000 mm^4 / (1500 mm)².

Calculate Pcr = π² × 69000 × 2500000 / 2250000 N = 757947.5 N ≈ 758 kN.

The critical buckling stress is σcr = Pcr / A, where A is the cross-sectional area.

The cross-sectional area A is not specified and cannot be determined from I alone without knowing the cross-sectional shape.

For a buckling/instability check, compare the applied compressive load Papplied against Pcr.

If Papplied < Pcr, the column is stable against elastic buckling.

If Papplied ≥ Pcr, the column will buckle elastically.

The safety factor against buckling is SF = Pcr / Papplied.

The slenderness ratio is λ = Le / r, where r = √(I/A) is the radius of gyration.

Without A, the slenderness ratio and critical buckling stress cannot be evaluated numerically.

If the critical buckling stress σcr exceeds the material yield strength σy, inelastic buckling may govern instead of elastic Euler buckling.

For 6061-T6 aluminum, typical yield strength is σy ≈ 276 MPa, though this should be verified for the specific temper and product form.

The governing relation for Euler buckling is the differential equation: EI × (d²w/dx²) + P × w = 0, where w is the lateral deflection.

The boundary conditions for fixed-fixed ends are w(0) = 0, w(L) = 0, dw/dx(0) = 0, dw/dx(L) = 0.

The lowest eigenvalue solution gives the critical buckling load with effective length factor K = 0.5.
