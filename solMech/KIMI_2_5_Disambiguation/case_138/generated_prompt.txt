The prismatic bar has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 120 mm, subjected to normal force P = 30000 N (tension) and transverse shear force V = 12000 N (acting parallel to width b).

Assumptions:
Saint-Venant's principle applies so that stress distributions are evaluated far from load application points.
Plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
Material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Small deformations apply so linear superposition is valid.
The transverse shear force V acts through the shear center so no torsion occurs.
The normal force P is applied at the centroid of the cross-section so no bending moment is induced.

Cross-sectional properties:
Cross-sectional area A = b × h = 40 mm × 120 mm = 4800 mm².
First moment of area Q_max about the neutral axis for the full section = (b × h/2) × (h/4) = b × h² / 8 = 40 × 120² / 8 = 72000 mm³.
Moment of inertia I = b × h³ / 12 = 40 × 120³ / 12 = 5.76 × 10⁶ mm⁴.

Average normal stress:
The average normal stress due to axial tension is uniform across the section.
σ_avg = P / A = 30000 N / 4800 mm² = 6.25 MPa (tension).

Average shear stress:
The average shear stress is defined as the total shear force divided by the cross-sectional area.
τ_avg = V / A = 12000 N / 4800 mm² = 2.5 MPa.

Maximum transverse shear stress (for reference):
The maximum transverse shear stress occurs at the neutral axis for a rectangular section.
τ_max = V × Q_max / (I × b) = (3/2) × (V / A) = 1.5 × τ_avg = 3.75 MPa.

Governing relations:
Normal stress from axial load: σ = P / A.
Shear stress from transverse load: τ(y) = V × Q(y) / (I × b).
Average shear stress: τ_avg = V / A.
Maximum shear stress for rectangle: τ_max = 3V / (2A).
