The cantilever beam has length L = 800 mm.

The beam is fixed at one end and free at the other end.

A downward point load P = 2500 N is applied at the free end.

There is no applied moment at the free end.

The beam material has Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa.

The beam cross-section has moment of inertia I = 1600000 mm^4.

The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The analysis uses Euler-Bernoulli beam theory.

The governing differential equation for beam deflection is EI(d^4w/dx^4) = q(x), where for this case q(x) = 0 except at the free end.

The boundary conditions are: at x = 0 (fixed end), w = 0 and dw/dx = 0; at x = L (free end), EI(d^2w/dx^2) = 0 and EI(d^3w/dx^3) = -P.

The maximum deflection occurs at the free end and equals PL^3/(3EI).

The maximum deflection value is (2500 N)(800 mm)^3 / (3 × 69000 MPa × 1600000 mm^4) = 3.86 mm downward.

The slope at the free end equals PL^2/(2EI) = 0.00725 rad = 0.415 degrees.

The bending moment distribution is M(x) = -P(L-x), varying linearly from -PL at the fixed end to zero at the free end.

The maximum bending moment magnitude is PL = 2.0 × 10^6 N·mm = 2.0 × 10^3 N·m at the fixed end.

The shear force is constant at V = -P = -2500 N throughout the beam.

The maximum bending stress occurs at the fixed end and equals Mc/I, where c is the distance from neutral axis to outer fiber.
