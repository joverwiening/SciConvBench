The shaft is a solid circular shaft made of structural steel with diameter d = 35 mm.

The shaft is subjected only to a torque T = 250000 N·mm with no applied shear force.

The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

Additional assumptions are: the shaft is prismatic with constant circular cross-section, the material is homogeneous and isotropic, plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the axis (Saint-Venant's principle), and the shaft is in pure torsion with no axial or bending loads.

The polar moment of inertia for a solid circular cross-section is J = πd⁴/32.

The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer radius r = d/2 and is given by τ_max = Tr/J = 16T/(πd³).

The angle of twist per unit length is dφ/dx = T/(GJ), where G is the shear modulus of structural steel, typically taken as G = 79 GPa or 79000 N/mm².

The total angle of twist over a length L is φ = TL/(GJ) = 32TL/(Gπd⁴).

For structural steel, use G = 79000 N/mm² unless a different value is specified.

The shear stress varies linearly from zero at the center to τ_max at the outer surface: τ(r) = Tr/J for 0 ≤ r ≤ d/2.
