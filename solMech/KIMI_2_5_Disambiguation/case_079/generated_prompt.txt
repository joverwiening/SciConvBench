Design a solid circular shaft diameter based on an angle-of-twist limit under pure torsion loading.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

Assume the shaft is homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic with constant circular cross-section.

Assume the torque is applied uniformly and the shaft is in pure torsion with no bending or axial loading.

The applied torque is T = 600000 N·mm.

The total allowable angle of twist is φ_allow = 1.5° over shaft length L = 800 mm.

The shear modulus of the shaft material is G = 76923.1 MPa = 76923.1 N/mm².

Convert the allowable angle of twist to radians: φ_allow = 1.5° × (π/180) = 0.02618 rad.

The governing relation for angle of twist in a circular shaft is φ = TL/(GJ), where J is the polar moment of inertia.

For a solid circular cross-section, J = πd⁴/32, where d is the shaft diameter.

Rearrange to solve for required J: J_req = TL/(G × φ_allow).

Substitute values: J_req = (600000 N·mm)(800 mm) / (76923.1 N/mm² × 0.02618 rad) = 238732.4 mm⁴.

Solve for required diameter: d = (32 × J_req / π)^(1/4) = (32 × 238732.4 / π)^(1/4) = 22.36 mm.

The minimum solid shaft diameter required to satisfy the angle-of-twist limit is d = 22.4 mm (or specify d = 23 mm for practical sizing with safety margin).
