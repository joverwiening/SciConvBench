The analysis shall determine the stress distribution in a short rectangular column subjected to an eccentric axial load.

Assume the column is short such that secondary bending moments due to lateral deflection (P-delta effects) are negligible and buckling does not occur.

The rectangular cross-section has width b = 100 mm and height h = 100 mm.

The eccentric axial load has magnitude P = 90000 N.

The eccentricity is e = 15 mm, acting perpendicular to the centroidal axis and parallel to one of the principal axes (assume bending about the strong axis, with eccentricity in the direction of height h).

The cross-sectional area is A = b × h = 100 mm × 100 mm = 10000 mm².

The moment of inertia about the bending axis is I = b × h³ / 12 = 100 mm × (100 mm)³ / 12 = 8.333 × 10⁶ mm⁴.

The section modulus is S = I / (h/2) = 8.333 × 10⁶ mm⁴ / 50 mm = 1.667 × 10⁵ mm³.

The direct axial stress is σ_axial = P / A = 90000 N / 10000 mm² = 9 MPa (tensile).

The bending moment induced by eccentricity is M = P × e = 90000 N × 15 mm = 1.35 × 10⁶ N·mm.

The maximum bending stress is σ_bending = M / S = 1.35 × 10⁶ N·mm / 1.667 × 10⁵ mm³ = 8.1 MPa.

The maximum tensile stress occurs at the fiber farthest from the eccentric load: σ_tensile_max = σ_axial + σ_bending = 9 MPa + 8.1 MPa = 17.1 MPa.

The maximum compressive stress occurs at the opposite fiber: σ_compressive_max = σ_axial - σ_bending = 9 MPa - 8.1 MPa = 0.9 MPa (tensile, since positive).

The neutral axis shift from the centroid is determined by setting total stress to zero: y_na = -I / (A × e) = -8.333 × 10⁶ mm⁴ / (10000 mm² × 15 mm) = -55.56 mm from centroid, or 5.56 mm from the edge on the compression side.

The governing relation for combined axial and bending stress is σ = P/A + (P×e×y)/I, where y is measured from the centroidal axis.
