The simulation analyzes maximum stress in a prismatic bar under suddenly applied axial load using energy methods.

Assume the bar has uniform mass distribution with mass per unit length m = ρA, where ρ = 7850 kg/m³ for steel, giving m = 2.355 kg/m.

The bar has length L = 800 mm.

The bar has constant cross-sectional area A = 300 mm².

The bar material has Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The suddenly applied axial load has magnitude P = 8000 N.

Assume the bar is initially at rest and unstressed.

Assume the load is applied instantaneously and remains constant thereafter.

Assume no damping is present in the system.

Assume the bar behaves as a spring-mass system with equivalent stiffness k = EA/L.

Assume the static displacement under load P is δ_st = PL/(EA).

Assume the suddenly applied load produces dynamic amplification with maximum displacement δ_max = 2δ_st.

Assume the maximum dynamic stress equals the stress corresponding to maximum dynamic displacement.

The equivalent spring stiffness is k = EA/L = (200000 MPa)(300 mm²)/(800 mm) = 75000 N/mm.

The static displacement is δ_st = P/k = (8000 N)/(75000 N/mm) = 0.1067 mm.

The maximum dynamic displacement is δ_max = 2δ_st = 0.2133 mm.

The maximum dynamic stress is σ_max = E(δ_max/L) = (200000 MPa)(0.2133 mm)/(800 mm) = 53.33 MPa.

Alternatively, the maximum dynamic stress is σ_max = 2σ_st = 2P/A = 2(8000 N)/(300 mm²) = 53.33 MPa.

The dynamic load factor for suddenly applied load is DLF = 2.
