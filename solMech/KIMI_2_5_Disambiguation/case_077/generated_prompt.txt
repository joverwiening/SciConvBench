The prismatic bar has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.

The bar is subjected to combined loading: an axial tension force P = 40000 N and a transverse shear force V = 15000 N acting parallel to the width b.

The cross-sectional area is A = b * h = 50 mm * 120 mm = 6000 mm².

The average normal stress due to axial tension is sigma_avg = P / A = 40000 N / 6000 mm² = 6.67 MPa (tensile).

The first moment of area Q_max for the rectangular section, evaluated at the neutral axis, is Q_max = (b * h/2) * (h/4) = b * h² / 8 = 50 mm * (120 mm)² / 8 = 90000 mm³.

The moment of inertia about the neutral axis is I = b * h³ / 12 = 50 mm * (120 mm)³ / 12 = 7.2 × 10⁶ mm⁴.

The thickness at the neutral axis is t = b = 50 mm.

The maximum transverse shear stress occurs at the neutral axis and is tau_max = V * Q_max / (I * t) = 15000 N * 90000 mm³ / (7.2 × 10⁶ mm⁴ * 50 mm) = 3.75 MPa.

The average transverse shear stress is defined as tau_avg = V / A = 15000 N / 6000 mm² = 2.5 MPa.

For design purposes, the maximum transverse shear stress in a rectangular section is tau_max = 1.5 * tau_avg = 3.75 MPa, which matches the calculation above.

The governing relations are: sigma_avg = P / A for average normal stress, and tau_avg = V / A for average shear stress, with tau_max = 3V / (2A) = 1.5 * tau_avg for maximum transverse shear stress in a rectangular section.

Additional assumptions include: plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory), isotropic homogeneous material, small deformations, linear elastic behavior, and that the transverse shear stress distribution follows the parabolic formula for rectangular sections.
