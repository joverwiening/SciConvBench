The circular shaft is subjected to combined bending and torsion loading under small-deformation, linear-elastic conditions.

The bending moment magnitude is M = 3000000 N·mm.

The torsional moment (torque) magnitude is T = 500000 N·mm.

The shaft diameter is d = 35 mm.

The material is assumed to be structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.

The yield strength is assumed to be Sy = 250 MPa for safety evaluation.

The shaft is assumed to be solid, homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic with no stress concentrations.

The loading is assumed to be static and applied at the critical cross-section where combined effects are maximum.

The governing relations for stress analysis are as follows.

The bending stress at the outer fiber is σ = 32M/(πd³).

The torsional shear stress at the outer surface is τ = 16T/(πd³).

The principal stresses are σ₁,₂ = (σ/2) ± √[(σ/2)² + τ²].

The maximum in-plane shear stress is τ_max = √[(σ/2)² + τ²].

The von Mises equivalent stress is σ_vm = √(σ² + 3τ²).

The principal strains are ε₁ = (1/E)[σ₁ - νσ₂] and ε₂ = (1/E)[σ₂ - νσ₁].

The maximum shear strain is γ_max = 2(1+ν)τ_max/E.

Numerical evaluation yields the following results.

The bending stress is σ = 71.3 MPa.

The torsional shear stress is τ = 11.9 MPa.

The principal stresses are σ₁ = 73.3 MPa and σ₂ = -2.0 MPa.

The maximum in-plane shear stress is τ_max = 37.6 MPa.

The von Mises equivalent stress is σ_vm = 73.2 MPa.

The safety factor against yielding is SF = Sy/σ_vm = 3.4.

The principal strains are ε₁ = 369 με and ε₂ = -125 με.

The maximum shear strain is γ_max = 494 με.
