The cantilever beam has length L = 1500 mm with rectangular cross-section having moment of inertia I = 2500000 mm^4.

The material is AISI 1020 steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.

A downward point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end. No bending moment is applied at the free end.

The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The strain energy U in a beam due to bending is given by U = integral from 0 to L of (M^2 / 2EI) dx.

For a cantilever with end load P, the bending moment at position x is M(x) = -P(L - x).

By Castigliano's second theorem, the deflection at the point of load application equals the partial derivative of strain energy with respect to that load.

The end deflection delta is delta = partial U / partial P = integral from 0 to L of (M / EI) * (partial M / partial P) dx.

Since partial M / partial P = -(L - x), the deflection becomes delta = integral from 0 to L of [P(L - x)^2 / EI] dx.

Evaluating the integral yields delta = P*L^3 / (3*E*I).

Substituting values: delta = (500 N)*(1500 mm)^3 / (3 * 200000 MPa * 2500000 mm^4) = 1.125 mm downward.
