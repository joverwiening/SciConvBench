The simply supported beam analysis is governed by the Euler-Bernoulli beam theory under small-deformation, linear-elastic assumptions.

The governing differential equation for beam deflection is:
EI * d^4v/dx^4 = w

For a simply supported beam with coordinate x measured from the left support (0 ≤ x ≤ L), the boundary conditions are:
v(0) = 0
v(L) = 0
M(0) = EI * d^2v/dx^2|_{x=0} = 0
M(L) = EI * d^2v/dx^2|_{x=L} = 0

The general solution for deflection under uniform distributed load w is:
v(x) = (w*x)/(24*EI) * (L^3 - 2*L*x^2 + x^3)

The slope is obtained by differentiation:
θ(x) = dv/dx = (w)/(24*EI) * (L^3 - 6*L*x^2 + 4*x^3)

Maximum deflection occurs at midspan (x = L/2):
v_max = (5*w*L^4)/(384*EI)

Maximum slope occurs at the supports. The slope magnitude is equal at both ends:
θ_max = (w*L^3)/(24*EI)

Numerical values for the given parameters:
L = 3500 mm
w = 0.5 N/mm
E = 200000 MPa = 200000 N/mm^2
I = 3000000 mm^4
EI = 6.0 × 10^11 N·mm^2

Maximum deflection at midspan:
v_max = (5 * 0.5 * 3500^4) / (384 * 6.0 × 10^11) = 15.27 mm

Maximum slope at supports:
θ_max = (0.5 * 3500^3) / (24 * 6.0 × 10^11) = 0.01491 rad = 0.854 degrees

The deflection curve is symmetric about midspan with maximum downward displacement at center. The slope is zero at midspan and maximum (equal magnitude, opposite sign) at the two supports.
