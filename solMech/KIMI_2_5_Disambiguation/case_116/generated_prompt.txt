The problem involves a hollow circular shaft under pure torsion. The following assumptions are made in addition to the stated small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics framework: the shaft is prismatic with constant cross-section, the material is homogeneous and isotropic, and plane sections remain plane (no warping restraint).

The governing relations for torsion of a hollow circular shaft are as follows.

The polar moment of inertia for a hollow circular cross-section is J = (π/2) * (ro^4 - ri^4).

The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer radius and is given by τ_max = T * ro / J.

The angle of twist over the shaft length is φ = T * L / (G * J).

The torsional stiffness is k_t = G * J / L.

For the given values: ri = 22 mm, ro = 25 mm, T = 600000 N·mm, L = 800 mm, and G = 77519.4 MPa.

Calculate J = (π/2) * (25^4 - 22^4) mm^4 = (π/2) * (390625 - 234256) mm^4 = (π/2) * 156369 mm^4 = 245449.0 mm^4.

Calculate τ_max = (600000 N·mm * 25 mm) / 245449.0 mm^4 = 61.11 MPa.

Calculate φ = (600000 N·mm * 800 mm) / (77519.4 MPa * 245449.0 mm^4) = 0.02524 rad = 1.447 degrees.

Calculate k_t = (77519.4 MPa * 245449.0 mm^4) / 800 mm = 2.378 × 10^7 N·mm/rad.
