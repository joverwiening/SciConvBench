The problem involves a hollow circular shaft under pure torsion with small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics assumptions.

The inner radius is ri = 15 mm.

The outer radius is ro = 20 mm.

The applied torque is T = 300000 N·mm.

The loading is purely torsional with no applied shear force.

The shear modulus is G = 39179.1 MPa.

Additional assumptions include: the shaft is prismatic with constant cross-section, the material is homogeneous and isotropic, plane sections remain plane (no warping restraint), and the shaft is sufficiently long that end effects are negligible.

The polar moment of inertia for the hollow circular cross-section is J = (π/2) * (ro^4 - ri^4) = (π/2) * (20^4 - 15^4) = (π/2) * (160000 - 50625) = (π/2) * 109375 = 171805.4 mm^4.

The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer radius and is calculated from τ_max = T * ro / J = 300000 * 20 / 171805.4 = 34.92 MPa.

The minimum shear stress occurs at the inner radius and is τ_min = T * ri / J = 300000 * 15 / 171805.4 = 26.19 MPa.

The angle of twist per unit length is dθ/dx = T / (G * J) = 300000 / (39179.1 * 171805.4) = 4.46 × 10^-5 rad/mm = 0.0446 rad/m.

For a shaft of length L, the total angle of twist is θ = T * L / (G * J).
