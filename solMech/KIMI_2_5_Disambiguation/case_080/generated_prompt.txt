The problem is to design a solid circular shaft diameter based on an angle-of-twist limit under pure torsion.

Assumptions:
The shaft is prismatic with constant circular cross-section.
The material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linear-elastic.
Small deformations apply so that linear torsion theory is valid.
Plane sections remain plane and radial lines remain straight (St. Venant torsion theory).
The shaft is subjected to pure torque with no bending or axial loading.

Governing relations:
The angle of twist for a uniform circular shaft under constant torque is given by φ = TL/(GJ), where J is the polar moment of inertia.
For a solid circular cross-section, J = πd⁴/32, where d is the diameter.
The allowable angle of twist constraint requires φ ≤ φ_allow.

Design equation:
Substituting J into the twist equation and solving for the required diameter gives d = [32TL/(πGφ_allow)]^(1/4).

Numerical values to substitute:
T = 1000000 N·mm
L = 1500 mm
G = 25939.8 MPa = 25939.8 N/mm²
φ_allow = 0.75° = 0.75 × π/180 rad = 0.013090 rad

The required diameter is d = [32 × 1000000 × 1500 / (π × 25939.8 × 0.013090)]^(1/4) = 49.3 mm.

The minimum solid shaft diameter that satisfies the angle-of-twist limit is approximately 49.3 mm. Standard size selection or rounding up would apply for actual design.
