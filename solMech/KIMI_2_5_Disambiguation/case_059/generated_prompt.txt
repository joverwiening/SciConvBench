Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The column is a structural steel member with length L = 2500 mm, Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa, and moment of inertia I = 1800000 mm⁴.

The end conditions are pinned-pinned with effective length factor K = 1.0.

The governing relation for Euler critical buckling load is P_cr = (π²EI) / (KL)².

Substitute the given values: P_cr = (π² × 200000 MPa × 1800000 mm⁴) / (1.0 × 2500 mm)².

Calculate the result: P_cr = (π² × 200000 × 1800000) / 6250000 N = 568489.86 N ≈ 568.5 kN.

The Euler critical buckling load for this column is approximately 568.5 kN.

Additional assumptions made: the column is perfectly straight, the load is applied axially without eccentricity, material is homogeneous and isotropic, and no residual stresses are present.

For a buckling/instability check, compare the applied compressive load P_applied against P_cr. If P_applied < P_cr, the column remains stable; if P_applied ≥ P_cr, buckling occurs. The safety factor against buckling is SF = P_cr / P_applied, which should typically exceed values specified by design codes (commonly 1.5–3.0 depending on application).
