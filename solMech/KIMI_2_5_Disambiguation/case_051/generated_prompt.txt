The analysis determines the transverse shear stress distribution in a rectangular beam cross-section under a given internal shear force.

Assumptions:
The beam is homogeneous and isotropic with linear-elastic material behavior.
Small deformations apply so that linear elasticity theory is valid.
The beam is prismatic with constant rectangular cross-section.
Plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory) except for shear deformation effects.
The shear stress is uniform across the width of the beam (valid for narrow rectangular sections where b < h).

Given parameters:
Internal shear force V = 20000 N.
Beam width b = 50 mm = 0.050 m.
Beam height h = 80 mm = 0.080 m.

Governing relations:
The first moment of area Q(y) for a rectangular section at distance y from the neutral axis is Q(y) = (b/2) * (h²/4 - y²).
The moment of inertia I for the full rectangular cross-section is I = b*h³/12.
The transverse shear stress formula is τ(y) = V*Q(y) / (I*b).

Calculated values:
Moment of inertia I = (0.050 m)*(0.080 m)³/12 = 2.133×10⁻⁶ m⁴ = 2.133×10⁶ mm⁴.
Maximum first moment of area at neutral axis (y=0): Q_max = b*h²/8 = (50 mm)*(80 mm)²/8 = 40000 mm³ = 4.0×10⁻⁵ m³.

Shear stress distribution:
The shear stress varies parabolically across the height: τ(y) = (6V)/(b*h³) * (h²/4 - y²).
Maximum shear stress occurs at the neutral axis (y=0): τ_max = 3V/(2*b*h) = 3*(20000 N)/(2*0.050 m*0.080 m) = 7.5×10⁶ Pa = 7.5 MPa.
Shear stress is zero at the top and bottom surfaces (y = ±h/2).

Physical scenario assumption:
A reasonable physical scenario is a simply supported beam of span L = 2 m with a concentrated load P = 40000 N applied at midspan, where the maximum shear force V = P/2 = 20000 N occurs in the regions between the supports and the load point.
