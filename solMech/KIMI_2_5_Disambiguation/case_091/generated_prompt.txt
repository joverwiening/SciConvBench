The prismatic bar has a total length of 1200 mm with a constant cross-sectional area of 500 mm² and is made of a linear-elastic material with Young's modulus of 200000 MPa.

The bar is fixed at point A (x = 0) with zero displacement.

Three concentrated axial loads are applied along the bar:
- At x = 300 mm (point B): 18000 N acting to the right (positive x-direction)
- At x = 900 mm (point C): -12000 N acting to the left (negative x-direction)
- At x = 1200 mm (point D): 10000 N acting to the right (positive x-direction)

The governing relations for this small-deformation, linear-elastic analysis are:

Equilibrium requires that the internal axial force N(x) is piecewise constant between load points, with jumps equal to the applied concentrated loads.

The constitutive relation follows Hooke's law: σ = Eε, where σ is axial stress, E is Young's modulus, and ε is axial strain.

Kinematics relates strain to displacement: ε = du/dx, where u(x) is the axial displacement.

The combined governing differential equation is: EA(d²u/dx²) = -q(x), where q(x) represents distributed load (zero here between point loads), EA is axial stiffness, and boundary conditions are u(0) = 0 at the fixed end with appropriate force boundary conditions at load points.

For each segment between load points, the displacement varies linearly with slope proportional to the internal force in that segment, and displacement must be continuous at load application points.
