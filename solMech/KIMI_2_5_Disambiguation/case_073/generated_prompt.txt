The shaft is a solid circular cylinder with length L = 1000 mm and radius r = 15 mm (diameter d = 30 mm).

The material is linear-elastic with shear modulus G = 25939.8 MPa.

The shaft is subjected to a concentrated torque T = 450000 N·mm applied at the free end.

The boundary condition is fully fixed against rotation at one end (built-in with zero twist), with the torque applied at the opposite free end.

The analysis assumes small deformations, linear-elastic material behavior, and homogeneous, isotropic material properties.

The polar moment of inertia for the solid circular cross-section is J = πr⁴/2 = π(15 mm)⁴/2 = 79521.56 mm⁴.

The torsional strain energy stored in the shaft is U = ∫[0 to L] (T²/2GJ) dx = T²L/2GJ for constant torque along the length.

By Castigliano's second theorem, the angle of twist at the point of torque application equals the partial derivative of the strain energy with respect to that torque.

The twist angle is φ = ∂U/∂T = TL/GJ.

Substituting values: φ = (450000 N·mm)(1000 mm) / [(25939.8 N/mm²)(79521.56 mm⁴)] = 0.2182 rad = 12.50 degrees.

The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer radius and is given by τ_max = Tr/J = (450000 N·mm)(15 mm)/(79521.56 mm⁴) = 84.88 MPa.

The shear stress distribution is linear with radius: τ(r) = Tr/J, varying from zero at the center to maximum at the outer surface.
