Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The rod is subjected to impact loading from a dropped weight with no axial preload.

The weight W = 1500 N is dropped from height h = 200 mm onto the rod with zero initial velocity.

The rod has length L = 800 mm and circular cross-section with diameter d = 25 mm, giving cross-sectional area A = πd²/4 = 490.87 mm².

The rod material is AISI 1020 steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The mass of the dropped weight is determined from W = mg, giving m = W/g = 1500/9.81 = 152.9 kg, using standard gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².

The static deflection due to the weight applied gradually is δ_st = WL/(AE) = (1500 N)(800 mm)/(490.87 mm² × 200000 MPa) = 0.0122 mm.

The impact factor for suddenly applied load is n = 1 + √(1 + 2h/δ_st) = 1 + √(1 + 2 × 200/0.0122) = 1 + √(32787) = 182.0.

The maximum dynamic deflection is δ_max = n × δ_st = 182.0 × 0.0122 mm = 2.22 mm.

The maximum impact force is P_max = n × W = 182.0 × 1500 N = 273000 N = 273 kN.

The maximum dynamic stress is σ_max = P_max/A = 273000 N/490.87 mm² = 556.2 MPa.

The maximum dynamic strain is ε_max = σ_max/E = 556.2/200000 = 0.00278 = 0.278%.

The strain energy absorbed by the rod at maximum deflection is U = ½P_maxδ_max = ½ × 273000 N × 2.22 mm = 303 N·m = 303 J.

The natural frequency of axial vibration of the rod is ω_n = √(AE/(mL)) = √((490.87 mm² × 200000 MPa)/(152.9 kg × 800 mm)) = 896 rad/s, corresponding to f_n = 142.6 Hz.

The duration of impact is approximately t_impact ≈ π/ω_n = 3.50 ms for the fundamental mode.

The assumption of no axial preload is adopted as the conservative baseline case; any existing preload would superpose linearly with impact effects.
