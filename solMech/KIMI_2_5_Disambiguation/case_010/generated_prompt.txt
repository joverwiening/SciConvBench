The composite bar system consists of two bars arranged in parallel between two rigid plates.

The length of both bars is L = 800 mm.

Bar 1 has cross-sectional area A1 = 150 mm² and Young's modulus E1 = 200000 MPa.

Bar 2 has cross-sectional area A2 = 200 mm² and Young's modulus E2 = 110000 MPa.

The applied mechanical load is P = 30000 N.

Additional assumptions: The bars are perfectly bonded to the rigid plates, ensuring equal deformation in both bars. The load is applied axially and distributed between the two bars. The material behavior remains linear elastic throughout the loading. The bars share the same initial length and deform equally due to the rigid plates.

The governing relations are as follows.

Compatibility requires equal strain in both bars: ε1 = ε2 = ε.

Equilibrium requires the sum of internal forces equals the applied load: P = P1 + P2.

The constitutive law gives internal forces: P1 = E1·A1·ε and P2 = E2·A2·ε.

The equivalent stiffness of the parallel system is: (EA)eq = E1·A1 + E2·A2 = (200000 MPa)(150 mm²) + (110000 MPa)(200 mm²) = 30000 kN + 22000 kN = 52000 kN.

The common strain is: ε = P / (EA)eq = 30000 N / 52000000 N = 5.769 × 10⁻⁴.

The displacement of the rigid plate is: δ = ε·L = (5.769 × 10⁻⁴)(800 mm) = 0.4615 mm.

The internal force in bar 1 is: P1 = E1·A1·ε = (200000 MPa)(150 mm²)(5.769 × 10⁻⁴) = 17308 N.

The internal force in bar 2 is: P2 = E2·A2·ε = (110000 MPa)(200 mm²)(5.769 × 10⁻⁴) = 12692 N.

The stress in bar 1 is: σ1 = P1/A1 = 17308 N / 150 mm² = 115.4 MPa.

The stress in bar 2 is: σ2 = P2/A2 = 12692 N / 200 mm² = 63.5 MPa.
