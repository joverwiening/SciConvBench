Assume a rectangular cross-section with height-to-span ratio of 1/12 and width-to-height ratio of 1/2 based on typical beam design practice.

The cantilever beam has length L = 1500 mm.

The rectangular cross-section has height h = 125 mm and width b = 62.5 mm.

The cross-sectional area is A = b × h = 7812.5 mm².

The second moment of area about the neutral axis is I = bh³/12 = 1.0176 × 10⁶ mm⁴.

The material is structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa = 200000 N/mm².

The beam is subjected to a uniformly distributed transverse load w = 1.5 N/mm acting downward along the entire length.

There is no distributed axial load.

The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The governing differential equation for beam deflection is EI(d⁴v/dx⁴) = w(x), where v(x) is the transverse deflection.

For a cantilever with fixed end at x = 0 and free end at x = L, the boundary conditions are: v(0) = 0, dv/dx(0) = 0, d²v/dx²(L) = 0, d³v/dx³(L) = 0.

The maximum deflection occurs at the free end (x = L) and equals v_max = wL⁴/(8EI).

The maximum deflection is v_max = 7.78 mm downward at the free end.

The deflection shape is v(x) = (wx²/24EI)(6L² - 4Lx + x²).

The maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed end and equals M_max = wL²/2 = 1.6875 × 10⁶ N·mm.

The maximum bending stress occurs at the fixed end outer fibers and equals σ_max = M_max·h/(2I) = 103.7 MPa.

The maximum shear force occurs at the fixed end and equals V_max = wL = 2250 N.

The maximum shear stress (for rectangular section) occurs at the neutral axis and equals τ_max = 3V_max/(2A) = 0.432 MPa.
