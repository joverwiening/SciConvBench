The problem involves determining the Poisson effect under uniaxial tension for an AISI steel rod.

Assumptions:
The material behaves as a linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic solid.
Deformations are small (infinitesimal strain theory applies).
The rod is prismatic with uniform circular cross-section.
The axial load is applied uniformly and centrically.
The lateral load mentioned is neglected for the uniaxial tension analysis focusing on Poisson effect.
Plane sections remain plane (Saint-Venant's principle applies away from load application points).
Temperature effects are neglected.

Given parameters:
Initial diameter d₀ = 20 mm.
Axial tensile load P = 35000 N.
Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa = 200 GPa.
Poisson's ratio ν = 0.30.

Governing relations:

The axial stress is σ = P/A₀ where A₀ = πd₀²/4 is the initial cross-sectional area.

The axial strain is ε_axial = σ/E = P/(A₀E).

The lateral strain due to Poisson effect is ε_lateral = -ν·ε_axial = -ν·P/(A₀E).

The change in diameter is Δd = ε_lateral·d₀ = -ν·P·d₀/(A₀E).

The final diameter is d = d₀ + Δd = d₀(1 + ε_lateral) = d₀(1 - ν·P/(A₀E)).

Calculated quantities:

Initial cross-sectional area A₀ = π×(20 mm)²/4 = 314.159 mm².

Axial stress σ = 35000 N / 314.159 mm² = 111.408 MPa.

Axial strain ε_axial = 111.408 MPa / 200000 MPa = 5.570×10⁻⁴ = 0.000557.

Lateral strain ε_lateral = -0.30 × 5.570×10⁻⁴ = -1.671×10⁻⁴ = -0.0001671.

Change in diameter Δd = -1.671×10⁻⁴ × 20 mm = -0.003342 mm = -3.342 μm.

Final diameter d = 20 mm - 0.003342 mm = 19.9967 mm.

The Poisson effect manifests as a contraction in the lateral dimension (diameter reduction) when the rod is subjected to axial tension, with the magnitude of lateral strain being 30% of the axial strain (equal to Poisson's ratio).
