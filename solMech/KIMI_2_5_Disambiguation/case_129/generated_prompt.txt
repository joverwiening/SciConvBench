Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The beam is subjected to a bending moment M.

The second moment of area of the cross-section is I = 1200000 mm⁴.

The material has a Young's modulus E, which remains as a symbolic variable in the final expressions.

The governing relation for curvature is κ = M / (E * I).

The governing relation for radius of curvature is ρ = 1 / κ = (E * I) / M.

The curvature κ has units of mm⁻¹ when M is in N·mm, E is in MPa (N/mm²), and I is in mm⁴.

The radius of curvature ρ has units of mm.

No specific cross-section geometry is required beyond the given I value.

The beam is assumed to be homogeneous and isotropic.

Plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).

The bending moment M is given or can be treated as a variable for general expressions.
