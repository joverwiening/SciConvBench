Assume a rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm based on common structural proportions for cantilever beams.

Assume plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after deformation (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).

Assume the beam is prismatic with constant cross-section along its length.

Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.

Assume the distributed load acts perpendicular to the beam axis and is constant along the entire length.

Assume the cantilever is fixed at x = 0 and free at x = L.

The second moment of area for the rectangular cross-section is I = b * h^3 / 12 = 50 * 100^3 / 12 = 4,166,667 mm^4.

The governing differential equation for beam deflection is EI * d^4v/dx^4 = w, where v is the transverse deflection.

The maximum deflection occurs at the free end (x = L) and equals v_max = w * L^4 / (8 * E * I).

Substituting values: v_max = 1.0 * 2500^4 / (8 * 200000 * 4,166,667) = 5.86 mm.

The deflection shape along the beam is v(x) = w * x^2 * (6*L^2 - 4*L*x + x^2) / (24 * E * I).

The maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed end and equals M_max = w * L^2 / 2 = 3,125,000 N·mm.

The maximum bending stress occurs at the outer fiber of the fixed end and equals sigma_max = M_max * h / (2 * I) = 37.5 MPa.
