The cantilever beam has length L = 1200 mm with flexural rigidity EI = 5 × 10^11 N·mm².

A downward point load P = 800 N is applied at the free end.

No moment is applied at the free end.

The governing differential equation for Euler-Bernoulli beam theory is EI d⁴v/dx⁴ = q(x), where q(x) is the distributed load.

For this case with only an end point load, the bending moment varies linearly as M(x) = -P(L - x) for 0 ≤ x ≤ L, with x measured from the fixed end.

The shear force is constant at V(x) = -P throughout the beam.

The slope equation is obtained by integrating the moment-curvature relation EI d²v/dx² = M(x).

The first integration gives EI dv/dx = P(L - x)²/2 + C1.

The second integration gives EI v = -P(L - x)³/6 + C1x + C2.

Apply boundary conditions at the fixed end (x = 0): v(0) = 0 and dv/dx(0) = 0.

From dv/dx(0) = 0: 0 = PL²/2 + C1, so C1 = -PL²/2.

From v(0) = 0: 0 = -PL³/6 + C2, so C2 = PL³/6.

The deflection curve is v(x) = (P/(6EI))[3Lx² - x³] for 0 ≤ x ≤ L, or equivalently v(x) = (P/(6EI))[2L³ - 3L²(L-x) + (L-x)³] using the free-end coordinate.

The maximum deflection occurs at the free end (x = L): v_max = PL³/(3EI).

Substituting values: v_max = (800 N)(1200 mm)³/(3 × 5 × 10^11 N·mm²) = (800)(1.728 × 10^9)/(1.5 × 10^12) mm = 0.9216 mm downward.

The maximum slope occurs at the free end: θ_max = dv/dx at x = L = PL²/(2EI) = (800)(1200)²/(2 × 5 × 10^11) = 1.152 × 10^-3 rad = 0.066 degrees.

The maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed end: M_max = -PL = -(800 N)(1200 mm) = -9.6 × 10^5 N·mm = -960 N·m (hogging, top fiber in tension).

The maximum bending stress is σ_max = M_max c/I, where c is the distance from neutral axis to outer fiber. This requires cross-section geometry which is not specified.

The shear force is constant at V = -800 N throughout the beam.

The maximum shear stress depends on cross-section shape and is τ_max = VQ/(Ib) for rectangular sections, requiring additional geometric information not provided.

Small deformation assumption validates the use of linearized curvature and neglect of axial stretching due to transverse deflection.

Linear elastic assumption permits superposition and use of Hooke's law for stress-strain relations.

The beam is prismatic with constant EI along its length.

Self-weight of the beam is neglected.
