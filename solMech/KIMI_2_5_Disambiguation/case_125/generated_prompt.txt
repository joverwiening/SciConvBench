The simulation analyzes bending stress in a rectangular beam under pure bending moment.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The beam has rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.

The applied bending moment is M = 4000000 N·mm with sagging direction (causes compression in top fibers and tension in bottom fibers).

The coordinate system is defined with origin at the centroid of the cross-section, y-axis positive upward, z-axis along the beam longitudinal axis.

The neutral axis coincides with the centroidal axis (y = 0) for homogeneous material and symmetric cross-section.

The moment of inertia about the neutral axis is I = b*h^3/12 = 50*120^3/12 = 7,200,000 mm^4.

The distance from neutral axis to extreme fibers is c = h/2 = 60 mm.

The bending stress at arbitrary fiber location y is given by sigma(y) = -M*y/I.

The negative sign ensures sagging moment produces compression (negative stress) when y is positive (top fibers) and tension (positive stress) when y is negative (bottom fibers).

Maximum bending stress occurs at extreme fibers y = ±60 mm: sigma_max = ±M*c/I = ±4000000*60/7200000 = ±33.33 MPa.

The stress varies linearly through the depth from -33.33 MPa at top surface to +33.33 MPa at bottom surface.

No shear stress is present due to pure bending (zero shear force).
