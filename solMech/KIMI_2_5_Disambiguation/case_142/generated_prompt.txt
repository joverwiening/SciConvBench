The specimen is a cylindrical tension-test specimen with initial diameter d₀ = 12.5 mm and initial gage length L₀ = 50 mm.

The applied axial load is P = 15000 N.

The material is treated as a generic linear-elastic material where Young's modulus E is determined from the test data as E = σ/ε, not assumed.

The deformation is small, and the material behavior is linear-elastic.

The initial cross-sectional area is A₀ = π(d₀/2)² = π(6.25 mm)² = 122.72 mm².

Engineering stress is defined as σ = P/A₀.

Engineering strain is defined as ε = ΔL/L₀, where ΔL is the change in gage length.

The modulus of elasticity is determined from the slope of the linear-elastic region: E = σ/ε.

For the given load P = 15000 N, the engineering stress is σ = 15000 N / 122.72 mm² = 122.23 MPa.

The engineering strain is ε = σ/E, which requires knowledge of E from the stress-strain curve slope; alternatively, if displacement data were provided, ε = ΔL/L₀.

The governing relations are:
σ = P/A₀
ε = ΔL/L₀
E = σ/ε (in the linear-elastic region)

Additional assumptions include: uniform stress distribution in the gage section, no necking (small-deformation regime), isotropic material behavior, and loading is purely axial with no bending or torsion.
