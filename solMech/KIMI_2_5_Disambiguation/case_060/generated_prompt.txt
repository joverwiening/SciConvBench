The column is a fixed-fixed Euler buckling column with small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics assumed.

The column length is L = 2000 mm.

The moment of inertia is I = 800000 mm^4.

The Young's modulus is E = 200000 MPa (structural steel).

The end support conditions are fixed-fixed.

The effective length factor for fixed-fixed boundary conditions is K = 0.5.

The effective length is Leff = K * L = 0.5 * 2000 mm = 1000 mm.

The Euler critical buckling load is Pcr = π² * E * I / (Leff)² = π² * 200000 MPa * 800000 mm^4 / (1000 mm)² = 1579.14 kN.

The governing relation for Euler buckling is Pcr = π² * E * I / (K * L)².

The critical buckling stress is σcr = Pcr / A, where A is the cross-sectional area.

The slenderness ratio is λ = Leff / r, where r = √(I/A) is the radius of gyration.

A buckling check compares the applied compressive load P to Pcr with a safety factor: P ≤ Pcr / FS, where FS is typically 1.5 to 3.0 for structural applications.

If the applied load is not specified, the critical load Pcr = 1579.14 kN serves as the instability threshold.

Additional assumptions: the column is prismatic (constant cross-section), homogeneous, isotropic, initially perfectly straight, and loaded concentrically through the centroidal axis; no residual stresses or imperfections are considered; lateral deflections remain small until buckling occurs.
