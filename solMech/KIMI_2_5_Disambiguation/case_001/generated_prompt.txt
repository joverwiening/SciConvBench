The stepped circular bar consists of two segments in series subjected to axial tension.

Assumptions:
The material behaves as linear elastic with small deformations.
The bar is prismatic within each segment with circular cross-section.
The load is applied axially and uniformly distributed.
Self-weight of the bar is negligible compared to the applied load.
Plane sections remain plane (Saint-Venant's principle applies away from the step).
The step transition is abrupt but stress concentrations are neglected for the gross section analysis.

Given properties:
Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa for both segments.
Segment 1: diameter d1 = 25 mm, length L1 = 300 mm, cross-sectional area A1 = π×(25)²/4 = 490.874 mm².
Segment 2: diameter d2 = 35 mm, length L2 = 1000 mm, cross-sectional area A2 = π×(35)²/4 = 962.113 mm².
Applied tensile load P = 30000 N.

Governing relations:
Axial stress in a segment: σ = P/A.
Axial strain in a segment: ε = σ/E = P/(AE).
Elongation of a segment: δ = ε×L = PL/(AE).
Total elongation: δ_total = δ1 + δ2 = P×[L1/(A1E) + L2/(A2E)].

Calculations:
Stress in segment 1: σ1 = 30000 N / 490.874 mm² = 61.115 MPa.
Stress in segment 2: σ2 = 30000 N / 962.113 mm² = 31.182 MPa.
Elongation of segment 1: δ1 = (30000 N × 300 mm) / (490.874 mm² × 200000 MPa) = 0.09167 mm.
Elongation of segment 2: δ2 = (30000 N × 1000 mm) / (962.113 mm² × 200000 MPa) = 0.15590 mm.
Total elongation: δ_total = 0.09167 mm + 0.15590 mm = 0.24757 mm.

Results:
Stress in segment 1 (smaller diameter): σ1 = 61.1 MPa (tensile).
Stress in segment 2 (larger diameter): σ2 = 31.2 MPa (tensile).
Total elongation of the stepped bar: δ_total = 0.248 mm.
