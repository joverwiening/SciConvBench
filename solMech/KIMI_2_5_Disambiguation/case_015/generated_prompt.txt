The cantilever beam has length L = 1200 mm with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa and second moment of area I = 2000000 mm^4.

A downward point load P = 2000 N is applied at the free end. No moment is applied at the free end.

The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics with Euler-Bernoulli beam theory. The beam is prismatic with constant EI along its length. Deflections are small such that the linearized curvature approximation remains valid. The material is homogeneous and isotropic.

The governing differential equation for beam deflection is EI v''''(x) = q(x), where q(x) = 0 for this case with only end loading.

For a cantilever beam fixed at x = 0 and free at x = L, the boundary conditions are: v(0) = 0 (zero deflection), v'(0) = 0 (zero slope), V(L) = -P (shear equals applied load), M(L) = 0 (zero moment at free end).

The bending moment distribution is M(x) = -P(L - x).

The shear force is constant at V(x) = -P.

The beam deflection equation is v(x) = (P x^2)/(6EI) * (3L - x).

The slope equation is v'(x) = (P x)/(2EI) * (2L - x).

The maximum deflection occurs at the free end: v_max = v(L) = PL^3/(3EI) = (2000 N)(1200 mm)^3 / (3 * 200000 MPa * 2000000 mm^4) = 2.88 mm downward.

The maximum slope occurs at the free end: v'_max = v'(L) = PL^2/(2EI) = (2000 N)(1200 mm)^2 / (2 * 200000 MPa * 2000000 mm^4) = 0.0036 rad.

The maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed end: M_max = M(0) = -PL = -2.4 × 10^6 N·mm = -2.4 kN·m (hogging, top fiber in tension).

The maximum bending stress occurs at the fixed end outer fibers: σ_max = M_max * c / I, where c is the distance from neutral axis to outer fiber. Since c is not provided, the section modulus S = I/c cannot be determined, and maximum stress cannot be computed from the given information.
