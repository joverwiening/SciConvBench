The shaft is made of structural steel with shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa.
The length of the shaft is L = 600 mm.
The diameter of the solid circular shaft is d = 50 mm.
The radius of the shaft is R = d/2 = 25 mm.
The magnitude of the applied torque is T = 500000 N·mm.
The loading is purely torsional with no applied shear force V.

Additional assumptions:
The shaft is prismatic with constant circular cross-section.
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the axis (no warping).
The shaft is subjected to small deformations within the linear elastic range.
There are no stress concentrations or geometric discontinuities.
The torque is applied uniformly and statically.

Governing relations:

The polar moment of inertia for a solid circular cross-section is J = (π/32) * d^4.

The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer radius and is given by τ_max = (T * R) / J = (16 * T) / (π * d^3).

The angle of twist for the entire length is given by φ = (T * L) / (G * J).

The shear stress varies linearly with radial position: τ(r) = (T * r) / J, where r is the radial coordinate from 0 to R.

Calculated response quantities:

J = (π/32) * (50 mm)^4 = 613592.3 mm^4.

τ_max = (16 * 500000 N·mm) / (π * (50 mm)^3) = 20.37 MPa.

φ = (500000 N·mm * 600 mm) / (77519.4 MPa * 613592.3 mm^4) = 0.00630 rad = 0.361 degrees.
