The problem involves a two-segment stepped circular shaft in torsion with end A fixed and torque applied at end C. The following assumptions apply: small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior; homogeneous, isotropic material; circular cross-sections remain plane and undistorted (Saint-Venant torsion theory); constant internal torque throughout the shaft since segments are in series and only end torque is applied; no transverse shear loading is present.

The geometric properties are: Segment AB has length L1 = 400 mm and diameter d1 = 25 mm (radius r1 = 12.5 mm); Segment BC has length L2 = 900 mm and diameter d2 = 30 mm (radius r2 = 15 mm).

The loading is: torque T = 750000 N·mm applied at end C about the shaft axis, with end A fixed. The internal torque is constant: T_AB = T_BC = T = 750000 N·mm.

The material property is: shear modulus G = 25939.8 MPa = 25939.8 N/mm².

The polar moments of inertia are: J1 = πd1⁴/32 = π(25)⁴/32 = 38349.5 mm⁴ for segment AB; J2 = πd2⁴/32 = π(30)⁴/32 = 79521.6 mm⁴ for segment BC.

The maximum shear stresses occur at the outer surfaces: τ_max1 = T·r1/J1 = (750000)(12.5)/(38349.5) = 244.5 MPa in segment AB; τ_max2 = T·r2/J2 = (750000)(15)/(79521.6) = 141.5 MPa in segment BC. The shear stress varies linearly from zero at the center to maximum at the outer radius in each segment.

The angle of twist for each segment is: φ1 = T·L1/(G·J1) = (750000)(400)/(25939.8 × 38349.5) = 0.3014 rad for segment AB; φ2 = T·L2/(G·J2) = (750000)(900)/(25939.8 × 79521.6) = 0.3273 rad for segment BC.

The total twist angle at end C relative to fixed end A is: φ_total = φ1 + φ2 = 0.3014 + 0.3273 = 0.6287 rad, or equivalently 36.0 degrees.
