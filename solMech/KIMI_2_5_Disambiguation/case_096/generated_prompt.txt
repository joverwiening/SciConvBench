The cantilever beam has length L = 800 mm with Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa and moment of inertia I = 800000 mm⁴.

A downward point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end. No moment is applied at the free end.

The governing differential equation for Euler-Bernoulli beam theory is: EI(d⁴w/dx⁴) = q(x), where w is the transverse deflection and q(x) is the distributed load. For this case with only an end point load, q(x) = 0.

The boundary conditions for a cantilever beam with fixed end at x = 0 and free end at x = L are: w(0) = 0, dw/dx(0) = 0, M(L) = EI(d²w/dx²)(L) = 0, and V(L) = -EI(d³w/dx³)(L) = -P.

The relevant response quantities are:

The bending moment distribution is M(x) = -P(L - x).

The shear force is V(x) = -P (constant along the beam).

The slope of the deflection curve is θ(x) = dw/dx = (P/EI)(Lx - x²/2).

The deflection curve is w(x) = (P/EI)(Lx²/2 - x³/6).

The maximum deflection occurs at the free end: w_max = PL³/(3EI) = (500 N)(800 mm)³/(3 × 69000 MPa × 800000 mm⁴) = 1.546 mm downward.

The maximum slope occurs at the free end: θ_max = PL²/(2EI) = (500 N)(800 mm)²/(2 × 69000 MPa × 800000 mm⁴) = 0.002899 rad = 0.166 degrees.

The maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed end: M_max = -PL = -(500 N)(800 mm) = -400000 N·mm = -400 N·m (hogging moment).

The maximum bending stress occurs at the fixed end and equals σ_max = M_max·c/I, where c is the distance from the neutral axis to the outer fiber.
