The problem involves impact loading of a dropped weight onto an axially loaded rod. The relevant response quantities are the dynamic stress, dynamic displacement, and impact factor.

Assumptions:
The rod behaves as a linear spring with stiffness k = EA/L.
The potential energy of the dropped weight converts entirely to strain energy in the rod at maximum displacement (conservation of energy, no energy losses).
The static preload is present and must be superposed with the dynamic response.
Small deformations apply, so geometric linearity holds.
The mass of the rod is negligible compared to the dropped weight (no wave propagation effects, quasi-static impact).

Given data:
Weight W = 800 N
Gravitational acceleration g = 9806.65 mm/s²
Mass m = W/g = 800/9806.65 = 0.08158 kg (or equivalently, W/g in N·s²/mm)
Drop height h = 150 mm
Diameter d = 16 mm
Cross-sectional area A = π × 16² / 4 = 201.06 mm²
Length L = 1500 mm
Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa

Derived quantities:
Rod stiffness k = EA/L = (200000 × 201.06) / 1500 = 26808.2 N/mm
Static displacement due to weight alone: δ_st = W/k = 800 / 26808.2 = 0.02984 mm

Governing relations for impact loading:

Energy conservation: The work done by gravity over the fall distance plus the static displacement equals the strain energy at maximum displacement.
m g (h + δ_max) = (1/2) k δ_max²

This yields the quadratic equation:
(1/2) k δ_max² - W δ_max - W h = 0

Solving for maximum dynamic displacement:
δ_max = (W/k) + √[(W/k)² + 2Wh/k] = δ_st + √[δ_st² + 2hδ_st]

Impact factor:
IF = δ_max / δ_st = 1 + √[1 + 2h/δ_st]

Maximum dynamic force:
F_max = k × δ_max = W × IF

Maximum dynamic stress:
σ_max = F_max / A = (W × IF) / A

Numerical results:
δ_st = 0.02984 mm
IF = 1 + √[1 + 2 × 150 / 0.02984] = 1 + √[10055] = 1 + 100.27 = 101.27
δ_max = 0.02984 × 101.27 = 3.022 mm
F_max = 800 × 101.27 = 81016 N = 81.02 kN
σ_max = 81016 / 201.06 = 402.9 MPa
