The problem involves a simply supported Euler-Bernoulli beam with a midspan point load.

Assumptions:
The beam undergoes small deformations.
The material behaves in a linear-elastic manner.
The beam is prismatic with constant cross-section.
Shear deformation is neglected (Euler-Bernoulli theory applies).
The beam is simply supported with pinned supports at both ends allowing rotation but preventing translation.
Self-weight of the beam is neglected.
The point load acts vertically downward at the exact midspan location.

Given parameters:
Point load P = 1500 N.
Beam length L = 3000 mm.
Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa = 200000 N/mm².
Moment of inertia I = 3500000 mm⁴.

Governing relations:
The governing differential equation for Euler-Bernoulli beam theory is EI(d⁴w/dx⁴) = q(x), where w is the transverse deflection and q(x) is the distributed load.

For a simply supported beam with midspan point load P, the midspan deflection is δ = PL³/(48EI).

For a simply supported beam with midspan point load P, the support slope is θ = PL²/(16EI).

Calculated response quantities:
Midspan deflection δ = (1500 N × (3000 mm)³) / (48 × 200000 N/mm² × 3500000 mm⁴) = 4.0179 mm downward.

Support slope θ = (1500 N × (3000 mm)²) / (16 × 200000 N/mm² × 3500000 mm⁴) = 0.0012054 rad = 0.0691 degrees.
