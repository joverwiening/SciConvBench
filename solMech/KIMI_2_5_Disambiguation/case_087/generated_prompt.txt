Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics unless otherwise required.

The tension specimen is made of ductile metal.

The initial gauge length is L₀ = 50 mm.

The initial cross-sectional area is A₀ = 113.1 mm².

The final gauge length after fracture is L_f = 66 mm.

The final cross-sectional area at the fracture surface after necking is A_f = 72.38 mm².

Percent elongation is calculated as the permanent plastic strain expressed as a percentage of the original gauge length.

The governing relation for percent elongation is: Percent Elongation = [(L_f - L₀) / L₀] × 100%.

Substituting values: Percent Elongation = [(66 mm - 50 mm) / 50 mm] × 100% = (16 mm / 50 mm) × 100% = 32%.

Reduction of area is calculated as the decrease in cross-sectional area at the fracture surface expressed as a percentage of the original cross-sectional area.

The governing relation for reduction of area is: Reduction of Area = [(A₀ - A_f) / A₀] × 100%.

Substituting values: Reduction of Area = [(113.1 mm² - 72.38 mm²) / 113.1 mm²] × 100% = (40.72 mm² / 113.1 mm²) × 100% = 36.0%.

Additional assumption: The final gauge length L_f is measured after the two fractured pieces are fitted back together.

Additional assumption: The final cross-sectional area A_f is measured at the minimum cross-section of the fracture surface where necking occurred.

Additional assumption: The deformation is primarily plastic at fracture, with elastic recovery being negligible compared to the plastic deformation (consistent with ductile fracture).

The percent elongation is 32%.
