The column is made of 6061-T6 aluminum with Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa.

The column length is L = 2200 mm.

The effective length factor is K = 2.0, corresponding to a fixed-free (cantilever) boundary condition.

The applied compressive load is P = 25000 N.

The required factor of safety against buckling is n = 2.5.

The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The critical buckling load is governed by Euler's formula: P_cr = (π²EI) / (KL)².

For a solid circular cross-section, the area moment of inertia is I = (πd⁴)/64, where d is the diameter.

The required critical load accounting for factor of safety is P_cr,req = n × P = 2.5 × 25000 N = 62500 N.

Substituting I into Euler's formula gives P_cr = (π²E(πd⁴/64)) / (KL)² = (π³Ed⁴) / (64(KL)²).

Solving for the required diameter: d = [ (64(KL)² × P_cr,req) / (π³E) ]^(1/4).

The effective length is KL = 2.0 × 2200 mm = 4400 mm.

The required diameter is d = [ (64 × (4400 mm)² × 62500 N) / (π³ × 69000 MPa) ]^(1/4) = 78.9 mm.

Use a standard diameter of d = 80 mm for practical design.

The actual moment of inertia with d = 80 mm is I = π(80 mm)⁴/64 = 2.011 × 10⁶ mm⁴.

The actual critical buckling load is P_cr = (π² × 69000 MPa × 2.011 × 10⁶ mm⁴) / (4400 mm)² = 71050 N.

The actual factor of safety is n_actual = P_cr / P = 71050 N / 25000 N = 2.84.

The design satisfies the required factor of safety of 2.5.

Additional assumptions: the column is perfectly straight, the load is applied axially without eccentricity, the material is homogeneous and isotropic, and local buckling or crippling effects are neglected.
