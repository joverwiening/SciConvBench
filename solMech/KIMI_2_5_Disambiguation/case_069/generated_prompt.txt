The analysis determines curvature and radius of curvature for a linear-elastic beam in pure bending using the Euler-Bernoulli beam theory.

Assumptions:
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after deformation (Euler-Bernoulli hypothesis).
The beam is prismatic with constant cross-section along its length.
Deformations are small such that linear strain-displacement relations apply.
The applied moment acts about the principal axis corresponding to the given second moment of area I.
No axial force or shear force is present (pure bending condition).

Governing relations:
The bending moment-curvature relation for linear-elastic material is M = EIκ, where M is the bending moment, E is Young's modulus, I is the second moment of area, and κ is the curvature.

Curvature is defined as κ = 1/ρ, where ρ is the radius of curvature.

The curvature can be expressed directly from the moment-curvature relation as κ = M/(EI).

The radius of curvature follows as ρ = EI/M.

Numerical evaluation:
Given I = 4000000 mm⁴ = 4×10⁻⁶ m⁴ and M = 3000000 N·mm = 3000 N·m.

The curvature is κ = M/(EI) = 3000/(E × 4×10⁻⁶) = 7.5×10⁸/E m⁻¹, where E must be expressed in Pascals.

The radius of curvature is ρ = EI/M = (E × 4×10⁻⁶)/3000 = (4×10⁻⁶/3000)E = 1.333×10⁻⁹ E m, where E must be expressed in Pascals.

Alternatively in consistent mm units: κ = 3000000/(E × 4000000) = 0.75/E mm⁻¹ and ρ = (E × 4000000)/3000000 = 1.333E mm, with E in MPa.

Final expressions:
Curvature κ = 0.75/E mm⁻¹ (E in MPa) or κ = 750/E m⁻¹ (E in GPa).
Radius of curvature ρ = 1.333E mm (E in MPa) or ρ = 1.333×10⁻³ E m (E in GPa).
