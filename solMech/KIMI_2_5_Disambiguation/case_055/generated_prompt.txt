The bar is a linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics problem with axial loading.

The cross-sectional area varies linearly along the length according to A(x) = A₀·(1 + k·x/L), with A₀ = 200 mm² at x = 0, k = 0.6, and L = 1200 mm.

The applied axial load is P = 20000 N, tensile, and is constant along the length (uniform axial force).

The material is linear-elastic with Young's modulus E (to be specified or left as symbolic).

The governing equations are:

The axial force is constant: N(x) = P = 20000 N.

The normal stress varies with position: σ(x) = N(x)/A(x) = P/[A₀·(1 + k·x/L)].

The axial strain varies with position: ε(x) = σ(x)/E = P/[E·A₀·(1 + k·x/L)].

The axial displacement u(x) is obtained by integrating the strain: du/dx = ε(x).

The total elongation of the bar is Δ = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x)dx = (P·L)/(E·A₀·k)·ln(1+k) = (P·L)/(E·A₀·0.6)·ln(1.6).

The displacement field is u(x) = (P·L)/(E·A₀·k)·ln(1 + k·x/L), with u(0) = 0 (fixed reference).

The maximum stress occurs at x = 0: σ_max = P/A₀ = 100 MPa.

The minimum stress occurs at x = L: σ_min = P/[A₀·(1+k)] = P/(1.6·A₀) = 62.5 MPa.
