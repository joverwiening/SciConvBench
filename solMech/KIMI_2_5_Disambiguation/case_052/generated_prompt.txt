The shaft is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 35 mm and radius r = d/2 = 17.5 mm.

The shaft rotates at constant angular speed n = 1800 rpm, giving angular velocity ω = 2πn/60 = 188.5 rad/s.

The transmitted power is P = 10 kW = 10,000 W.

The torque is computed from power-angular velocity relation T = P/ω = 10,000/188.5 = 53.05 N·m.

The polar moment of inertia for solid circular cross-section is J = πd⁴/32 = π(0.035)⁴/32 = 1.473 × 10⁻⁷ m⁴.

The maximum shear stress due to torsion occurs at outer radius and is given by τ_max = Tr/J = T(d/2)/J = (53.05)(0.0175)/(1.473 × 10⁻⁷) = 6.30 MPa.

The angle of twist per unit length is dφ/dx = T/(GJ), where G is the shear modulus of the material.

The total angle of twist over length L is φ = TL/(GJ).

The shaft is subjected to pure torsion loading only; no transverse shear force or bending moment is present.

Material is assumed homogeneous, isotropic, and linear elastic with shear modulus G (typical value for steel: G ≈ 79 GPa if needed for numerical evaluation).

Small deformation assumption applies, so geometric linearity holds and superposition is valid if combined loading were present.

The torsion check compares computed maximum shear stress against allowable shear stress: τ_max ≤ τ_allowable.

The power-torque conversion uses the fundamental relation P = Tω for steady-state rotation.

No shear loading is present; the problem statement's mention of "shear loading" refers to the torsional shear stress distribution, not transverse shear.
