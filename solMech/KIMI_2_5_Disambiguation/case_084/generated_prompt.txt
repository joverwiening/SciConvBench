Assume a built-up I-beam or T-section configuration where a flange plate is attached to a web by fasteners spaced along the longitudinal interface.

The governing shear flow equation for built-up beam analysis is q = VQ/I, where q is the shear flow (force per unit length) at the interface, V is the applied shear force, Q is the first moment of area of the attached flange about the neutral axis, and I is the moment of inertia of the entire cross-section about the neutral axis.

Calculate the shear flow: q = (25000 N)(45000 mm³)/(18000000 mm⁴) = 62.5 N/mm.

The shear flow represents the longitudinal force per unit length that must be transferred across the flange-web interface.

For fastener spacing design, the allowable load per fastener F_allow governs the maximum spacing. The required fastener spacing s is determined by equating the shear force transferred over spacing s to the fastener capacity: s = F_allow/q.

Calculate the maximum fastener spacing: s = 1800 N / 62.5 N/mm = 28.8 mm.

Use a practical fastener spacing of s = 28 mm or less to satisfy strength requirements.

The relevant response quantities are: shear flow q = 62.5 N/mm, and maximum fastener spacing s_max = 28.8 mm (round down to 28 mm for design).

Additional assumptions include: fasteners are arranged in a single line along the interface, fasteners carry shear only with no moment transfer, the interface is fully effective in transferring shear, and spacing is uniform along the beam length. Fastener spacing may need reduction near supports where shear force is higher if V varies along the span.
