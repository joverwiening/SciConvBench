The task is to determine the midspan deflection of a simply supported beam with central point load using Castigliano's theorem.

Assumptions:
The beam is prismatic with constant cross-section.
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Small deformations apply, so linear-elastic theory is valid.
Shear deformation is neglected (Bernoulli-Euler beam theory).
The beam has no initial curvature or prestress.

Given parameters:
Beam length L = 2500 mm.
Central point load P = 1000 N.
Moment of inertia I = 3000000 mm^4.
Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa = 200000 N/mm².

Governing relations using Castigliano's second theorem:
The partial derivative of the strain energy with respect to a force gives the displacement at the point of application in the direction of that force.
For a beam, the strain energy due to bending is U = ∫(M²/2EI)dx from 0 to L.
The deflection at the point of load application is δ = ∂U/∂P = ∫(M/EI)(∂M/∂P)dx from 0 to L.

For a simply supported beam with central point load P:
By symmetry, analyze half the beam from x=0 to x=L/2.
The bending moment at position x is M(x) = (P/2)·x for 0 ≤ x ≤ L/2.
The partial derivative is ∂M/∂P = x/2.

The midspan deflection is:
δ = (2/EI) ∫ from 0 to L/2 of [(Px/2)(x/2)] dx = (2/EI) ∫ from 0 to L/2 of (Px²/4) dx
δ = (P/2EI) · [x³/3] from 0 to L/2 = (P/2EI) · (L³/24) = PL³/(48EI)

Calculation:
δ = (1000 N × (2500 mm)³) / (48 × 200000 N/mm² × 3000000 mm⁴)
δ = (1000 × 15625000000) / (48 × 200000 × 3000000) mm
δ = 1.5625 × 10¹³ / 2.88 × 10¹³ mm
δ = 0.5425 mm

The midspan deflection is approximately 0.543 mm downward.
