The problem involves determining the relevant response quantities for a short rectangular column subjected to an eccentric axial load on a rectangular section.

Assumptions:
The material follows small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The column is short, so buckling effects are neglected.
The eccentric load is applied parallel to the longitudinal axis but offset from the centroid.
Plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies).
The load is static and applied gradually.

Given parameters:
Eccentricity distance e = 20 mm
Height of cross-section h = 120 mm
Width of cross-section b = 60 mm
Magnitude of axial load P = 60000 N

Governing relations:

The eccentric axial load creates a combined loading state of axial compression (or tension) plus bending moment.

The direct axial stress due to the concentric component of the load is uniform across the section:
sigma_axial = P / A
where A = b * h = 60 mm * 120 mm = 7200 mm²

The bending moment caused by the eccentricity is:
M = P * e = 60000 N * 20 mm = 1.2 × 10^6 N·mm

The bending stress varies linearly across the section height:
sigma_bending = M * y / I
where y is the distance from the neutral axis and I is the second moment of area about the bending axis.

For bending about the strong axis (assuming eccentricity in the direction of height h):
I = b * h³ / 12 = 60 * (120)³ / 12 = 8.64 × 10^6 mm⁴

The maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fibers at y = ±h/2 = ±60 mm:
sigma_bending_max = M * (h/2) / I = (1.2 × 10^6 N·mm * 60 mm) / (8.64 × 10^6 mm⁴) = 8.333 MPa

The combined stress distribution is:
sigma_total(y) = P/A + M*y/I = P/A + P*e*y/I

Maximum compressive stress (at y = -h/2, assuming positive e causes compression on this side):
sigma_max_compression = P/A + M*(h/2)/I = 60000/7200 + 8.333 = 8.333 + 8.333 = 16.667 MPa

Maximum tensile stress (at y = +h/2):
sigma_max_tension = P/A - M*(h/2)/I = 8.333 - 8.333 = 0 MPa

The neutral axis location where sigma_total = 0:
y_na = -I/(A*e) = -(8.64 × 10^6)/(7200 * 20) = -60 mm

This indicates the neutral axis is at the extreme fiber on the tension side, meaning the entire section is in compression (no tension zone) for this loading combination.

The section modulus for this eccentric loading case is:
S = I / (h/2) = 8.64 × 10^6 / 60 = 1.44 × 10^5 mm³

The kern distance (limit of eccentricity for no tension) for a rectangular section is h/6 = 20 mm. Since e = 20 mm equals exactly the kern limit, the stress at one edge is exactly zero, confirming the calculation above.
