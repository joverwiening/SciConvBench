Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The system consists of a stepped circular bar subjected to axial tension loading.

The bar has two segments with different cross-sectional areas: segment 1 with diameter d1 and length L1, segment 2 with diameter d2 and length L2.

The material is homogeneous and isotropic with Young's modulus E, identical for both segments.

The axial tension force P is applied uniformly at the ends, creating uniform stress in each segment.

Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the axis (Saint-Venant's principle applies away from the step transition).

The step transition region is neglected; stress concentrations at the fillet are not calculated.

The governing relations are as follows.

The cross-sectional area of segment i is Ai = π(di/2)^2 = πdi^2/4.

The normal stress in segment i is σi = P/Ai.

The strain in segment i is εi = σi/E = P/(EAi).

The elongation of segment i is δi = εiLi = PLi/(EAi).

The total elongation of the stepped bar is δ_total = δ1 + δ2 = P/(E) × (L1/A1 + L2/A2).

The stress is higher in the segment with smaller diameter.

The elongation is inversely proportional to the cross-sectional area for each segment.
