The vertical bar has length L = 3500 mm and hangs from a rigid support at its top end, extending downward under gravity.

The cross-sectional area is A = 200 mm².

The material properties are Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa and mass density ρ = 7850 kg/m³.

The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The bar is subjected to an axial load consisting of its own self-weight distributed along its length.

The governing differential equation for axial displacement u(x) is derived from equilibrium of a differential element: d/dx(EA du/dx) = -ρgA, where x is measured downward from the support at x = 0 to the free end at x = L.

The boundary conditions are: u(0) = 0 (fixed displacement at support), and EA du/dx|_{x=L} = 0 (zero axial force at free end, since no external load is applied there).

The axial force distribution is N(x) = ρgA(L - x), varying linearly from maximum at the support to zero at the free end.

The normal stress distribution is σ(x) = N(x)/A = ρg(L - x), compressive throughout with magnitude varying linearly from ρgL at the support to zero at the free end.

The strain distribution is ε(x) = σ(x)/E = ρg(L - x)/E.

The elongation of the bar (total downward displacement of the free end relative to the support) is δ = ρgL²/(2E).

Numerically, with g = 9.81 m/s², the elongation is δ = (7850 kg/m³)(9.81 m/s²)(3.5 m)²/(2 × 69 × 10⁹ Pa) = 6.84 × 10⁻⁶ m = 0.00684 mm.

The displacement field is u(x) = (ρg/E)(Lx - x²/2), giving parabolic variation from zero at the support to maximum at the free end.
