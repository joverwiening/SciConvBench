The beam is modeled using small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The cross-section is characterized by a second moment of area I = 2500000 mm^4.

The applied bending moment is M = 3000000 N·mm.

The Young's modulus of the beam material is E = 200000 MPa.

The flexural rigidity of the beam is EI = (200000 MPa) × (2500000 mm^4) = 5 × 10^11 N·mm^2.

The curvature κ is given by the relation κ = M / (EI).

Substituting values: κ = 3000000 N·mm / (5 × 10^11 N·mm^2) = 6 × 10^-6 1/mm.

The radius of curvature ρ is the reciprocal of curvature: ρ = 1 / κ = EI / M.

Substituting values: ρ = 5 × 10^11 N·mm^2 / 3000000 N·mm = 166666.67 mm, or approximately 166.67 m.

The governing relations are:
The Euler-Bernoulli beam curvature-moment relation: κ = M / (EI).
The radius of curvature definition: ρ = 1 / κ = EI / M.

Additional assumptions include: the beam is initially straight, homogeneous, and isotropic; plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after deformation; and shear deformation is neglected (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies).
