The vertical bar is hanging from a rigid support at its top end with length L = 3500 mm, cross-sectional area A = 200 mm², Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa, and mass density ρ = 8900 kg/m³.

The bar is subjected to axial load due to self-weight only, with no external applied loads.

The deformation is small and the material behavior is linear-elastic.

The gravitational acceleration is g = 9.81 m/s².

The internal axial force varies linearly with position: at a distance x measured downward from the top support, the axial force is N(x) = ρ * g * A * (L - x).

The normal stress distribution is σ(x) = N(x) / A = ρ * g * (L - x).

The maximum tensile stress occurs at the top support where σ_max = ρ * g * L.

The strain distribution is ε(x) = σ(x) / E = ρ * g * (L - x) / E.

The elongation of a differential element dx at position x is dδ = ε(x) dx.

The total elongation of the bar is obtained by integration: δ = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x) dx = ∫₀ᴸ [ρ * g * (L - x) / E] dx.

Evaluating the integral yields δ = ρ * g * L² / (2 * E).

Numerically: δ = (8900 kg/m³) * (9.81 m/s²) * (3.5 m)² / (2 * 69000 × 10⁶ Pa) = 7.75 × 10⁻⁶ m = 0.00775 mm.

The elongation due to self-weight equals one-half the elongation that would occur if the total weight were applied as a concentrated load at the free end.

The displacement at any point x measured from the top is u(x) = ρ * g * (2 * L * x - x²) / (2 * E).
