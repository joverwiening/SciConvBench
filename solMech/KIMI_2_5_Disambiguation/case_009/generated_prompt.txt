The composite bar consists of two bars arranged in parallel between two rigid plates, subjected to axial tension.

The total applied tensile load is P = 20000 N applied to the top plate.

Bar 1 is AISI 1020 steel with cross-sectional area A₁ = 300 mm² and elastic modulus E₁ = 200000 MPa.

Bar 2 is cast iron with cross-sectional area A₂ = 250 mm² and elastic modulus E₂ = 110000 MPa.

Both bars have length L = 400 mm.

The governing assumptions are: small-deformation linear-elastic solid mechanics, perfect bonding between bars and rigid plates, rigid plates enforce equal displacement in both bars, no lateral contraction effects considered (uniaxial stress state), and load is applied concentrically to prevent bending.

The compatibility condition requires equal strain in both bars: ε₁ = ε₂ = ε.

The equilibrium condition requires the sum of internal forces equals the applied load: P₁ + P₂ = P.

The constitutive relations are: σ₁ = E₁ε and σ₂ = E₂ε, or equivalently P₁ = E₁A₁ε and P₂ = E₂A₂ε.

The governing equation for the system is: P = (E₁A₁ + E₂A₂)ε.

The common strain is: ε = P / (E₁A₁ + E₂A₂).

The internal forces in each bar are: P₁ = (E₁A₁ / (E₁A₁ + E₂A₂)) × P and P₂ = (E₂A₂ / (E₁A₁ + E₂A₂)) × P.

The stresses in each bar are: σ₁ = P₁/A₁ and σ₂ = P₂/A₂.

The total elongation of the composite bar is: δ = εL.

The effective stiffness of the composite system is: K_eff = E₁A₁/L + E₂A₂/L.

The effective modulus of the composite can be defined as: E_eff = (E₁A₁ + E₂A₂) / (A₁ + A₂) for reference purposes.
