The shaft is subjected to torsion loading only from power transmission, with no applied shear force.

The rotational speed of the shaft is 1200 rpm, which equals 125.66 rad/s.

The power being transmitted through the shaft is 5 kW.

The shaft diameter is 20 mm, giving a radius of 10 mm.

The shaft material is assumed to be homogeneous, isotropic, and linear-elastic with small deformations.

The power-torque conversion uses the relation P = T × ω, where P is power, T is torque, and ω is angular velocity.

The torque is calculated as T = P / ω = 5000 W / 125.66 rad/s = 39.79 N·m.

The polar moment of inertia for the solid circular cross-section is J = πd⁴/32 = π×(0.020 m)⁴/32 = 1.571×10⁻⁸ m⁴.

The maximum shear stress due to torsion occurs at the outer radius and is given by τ_max = T×r/J = (39.79 N·m)×(0.010 m)/(1.571×10⁻⁸ m⁴) = 25.33 MPa.

The angle of twist per unit length is dφ/dx = T/(G×J), where G is the shear modulus of the material.

The total angle of twist over a length L is φ = T×L/(G×J).

No shear loading is present, so no additional shear stress from transverse shear needs to be considered.
