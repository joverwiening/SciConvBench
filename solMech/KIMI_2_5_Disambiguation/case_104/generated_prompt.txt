The vertical bar has length L = 3000 mm = 3.0 m.

The bar has constant cross-sectional area A = 300 mm² = 300 × 10⁻⁶ m².

The material has Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa = 69 × 10⁹ Pa.

The material has mass density ρ = 8900 kg/m³.

The bar is hanging vertically downward from a rigid support at its top end, with gravity acting downward.

The acceleration due to gravity is g = 9.81 m/s².

The total weight of the bar is W = ρgAL.

The axial force at any cross-section located at distance x measured downward from the support equals the weight of the material below that section: N(x) = ρgA(L - x).

The normal stress distribution is σ(x) = N(x)/A = ρg(L - x).

The maximum tensile stress occurs at the top support (x = 0): σ_max = ρgL.

The minimum tensile stress is zero at the free bottom end (x = L).

The strain distribution is ε(x) = σ(x)/E = ρg(L - x)/E.

The elongation of a differential element dx at position x is dδ = ε(x)dx.

The total elongation is obtained by integration: δ = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x)dx = ∫₀ᴸ [ρg(L - x)/E]dx = ρgL²/(2E).

Numerically: δ = (8900 kg/m³)(9.81 m/s²)(3.0 m)² / (2 × 69 × 10⁹ Pa) = 5.69 × 10⁻⁶ m = 0.00569 mm.

The elongation due to self-weight equals half the elongation that would occur if the total weight were applied as a concentrated end load.

The displacement at any point x measured from the support is u(x) = ρg(2Lx - x²)/(2E).

The displacement is maximum at the free end: u(L) = δ = ρgL²/(2E).
