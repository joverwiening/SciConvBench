Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics with isotropic, homogeneous material behavior.

The beam has rectangular cross-section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 80 mm.

The internal shear force at the section of interest is V = 25000 N.

The transverse shear stress in a rectangular beam is governed by the shear formula: tau = VQ/(It), where V is the internal shear force, Q is the first moment of area about the neutral axis for the area above (or below) the point of interest, I is the second moment of area of the full cross-section about the neutral axis, and t is the width at the point of interest.

For a rectangular cross-section, the second moment of area about the neutral axis is I = bh^3/12 = (40 mm)(80 mm)^3/12 = 1.7067 × 10^6 mm^4.

The first moment of area Q for a rectangular section at distance y from the neutral axis is Q = (b/2)(h^2/4 - y^2).

The width t equals b = 40 mm for all y-locations in a rectangular cross-section.

For part (a), evaluate transverse shear stress at the neutral axis (y = 0): Q_max = bh^2/8 = (40 mm)(80 mm)^2/8 = 32000 mm^3, and tau_max = VQ_max/(Ib) = (25000 N)(32000 mm^3)/((1.7067 × 10^6 mm^4)(40 mm)) = 11.72 MPa.

For part (b), evaluate transverse shear stress at y = 20 mm from the neutral axis: Q = (40 mm/2)((80 mm)^2/4 - (20 mm)^2) = (20 mm)(1600 mm^2 - 400 mm^2) = 24000 mm^3, and tau = VQ/(Ib) = (25000 N)(24000 mm^3)/((1.7067 × 10^6 mm^4)(40 mm)) = 8.79 MPa.

The transverse shear stress distribution is parabolic through the beam height, with maximum value at the neutral axis and zero at the top and bottom surfaces.
