The rod is made of brass with isotropic, linear-elastic behavior under small deformations.

The rod has a circular cross-section with initial diameter d0 = 20 mm.

The rod has initial length L = 1000 mm.

The rod is subjected to uniaxial tension only with axial tensile load P = 25000 N. No lateral load is applied.

The material properties for brass are Young's modulus E = 105000 MPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.34.

The relevant response quantities to determine are the axial stress, axial strain, lateral strain (diameter change), and the resulting diameter after deformation, with focus on demonstrating the Poisson effect.

The governing relations are as follows.

Calculate the initial cross-sectional area: A0 = π × (d0/2)² = π × (10 mm)² = 100π mm².

Calculate the axial stress: σ = P / A0 = 25000 N / (100π mm²) = 250/π MPa ≈ 79.58 MPa.

Calculate the axial strain: ε_axial = σ / E = (250/π MPa) / (105000 MPa) = 250/(105000π) ≈ 7.579 × 10⁻⁴.

Calculate the lateral strain due to Poisson effect: ε_lateral = −ν × ε_axial = −0.34 × 250/(105000π) = −85/(105000π) ≈ −2.577 × 10⁻⁴.

The negative sign indicates contraction in the lateral direction.

Calculate the change in diameter: Δd = ε_lateral × d0 = −85/(105000π) × 20 mm = −1700/(105000π) mm ≈ −0.00515 mm.

Calculate the final diameter: d = d0 + Δd = 20 mm − 1700/(105000π) mm ≈ 19.99485 mm.

The Poisson effect is manifested as a reduction in diameter (lateral contraction) proportional to the axial elongation, with the constant of proportionality being Poisson's ratio ν = 0.34.
