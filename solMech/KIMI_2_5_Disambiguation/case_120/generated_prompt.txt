The bar is a linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics problem with axial loading.

The cross-sectional area varies linearly along the bar length according to A(x) = A₀·(1 + k·x/L), where A₀ = 200 mm² is the area at the left end (x = 0), k = 1.0 is the dimensionless taper parameter, and L = 500 mm is the total bar length.

The bar has constant length L = 500 mm.

A constant axial tensile force P = 12000 N acts along the entire bar. The load is concentrated and constant, not distributed, with tensile direction.

The material has Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The governing relations for axial deformation are derived from equilibrium, constitutive law, and kinematics.

The internal axial force is constant: N(x) = P = 12000 N for all x in [0, L].

The normal stress varies with position: σ(x) = N(x)/A(x) = P/[A₀·(1 + k·x/L)].

The axial strain varies with position: ε(x) = σ(x)/E = P/[E·A₀·(1 + k·x/L)].

The axial displacement u(x) is obtained by integrating the strain. With boundary condition u(0) = 0 (fixed at left end), the displacement is: u(x) = (P·L)/(E·A₀·k)·ln(1 + k·x/L).

The total elongation of the bar is: ΔL = u(L) - u(0) = (P·L)/(E·A₀·k)·ln(1 + k).

For the given numerical values: A₀ = 200 mm², k = 1.0, L = 500 mm, P = 12000 N, E = 200000 MPa = 200000 N/mm², the total elongation is ΔL = (12000 × 500)/(200000 × 200 × 1.0) × ln(2) = 0.15 × 0.6931 = 0.104 mm.
