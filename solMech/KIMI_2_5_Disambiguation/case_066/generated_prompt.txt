The shaft is a solid circular shaft with length L = 1000 mm and diameter d = 45 mm (radius R = 22.5 mm).

The shaft is subjected only to a torque T = 400000 N·mm with no applied shear force.

The material is linear-elastic with shear modulus G = 76923.1 MPa (equivalent to 76923.1 N/mm²).

The polar moment of inertia for the solid circular cross-section is J = πd⁴/32 = π(45 mm)⁴/32 = 201288.9 mm⁴.

The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface and is calculated as τ_max = TR/J = (400000 N·mm)(22.5 mm)/(201288.9 mm⁴) = 44.7 MPa.

The shear stress varies linearly with radius: τ(r) = Tr/J for 0 ≤ r ≤ R.

The angle of twist for the entire length is φ = TL/(GJ) = (400000 N·mm)(1000 mm)/((76923.1 N/mm²)(201288.9 mm⁴)) = 0.0258 rad = 1.48 degrees.

The rate of twist is dφ/dx = T/(GJ) = 0.0000258 rad/mm.

Additional assumptions: the shaft is prismatic (constant cross-section), homogeneous, isotropic, material properties are constant throughout, and Saint-Venant's principle applies so that end effects are neglected.
