Q: When you say “take the bending moment to be the constant peak value M = P L over the full span,” should I follow that instruction literally (i.e., use M(x) = P·L for 0 ≤ x ≤ L), even though it is not the actual cantilever moment distribution?
A: No—don’t follow that literally.

In the COMPLETE requirement, you should **set up the actual bending moment distribution** for a cantilever with an end point load, i.e. use **\(M(x)\)** as a function of \(x\) (e.g., \(M(x)=P(L-x)\) with \(x\) measured from the fixed end), then integrate \(U=\int_0^L \frac{M(x)^2}{2EI}\,dx\) and differentiate \(U\) with respect to \(P\).
