Q: When you say “set εz = 0 because σz = 0,” should I enforce εz = 0 as a plane-strain constraint even though generalized Hooke’s law would generally give εz ≠ 0 when σx and σy are nonzero?
A: No—do **not** enforce \( \varepsilon_z = 0\). The complete requirement only specifies \( \sigma_z = 0\) (plane stress), so you should compute \( \varepsilon_z\) from generalized Hooke’s law; it will generally be nonzero due to Poisson effects from \( \sigma_x\) and \( \sigma_y\).
