Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.
Model a 1D axially loaded prismatic bar with constant cross-sectional area A = 200 mm^2 and modulus E = 200000 MPa.
Use node locations A (x = 0), B (x = 700 mm), C (x = 1300 mm), D (x = 1800 mm).
Use segment lengths AB = 700 mm, BC = 600 mm, CD = 500 mm.
Apply concentrated axial forces (positive to the right) of +18000 N at B, −8000 N at C, and +20000 N at D.
Impose the boundary condition u_A = 0 at A.
Enforce global axial equilibrium to determine the reaction at A.
Compute the internal axial force N(x) as piecewise-constant over each segment AB, BC, and CD, with jumps at B and C equal to the applied point loads at those locations.
Use the sign convention that positive internal axial force corresponds to tension.
Report the internal axial force in each segment as N_AB, N_BC, and N_CD in N.
Compute the normal stress in each segment as σ = N/A and report σ_AB, σ_BC, and σ_CD in MPa.
Compute the axial strain in each segment as ε = σ/E.
Compute the elongation/contraction of each segment as δ = (N L)/(A E) and sum segment contributions to obtain the displacement of D relative to A.
Report u_D − u_A as the displacement of point D relative to A in mm.
