Q: When you say “take the normal stress to be identical in the two segments,” do you want me to follow that instruction even though it contradicts axial mechanics for a stepped bar (same force but different areas implies different stresses)?
A: No—do **not** force the stresses to be identical.

Follow standard axial mechanics for a stepped bar: the **axial force is the same** in both segments (equal to \(P\)), so the **normal stress differs** by area:
\[
\sigma_1=\frac{P}{A_1},\qquad \sigma_2=\frac{P}{A_2}.
\]

For deformation, treat the segments as **in series**, so the **total elongation** is the sum:
\[
\delta=\frac{PL_1}{A_1E}+\frac{PL_2}{A_2E}.
\]

The “identical stress” / “common elongation before combining stiffnesses” wording is not part of the complete requirement and should be ignored.
