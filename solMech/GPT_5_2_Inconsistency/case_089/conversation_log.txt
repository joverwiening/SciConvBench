Q: Your instruction says to “treat the internal axial force as uniform along the full length” using the net applied load, but the request also asks for the internal axial force in each segment, which would generally differ when point loads act at B and C.

Should I follow the physically correct segmented approach (different internal force in AB, BC, and CD based on equilibrium across each load point)?
A: Follow the physically correct segmented approach.

Compute the internal axial force separately in AB, BC, and CD using equilibrium with the point loads at B, C, and D (with A providing the reaction since \(u_A=0\)). Then compute stress per segment as \(\sigma = N/A\) and the displacement of D as the sum of segment elongations/shortenings \(\Delta = \sum (N L)/(A E)\).
