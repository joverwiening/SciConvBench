Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.
Model a 1D axially loaded prismatic bar with constant cross-sectional area A = 300 mm^2 and modulus E = 200000 MPa.
Define points A (left end), B, C, D (right end) with segment lengths AB = 500 mm, BC = 600 mm, CD = 400 mm.
Apply concentrated axial forces at the points with positive to the right: at B, +18000 N; at C, −12000 N; at D, +16000 N.
Impose the boundary condition u_A = 0 at the fixed left end.
Use the sign convention that internal axial force N is positive in tension and negative in compression, and normal stress is σ = N/A with σ positive in tension.
Compute the support reaction at A from global axial equilibrium of the entire bar.
Compute the internal axial force in each segment (AB, BC, CD) using a cut in that segment and equilibrium of the left free body including the reaction at A and any applied point loads to the left of the cut.
Report N_AB, N_BC, and N_CD with sign per the tension-positive convention.
Compute the normal stress in each segment as σ_AB = N_AB/A, σ_BC = N_BC/A, and σ_CD = N_CD/A, reported in MPa with sign.
Compute the axial strain in each segment as ε_i = σ_i/E.
Compute the elongation/contraction of each segment as δ_i = (N_i L_i)/(A E) using that segment’s N_i and length L_i.
Compute the displacement of point D relative to A as u_D − u_A = δ_AB + δ_BC + δ_CD, with u_A = 0, and report u_D in mm with sign.
