Assume the beam is made of structural steel.
Assume a Young's modulus of 210 GPa.
Assume a Poisson's ratio of 0.3.
Assume a yield strength of 250 MPa.
Assume the beam is simply supported.
Assume the beam length is 2 meters.
Assume the bending moment is applied uniformly along the beam's length.
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The applied bending moment is M = 3,000,000 N·mm (sagging).
The neutral axis is located at the mid-height of the cross-section.
The moment of inertia for the rectangular cross-section is I = (b * h^3) / 12.
The bending stress at an arbitrary fiber location y (measured from the neutral axis) is given by σ = (M * y) / I.
The maximum bending stress occurs at the outermost fibers (y = ± h/2).
Calculate the moment of inertia I.
Calculate the bending stress σ for any given distance y from the neutral axis.
Check if the maximum bending stress exceeds the assumed yield strength.
