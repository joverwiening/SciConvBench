The bar is prismatic with a constant cross-sectional area A = 300 mm².
The bar length is L = 800 mm.
The material is linear-elastic with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.
A suddenly applied axial load of magnitude P = 8000 N is applied.
Assume the load is applied instantaneously with no initial kinetic energy in the bar.
Assume the bar is massless, and the system's inertia is modeled by an equivalent lumped mass or via conservation of energy.
The static axial stress under load P is σ_static = P / A.
For a suddenly applied load, the maximum stress is twice the static stress: σ_max = 2 * σ_static.
The governing relation for maximum stress via energy is derived from equating the work done by the load to the strain energy in the bar at maximum deformation.
The work done by the constant load P through the maximum displacement δ_max is W = P * δ_max.
The strain energy stored at maximum displacement is U = (1/2) * (A*E / L) * δ_max².
Setting W = U gives P * δ_max = (1/2) * (A*E / L) * δ_max², solving to δ_max = 2 * (P*L)/(A*E).
The corresponding maximum stress is σ_max = E * (δ_max / L) = 2 * (P / A).
Calculate the static stress: σ_static = 8000 N / 300 mm² = 26.67 MPa.
Calculate the maximum dynamic stress: σ_max = 2 * 26.67 MPa = 53.33 MPa.
The relevant response quantities are the static stress σ_static and the maximum dynamic stress σ_max.
