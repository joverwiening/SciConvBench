Assume a rectangular cross-section for the beam, as it is a common and fundamental shape for illustrative examples.
Assume a beam length of 1000 mm for calculation context.
The beam is made of AISI 1020 steel, which is linear-elastic with a Young's modulus (E) of approximately 200 GPa (200,000 N/mm²).
The applied pure bending moment is M = 3,000,000 N·mm.
Assume the bending moment is constant along the length of the beam (pure bending).
The relevant second moment of area (I) for a rectangular cross-section with width (b) and height (h) is I = (b * h³) / 12.
Assume a rectangular cross-section with dimensions b = 50 mm and h = 100 mm for calculation.
Therefore, I = (50 mm * (100 mm)³) / 12 = 4,166,667 mm⁴.
The governing relation for curvature (κ) in linear-elastic bending is κ = M / (E * I).
Calculate the curvature: κ = 3,000,000 N·mm / (200,000 N/mm² * 4,166,667 mm⁴) = 3.6e-6 mm⁻¹.
The radius of curvature (R) is the reciprocal of the curvature: R = 1 / κ.
Calculate the radius of curvature: R = 1 / (3.6e-6 mm⁻¹) ≈ 277,778 mm.
