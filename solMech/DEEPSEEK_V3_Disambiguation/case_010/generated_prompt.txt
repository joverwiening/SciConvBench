The problem is a composite bar system with two parallel bars between rigid plates under axial tensile loading.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior.
Bar 1 is structural steel.
Bar 2 is cast iron.
The cross-sectional area of Bar 1 (A1) is 150 mm².
The cross-sectional area of Bar 2 (A2) is 200 mm².
The total tensile load (P) applied to the rigid plates is 30000 N.
The length (L) of both bars is 800 mm.
Assume both bars are perfectly bonded to the rigid plates, ensuring equal axial elongation.
Assume the load is applied centrally and aligned, preventing bending.
The relevant response quantities are the stress in each bar, the strain in each bar, the internal force carried by each bar, and the total elongation of the assembly.
Use the following material properties: for structural steel, Young's modulus E1 = 200 GPa; for cast iron, Young's modulus E2 = 100 GPa.
The governing kinematic relation is: strain in Bar 1 (ε1) equals strain in Bar 2 (ε2) equals the total strain (ε).
The governing constitutive (stress-strain) relations are: σ1 = E1 * ε1 and σ2 = E2 * ε2.
The governing equilibrium relation is: total load P equals the sum of the forces in each bar: P = P1 + P2 = (σ1 * A1) + (σ2 * A2).
Solve the system of equations: ε = ε1 = ε2, P = (E1 * ε * A1) + (E2 * ε * A2).
Calculate the common strain ε = P / (E1*A1 + E2*A2).
Calculate the stress in each bar: σ1 = E1 * ε and σ2 = E2 * ε.
Calculate the force carried by each bar: P1 = σ1 * A1 and P2 = σ2 * A2.
Calculate the total elongation of the assembly: δ = ε * L.
