The shaft is a solid circular cylinder made of 6061-T6 aluminum.
The shaft has a total length L = 1500 mm.
The shaft has a diameter d = 40 mm.
The shaft is fixed (fully constrained) at the end x = 0 mm.
A torque T2 = -150000 N·mm is applied at the cross-section located at x = 750 mm.
A torque T1 = 300000 N·mm is applied at the free end located at x = 1500 mm.
The material is assumed to be linear-elastic, isotropic, and to undergo small deformations.
The shaft is assumed to be prismatic (constant cross-section).
The analysis will determine the internal torque distribution along the shaft length.
The analysis will determine the shear stress distribution in each shaft segment.
The analysis will determine the angle of twist for each shaft segment and the total angle of twist at the free end.
The polar moment of inertia J for the solid circular cross-section is calculated as J = (π * d^4) / 32.
The shear modulus G for 6061-T6 aluminum is required; use G = 26 GPa (2.6e4 N/mm²) unless otherwise specified.
The internal torque T(x) is found by applying equilibrium from the free end.
For segment C (750 mm ≤ x ≤ 1500 mm), the internal torque is constant and equal to T1 = 300000 N·mm.
For segment B (0 mm ≤ x < 750 mm), the internal torque is constant and equal to T1 + T2 = 150000 N·mm.
The maximum shear stress τ_max in each segment is calculated as τ_max = (T * r) / J, where r = d/2.
The angle of twist φ for a segment of length L_seg with constant internal torque T_seg is φ_seg = (T_seg * L_seg) / (G * J).
The total angle of twist at the free end (x=1500 mm) relative to the fixed end (x=0 mm) is the sum of the twists of all segments.
