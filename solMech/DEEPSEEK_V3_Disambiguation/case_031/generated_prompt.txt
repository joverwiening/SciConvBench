The problem is to determine the elongation of a vertical bar due to its own weight under small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The bar is made of a linearly elastic material with Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa and density ρ = 8900 kg/m³.
The bar has a length L = 3500 mm.
The bar has a constant cross-sectional area A = 200 mm².
The bar is oriented vertically, hanging from a rigid support at its top end.
Gravity acts downward along the bar's length.
The only load acting on the bar is its self-weight; there is no external axial load.
The relevant response quantity is the total elongation (increase in length) of the bar, denoted as δ.
The governing relation for the elongation δ of a vertical bar under self-weight is δ = (ρ * g * L²) / (2 * E).
The acceleration due to gravity g is assumed to be 9.81 m/s².
The units for the calculation must be consistent; it is assumed to use SI base units (Pa, kg, m, s) for the governing relation.
The final answer for elongation δ will be provided in a consistent unit, typically meters or millimeters.
