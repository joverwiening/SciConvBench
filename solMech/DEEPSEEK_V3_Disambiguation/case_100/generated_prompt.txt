The task is to determine the end deflection of a cantilever beam under an end point load using Castigliano's theorem.
Assume a prismatic beam with constant cross-section and material properties.
Assume the beam is homogeneous, isotropic, and follows linear-elastic (Hooke's law) material behavior.
Assume small deformations and Euler-Bernoulli beam theory (plane sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis).
Neglect shear deformation for this calculation.
Define the beam length as L, with the fixed support at x=0 and the free end at x=L.
Apply a downward vertical point load P at the free end (x=L).
The bending moment at a section located at distance x from the fixed support is M(x) = -P * (L - x).
The strain energy U due to bending is U = ∫[0 to L] (M(x)^2 / (2EI)) dx, where E is Young's modulus and I is the area moment of inertia of the cross-section.
Apply Castigliano's theorem: the deflection δ at the point of load application in the direction of P is δ = ∂U/∂P.
Compute the partial derivative: ∂U/∂P = ∂/∂P [ ∫[0 to L] ( (-P*(L-x))^2 / (2EI) ) dx ].
Perform the differentiation inside the integral: ∂/∂P [ P^2 * (L-x)^2 / (2EI) ] = (2P * (L-x)^2) / (2EI) = (P * (L-x)^2) / (EI).
The deflection is δ = ∫[0 to L] (P * (L-x)^2) / (EI) dx.
Evaluate the integral: δ = (P / EI) ∫[0 to L] (L^2 - 2Lx + x^2) dx.
Compute the integral: ∫[0 to L] (L^2 - 2Lx + x^2) dx = [ L^2*x - L*x^2 + (1/3)x^3 ] evaluated from 0 to L.
The result is L^3 - L^3 + (1/3)L^3 = (1/3)L^3.
Therefore, the end deflection is δ = (P / EI) * (L^3 / 3) = (P L^3) / (3EI).
The governing relation is the Castigliano's theorem: deflection = partial derivative of strain energy with respect to the applied load.
The final result for the cantilever end deflection under an end point load is δ = (P L^3) / (3EI).
