Assume a uniaxial state of stress within the gauge section of the specimen.
Assume the material is isotropic, homogeneous, and follows a linear-elastic constitutive law.
The specimen has an initial, uniform cross-sectional area A0 and an initial gauge length L0.
An axial force F is applied, resulting in an axial engineering stress σ = F / A0.
The specimen experiences an axial engineering strain ε = (L - L0) / L0, where L is the current gauge length.
The relevant response quantities are the engineering stress σ and the engineering strain ε.
The modulus of elasticity E is defined by the linear relation σ = E * ε within the elastic regime.
The governing relation for the linear-elastic response is Hooke's Law in one dimension: σ = Eε.
The analysis is valid only for deformations where ε is small and the material response remains linear and elastic.
