The bar is a linearly elastic, small-deformation solid with a linearly varying cross-sectional area.
The bar length L is 800 mm.
The cross-sectional area at the left end (x=0) is A0 = 250 mm².
The cross-sectional area at the right end (x=L) is A(L) = 450 mm², defined by a factor k=0.8 such that A(L) = A0*(1+k).
The area varies linearly along the length: A(x) = A0 * (1 + (k*x)/L).
A constant axial tensile load P = 30000 N is applied.
The material has a Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The load is applied quasistatically, and stress is uniformly distributed across any given cross-section.
The relevant response quantity is the total axial deformation (elongation) Δ.
The governing relation for elongation is derived from integrating the infinitesimal strain: Δ = ∫₀ᴸ (P/(E*A(x))) dx.
The closed-form solution for the elongation is Δ = (P*L/(E*A0*k)) * ln(1+k).
