Assume the beam is made of a generic isotropic, linear-elastic material.
Assume the material is structural steel with a Young's modulus of 210,000 N/mm².
Assume the material's Poisson's ratio is 0.3.
Assume the beam length is 1000 mm.
The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The beam is subjected to a pure sagging bending moment M = 4,000,000 N·mm.
The neutral axis is located at the mid-height of the cross-section.
The second moment of area (I) for the rectangular cross-section is calculated as I = (b * h³) / 12.
The bending stress (σ) at an arbitrary fiber location, defined by distance y from the neutral axis, is given by the flexure formula: σ = (M * y) / I.
The maximum tensile and compressive bending stresses occur at the extreme fibers, where y = ± h/2.
The shear stress is assumed to be zero for this case of pure bending.
The analysis is valid within the limits of small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
