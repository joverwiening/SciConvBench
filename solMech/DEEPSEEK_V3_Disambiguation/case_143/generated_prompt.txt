Assume the shaft is made of a common engineering steel with a Young's modulus (E) of 210 GPa and a Poisson's ratio (ν) of 0.3.
Assume the shaft length (L) is 1 meter for calculation purposes, though stress and strain results are independent of length.
The shaft has a solid circular cross-section with a diameter (d) of 25 mm.
The shaft is subjected to a torque (T) of 400,000 N·mm.
The shaft is subjected to an axial tensile load (P) of 15,000 N.
Calculate the cross-sectional area (A) as A = π*(d/2)^2.
Calculate the polar moment of inertia (J) for the cross-section as J = (π*d^4)/32.
The axial stress (σ_axial) is uniform over the cross-section and equals P/A.
The shear stress due to torsion (τ_torsion) varies linearly from zero at the center to a maximum at the outer radius (r = d/2), calculated as τ_max = (T * r) / J.
For combined stress evaluation, consider a point on the outer surface of the shaft where stresses are maximum.
At this point, the state of stress is defined by the axial stress σ_x = σ_axial and the shear stress τ_xy = τ_max, with all other stress components being zero.
Calculate the principal stresses (σ1, σ2) using the formula for a plane stress state: σ1,2 = (σ_x/2) ± √((σ_x/2)^2 + τ_xy^2).
Calculate the maximum shear stress (τ_max_inplane) as τ_max_inplane = √((σ_x/2)^2 + τ_xy^2).
Calculate the axial strain (ε_axial) using Hooke's Law: ε_axial = σ_axial / E.
Calculate the shear strain (γ) at the outer surface using the shear modulus (G = E/(2*(1+ν))): γ = τ_max / G.
Calculate the principal strains (ε1, ε2) using the strain transformation relations or by converting from principal stresses via Hooke's Law for plane stress: ε1 = (1/E)*(σ1 - ν*σ2), ε2 = (1/E)*(σ2 - ν*σ1).
Report the numerical values for the cross-sectional area (A), polar moment of inertia (J), axial stress (σ_axial), maximum torsional shear stress (τ_max), principal stresses (σ1, σ2), maximum in-plane shear stress (τ_max_inplane), axial strain (ε_axial), shear strain (γ), and principal strains (ε1, ε2).
