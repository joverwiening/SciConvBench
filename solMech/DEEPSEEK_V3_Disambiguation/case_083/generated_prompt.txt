Assume the shaft is made of a typical structural steel with a Young's modulus (E) of 200 GPa and a Poisson's ratio (ν) of 0.3.
Assume the shaft is homogeneous, isotropic, and obeys linear-elastic, small-deformation theory.
The shaft has a solid circular cross-section with a diameter (d) of 25 mm.
The shaft is subjected to a torque (T) of 300,000 N·mm.
The shaft is subjected to an axial tensile load (P) of 25,000 N.
Calculate the cross-sectional area (A) as A = π*(d/2)^2.
Calculate the polar moment of inertia (J) for the cross-section as J = (π*d^4)/32.
Calculate the normal stress due to axial load (σ_axial) as σ_axial = P/A.
Calculate the shear stress due to torsion (τ_torsion) at the outer fiber as τ_torsion = (T * (d/2)) / J.
The state of stress at a point on the outer surface is a combination of σ_x = σ_axial, τ_xy = τ_torsion, and σ_y = 0.
Calculate the principal stresses (σ1, σ2) using the formula for a plane stress state: σ1,2 = (σ_x/2) ± √((σ_x/2)^2 + τ_xy^2).
Calculate the maximum shear stress (τ_max) as τ_max = √((σ_x/2)^2 + τ_xy^2).
Calculate the principal strains (ε1, ε2) using generalized Hooke's Law for plane stress: ε1 = (1/E)*(σ1 - ν*σ2) and ε2 = (1/E)*(σ2 - ν*σ1).
Calculate the maximum shear strain (γ_max) as γ_max = (1/G)*τ_max, where the shear modulus G = E/(2*(1+ν)).
The relevant response quantities are the axial stress, torsional shear stress, principal stresses, maximum shear stress, principal strains, and maximum shear strain.
