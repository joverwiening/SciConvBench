Assume the column is made of structural steel with a Young's modulus (E) of 200 GPa and a yield strength (σ_y) of 250 MPa.
Assume the column length (L) is 1.0 m, which is short relative to its cross-section, making global buckling unlikely.
The rectangular cross-section has a width (b) of 100 mm and a height (h) of 100 mm.
The column is subjected to an eccentric axial compressive load (P) of 90000 N.
The load eccentricity (e) is 15 mm, measured from the centroid along the width direction (x-axis), causing bending about the centroidal z-axis.
The relevant response quantities are the combined axial and bending stresses, the maximum compressive and tensile stresses, and the mid-height lateral deflection.
The governing relation for combined axial and bending stress at any point (y) from the neutral axis is σ = P/A ± (P*e*y)/I_z.
The cross-sectional area is A = b * h = 0.01 m².
The moment of inertia about the z-axis is I_z = (b * h³)/12 = 8.333e-6 m⁴.
The maximum compressive stress occurs at the extreme fiber on the same side as the eccentricity (y = -h/2).
The maximum tensile stress occurs at the opposite extreme fiber (y = h/2).
The mid-height lateral deflection (δ) for a short column with fixed-pinned ends under eccentric load is approximated by δ = e * [sec((π/2)*√(P/P_cr)) - 1], where P_cr is the Euler buckling load.
The Euler buckling load for the weak axis (z-axis) with an effective length factor K=0.7 for fixed-pinned is P_cr = (π² * E * I_z) / (K*L)².
Check that the maximum combined stress does not exceed the material yield strength.
Assume the column is prismatic, homogeneous, and isotropic, and that plane sections remain plane.
