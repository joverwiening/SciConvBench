The stepped solid circular shaft is made of 6061-T6 aluminum, assumed to be linear-elastic.
Segment AB has a diameter of 25 mm and a length of 400 mm.
Segment BC has a diameter of 30 mm and a length of 900 mm.
A torque of 750000 N·mm is applied at the free end C, with end A fixed.
No shear force or other transverse loading is applied.
The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics and pure torsion.
The shaft is prismatic within each segment, with an abrupt change in diameter at B.
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
The governing relation for shear stress in a circular shaft under torsion is τ = (T * r) / J, where T is the internal torque, r is the radial coordinate, and J is the polar moment of inertia.
The governing relation for the angle of twist in a prismatic segment is φ = (T * L) / (J * G), where L is the segment length and G is the shear modulus.
The internal torque is constant and equal to the applied torque T throughout the entire shaft.
The polar moment of inertia for segment AB is J_AB = (π * (d1)^4) / 32.
The polar moment of inertia for segment BC is J_BC = (π * (d2)^4) / 32.
The maximum shear stress in segment AB occurs at its outer radius and is τ_max_AB = (T * (d1/2)) / J_AB.
The maximum shear stress in segment BC occurs at its outer radius and is τ_max_BC = (T * (d2/2)) / J_BC.
The total angle of twist at end C relative to fixed end A is the sum of the twists in each segment: φ_total = φ_AB + φ_BC = (T * L1) / (J_AB * G) + (T * L2) / (J_BC * G).
The shear modulus G for 6061-T6 aluminum is required to compute the twist.
