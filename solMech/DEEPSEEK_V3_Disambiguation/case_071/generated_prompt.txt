Assume the structural member is made of a common structural steel.
Use a Young's modulus (E) of 200 GPa and a Poisson's ratio (ν) of 0.3.
The member is a rectangular prismatic bar with a width (b) of 50 mm and a height (h) of 80 mm.
The bar is subjected to an axial tension force (P) of 40000 N.
The bar is subjected to a transverse shear force (V) of 12000 N.
Assume the loads are applied such that they create a state of plane stress in the member.
Calculate the average normal stress (σ_avg) from the tension load as P divided by the cross-sectional area (A = b * h).
Calculate the average shear stress (τ_avg) from the shear load as V divided by the cross-sectional area (A = b * h).
The cross-sectional area A is 4000 mm².
The average normal stress σ_avg is 10 MPa.
The average shear stress τ_avg is 3 MPa.
For a combined state of average normal and shear stress, the stress tensor in the plane is [[σ_avg, τ_avg], [τ_avg, 0]].
Calculate the principal stresses (σ₁, σ₂) using the formula for a 2D stress state: σ₁,₂ = (σ_avg/2) ± √((σ_avg/2)² + τ_avg²).
The maximum principal stress σ₁ is approximately 11.54 MPa.
The minimum principal stress σ₂ is approximately -1.54 MPa.
Calculate the maximum shear stress (τ_max) as (σ₁ - σ₂)/2.
The maximum shear stress τ_max is approximately 6.54 MPa.
Calculate the principal strains (ε₁, ε₂) using Hooke's Law for plane stress: ε₁ = (1/E)(σ₁ - νσ₂) and ε₂ = (1/E)(σ₂ - νσ₁).
The principal strain ε₁ is approximately 6.00e-05.
The principal strain ε₂ is approximately -2.31e-05.
The relevant response quantities are the average normal stress, average shear stress, principal stresses, maximum shear stress, and principal strains.
