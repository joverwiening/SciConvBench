The problem is to design the diameter of a solid circular shaft made of AISI 1020 steel.
The shaft is subjected to a constant torque of 600000 N·mm along its entire length of 800 mm.
The material is linear-elastic with a shear modulus of 76923.1 MPa.
The design is governed by an angle-of-twist limit of 1.5 degrees over the shaft's length.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics and a prismatic, solid circular cross-section.
Assume the torque is applied at the ends and the shaft is free to twist.
The governing relation for angle of twist φ is φ = (T * L) / (J * G), where J is the polar moment of inertia.
For a solid circular shaft of diameter d, the polar moment of inertia is J = (π * d⁴) / 32.
The design requirement is φ_calculated ≤ φ_allowable.
Solve the equation φ_allowable = (T * L) / ( (π * d⁴ / 32) * G ) for the minimum required diameter d.
Substitute the given values: φ_allow = 1.5°, T = 6e5 N·mm, L = 800 mm, G = 76923.1 MPa = 76923.1 N/mm².
Convert the allowable angle of twist to radians: φ_allow_rad = 1.5 * (π / 180).
Calculate the required diameter d from the rearranged formula: d⁴ = (32 * T * L) / (π * G * φ_allow_rad).
Compute the numerical value for d and report it as the design diameter.
