The problem is a hollow circular shaft under pure torsion.
The shaft is made of a linear-elastic, isotropic material.
The shaft geometry is defined by an outer radius of 30 mm and an inner radius of 24 mm.
The shaft length is 800 mm.
The material has a shear modulus G of 76923.1 MPa.
The shaft is subjected to a constant applied torque T of 450000 N·mm.
The analysis assumes small deformations and Saint-Venant's principle applies away from the ends.
The polar moment of inertia J for the hollow cross-section is (π/2) * (R_outer^4 - R_inner^4).
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer radius and is calculated as τ_max = (T * R_outer) / J.
The angle of twist φ over the shaft length is calculated as φ = (T * L) / (G * J).
The shear stress distribution is linear from zero at the center to a maximum at the outer surface.
The governing relation for shear stress is τ(r) = (T * r) / J, where r is the radial coordinate.
The governing relation for the angle of twist is φ = (T * L) / (G * J).
