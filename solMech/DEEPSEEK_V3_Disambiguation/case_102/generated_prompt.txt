The bar is made of AISI 1020 steel, which is linear-elastic.
The bar is a circular rod with a diameter of 25 mm.
The bar has a length of 800 mm.
The bar is fixed at its top end.
A collar is attached to the bottom end of the bar; the collar's diameter is assumed to be sufficiently larger than the bar's diameter to act as a rigid impact surface.
A static axial tensile load of 10 kN is applied to the bar, representing a reasonable preload for such a scenario.
A weight of 1500 N is dropped onto the collar from a height of 200 mm.
The impact is modeled as a sudden axial force applied at the collar.
The system is analyzed under small-deformation, linear-elastic assumptions.
The relevant response quantities are the maximum dynamic axial stress in the bar, the maximum dynamic axial deflection of the bar, and the dynamic amplification factor.
The static axial stress is calculated from the preload and cross-sectional area.
The static axial deflection is calculated from the preload, length, cross-sectional area, and Young's modulus.
The equivalent static impact load is calculated from the potential energy of the dropped weight and the static stiffness of the bar.
The maximum dynamic load is the sum of the static preload and the equivalent static impact load.
The maximum dynamic stress is the maximum dynamic load divided by the cross-sectional area.
The maximum dynamic deflection is the maximum dynamic load divided by the static stiffness.
The dynamic amplification factor is the ratio of the maximum dynamic load to the static preload.
The governing relation for static stress is sigma_static = P_static / A.
The governing relation for static deflection is delta_static = (P_static * L) / (A * E).
The governing relation for the bar's axial stiffness is k = (A * E) / L.
The governing relation for the impact energy is U = W * h.
The governing relation for the equivalent static impact load is P_impact = sqrt(2 * k * U).
The governing relation for the maximum dynamic load is P_dynamic_max = P_static + P_impact.
The governing relation for the maximum dynamic stress is sigma_dynamic_max = P_dynamic_max / A.
The governing relation for the maximum dynamic deflection is delta_dynamic_max = P_dynamic_max / k.
The governing relation for the dynamic amplification factor is DAF = P_dynamic_max / P_static.
