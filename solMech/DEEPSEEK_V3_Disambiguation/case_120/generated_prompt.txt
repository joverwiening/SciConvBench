The bar is made of AISI 1020 steel, assumed to be linear-elastic.
The bar length L is 500 mm.
The cross-sectional area varies linearly from A0 = 200 mm² at x = 0 to AL = 400 mm² at x = L.
A concentrated tensile axial load P = 12000 N is applied.
The analysis assumes small deformations and static equilibrium.
The bar is prismatic in the sense of having a straight axis and a cross-section that varies only in area.
The governing relation is the one-dimensional elastic constitutive law: σ(x) = E * ε(x).
The axial strain is defined as ε(x) = du/dx, where u(x) is the axial displacement.
The axial stress is σ(x) = N(x) / A(x), where N(x) is the internal axial force.
For static equilibrium with only end loads, the internal axial force N(x) is constant and equal to P.
The cross-sectional area as a function of axial coordinate x is A(x) = A0 + (AL - A0)*(x/L).
The axial stress as a function of x is σ(x) = P / A(x).
The axial strain as a function of x is ε(x) = σ(x) / E = P / (E * A(x)).
The axial displacement u(x) is found by integrating the strain: u(x) = ∫0^x ε(ξ) dξ.
The total axial deformation (elongation) is δ = u(L) = ∫0^L [P / (E * A(x))] dx.
The integral for δ evaluates to δ = (P * L / (E * (AL - A0))) * ln(AL / A0).
