Assume the prismatic bar is made of structural steel with a Young's modulus of 200 GPa.
Assume a solid circular cross-section for the bar, consistent with common practice for axially loaded members.
The cross-sectional area is 500 mm².
The total bar length is 1400 mm, segmented as AB = 700 mm, BC = 400 mm, and CD = 300 mm.
The bar is subjected to axial point loads: a 15000 N force to the right at point B, a 12000 N force to the left at point C, and a 10000 N force to the right at point D.
The left end (point A) is fixed, providing zero axial displacement.
The governing relation is the one-dimensional Hooke's law: σ = E * ε, where σ is axial stress, E is Young's modulus, and ε is axial strain.
The governing relation for axial deformation is δ = (P * L) / (A * E), where δ is axial deformation, P is axial force, L is length, A is area, and E is Young's modulus.
The relevant response quantities to determine are the axial force (P) in each segment (AB, BC, CD).
The relevant response quantities to determine are the axial stress (σ) in each segment.
The relevant response quantities to determine are the axial strain (ε) in each segment.
The relevant response quantities to determine are the total axial deformation (δ) at each point (B, C, D) relative to the fixed support at A.
The solution requires constructing an axial force diagram by considering equilibrium of free-body diagrams for each segment.
