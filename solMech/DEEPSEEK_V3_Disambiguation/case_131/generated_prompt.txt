Assume the material is isotropic, linear-elastic steel with Young's modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.
Assume a state of plane stress exists in the member.
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 40 mm and height h = 120 mm.
The axial tension load is P = 40000 N, applied as an internal axial force through the centroid.
The shear load is V = 8000 N, resulting in an average shear stress τ_avg = V/A.
Calculate the cross-sectional area A = b * h.
Calculate the average normal stress σ_x = P / A.
Calculate the average shear stress τ_xy = V / A.
The stress state at a point is defined by σ_x, σ_y = 0, and τ_xy.
Calculate the in-plane principal stresses σ_1 and σ_2 using the formula: σ_1,2 = (σ_x/2) ± √((σ_x/2)² + τ_xy²).
Calculate the maximum in-plane shear stress τ_max = √((σ_x/2)² + τ_xy²).
Calculate the principal angles θ_p using the formula: tan(2θ_p) = (2τ_xy) / σ_x.
Calculate the corresponding principal strains ε_1 and ε_2 using plane-stress Hooke's Law: ε_1 = (1/E)(σ_1 - νσ_2) and ε_2 = (1/E)(σ_2 - νσ_1).
Calculate the maximum in-plane shear strain γ_max = (1/G) * τ_max, where G = E/(2(1+ν)).
Report all calculated stress and strain quantities with units.
