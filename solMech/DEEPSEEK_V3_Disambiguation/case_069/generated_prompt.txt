Assume a rectangular cross-section for the beam as a common, simple shape.
Assume the beam length is 5000 mm as a typical span.
Assume the beam is made of structural steel with a Young's modulus of 200 GPa.
Assume the beam is prismatic and homogeneous.
Assume the material obeys linear-elastic, isotropic behavior.
Assume small deformations and plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
The applied bending moment is M = 3,000,000 N·mm about the neutral axis.
For a rectangular cross-section, the second moment of area (I) must be calculated.
Assume a cross-section width (b) of 100 mm and height (h) of 200 mm.
Calculate the second moment of area: I = (b * h^3) / 12.
The governing relation for curvature (κ) is κ = M / (E * I).
The radius of curvature (R) is the reciprocal of curvature: R = 1 / κ.
The primary response quantities are the curvature (κ) and the radius of curvature (R).
