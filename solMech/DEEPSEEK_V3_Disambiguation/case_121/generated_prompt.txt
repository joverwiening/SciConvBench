Assume the column is made of structural steel with a Young's modulus of 200 GPa and a yield strength of 250 MPa.
Assume the column length is 2.0 m, which is short relative to its slenderness ratio for buckling considerations.
Assume the column is pinned at both ends (simple supports).
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 120 mm and height h = 200 mm.
The applied compressive axial load is P = 40000 N.
The load is applied with an eccentricity e = 25 mm along the width direction (bending about the centroidal z-axis).
The bending moment arises solely from the eccentric load: M = P * e = 1000000 N-mm.
The relevant response quantities are the axial stress, the bending stress, and the combined stress at the cross-section.
Calculate the cross-sectional area: A = b * h = 24000 mm².
Calculate the moment of inertia about the z-axis (bending axis): I_z = (b * h³) / 12 = 8.00e7 mm⁴.
Calculate the section modulus about the z-axis: S_z = I_z / (h/2) = 8.00e5 mm³.
The axial stress is uniform and compressive: σ_axial = -P / A = -1.667 MPa.
The maximum bending stress (compressive on one face, tensile on the opposite) is: σ_bending = ± M / S_z = ± 1.250 MPa.
The combined normal stress at the extreme fiber on the eccentric side is σ_max = σ_axial - σ_bending = -2.917 MPa (compressive).
The combined normal stress at the extreme fiber on the opposite side is σ_min = σ_axial + σ_bending = -0.417 MPa (compressive).
Check that the maximum compressive stress (-2.917 MPa) is less than the material yield strength (250 MPa).
The column is analyzed as a short member under combined compression and bending, neglecting second-order (P-Δ) effects due to the assumed short length and small deformations.
