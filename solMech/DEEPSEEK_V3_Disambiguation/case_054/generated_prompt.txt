The bar is a linearly elastic solid under small deformation.
The bar has a length L = 800 mm.
The cross-sectional area varies linearly from A0 = 250 mm² at the left end (x=0) to AL = 400 mm² at the right end (x=L).
The axial load is tensile with magnitude P = 12000 N.
The material has Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The bar is prismatic in the sense that the cross-section shape is constant, only its area varies.
The load is applied axially and uniformly over the end cross-section.
The bar is fixed at the left end (x=0).
The governing relation for axial displacement u(x) is derived from the one-dimensional constitutive and equilibrium equations.
The axial stress is σ(x) = P / A(x).
The axial strain is ε(x) = σ(x) / E.
The displacement field is found by integrating the strain: u(x) = ∫₀ˣ ε(ξ) dξ.
The total elongation (axial deformation) of the bar is ΔL = u(L).
The area function is A(x) = A0 + (AL - A0)*(x/L).
The specific response quantities to determine are: the axial stress distribution σ(x), the axial strain distribution ε(x), the displacement field u(x), and the total elongation ΔL.
