The system is a solid circular shaft made of structural steel.
The shaft has a total length L = 1200 mm and a diameter d = 35 mm.
The shaft is subjected to two concentrated torques along its length.
Torque T2 = -250000 N·mm is applied at a distance of 800 mm from the fixed end.
Torque T1 = 200000 N·mm is applied at the free end, x = L = 1200 mm.
Assume the shaft is fixed at x = 0.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior.
The relevant material property is the shear modulus G.
The polar moment of inertia J for the solid circular cross-section is constant.
The primary response quantity is the internal torque T(x) along the shaft length.
The internal torque distribution will be piecewise constant between applied torques.
The angle of twist φ(x) is a second key response quantity.
The maximum shear stress τ_max(x) in the shaft is a third key response quantity.
The governing relation for internal torque is from equilibrium: dT/dx = 0 between loads.
The governing relation for angle of twist is dφ/dx = T(x) / (G*J).
The governing relation for shear stress is τ_max(x) = T(x) * r / J, where r = d/2.
The analysis requires calculating T(x), φ(x), and τ_max(x) for each segment.
The boundary condition for twist is φ(0) = 0 at the fixed end.
The solution will provide the twist at the free end and the stress distribution.
