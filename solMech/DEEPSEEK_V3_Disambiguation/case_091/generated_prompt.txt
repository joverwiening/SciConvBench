The bar is a prismatic, linear-elastic structural steel bar with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa and a constant cross-sectional area A = 500 mm².
The bar has a total length of 1200 mm, divided into segments AB (300 mm), BC (600 mm), and CD (300 mm).
The bar is subjected to axial point loads: a tensile force of 18000 N at point B, a compressive force of 12000 N at point C, and a tensile force of 10000 N at point D.
Assume the bar is fixed at end A to prevent rigid body motion.
The relevant response quantities to determine are the axial force (N), axial stress (σ), axial strain (ε), and axial displacement (u) in each segment of the bar.
The governing relations are: N is determined from static equilibrium; σ = N/A; ε = σ/E = N/(EA); and u(x) is found by integrating ε, with displacement continuity at B and C.
The boundary condition is u(A) = 0.
Calculate the internal axial force in segment AB as N_AB = 10000 N (tension).
Calculate the internal axial force in segment BC as N_BC = -2000 N (compression).
Calculate the internal axial force in segment CD as N_CD = 10000 N (tension).
Calculate the axial stress in each segment using σ = N/A.
Calculate the axial strain in each segment using ε = N/(EA).
Calculate the displacement at point B relative to A: u_B = (N_AB * L_AB) / (E A).
Calculate the displacement at point C relative to B: u_C = u_B + (N_BC * L_BC) / (E A).
Calculate the displacement at point D relative to C: u_D = u_C + (N_CD * L_CD) / (E A).
