The rod is made of structural steel, assumed to be a linear-elastic, isotropic material.
The rod is prismatic with a circular cross-section of diameter d = 20 mm.
The rod is subjected to a uniform temperature increase of ΔT = +35 °C.
The rod is fully restrained in the axial direction, meaning its total axial strain is zero.
There is no externally applied axial mechanical load.
The relevant material properties for structural steel are: Young's modulus E = 200 GPa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3, and coefficient of linear thermal expansion α = 12 × 10⁻⁶ /°C.
The thermal strain due to the temperature change is ε_th = α * ΔT.
The total axial strain is the sum of the mechanical strain and the thermal strain: ε_total = ε_mech + ε_th.
Under full axial restraint, ε_total = 0.
Therefore, the mechanical strain is ε_mech = -α * ΔT.
The axial stress induced by the restraint is σ = E * ε_mech = -E * α * ΔT.
The axial force in the rod is the product of this stress and the cross-sectional area, A = π*(d/2)².
The relevant response quantities are: the induced thermal strain (ε_th), the resulting mechanical strain (ε_mech), the induced axial stress (σ), and the induced axial force (F).
