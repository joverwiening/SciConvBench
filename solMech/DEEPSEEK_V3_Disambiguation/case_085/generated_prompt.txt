The beam is subjected to a combined axial and bending load.
The beam is prismatic with a rectangular cross-section of width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The beam material is linear-elastic and undergoes small deformations.
The beam length is assumed to be sufficiently long for Euler-Bernoulli beam theory to apply.
The applied bending moment at the critical section is M = 3,000,000 N·mm.
The applied compressive axial force is assumed to be zero (P_c = 0 N) for clarity, as no magnitude was provided.
The applied tensile axial force is assumed to be zero (P_t = 0 N) for clarity, as no magnitude was provided.
The relevant response quantities are the normal stresses due to bending.
The flexure formula for a rectangular cross-section is σ = -M*y / I.
The moment of inertia for the rectangular cross-section is I = (b * h^3) / 12.
The distance y is measured from the neutral axis (centroidal axis).
The maximum bending stress occurs at y = ± h/2.
The governing relation for combined axial and bending stress is σ_total = P/A ± M*y/I, where P is the net axial force (tension positive, compression negative).
For this case, with P = 0, the relation reduces to the flexure formula.
