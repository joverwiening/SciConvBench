Assume the beam is prismatic with a constant cross-section.
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and obeys Hooke's law.
Assume the beam is initially straight and the loading is static.
The beam length L is 2400 mm.
A single point load P of 1200 N is applied at the mid-span (x = 1200 mm), acting downward.
No other external bending loads or moments are applied.
The relevant response quantity is the vertical deflection at the point of load application (mid-span).
Use the unit-load method (dummy load method) to compute this deflection.
The governing relation is: deflection = ∫ (M * m) / (EI) dx, integrated over the length of the beam.
Here, M is the bending moment due to the real load (P).
Here, m is the bending moment due to a unit dummy load applied at the point where deflection is sought.
E is the modulus of elasticity of the beam material.
I is the second moment of area of the beam's cross-section.
The integration limits are from x = 0 to x = L.
For a simply supported beam with a central point load, the real moment diagram M is triangular.
For the unit dummy load applied at mid-span, the moment diagram m is also triangular.
The product integral (M * m) / (EI) yields the mid-span deflection formula: (P * L^3) / (48 * E * I).
The final result is expressed in millimeters, dependent on the values of E and I.
