The domain is solid mechanics, specifically material testing.
Assume a uniaxial, quasi-static tension test on a prismatic specimen with a uniform cross-section.
Assume the material is isotropic and follows a linear-elastic, small-deformation constitutive law.
The specimen is loaded by a purely axial force, resulting in a uniaxial stress state.
The primary response quantity is the engineering stress, defined as the applied axial force divided by the original cross-sectional area.
The primary response quantity is the engineering strain, defined as the change in axial length divided by the original axial length.
The governing relation for the linear-elastic region is Hooke's Law: engineering stress equals Young's modulus multiplied by engineering strain.
The Young's modulus is the slope of the linear portion of the engineering stress versus engineering strain curve.
Assume the test is displacement-controlled or force-controlled such that the response can be measured up to the proportional limit.
Assume measurements of force and displacement are converted to engineering stress and strain using the original specimen dimensions.
The Poisson's effect is present but not a primary reported quantity for the standard modulus calculation.
The output shall be the engineering stress-strain curve and the calculated Young's modulus.
