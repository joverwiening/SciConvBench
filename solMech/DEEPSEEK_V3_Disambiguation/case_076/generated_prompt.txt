Assume a rectangular cross-section for the beam with a width of 200 mm and a height of 400 mm.
Assume the material is linear-elastic with a Young's modulus of 210 GPa.
The beam is simply supported with a span L of 4000 mm.
A concentrated vertical load P of 12000 N acts downward at the midspan (2000 mm from support A).
The beam is subjected to bending only, with no applied axial tension or external moment loads.
The relevant response quantities are the internal shear force V(x) and the internal bending moment M(x) along the beam length.
The support reactions are calculated first: each vertical reaction at the simple supports equals P/2, or 6000 N upward.
The shear force V(x) is constant at 6000 N from the left support (x=0) to just left of midspan (x < L/2).
The shear force V(x) jumps to -6000 N at midspan due to the concentrated load and remains constant to the right support (x > L/2).
The bending moment M(x) varies linearly from 0 at the left support (x=0) to a maximum at midspan.
The maximum bending moment occurs at midspan (x = L/2) and is calculated as M_max = (P * L) / 4, which equals 12e6 N-mm.
The governing relation for shear force in the left half (0 ≤ x < L/2) is V(x) = P/2.
The governing relation for shear force in the right half (L/2 < x ≤ L) is V(x) = -P/2.
The governing relation for bending moment in the left half (0 ≤ x ≤ L/2) is M(x) = (P/2) * x.
The governing relation for bending moment in the right half (L/2 ≤ x ≤ L) is M(x) = (P/2) * (L - x).
