Assume a span-to-depth ratio of 10 to determine the beam length.
The beam length L is 800 mm, calculated as L = 10 * h, where h = 80 mm.
The beam cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 80 mm.
The material is linear-elastic, isotropic, and follows small-deformation theory.
Assume a simply supported beam for boundary conditions.
Assume a transverse point load P is applied at mid-span.
Assume the magnitude of the transverse point load P is 40 kN, which is consistent with generating a shear force of 20 kN at the support.
The section of interest for shear stress calculation is at the support, where the shear force V is maximum and equals 20 kN (20000 N).
The transverse shear stress distribution through the depth of the rectangular cross-section is parabolic.
The maximum transverse shear stress occurs at the neutral axis (mid-height).
The governing relation for transverse shear stress τ at a distance y from the neutral axis in a rectangular beam is τ = (V * Q) / (I * b), where V is the shear force, Q is the first moment of area of the section above y, I is the second moment of area of the full cross-section, and b is the width.
For a rectangular cross-section, I = (b * h^3) / 12.
The first moment Q at a distance y from the neutral axis is Q = (b / 2) * ((h^2 / 4) - y^2).
The maximum shear stress at the neutral axis (y=0) is τ_max = (3 * V) / (2 * A), where A = b * h is the cross-sectional area.
Calculate the maximum transverse shear stress using τ_max = (3 * 20000 N) / (2 * (50 mm * 80 mm)).
