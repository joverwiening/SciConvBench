Assume the beam length is L = 1000 mm.
Assume the beam is homogeneous, isotropic, and follows linear-elastic (Hookean) material behavior.
Assume the beam is prismatic (constant cross-section along its length).
Assume the bending moment M is applied purely, with no accompanying axial or shear force.
Assume the beam is in a state of pure bending, satisfying Euler-Bernoulli beam theory (plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis).
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.
The applied sagging bending moment is M = 4,000,000 N·mm.
The neutral axis is located at the mid-height of the cross-section.
The moment of inertia for the rectangular cross-section about the neutral axis is I = (b * h^3) / 12.
The bending stress σ at an arbitrary fiber location, defined by distance y from the neutral axis (positive upward), is given by the flexure formula: σ = - (M * y) / I.
The maximum tensile bending stress occurs at the bottom fiber (y = -h/2).
The maximum compressive bending stress occurs at the top fiber (y = h/2).
