Assume a simply supported beam with a length of 5000 mm.
Assume the beam is made of a linear-elastic, isotropic material.
Assume the beam cross-section is rectangular with a constant width.
Assume the beam is subjected to pure bending from the applied moment, with no shear deformation considered.
The governing bending stress formula is σ = M*y / I, where M is the bending moment, y is the distance from the neutral axis, and I is the second moment of area.
For a rectangular cross-section of width b and height h, the second moment of area is I = (b * h^3) / 12.
The maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fiber, where y = h/2.
The maximum bending stress must not exceed the allowable stress: σ_max = (M_max * (h/2)) / ((b * h^3)/12) = (6 * M_max) / (b * h^2) ≤ σ_allow.
The required beam height h is determined by rearranging the inequality: h ≥ sqrt( (6 * M_max) / (b * σ_allow) ).
The beam width b must be specified to calculate a numerical value for the required height.
