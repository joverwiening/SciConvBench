The analysis is for a solid circular column under compressive axial load.
The material is linear-elastic, isotropic, and follows small-deformation theory.
The column is prismatic, with a constant circular cross-section along its length.
The column is long and slender, making Euler buckling a potential failure mode.
The column ends are pinned (simply supported) for the buckling analysis.
The primary failure modes considered are yielding of the material and elastic (Euler) buckling.
The relevant geometric properties are: column length (L), and cross-sectional radius (R) or diameter (D).
The relevant material properties are: Young's modulus (E), and yield strength (σ_y).
The applied load is a concentric compressive force (P).
The critical load for Euler buckling is P_cr = (π² E I) / L², where I is the minimum area moment of inertia.
For a solid circular cross-section, I = (π D⁴) / 64 or I = (π R⁴) / 4.
The critical buckling stress is σ_cr = P_cr / A, where A is the cross-sectional area.
For a solid circle, A = π R².
The yield load is P_y = σ_y * A.
The column will yield if P_y < P_cr and P reaches P_y.
The column will buckle elastically if P_cr < P_y and P reaches P_cr.
A buckling/instability check is performed by calculating the slenderness ratio λ = L / r, where r is the radius of gyration (r = √(I/A)).
For a solid circle, r = R/2.
The governing relation for the buckling check is: the applied stress σ = P/A must be less than σ_cr, with an appropriate factor of safety.
The analysis compares the two failure loads: P_yield and P_critical.
The governing relation for the yield check is: the applied stress σ = P/A must be less than σ_y, with an appropriate factor of safety.
The mode of failure is determined by the lower of the two critical loads.
