Assume the structural member is made of a common structural steel.
The cross-section is a rectangle with width b = 60 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The member is subjected to an axial tension load P = 40000 N.
The member is subjected to a transverse shear load V = 16000 N.
The shear load is applied parallel to the cross-sectional width (b-direction).
Assume a state of plane stress exists in the member.
The average normal stress (sigma_x) is calculated as P / (b * h).
The average shear stress (tau_xy) is calculated as V / (b * h).
Calculate the principal stresses (sigma_1, sigma_2) from the stress state (sigma_x, 0, tau_xy).
Calculate the principal strains using Hooke's Law for plane stress.
Assume a Young's modulus (E) of 200 GPa and a Poisson's ratio (nu) of 0.3 for steel.
The governing relations are: sigma_x = P/A, tau_xy = V/A, where A = b*h.
The principal stress equations are: sigma_1,2 = (sigma_x/2) ± sqrt((sigma_x/2)^2 + tau_xy^2).
The maximum shear stress is: tau_max = sqrt((sigma_x/2)^2 + tau_xy^2).
The principal strains are: epsilon_1 = (1/E)*(sigma_1 - nu*sigma_2), epsilon_2 = (1/E)*(sigma_2 - nu*sigma_1).
