The bar is a prismatic structural steel bar with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa and a constant cross-sectional area A = 200 mm².
The bar has a total length of 1800 mm, divided into three segments: AB = 700 mm, BC = 600 mm, and CD = 500 mm.
The bar is subjected to three axial point loads: a tensile force of 18000 N applied at point B, a compressive force of 8000 N applied at point C, and a tensile force of 20000 N applied at point D.
Assume the bar is fixed at end A to prevent rigid body motion.
The analysis assumes small deformations and linear-elastic material behavior.
The relevant response quantities to determine are the axial force (N), axial stress (σ), axial strain (ε), and axial displacement (u) for each segment of the bar.
The governing relation for axial force is equilibrium: N is constant within each segment between loads.
The governing constitutive relation is Hooke's Law: σ = E * ε, where σ = N / A.
The governing kinematic relation for displacement is du/dx = ε, leading to u(x) = ∫ (N/(EA)) dx.
Calculate the axial force in each segment (AB, BC, CD) using static equilibrium.
Calculate the axial stress in each segment as σ = N / A.
Calculate the axial strain in each segment as ε = σ / E.
Calculate the axial displacement at points B, C, and D relative to the fixed end A by integrating the strain over each segment.
