Assume a prismatic, homogeneous, isotropic column with a constant cross-section.
Assume the material obeys Hooke's Law (linear elastic) and deformations remain small.
The column is initially perfectly straight and the compressive load is applied centrally (no eccentricity).
The governing equation for the column's deflection is the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation: EI * (d^4v/dx^4) + P * (d^2v/dx^2) = 0.
The Euler critical buckling load is P_cr = (π² * E * I) / (K * L)², where K is the effective length factor determined by end conditions.
The relevant response quantity is the critical load P_cr.
Perform a buckling check by comparing the applied compressive load P to the critical load P_cr; buckling occurs if P ≥ P_cr.
The factor of safety against buckling is P_cr / P.
