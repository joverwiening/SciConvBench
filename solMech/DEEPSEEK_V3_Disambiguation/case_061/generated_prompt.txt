Assume the material is structural steel with Young's Modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.
Assume the column length L is 1.0 m, which is short relative to its cross-section for a stability check.
Assume the column has pinned-pinned boundary conditions at both ends.
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 60 mm and depth h = 120 mm.
The column is subjected to a compressive axial load P = 60 kN applied with an eccentricity e = 20 mm along the width direction (bending about the centroidal z-axis).
The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior.
The relevant response quantities are axial stress, bending stress, combined normal stress, lateral deflection, and a check for elastic buckling.
Calculate the axial stress as σ_axial = P / A, where cross-sectional area A = b * h.
Calculate the moment about the z-axis as M_z = P * e.
Calculate the section modulus about the z-axis as S_z = (b * h^2) / 6.
Calculate the maximum bending stress as σ_bending_max = M_z / S_z.
Calculate the combined maximum compressive normal stress at the extreme fiber as σ_max_compressive = |σ_axial| + σ_bending_max.
Calculate the combined maximum tensile normal stress at the opposite extreme fiber as σ_max_tensile = σ_axial - σ_bending_max.
Calculate the lateral deflection at the mid-height using the formula for a pin-ended column with eccentric load: δ_max = e * [ sec( (π/2) * sqrt(P/P_cr) ) - 1 ], where the Euler buckling load P_cr = (π^2 * E * I_z) / L^2, and I_z = (b * h^3) / 12.
Verify that the maximum combined stress remains below the material's yield strength.
Verify that the applied load P is less than the critical buckling load P_cr to ensure the column remains stable in the elastic range.
