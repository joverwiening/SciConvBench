Assume the beam is made of structural steel, a common material for simply supported beams in bending applications.
Assume a span length of 5 meters (5000 mm) for a typical simply supported beam scenario.
The beam is simply supported and subjected to a pure bending moment.
The maximum applied bending moment is M_max = 6,000,000 N·mm.
The allowable bending stress for the material is σ_allow = 90 MPa.
The beam cross-section is rectangular with width b and height h.
The relevant response quantity is the required section modulus S_req = M_max / σ_allow.
Calculate the required section modulus: S_req = (6,000,000 N·mm) / (90 N/mm²) = 66,667 mm³.
For a rectangular section, the section modulus is S = (b * h²) / 6.
Assume a typical width-to-depth ratio; for a rectangular beam in bending, assume a preliminary width b = 200 mm.
Solve for the required height h: h = sqrt( (6 * S_req) / b ).
Calculate the required height: h = sqrt( (6 * 66,667 mm³) / 200 mm ) ≈ 44.7 mm.
Design the rectangular beam height to be at least 45 mm to satisfy the allowable bending stress criterion.
The governing relation is the flexure formula: σ_max = M_max / S ≤ σ_allow.
The primary design equation is S_req ≥ M_max / σ_allow.
