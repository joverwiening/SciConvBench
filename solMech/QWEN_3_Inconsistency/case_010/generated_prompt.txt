Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.  
The two prismatic bars have equal length L = 800 mm and are mounted in parallel between two rigid plates.  
Bar 1 is made of structural steel with cross-sectional area A1 = 150 mm² and elastic modulus E1 = 200000 MPa.  
Bar 2 is made of cast iron with cross-sectional area A2 = 200 mm² and elastic modulus E2 = 110000 MPa.  
Both bars are initially unstressed and of exactly the same length before the load is applied.  
A total tensile load P = 30000 N is applied to the top plate, shared between the two bars.  
The bars are perfectly bonded to rigid plates, so they undergo the same elongation (compatibility condition).  
The force in each bar is determined by enforcing equilibrium and compatibility.  
Equilibrium requires that the sum of the forces in the two bars equals the applied load: F1 + F2 = 30000 N.  
Compatibility requires that the elongation of both bars is equal: (F1 × L) / (A1 × E1) = (F2 × L) / (A2 × E2).  
Solve the two equations simultaneously to find F1 and F2.  
The stress in Bar 1 is σ1 = F1 / A1.  
The stress in Bar 2 is σ2 = F2 / A2.  
The common elongation of the bars is δ = (F1 × L) / (A1 × E1) (or equivalently using Bar 2’s properties).  
Use the calculated forces to determine stresses and elongation.
