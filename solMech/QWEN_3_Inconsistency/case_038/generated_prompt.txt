Use N–mm–MPa units throughout the calculation.
Apply Euler–Bernoulli beam theory to a simply supported beam with span L = 3500 mm.
The beam is subjected to a uniformly distributed load w = 0.5 N/mm over its entire span.
The material is AISI 1020 steel with a modulus of elasticity E = 200000 MPa.
The cross-sectional moment of inertia is I = 3000000 mm^4.
Due to symmetric loading and support conditions, the maximum deflection occurs at the midspan, x = L/2 = 1750 mm.
The deflection at the supports is zero.
The slope at the supports is not zero; the condition of zero end slopes does not apply for a simply supported beam under uniform load—this was a misinterpretation. Correct boundary conditions are: deflection = 0 at x = 0 and x = L.
Compute the maximum deflection using the formula: δ_max = (5 * w * L^4) / (384 * E * I).
Compute the slope at the left support (x = 0) using the formula: θ = (w * L^3) / (24 * E * I).
The maximum deflection occurs at x = 1750 mm.
The slope at x = 0 is positive and represents the rotation at the left support.
