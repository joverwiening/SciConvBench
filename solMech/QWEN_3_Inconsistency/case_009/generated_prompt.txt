Assume the load is shared between the bars in proportion to their axial stiffnesses (EA/L), as this is the standard practice for parallel members undergoing equal elongation.  

  
Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.  
Model the two prismatic bars as linear elastic springs in parallel, each with axial stiffness k = EA/L.  
Bar 1 is AISI 1020 steel with cross-sectional area A1 = 300 mm² and elastic modulus E1 = 200000 MPa.  
Bar 2 is cast iron with cross-sectional area A2 = 250 mm² and elastic modulus E2 = 110000 MPa.  
Both bars have length L = 400 mm.  
The axial stiffness of Bar 1 is k1 = E1×A1/L = 200000×300/400 = 150000 N/mm.  
The axial stiffness of Bar 2 is k2 = E2×A2/L = 110000×250/400 = 68750 N/mm.  
The total axial stiffness is k_total = k1 + k2 = 150000 + 68750 = 218750 N/mm.  
A total tensile load P = 20000 N is applied to the top rigid plate.  
The elongation of the plate is δ = P / k_total = 20000 / 218750 = 0.09143 mm.  
The force in Bar 1 is F1 = k1 × δ = 150000 × 0.09143 = 13714.5 N.  
The force in Bar 2 is F2 = k2 × δ = 68750 × 0.09143 = 6285.5 N.  
The stress in Bar 1 is σ1 = F1 / A1 = 13714.5 / 300 = 45.715 MPa.  
The stress in Bar 2 is σ2 = F2 / A2 = 6285.5 / 250 = 25.142 MPa.  
The common elongation of both bars is 0.09143 mm.
