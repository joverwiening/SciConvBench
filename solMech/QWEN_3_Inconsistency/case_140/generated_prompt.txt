Use N–mm–MPa units.  
The shaft has a diameter of 25 mm.  
An axial tensile force of 15000 N is applied.  
A torque of 400000 N·mm is applied.  
The material is assumed to be ductile steel, a common engineering material for shafts, with no specific failure criteria or safety factors required for this analysis.  
The cross-sectional area A is calculated using the formula A = π d² / 4.  
The polar moment of inertia J is calculated using the formula J = π d⁴ / 32.  
The distance from the center to the outer surface c is equal to d / 2.  
The average axial stress σ is computed as σ = P / A.  
The torsional shear stress at the outer surface τ is computed as τ = T c / J.  
Plane stress conditions are assumed with σx = σ, σy = 0, and τxy = τ.  
The principal stresses are computed using the formula σ₁,₂ = (σx + σy)/2 ± √[((σx − σy)/2)² + τxy²].  
The maximum in-plane shear stress is computed using the formula τ_max = √[((σx − σy)/2)² + τxy²].  
The torsional shear stress at the outer surface is not zero; the premise that it vanishes is incorrect and is therefore disregarded.  
All calculations proceed with the actual torsional shear stress present.
