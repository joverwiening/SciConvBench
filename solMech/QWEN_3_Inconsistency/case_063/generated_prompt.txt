Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.  
The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.  
The internal bending moment at the section is M = 3000000 N·mm, causing sagging (positive bending).  
The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section.  
The distance y is measured from the neutral axis, positive upward.  
The bending stress σ is calculated using the formula σ = (M * y) / I, where I is the second moment of area of the cross-section.  
The second moment of area I for a rectangular section is (b * h³) / 12.  
I = (50 * 100³) / 12 = 4166666.67 mm⁴.  
The stress at y = 25 mm above the neutral axis is σ = (3000000 * 25) / 4166666.67 = 18.0 MPa (tensile).  
The top fiber is located at y = +50 mm (positive, above neutral axis).  
The stress at the top fiber is σ_top = (3000000 * 50) / 4166666.67 = 36.0 MPa (tensile).  
The bottom fiber is located at y = -50 mm (negative, below neutral axis).  
The stress at the bottom fiber is σ_bottom = (3000000 * -50) / 4166666.67 = -36.0 MPa (compressive).  
The bending stress is linear with respect to y and is tensile above the neutral axis, compressive below.  
The assumption of pure bending applies; shear effects are neglected.
