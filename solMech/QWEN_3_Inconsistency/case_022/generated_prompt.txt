Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.  
Model the stepped solid circular shaft as two in-series segments: AB and BC.  
Segment AB has length L1 = 300 mm and diameter d1 = 25 mm.  
Segment BC has length L2 = 700 mm and diameter d2 = 30 mm.  
The shaft is made of structural steel with shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa.  
A torque T = 450000 N·mm is applied at end C.  
End A is fully fixed, so no rotation occurs at A.  
The same internal torque T = 450000 N·mm acts in both segments because they are in series under the applied torque.  
Calculate the polar moment of inertia J1 for segment AB using J1 = π × (d1⁴) / 32.  
Calculate the polar moment of inertia J2 for segment BC using J2 = π × (d2⁴) / 32.  
Compute torsional stiffness k1 for segment AB using k1 = G × J1 / L1.  
Compute torsional stiffness k2 for segment BC using k2 = G × J2 / L2.  
Since the segments are in series and the full torque is transmitted through both, the total angle of twist is the sum of individual twists.  
Calculate angle of twist φ1 for segment AB using φ1 = (T × L1) / (G × J1).  
Calculate angle of twist φ2 for segment BC using φ2 = (T × L2) / (G × J2).  
Compute total angle of twist at C relative to A as φ_total = φ1 + φ2.  
Calculate maximum shear stress τ1 in segment AB using τ1 = (T × d1 / 2) / J1.  
Calculate maximum shear stress τ2 in segment BC using τ2 = (T × d2 / 2) / J2.  
Identify the segment with the higher maximum shear stress as the governing segment.  
Report both maximum shear stresses, the governing segment, and the total angle of twist at C.
