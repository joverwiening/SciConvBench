Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.  
The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 100 mm.  
The bending moment M = 4,000,000 N·mm is sagging, producing positive curvature.  
The neutral axis is located at the centroid of the cross-section, where y = 0 mm.  
The moment of inertia I for the cross-section is calculated as I = (b × h³) / 12.  
I = (40 × 100³) / 12 = 3,333,333.33 mm⁴.  
The bending stress σ at any point is calculated using σ = (M × y) / I.  
At y = 0 mm (neutral axis), σ = (4,000,000 × 0) / 3,333,333.33 = 0 MPa.  
The top fiber is located at y = +50 mm (positive upward from neutral axis).  
The stress at the top fiber is σ_top = (4,000,000 × 50) / 3,333,333.33 = 60 MPa (tensile).  
The bottom fiber is located at y = -50 mm.  
The stress at the bottom fiber is σ_bottom = (4,000,000 × -50) / 3,333,333.33 = -60 MPa (compressive).  
The assumption that bending stress is uniform over the section depth is incorrect for pure bending; stress varies linearly with y.  
Therefore, the stress at the neutral axis is 0 MPa, at the top fiber is +60 MPa, and at the bottom fiber is -60 MPa.
