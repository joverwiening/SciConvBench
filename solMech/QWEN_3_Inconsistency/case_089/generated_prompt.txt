Use N–mm–MPa units throughout the analysis.  
The bar has a constant cross-sectional area A = 500 mm².  
The bar is prismatic and made of structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.  
The total length of the bar is divided into segments: AB = 700 mm, BC = 400 mm, and CD = 300 mm.  
The left end A is fixed, so displacement u_A = 0.  
Axial forces are applied at points: 15000 N at B (to the right), -12000 N at C (to the left), and 10000 N at D (to the right).  
Calculate the internal axial force in each segment using free-body diagrams.  
For segment AB, the internal axial force is equal to the force at B, which is 15000 N.  
For segment BC, the internal axial force is the sum of forces to the right of section AB: 15000 N - 12000 N = 3000 N.  
For segment CD, the internal axial force is the sum of forces to the right of section BC: 15000 N - 12000 N + 10000 N = 13000 N.  
Compute normal stress in each segment as σ = F / A.  
Normal stress in AB is 15000 / 500 = 30 MPa.  
Normal stress in BC is 3000 / 500 = 6 MPa.  
Normal stress in CD is 13000 / 500 = 26 MPa.  
Compute axial deformation for each segment using δ = (F × L) / (A × E).  
Deformation of AB: (15000 × 700) / (500 × 200000) = 0.105 mm.  
Deformation of BC: (3000 × 400) / (500 × 200000) = 0.012 mm.  
Deformation of CD: (13000 × 300) / (500 × 200000) = 0.039 mm.  
Total displacement of point D relative to A is the sum of deformations: 0.105 + 0.012 + 0.039 = 0.156 mm.  
The displacement of point D is 0.156 mm to the right relative to A.
