Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system.  
Assume linear elastic material behavior.  
Assume uniform temperature change throughout the rod.  
The rod is prismatic with a length of 1200 mm and a diameter of 20 mm.  
The rod is made of structural steel with a Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.  
The coefficient of thermal expansion α is 1e-05 per °C.  
The rod is constrained between two rigid, unyielding supports, preventing any axial deformation.  
The rod experiences a uniform temperature increase of 35 °C.  
The actual axial strain is not equal to the free thermal strain because the rod is constrained.  
Thermal strain ε_thermal = α × ΔT.  
Since the rod cannot expand, the mechanical strain ε_mechanical = -ε_thermal to ensure zero total strain.  
Thermal stress σ = E × ε_mechanical.  
Thermal stress σ = -E × α × ΔT.  
The negative sign indicates the stress is compressive.  
Calculate the cross-sectional area A = π × (d/2)².  
Axial force F = σ × A.  
The thermal stress that develops in the rod is compressive.  
The corresponding axial force is compressive and acts to push against the supports.
