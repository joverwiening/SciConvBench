Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.  
Assume linear elastic material behavior.  
The shaft is a hollow circular tube with outer diameter do = 50 mm and inner diameter di = 42 mm.  
The wall thickness is 4 mm, confirming a thin-walled closed section.  
The shaft length L is 1200 mm.  
A constant torque T of 450000 N·mm is applied.  
Model the shaft as a thin-walled closed tube using the mean-area shear-flow relation.  
Use the polar moment of inertia J = π(do⁴ − di⁴)/32.  
Use shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa.  
Compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface using τ_max = T × r_o / J, where r_o = do/2.  
Alternatively, verify using the shear flow relation q = T / (2 × A_m), where A_m is the area enclosed by the mean radius.  
The mean radius r_m = (do + di)/4 = (50 + 42)/4 = 23 mm.  
The mean area A_m = π × r_m².  
Shear stress τ = q / t, where t = 4 mm is the wall thickness.  
Use the torsion formula for angle of twist φ = (T × L) / (G × J).  
Report the maximum shear stress in MPa.
