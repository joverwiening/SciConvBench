Use N–mm–MPa units for all calculations.
Assume the bar is vertical, prismatic, and linearly elastic.
The bar has a length L = 3000 mm.
The cross-sectional area A = 300 mm².
The modulus of elasticity E = 69000 MPa.
The material density ρ = 8900 kg/m³.
Gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².
Convert density and gravity to consistent N–mm–MPa units: ρg = 8900 × 9.81 × 10⁻⁶ N/mm³ = 0.087309 MPa.
The distributed self-weight load per unit length is ρgA = 0.087309 × 300 = 26.1927 N/mm.
The axial force at position x (measured downward from the top) is P(x) = ρgA(L − x) = 26.1927 × (3000 − x) N.
The elongation is computed by integrating δ = ∫₀ᴸ P(x)/(AE) dx from 0 to 3000 mm.
Substitute P(x) = 26.1927 × (3000 − x), A = 300, and E = 69000.
Simplify integrand: P(x)/(AE) = (26.1927 × (3000 − x)) / (300 × 69000) = (26.1927 / 20700000) × (3000 − x).
Compute δ = (26.1927 / 20700000) × ∫₀³⁰⁰⁰ (3000 − x) dx.
Evaluate integral: ∫₀³⁰⁰⁰ (3000 − x) dx = [3000x − x²/2] from 0 to 3000 = 3000×3000 − (3000²)/2 = 9×10⁶ − 4.5×10⁶ = 4.5×10⁶ mm².
Compute δ = (26.1927 / 20700000) × 4.5×10⁶ = (26.1927 × 4.5×10⁶) / 2.07×10⁷ = 117867.15 / 20700000 ≈ 0.05694 mm.
The elongation of the bar due to its own weight is 0.05694 mm.
The result is consistent with the theoretical formula δ = (ρgL²)/(2E), which yields the same value when computed in consistent units.
