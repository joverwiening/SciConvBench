Use N–mm–MPa units throughout the calculations.  
Given: shaft diameter d = 35 mm, bending moment M = 3 000 000 N·mm, torque T = 500 000 N·mm.  

Compute the second moment of area I = π d⁴/64 = 73 662 mm⁴.  
Compute the polar moment of inertia J = π d⁴/32 = 147 324 mm⁴.  

Compute the distance from the neutral axis to the outer surface c = d/2 = 17.5 mm.  

(a) Maximum bending normal stress at the outer surface: σ = M c / I = (3 000 000 N·mm)(17.5 mm) / 73 662 mm⁴ ≈ 713 MPa.  

(b) Torsional shear stress at the outer surface: τ = T c / J = (500 000 N·mm)(17.5 mm) / 147 324 mm⁴ ≈ 59 MPa.  

(c) Treat the surface stress state as plane stress with σₓ = 713 MPa, σ_y = 0, τ_xy = 59 MPa.  
Compute the principal stresses:  
σ₁,₂ = (σₓ+σ_y)/2 ± √[ ((σₓ−σ_y)/2)² + τ_xy² ]  
σ₁ = 356.5 MPa + √(356.5² + 59²) ≈ 718 MPa  
σ₂ = 356.5 MPa – √(356.5² + 59²) ≈ –5 MPa  

Compute the maximum in‑plane shear stress: τ_max = √[ ((σₓ−σ_y)/2)² + τ_xy² ] ≈ 361 MPa.
