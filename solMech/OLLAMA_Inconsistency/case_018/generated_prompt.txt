Use N‑mm‑MPa units throughout the calculations.  
The shaft has constant diameter d = 40 mm, length L = 1500 mm, and is fixed at x = 0.  
Torque T1 = 300 000 N·mm is applied at the free end x = L in the positive sense.  
Torque T2 = –150 000 N·mm is applied at x = a = 750 mm in the opposite sense.  
Material shear modulus G = 25 939.8 MPa (N/mm²).  

1. Compute the polar moment of inertia of the circular cross‑section:  
   J = π d⁴ / 32 = π (40 mm)⁴ / 32 = 80 000 π mm⁴ ≈ 2.51327 × 10⁵ mm⁴.  

2. Determine the internal torque in each segment:  
   • For 0 ≤ x < a, the internal torque equals the algebraic sum of torques to the right of the section:  
     T_int(0→a) = T1 + T2 = 300 000 N·mm – 150 000 N·mm = 150 000 N·mm.  
   • For a ≤ x ≤ L, only T1 acts to the right, so  
     T_int(a→L) = T1 = 300 000 N·mm.  

3. Compute the maximum shear stress in each segment using τ_max = T_int c / J, where c = d/2 = 20 mm:  
   • τ_max(0→a) = 150 000 N·mm · 20 mm / J = 3 000 000 / J ≈ 11.94 MPa.  
   • τ_max(a→L) = 300 000 N·mm · 20 mm / J = 6 000 000 / J ≈ 23.88 MPa.  

4. Compute the angle of twist for each segment using θ = T_int L_seg / (J G):  
   • Segment 0→a (L₁ = a = 750 mm):  
     θ₁ = 150 000 N·mm · 750 mm / (J G) = 1.125 × 10⁸ / (2.51327 × 10⁵ · 25 939.8) ≈ 0.01726 rad.  
   • Segment a→L (L₂ = L – a = 750 mm):  
     θ₂ = 300 000 N·mm · 750 mm / (J G) = 2.25 × 10⁸ / (2.51327 × 10⁵ · 25 939.8) ≈ 0.03452 rad.  

5. Sum the twists to obtain the total angle of twist at the free end:  
   θ_total = θ₁ + θ₂ ≈ 0.01726 rad + 0.03452 rad = 0.05178 rad ≈ 2.97°.  

The internal torque distribution, maximum shear stresses, and total angle of twist are thus fully defined.
