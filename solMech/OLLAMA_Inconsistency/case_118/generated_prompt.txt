Use the given data in N‑mm‑MPa units.

Convert the rotational speed to angular velocity: ω = 2π · (rpm / 60). For 1200 rpm, ω = 2π · (1200/60) = 40π rad/s ≈ 125.66 rad/s.

Compute the transmitted torque from the power relation P = T·ω. Convert the power to N·mm/s (5 kW = 5 000 W = 5 000 N·m/s = 5 000 000 N·mm/s). Then T = P/ω = 5 000 000 N·mm / 125.66 rad/s ≈ 3.979 × 10⁴ N·mm (≈ 39.79 N·m).

Determine the polar moment of inertia of the solid circular shaft: J = π d⁴ / 32, with d = 20 mm. Thus J = π · (20 mm)⁴ / 32 = π · 160 000 / 32 = π · 5 000 ≈ 1.5708 × 10⁴ mm⁴.

Find the maximum shear stress using torsion theory: τ_max = T·c / J, where the outer radius c = d/2 = 10 mm. Hence τ_max = (3.979 × 10⁴ N·mm · 10 mm) / 1.5708 × 10⁴ mm⁴ ≈ 25.3 MPa.

Compute the angle of twist over the length L = 1000 mm: θ = T·L / (J·G), with G = 76923.1 MPa = 76923.1 N/mm². θ = (3.979 × 10⁴ N·mm · 1000 mm) / (1.5708 × 10⁴ mm⁴ · 76923.1 N/mm²) ≈ 0.0329 rad, which is about 1.9°.
