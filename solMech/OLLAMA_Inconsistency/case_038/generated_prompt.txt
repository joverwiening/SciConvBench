Determine the maximum vertical deflection of the simply supported beam, the location where it occurs, and the slope at the left support (x = 0) using Euler–Bernoulli beam theory with N‑mm‑MPa units.

1. Use the bending moment for a uniformly distributed load on a simply supported beam:  
   M(x) = w x (L − x) / 2.

2. Relate moment to curvature:  
   EI y''(x) = M(x).

3. Integrate twice to obtain the deflection expression:  
   y(x) = w (2 L x³ − x⁴) / (24 EI) + C₁ x + C₂.

4. Apply the simply supported boundary conditions y(0)=0 and y(L)=0 to solve for the integration constants:  
   C₂ = 0,  
   C₁ = –w L³ / (24 EI).

5. Write the final deflection formula:  
   y(x) = w [2 L x³ − x⁴] / (24 EI) − w L³ x / (24 EI).

6. Find the location of maximum deflection by setting the slope y'(x) to zero:  
   y'(x) = w [ L x² / 4 − x³ / 6 − L³ / 24 ] / EI = 0.  
   Solve for x; the symmetric solution is x = L / 2.

7. Compute the maximum deflection by evaluating y(L/2):  
   y_max = –5 w L⁴ / (384 EI).  
   Insert the given values:  
   w = 0.5 N/mm, L = 3500 mm, E = 200 000 MPa, I = 3 000 000 mm⁴.  
   EI = 200 000 MPa × 3 000 000 mm⁴ = 6 × 10¹¹ N·mm².  
   L⁴ = (3500 mm)⁴ = 1.500625 × 10¹⁴ mm⁴.  
   y_max = –5 × 0.5 × 1.500625 × 10¹⁴ / (384 × 6 × 10¹¹) ≈ –1.63 mm (downward).

8. Determine the slope at the left support (x = 0) from the derivative expression:  
   y'(0) = –w L³ / (24 EI).  
   L³ = (3500 mm)³ = 4.2875 × 10¹⁰ mm³.  
   y'(0) = –0.5 × 4.2875 × 10¹⁰ / (24 × 6 × 10¹¹) ≈ –1.49 × 10⁻³ rad (≈ –0.085°).

9. Summarize the results:  
   - Maximum deflection magnitude ≈ 1.63 mm, occurring at the mid‑span x = 1750 mm.  
   - Slope at the left support ≈ –1.5 × 10⁻³ rad (downward).
