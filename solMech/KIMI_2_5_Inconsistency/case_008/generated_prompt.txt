The system consists of two prismatic bars mounted in parallel between two rigid plates.

Bar 1 is AISI 1020 steel with length L = 500 mm, cross-sectional area A1 = 300 mm², and elastic modulus E1 = 200000 MPa.

Bar 2 is 6061-T6 aluminum with length L = 500 mm, cross-sectional area A2 = 200 mm², and elastic modulus E2 = 69000 MPa.

A total tensile load P = 20000 N is applied to the top plate.

Both bars are perfectly bonded to rigid plates and undergo the same elongation δ due to compatibility.

The equilibrium equation requires that the sum of internal forces equals the applied load: P1 + P2 = P.

The force-elongation relationships are P1 = (A1 × E1 / L) × δ and P2 = (A2 × E2 / L) × δ.

Substitute the force expressions into equilibrium: (A1×E1/L + A2×E2/L) × δ = P.

Calculate the stiffness terms: k1 = A1×E1/L = 300 × 200000 / 500 = 120000 N/mm, and k2 = A2×E2/L = 200 × 69000 / 500 = 27600 N/mm.

The total stiffness is k_total = 120000 + 27600 = 147600 N/mm.

The common elongation is δ = P / k_total = 20000 / 147600 = 0.135501 mm.

The force in bar 1 is P1 = k1 × δ = 120000 × 0.135501 = 16260.2 N.

The force in bar 2 is P2 = k2 × δ = 27600 × 0.135501 = 3739.8 N.

The stress in bar 1 is σ1 = P1 / A1 = 16260.2 / 300 = 54.20 MPa.

The stress in bar 2 is σ2 = P2 / A2 = 3739.8 / 200 = 18.70 MPa.

Verify equilibrium: 16260.2 + 3739.8 = 20000 N.
