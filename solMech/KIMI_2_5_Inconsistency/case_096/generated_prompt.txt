Use N–mm–MPa units and Euler–Bernoulli beam theory.

The beam is a cantilever of length L = 2000 mm, fixed at x = 0 and free at x = L.

The beam carries a uniform distributed load w = 1.0 N/mm acting downward over the entire span.

The distributed load is replaced by its resultant force F = wL = 2000 N applied at the centroid of the distributed load, which is located at x = L/2 = 1000 mm from the fixed end.

The material is structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The second moment of area is I = 1500000 mm^4.

The flexural rigidity is EI = 200000 × 1500000 = 3.0 × 10^11 N·mm^2.

For a cantilever with a concentrated load P applied at distance a from the fixed end, the slope at the free end is θ = Pa^2/(2EI) and the deflection at the free end is δ = Pa^2(3L - a)/(6EI).

Substitute P = 2000 N, a = 1000 mm, L = 2000 mm, and EI = 3.0 × 10^11 N·mm^2.

Calculate the slope at the free end: θ = (2000)(1000)^2 / (2 × 3.0 × 10^11) = 0.003333 rad.

Calculate the maximum deflection at the free end: δ = (2000)(1000)^2 × (3 × 2000 - 1000) / (6 × 3.0 × 10^11) = 5.556 mm.

Report the slope at the free end as 0.00333 rad (or 0.191 degrees).

Report the maximum deflection at the free end as 5.56 mm downward.
