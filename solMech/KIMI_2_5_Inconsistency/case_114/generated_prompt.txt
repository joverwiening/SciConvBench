The user wants to compute the normal stress at a specific point on a beam's cross-section using the unsymmetric bending stress formula.

The given data is:
- Coordinates of the point: (y, z) = (30 mm, 15 mm)
- Second moments of area: Iy = 2500000 mm^4, Iz = 1200000 mm^4
- Product of inertia: Iyz = 800000 mm^4
- Bending moments: My = 4000000 N·mm, Mz = 2000000 N·mm
- Units: N–mm–MPa (so stress will be in MPa = N/mm²)

The formula to use is:
σ = -(My·Iz − Mz·Iyz)/(Iy·Iz − Iyz²) · z + -(Mz·Iy − My·Iyz)/(Iy·Iz − Iyz²) · y

First, compute the denominator (common to both terms):
Iy·Iz − Iyz² = (2500000)(1200000) − (800000)²
= 3.0×10¹² − 6.4×10¹¹
= 2.36×10¹² mm⁸

First coefficient (for z term):
-(My·Iz − Mz·Iyz) = -[(4000000)(1200000) − (2000000)(800000)]
= -[4.8×10¹² − 1.6×10¹²]
= -3.2×10¹² N·mm⁵

First term coefficient: -3.2×10¹² / 2.36×10¹² = -1.355932... N/mm³

Second coefficient (for y term):
-(Mz·Iy − My·Iyz) = -[(2000000)(2500000) − (4000000)(800000)]
= -[5.0×10¹² − 3.2×10¹²]
= -1.8×10¹² N·mm⁵

Second term coefficient: -1.8×10¹² / 2.36×10¹² = -0.762712... N/mm³

Now compute stress:
σ = (-1.355932)(15) + (-0.762712)(30)
= -20.33898 + (-22.88136)
= -43.22034 MPa

Or more precisely:
σ = -43.2 MPa (compression)

The normal stress at the point is -43.2 MPa (compression, negative sign indicates compression).
