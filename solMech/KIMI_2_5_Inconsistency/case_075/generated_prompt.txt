The beam is simply supported with span L = 4000 mm.

Support A is at x = 0 and support B is at x = 4000 mm.

A concentrated downward load P = 12000 N acts at x = 2000 mm (midspan).

The cut-section sign convention uses positive shear V downward on the left cut face and positive bending moment M as hogging (tension at top fibers).

The section of interest is at x = 1000 mm from support A, which is to the left of the load (0 < 1000 < 2000).

First, determine the support reactions using global equilibrium.

Sum of moments about A equals zero: R_B × 4000 − 12000 × 2000 = 0, giving R_B = 6000 N upward.

Sum of vertical forces equals zero: R_A + R_B − 12000 = 0, giving R_A = 6000 N upward.

For the section at x = 1000 mm, make a cut and consider the left segment (from A to the cut).

The left segment has upward reaction R_A = 6000 N at x = 0, and no other loads between A and the cut.

For vertical equilibrium of the left segment: V + R_A = 0, where V is the shear force on the left cut face (positive downward per the specified convention).

Thus V = −R_A = −6000 N.

For moment equilibrium of the left segment, take moments about the cut section (at x = 1000 mm):

The reaction R_A creates a counterclockwise moment: R_A × 1000 = 6000 × 1000 = 6,000,000 N·mm.

For equilibrium, the internal bending moment M (positive hogging, tension at top) must balance this.

Hogging moment creates tension at top fibers, which corresponds to a clockwise moment on the left segment.

Thus M = −R_A × 1000 = −6,000,000 N·mm.

The internal shear force V at x = 1000 mm is −6000 N.

The internal bending moment M at x = 1000 mm is −6,000,000 N·mm (or −6×10⁶ N·mm).
