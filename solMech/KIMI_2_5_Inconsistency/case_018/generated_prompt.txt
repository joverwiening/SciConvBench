Use N–mm–MPa units throughout all calculations.

The shaft is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 40 mm and total length L = 1500 mm.

The shaft is fixed at x = 0 (left end) and free at x = L (right end).

Torque T1 = 300000 N·mm is applied at x = L in the positive sense (counter-clockwise when viewed from positive x).

Torque T2 = -150000 N·mm is applied at x = a = 750 mm in the negative sense (clockwise when viewed from positive x).

The material is 6061-T6 aluminum with shear modulus G = 25939.8 MPa.

The shaft has constant diameter, so the polar moment of inertia J is constant along the entire length.

Define two segments: Segment 1 from x = 0 to x = a = 750 mm, and Segment 2 from x = a = 750 mm to x = L = 1500 mm.

For part (a), construct the internal torque diagram by determining the internal torque in each segment using static equilibrium. The internal torque in Segment 1 (0 ≤ x ≤ a) equals the algebraic sum of all torques to the right of any cut in that segment. The internal torque in Segment 2 (a ≤ x ≤ L) equals the algebraic sum of all torques to the right of any cut in that segment.

For part (b), compute the maximum shear stress in each segment using τ_max = T_internal · r / J, where r = d/2 = 20 mm is the outer radius and J = πd⁴/32 is the polar moment of inertia. Report the maximum shear stress for Segment 1 and Segment 2 separately.

For part (c), compute the angle of twist at x = L relative to the fixed end by summing the twist contributions from each segment. Use φ = Σ(T_internal · L_segment)/(G · J) for each segment, where L_segment is the length of each segment (750 mm each). The total angle of twist at x = L equals the sum of the twist in Segment 1 plus the twist in Segment 2. Report the final angle in degrees.
