Use N–mm–MPa units throughout the analysis.

The bar is prismatic with length L = 800 mm and cross-sectional area A = 300 mm^2.

The material is structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The bar behaves as a linear elastic axial member with stiffness k = EA/L.

A tensile load P = 8000 N is applied suddenly from zero load and remains constant thereafter.

The static stress is σ_static = P/A = 8000 N / 300 mm^2 = 26.667 MPa.

For a suddenly applied constant load on a linear spring, the work done by the load equals P times the maximum displacement (W = P·δ_max), not half the final load times displacement as in gradual loading.

The strain energy stored in the bar at maximum displacement is U = (1/2)k·δ_max^2 = (1/2)P·δ_max, since at maximum displacement the spring force equals the applied load (k·δ_max = P).

Equating work done to strain energy: P·δ_max = (1/2)k·δ_max^2, which yields δ_max = 2P/k = 2δ_static.

The maximum force in the bar at peak displacement is F_max = k·δ_max = k·(2P/k) = 2P.

The maximum stress is σ_max = F_max/A = 2P/A = 2 × 26.667 MPa = 53.333 MPa.

The maximum stress in the bar during the transient is 53.3 MPa (or exactly 160/3 MPa).
