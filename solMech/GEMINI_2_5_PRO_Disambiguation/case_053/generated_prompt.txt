The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The shaft is a solid circular cylinder with a diameter of 20 mm.
The shaft material is 6061-T6 aluminum.
The only applied load is a pure torque transmitted by the shaft.
The torque is generated by a motor with a power output of 5 kW.
The shaft rotates at a constant speed of 1200 rpm.
The allowable shear stress for 6061-T6 aluminum is assumed to be 72.5 MPa.
This assumption is based on a typical yield shear stress of 145 MPa and a factor of safety of 2.0.
Calculate the torque (T) from the power (P) and rotational speed (ω) using the relation T = P / ω.
Calculate the maximum shear stress (τ_max) on the shaft's outer surface.
The governing relation for maximum shear stress is τ_max = (T * r) / J, where r is the shaft radius.
The polar moment of inertia (J) for a solid circular shaft is J = (π/2) * r^4.
Compare the calculated maximum shear stress to the allowable shear stress to check the design adequacy.
The final output must state the calculated torque, the maximum shear stress, and the result of the comparison against the allowable shear stress.
