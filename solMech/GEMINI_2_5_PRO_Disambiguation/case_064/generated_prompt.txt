The objective is to determine the bending stress as a function of fiber location for a specified beam cross-section and internal moment.
The analysis assumes the material is linear-elastic, homogeneous, and isotropic.
The analysis assumes that plane cross-sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after bending.
The beam has a rectangular cross-section with a width of 50 mm and a height of 100 mm.
The internal bending moment at the cross-section of interest is 3,000,000 N·mm, causing sagging.
A coordinate system is established with its origin at the centroid of the cross-section.
The y-axis is vertical and positive upwards from the centroid.
The neutral axis coincides with the horizontal centroidal axis of the cross-section.
The second moment of area (I) about the neutral axis is calculated as (width * height^3) / 12, which equals 4,166,667 mm^4.
The governing relation for bending stress (σ) at a distance y from the neutral axis is the flexure formula: σ = -M*y / I.
For this case, the bending stress is σ(y) = -(3,000,000 N·mm * y) / 4,166,667 mm^4.
The resulting relation is σ(y) = -0.72 * y, where y is in mm and the resulting stress σ is in MPa.
The distance y is valid for any fiber location within the range of -50 mm to +50 mm.
