The analysis is for a simply supported beam under a uniformly distributed transverse load.
The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The analysis is based on Euler-Bernoulli beam theory.
The beam has a length (L) of 2500 mm.
The beam material has a Young's Modulus (E) of 200000 MPa.
The beam cross-section has a second moment of area (I) of 2000000 mm^4.
A uniformly distributed transverse load (w) of 0.7 N/mm is applied along the entire beam length.
The governing differential equation is E*I*(d^2v/dx^2) = M(x), where v is deflection and M is bending moment.
The deflection as a function of position x is v(x) = (w*x / (24*E*I)) * (L^3 - 2*L*x^2 + x^3).
The slope as a function of position x is θ(x) = dv/dx = (w / (24*E*I)) * (L^3 - 6*L*x^2 + 4*x^3).
The maximum deflection occurs at the mid-span (x = L/2) and is calculated to be 0.89 mm.
The maximum slope occurs at the supports (x = 0 and x = L) and is calculated to be 0.00114 radians.
