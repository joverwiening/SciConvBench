The analysis assumes a homogeneous and isotropic material.
The temperature change is uniform throughout the rod.
The axial restraints are perfectly rigid, resulting in zero total axial strain.
The rod is assumed to be stable and does not buckle under the induced compression.
The governing relation for total strain is the sum of thermal and mechanical strain.
Thermal strain is defined as the product of the coefficient of thermal expansion and the temperature change.
Mechanical strain is defined by Hooke's Law as the axial stress divided by the Young's Modulus.
The boundary condition of full axial restraint imposes that the total axial strain is zero.
The rod has a coefficient of thermal expansion of 1e-05 1/°C.
The rod has a Young's Modulus of 200000 MPa.
The rod has a circular cross-section with a diameter of 20 mm.
The rod is subjected to a uniform temperature increase of 35 °C.
There are no externally applied axial forces.
The calculated thermal strain is 3.5e-04.
The calculated mechanical strain is -3.5e-04.
The resulting axial stress is -70 MPa.
The resulting axial compressive force is approximately 21991 N.
