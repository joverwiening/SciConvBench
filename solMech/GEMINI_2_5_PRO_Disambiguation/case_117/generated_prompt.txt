The analysis assumes a prismatic beam with a rectangular cross-section of width b and height h.
The material is linear-elastic, homogeneous, and isotropic.
The analysis assumes small deformations.
The transverse shear stress is assumed to be uniformly distributed across the width of the beam's cross-section.
The governing relation for transverse shear stress (τ) is the Jourawski formula: τ = VQ / (Ib).
V is the internal shear force at the cross-section of interest.
I is the second moment of area of the entire rectangular cross-section about the neutral axis, given by I = (bh^3)/12.
b is the width of the rectangular cross-section.
Q is the first moment of area about the neutral axis of the portion of the cross-section above the point where stress is calculated.
For a rectangular cross-section, Q at a distance y from the neutral axis is Q = (b/2) * ((h/2)^2 - y^2).
Substituting the expressions for Q and I results in a parabolic stress distribution: τ = (V / (2I)) * ((h/2)^2 - y^2).
The maximum transverse shear stress occurs at the neutral axis (y=0).
The value of the maximum shear stress is τ_max = 1.5 * (V/A), where A = b*h is the total cross-sectional area.
The transverse shear stress is zero at the top and bottom surfaces of the beam (y = +/- h/2).
