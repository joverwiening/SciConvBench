The analysis will be performed on a solid circular shaft of length L and radius R.
The shaft is made of a homogeneous, isotropic, linear-elastic material with a shear modulus G.
The shaft is fixed at one end and free at the other end.
A concentrated torque, T, is applied to the free end of the shaft.
The analysis assumes small deformations and that plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the shaft axis.
The objective is to compute the angle of twist at the free end using Castigliano's second theorem.
The governing relation for the total strain energy (U) due to torsion is the integral of the strain energy density over the volume.
For a shaft under a torque T(x), the strain energy is U = integral from 0 to L of [T(x)^2 / (2 * G * J)] dx.
The internal torque, T(x), is constant and equal to the applied torque T along the entire length of the shaft.
The polar moment of inertia, J, for the solid circular cross-section is J = (pi * R^4) / 2.
The angle of twist, phi, at the point of application of the torque is found by taking the partial derivative of the strain energy with respect to the torque: phi = dU/dT.
The resulting expression for the angle of twist is phi = (T * L) / (G * J).
The maximum shear stress, tau_max, occurs at the outer surface of the shaft and is given by tau_max = (T * R) / J.
