Assume a cantilevered circular shaft fixed at end A.
The shaft has a length L and a radius r.
The material is linear-elastic, homogeneous, and isotropic with Young's modulus E and Poisson's ratio v.
A torque T is applied to the free end of the shaft.
A transverse point force P is applied to the free end of the shaft, causing bending.
The analysis will determine the principal stresses and strains at the point of maximum combined stress.
This point is located on the outer surface of the shaft at the fixed support A.
The maximum bending moment at the support is M = P * L.
The polar moment of inertia is J = (pi * r^4) / 2.
The area moment of inertia is I = (pi * r^4) / 4.
The maximum normal stress due to bending at the point of interest is sigma_x = (M * r) / I.
The maximum shear stress due to torsion at the point of interest is tau_xy = (T * r) / J.
Transverse shear stress due to the force P is zero at this point and is neglected.
The principal stresses are calculated using the formula: sigma_1,2 = (sigma_x / 2) +/- sqrt( (sigma_x / 2)^2 + tau_xy^2 ).
The maximum in-plane shear stress is tau_max = sqrt( (sigma_x / 2)^2 + tau_xy^2 ).
The principal strains are calculated from the principal stresses using Hooke's Law.
The first principal strain is epsilon_1 = (1/E) * (sigma_1 - v * sigma_2).
The second principal strain is epsilon_2 = (1/E) * (sigma_2 - v * sigma_1).
