The task is to determine the Euler critical buckling load for a column.
The column is subjected to compressive loading.
Small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics are assumed.
The column has a length L = 3000 mm.
The Young's Modulus of the column material is E = 69000 MPa.
The end conditions of the column are fixed-fixed.
For fixed-fixed end conditions, the effective length factor K = 0.5.
A solid square cross-section of 100 mm by 100 mm is assumed based on best practices.
The area moment of inertia (I) for this cross-section is (100 mm)^4 / 12 = 8.333 x 10^6 mm^4.
The governing relation for the Euler critical buckling load (P_cr) is P_cr = (pi^2 * E * I) / (K*L)^2.
The calculated Euler critical buckling load is P_cr = (pi^2 * 69000 MPa * 8.333 x 10^6 mm^4) / (0.5 * 3000 mm)^2.
A buckling/instability check involves comparing any applied compressive load (P_applied) to the calculated critical buckling load (P_cr).
If P_applied > P_cr, the column is predicted to buckle.
If P_applied <= P_cr, the column is predicted to remain stable under the applied load.
