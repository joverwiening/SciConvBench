The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior.
The beam is homogeneous and isotropic.
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after bending.
The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section.
Shear deformations are negligible, implying pure bending conditions.
The moment of inertia for the rectangular cross-section is calculated as I = (b*h^3)/12.
Given b = 50 mm and h = 120 mm, the moment of inertia I = (50 mm * (120 mm)^3) / 12 = 7,200,000 mm^4.
The internal bending moment is M = 4,000,000 N·mm (sagging).
The bending stress at an arbitrary fiber location 'y' from the neutral axis is given by the flexure formula: sigma_x = -M*y/I.
The coordinate 'y' is measured from the neutral axis, with positive 'y' upwards.
Substituting the given values, the bending stress is sigma_x = -(4,000,000 N·mm) * y / (7,200,000 mm^4).
This simplifies to sigma_x = -0.5556 * y N/mm^2.
The maximum compressive stress occurs at the top fiber (y = +60 mm), sigma_x_comp = -33.336 N/mm^2.
The maximum tensile stress occurs at the bottom fiber (y = -60 mm), sigma_x_tens = +33.336 N/mm^2.
