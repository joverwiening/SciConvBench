The problem involves a prismatic bar subjected to axial point loads under small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics assumptions.
The cross-sectional area of the bar is 200 mm^2.
The Young's Modulus of the bar material is 200000 MPa.
The total length of the bar is 1800 mm.
Three point loads are applied to the bar.
The first point load is 18000 N at a distance of 700 mm from the left end of the bar.
The second point load is -8000 N at a distance of 1300 mm from the left end of the bar.
The third point load is 20000 N at a distance of 1800 mm from the left end of the bar.
The bar is assumed to be fixed at its left end (x=0) to establish a reference for displacement.
The bar is assumed to be in static equilibrium.
The loads are applied axially along the centroidal axis of the bar.
The relevant response quantities to be determined are the axial force distribution F(x), axial stress distribution sigma(x), axial strain distribution epsilon(x), and axial displacement distribution u(x) along the length of the bar.
The governing relations are:
Axial force F(x) is determined by summing external axial forces to the left of the section.
Axial stress sigma(x) = F(x) / A.
Axial strain epsilon(x) = sigma(x) / E.
Axial displacement u(x) = integral from 0 to x of epsilon(xi) d(xi).
