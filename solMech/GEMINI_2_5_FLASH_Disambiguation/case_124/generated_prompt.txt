The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The beam is a rectangular cross-section with a width b = 40 mm and a height h = 100 mm.
The internal bending moment at the section of interest is M = 4,000,000 N·mm (sagging).
The moment of inertia (I) for the rectangular cross-section about the neutral axis is calculated as I = (b * h^3) / 12.
The neutral axis is located at the centroid of the rectangular cross-section, which is h/2 from the top and bottom surfaces.
The bending stress (sigma) at an arbitrary fiber location 'y' from the neutral axis is given by the flexure formula: sigma = (M * y) / I.
The variable 'y' ranges from -h/2 to +h/2, where positive 'y' denotes distance towards the bottom fiber and negative 'y' towards the top fiber.
For a sagging moment, fibers with positive 'y' (bottom) will experience tensile stress, and fibers with negative 'y' (top) will experience compressive stress.
The length and boundary conditions of the beam are not required for determining the bending stress at a section where the internal bending moment is already specified.
Additional assumptions include that the beam is homogeneous, isotropic, and adheres to Euler-Bernoulli beam theory (plane sections remain plane).
