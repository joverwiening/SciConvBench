The prismatic beam has a rectangular cross-section with a width of 50 mm and a height of 100 mm.
The internal bending moment applied to the beam is 3,000,000 N·mm.
Small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics are assumed.
The beam material is assumed to be homogeneous and isotropic.
The axial compression load is assumed to be 100 kN.
The axial tension load is assumed to be 50 kN.
The Young's Modulus of the beam material is assumed to be 200 GPa.
The bending moment is assumed to cause compression on the top surface and tension on the bottom surface.
The axial loads are assumed to be applied along the centroidal axis.
Stress concentrations are neglected.
The beam is straight and has a uniform cross-section.
The cross-sectional area A is calculated as the product of width and height.
A = b * h.
The moment of inertia I for the rectangular cross-section about the neutral axis is calculated as (b * h^3) / 12.
The distance from the neutral axis to the extreme fiber c is calculated as h / 2.
The axial stress due to compression (σ_compression) is calculated as the axial compression load divided by the cross-sectional area.
σ_compression = P_compression / A.
The axial stress due to tension (σ_tension) is calculated as the axial tension load divided by the cross-sectional area.
σ_tension = P_tension / A.
The maximum bending stress (σ_bending) is calculated using the flexure formula: M * c / I.
The total normal stress at any point is the superposition of axial stresses and bending stress.
