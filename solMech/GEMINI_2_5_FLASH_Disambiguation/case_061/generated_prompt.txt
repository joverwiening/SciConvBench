The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
The cross-section remains plane under loading.
The column is short, so buckling and second-order effects are negligible.
A rectangular cross-section with width b = 60 mm and height h = 120 mm is considered.
The cross-sectional area A = b * h = 7200 mm^2.
The moment of inertia about the y-axis (parallel to height) is I_y = h * b^3 / 12 = 2,160,000 mm^4.
A compressive axial load P = 60000 N is applied.
The load has an eccentricity e = 20 mm along the width direction (x-axis).
This eccentric load creates a bending moment M_y = P * e = 1,200,000 N-mm about the y-axis.
The normal stress distribution across the cross-section is given by sigma_x = -P/A - M_y*x/I_y, where x is the distance from the centroid along the width.
The maximum compressive stress occurs at x = b/2 = 30 mm.
The minimum compressive stress (or maximum tensile stress if applicable) occurs at x = -b/2 = -30 mm.
The maximum compressive stress is sigma_max = -P/A - M_y*(b/2)/I_y.
The minimum compressive stress is sigma_min = -P/A - M_y*(-b/2)/I_y.
