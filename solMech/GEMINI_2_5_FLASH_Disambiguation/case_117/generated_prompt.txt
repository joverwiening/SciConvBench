The analysis assumes small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The material properties are not required as the internal shear force is given directly.
The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 60 mm and height h = 80 mm.
The internal shear force is V = 16000 N.
The moment of inertia I for the rectangular cross-section about the neutral axis is calculated as I = (b * h^3) / 12.
I = (60 mm * (80 mm)^3) / 12 = 2,560,000 mm^4.
The first moment of area Q for a rectangular section above a distance y from the neutral axis is Q(y) = b * (h^2/8 - y^2/2).
The transverse shear stress τ at a distance y from the neutral axis is given by the formula τ(y) = (V * Q(y)) / (I * b).
The maximum transverse shear stress occurs at the neutral axis (y = 0).
At the neutral axis, Q_max = b * (h^2/8) = 60 mm * (80 mm)^2 / 8 = 48,000 mm^3.
The maximum transverse shear stress τ_max = (V * Q_max) / (I * b).
τ_max = (16000 N * 48000 mm^3) / (2560000 mm^4 * 60 mm).
τ_max = 5 MPa.
The shear stress distribution is parabolic, with zero stress at the top and bottom surfaces and maximum stress at the neutral axis.
