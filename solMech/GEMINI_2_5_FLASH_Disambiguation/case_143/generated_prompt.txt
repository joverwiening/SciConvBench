The material of the solid circular shaft is assumed to be steel with a Young's Modulus (E) of 200 GPa (200,000 N/mm²) and a Poisson's Ratio (ν) of 0.3.
The solid circular shaft has a diameter (d) of 25 mm, resulting in a radius (r) of 12.5 mm.
The cross-sectional area (A) of the shaft is calculated as π * (d/2)^2.
The polar moment of inertia (J) of the shaft is calculated as π * (d/2)^4 / 2.
The applied axial load (P) is 15000 N.
The applied torque (T) is 400000 N·mm.
The axial stress (σ_x) due to the axial load is calculated as P/A.
The maximum shear stress (τ_xy) due to torsion occurs at the outer surface and is calculated as T*r/J.
The stress state at the outer surface of the shaft is defined by σ_x and τ_xy, with other normal stresses (σ_y, σ_z) and shear stresses (τ_xz, τ_yz) being zero in the x-y plane (where x is axial and y is tangential).
The principal stresses (σ_1, σ_2, σ_3) are determined from this stress state using stress transformation equations or Mohr's Circle.
The principal strains (ε_1, ε_2, ε_3) are determined from the principal stresses using the generalized Hooke's Law: ε_i = (1/E) * [σ_i - ν*(σ_j + σ_k)] for i, j, k being distinct principal directions.
