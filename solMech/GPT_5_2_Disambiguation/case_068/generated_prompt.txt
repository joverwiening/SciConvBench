Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior.
Assume a straight, prismatic, solid circular shaft with constant diameter D = 30 mm and length L = 800 mm.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion of a circular shaft (no warping restraint effects; cross-sections remain plane and rotate as rigid disks).
Assume the shaft is subjected to a constant applied torque T = 500,000 N·mm about its longitudinal axis.
Use shear modulus G = 25,939.8 MPa = 25,939.8 N/mm^2.
Use consistent N–mm–MPa units throughout.

Compute the polar second moment of area for a solid circular shaft as J = (π/32) D^4.
Compute the torsional shear stress distribution as τ(r) = (T r) / J for 0 ≤ r ≤ D/2.
Compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface as τ_max = (T (D/2)) / J = (16 T) / (π D^3).
Compute the angle of twist over the length as φ = (T L) / (J G).
Compute the twist rate as dφ/dx = T / (J G).

Report response quantities as τ(r) (or τ_max) in MPa and φ in radians (optionally also in degrees via φ_deg = φ·180/π).
