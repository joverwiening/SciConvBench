Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion of a straight, prismatic, circular hollow shaft with constant cross-section.
Assume the applied torque is constant along the length and the shaft is free of stress concentrations.
Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.
Given: T = 450000 N·mm.
Given: L = 800 mm.
Given: d_o = 60 mm.
Given: d_i = 48 mm.
Given: G = 76923.1 MPa (i.e., N/mm^2).
Use the polar second moment of area: J = π(d_o^4 − d_i^4)/32.
Compute J numerically: J = π(60^4 − 48^4)/32 = 8.344×10^5 mm^4 (approximately).
Define outer radius: r_o = d_o/2 = 30 mm.
Use torsion shear stress distribution: τ(r) = T r / J.
Report maximum shear stress at the outer surface: τ_max = τ(r_o) = T r_o / J.
Compute τ_max numerically: τ_max = (450000×30)/(8.344×10^5) = 16.18 MPa (approximately).
Use angle of twist relation for uniform torsion: θ = T L / (J G).
Compute θ numerically: θ = (450000×800)/((8.344×10^5)×76923.1) = 0.00561 rad (approximately).
Optionally express twist in degrees if desired: θ ≈ 0.321°.
