Model the member as an Euler–Bernoulli cantilever beam with small deflection, linear-elastic material behavior.
Use a prismatic beam with constant E and constant second moment of area I along the span.
Let x be the coordinate measured from the fixed end (x = 0) to the free end (x = L).
Use L = 2000 mm, E = 200,000 N/mm², I = 1,500,000 mm⁴.
Apply a uniformly distributed transverse load w = 1.0 N/mm downward over 0 ≤ x ≤ L.
Neglect shear deformation and rotary inertia.
Use the beam governing relation E I y''''(x) = w, with y(x) positive upward so w is negative if taken downward.
Use cantilever boundary conditions y(0) = 0 and y'(0) = 0 at the fixed end, and M(L) = E I y''(L) = 0 and V(L) = E I y'''(L) = 0 at the free end.
Compute the free-end slope magnitude as θ(L) = w L^3 / (6 E I) (downward rotation for downward load).
Compute the maximum (free-end) deflection magnitude as δmax = y(L) = w L^4 / (8 E I) (downward deflection for downward load).
Evaluate numerically: θ(L) = (1.0)(2000^3) / (6·200,000·1,500,000) = 0.004444 rad downward.
Evaluate numerically: δmax = (1.0)(2000^4) / (8·200,000·1,500,000) = 6.667 mm downward.
