Assume small deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion of a prismatic, solid circular shaft with no warping restraint effects.
Assume the applied torque is static and constant along the full length.
Use shaft geometry: diameter d = 25 mm, radius R = 12.5 mm, length L = 1000 mm.
Use material shear modulus: G = 76923.1 N/mm².
Use applied torque: T = 350000 N·mm.

Compute the polar second moment of area for a solid circular shaft as J = (π/32) d^4 = (π/2) R^4.
Compute the torsional shear stress distribution as τ(r) = T r / J for 0 ≤ r ≤ R.
Compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface as τ_max = T R / J.

Compute the strain energy in torsion as U = ∫[0→L] (T(x)^2 / (2 G J)) dx.
For constant torque and constant section, reduce to U = T^2 L / (2 G J).

Apply Castigliano’s theorem for rotation at the torque application point as θ = ∂U/∂T.
For constant torque and constant section, obtain θ = ∂/∂T [T^2 L / (2 G J)] = T L / (G J).
Report θ in radians (dimensionless), consistent with N·mm, mm, and N/mm².

Compute torsional rigidity as GJ.
Compute angle of twist per unit length as dθ/dx = T / (GJ).
Compute shear strain distribution as γ(r) = τ(r) / G = T r / (G J).
Compute maximum shear strain as γ_max = τ_max / G = T R / (G J).

Optionally compute torsional compliance as Cθ = θ/T = L/(GJ).
Optionally compute equivalent von Mises stress for pure torsion as σ_vm(r) = √3 τ(r), with σ_vm,max = √3 τ_max.
