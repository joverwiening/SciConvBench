Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, isotropic material behavior.
Use a solid circular shaft with diameter d = 35 mm and length L = 1000 mm.
Use AISI 1020 steel with shear modulus G = 76923.1 MPa (N/mm²) and do not assume any allowable stress for required outputs.
Assume uniform circular cross-section along the length and Saint-Venant torsion (no stress concentrations, no warping restraint effects beyond standard circular-shaft torsion theory).
Assume steady rotation at n = 1800 rpm for power–torque conversion.
Treat any “shear loading” as the torsional shear stress caused by the applied torque unless an additional transverse shear force is explicitly provided.
Compute angular speed as ω = 2πn/60 in rad/s.
Relate power and torque by P = Tω, with consistent units (e.g., if P is in W then T is in N·m; if P is in kW then T(N·m) = 9550 P(kW)/n(rpm)).
Use polar second moment of area for a solid circular shaft: J = πd^4/32.
Compute maximum torsional shear stress at the outer surface: τ_max = Tc/J, where c = d/2, equivalently τ_max = 16T/(πd^3).
Compute the torsional shear strain distribution if needed: γ(r) = r·(dθ/dx) with maximum at r = c.
Compute angle of twist between ends: θ = TL/(JG), in radians, and convert to degrees by θ_deg = θ·180/π if desired.
Compute torsional stiffness if needed: k_t = T/θ = JG/L.
Report the relevant response quantities as functions of applied torque T and/or transmitted power P at 1800 rpm: torque from power, τ_max from torque, and θ from torque.
If an optional torsion check is requested later, select a reasonable allowable shear stress based on best practice for AISI 1020 steel and a stated safety factor, and compare τ_max to that allowable.
