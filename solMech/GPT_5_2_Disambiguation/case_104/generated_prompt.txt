Model the member as a vertical, prismatic, linearly elastic bar undergoing small axial deformation only.
Use the coordinate x measured downward from the top support, with x = 0 at the top and x = L at the bottom.
Use L = 3000 mm (3.0 m), constant cross-sectional area A = 300 mm^2 (3.0e-4 m^2), and Young’s modulus E = 69000 MPa (6.9e10 Pa).
Use mass density ρ = 8900 kg/m^3 and gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s^2, so the body force per unit volume is ρg and the specific weight is γ = ρg.
Assume the bar hangs from a rigid support at the top, so the axial displacement boundary condition is u(0) = 0.
Assume the bottom end is traction-free, so the axial force boundary condition is N(L) = 0.
Define the axial force N(x), axial stress σ(x), axial strain ε(x), and axial displacement u(x) as the relevant response quantities.
Relate stress and axial force by σ(x) = N(x)/A.
Relate strain and displacement by ε(x) = du/dx.
Use linear elasticity (Hooke’s law) σ(x) = E ε(x).
Write axial equilibrium for a bar under self-weight as dN/dx + ρ g A = 0.
Integrate equilibrium with N(L) = 0 to obtain N(x) = ρ g A (L − x).
Compute the stress distribution as σ(x) = ρ g (L − x), with maximum at the top σ(0) = ρ g L and zero at the bottom σ(L) = 0.
Compute the strain distribution as ε(x) = σ(x)/E = (ρ g/E)(L − x).
Compute the displacement field from du/dx = (ρ g/E)(L − x) with u(0) = 0, giving u(x) = (ρ g/E)(L x − x^2/2).
Compute the total elongation (downward displacement of the bottom end) as δ = u(L) = ρ g L^2/(2E).
Evaluate δ numerically using consistent SI units: δ = (8900·9.81·3.0^2)/(2·6.9e10) m ≈ 5.69e-6 m ≈ 0.00569 mm.
Report the maximum axial force at the top as N(0) = ρ g A L.
Evaluate N(0) numerically: N(0) = 8900·9.81·(3.0e-4)·3.0 N ≈ 78.6 N.
Report the maximum stress at the top as σ(0) = ρ g L.
Evaluate σ(0) numerically: σ(0) = 8900·9.81·3.0 Pa ≈ 2.62e5 Pa ≈ 0.262 MPa.
