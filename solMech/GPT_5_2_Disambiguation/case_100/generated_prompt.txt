Use small-deformation, linear-elastic Euler–Bernoulli beam theory.
Model a prismatic cantilever beam fixed at x = 0 and free at x = L.
Use L = 1500 mm, E = 200,000 MPa (N/mm²), and constant I = 2,500,000 mm⁴.
Apply a single concentrated load P = 500 N downward at the free end x = L.
Assume plane sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis (shear deformation neglected).
Assume deflections and rotations are small so geometric nonlinearity is neglected.
Take the transverse deflection at the free end in the load direction as the response quantity of interest, δ(L).

Define the internal bending moment for 0 ≤ x ≤ L as M(x) = P(L − x), using the sign convention consistent with downward end load producing sagging curvature.
Write the strain energy in bending as U = ∫₀ᴸ [M(x)²/(2EI)] dx.
Apply Castigliano’s theorem for the deflection in the direction of P as δ(L) = ∂U/∂P.
Compute U = ∫₀ᴸ [P²(L − x)²/(2EI)] dx = P²L³/(6EI).
Differentiate to obtain δ(L) = ∂/∂P [P²L³/(6EI)] = PL³/(3EI).

Evaluate numerically in consistent N–mm units: E = 200,000 N/mm², L = 1500 mm, I = 2,500,000 mm⁴, P = 500 N.
Compute δ(L) = (500)(1500³) / (3·200,000·2,500,000) mm = 1.125 mm downward.
