Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.
Assume a prismatic short column so Euler buckling and second-order (P–Δ) effects are neglected.
Assume the load is applied parallel to the column axis with constant eccentricity along the member length.
Use a rectangular cross-section with width b = 100 mm and height h = 100 mm.
Take the centroid at the section center with x along the width b and y along the height h.
Use the sign convention: tension positive, compression negative.
Use the applied axial load P = −90,000 N (compression).
Use eccentricity magnitude e = 15 mm along +x (width direction), producing bending about the centroidal z-axis.
Compute cross-sectional area A = b h.
Compute second moment of area about the z-axis (bending due to x-eccentricity) as I_z = (h b^3)/12.
Compute the applied bending moment about the z-axis as M_z = P e.
Compute the normal stress distribution (combined axial + bending) as σ(x) = P/A − (M_z x)/I_z, with x measured from the centroid and x ∈ [−b/2, +b/2].
Evaluate extreme-fiber stresses at x = +b/2 and x = −b/2 as σ_max = P/A − (M_z (b/2))/I_z and σ_min = P/A + (M_z (b/2))/I_z.
Compute the neutral axis location (if it exists within the section) from σ(x0) = 0 giving x0 = (P/A)(I_z/M_z).
Determine whether the section is entirely in compression or has combined compression and tension by checking the signs of σ at x = ±b/2.
Report the maximum compressive stress magnitude and maximum tensile stress magnitude on the section from the extreme-fiber values.
Optionally compute the resultant strain distribution under linear elasticity as ε(x) = σ(x)/E if Young’s modulus E is provided; otherwise leave strains in terms of E.
Optionally compute curvature κ about the z-axis as κ = M_z/(E I_z) if E is provided; otherwise leave κ in terms of E.
