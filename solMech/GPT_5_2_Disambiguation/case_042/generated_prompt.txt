Assume a straight, prismatic bar with constant cross-sectional area A and length L.
Assume small deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior with Young’s modulus E.
Assume uniaxial stress and strain (Saint-Venant), neglecting shear deformation and stress concentrations.
Assume the bar has one end fixed and the axial tensile load P is applied suddenly at the free end, rising instantaneously from 0 to P and then held constant.
Assume no damping and no plasticity, so the response is elastic and oscillatory and energy is conserved between the external work input and the bar’s strain energy at peak extension.
Use A = 300 mm², L = 800 mm, E = 200000 MPa, P = 8000 N.

Define the static axial stiffness k = AE/L.
Compute k = (200000 N/mm²)(300 mm²)/(800 mm) = 75000 N/mm.
Compute the static end displacement under load P as δ_st = P/k = PL/(AE).
Compute δ_st = 8000/75000 = 0.1066667 mm.

For a suddenly applied constant load (step load), use energy at maximum extension δ_max:
External work done by the load up to δ_max is W = P δ_max.
Strain energy stored in the bar at δ_max is U = (1/2) k δ_max².
At maximum extension (zero kinetic energy), set W = U, so P δ_max = (1/2) k δ_max².
Solve for δ_max = 2P/k = 2 δ_st.
Compute δ_max = 2(0.1066667) = 0.2133333 mm.

Compute maximum axial force in the bar at peak extension as N_max = k δ_max = 2P.
Compute N_max = 16000 N.

Compute maximum axial stress as σ_max = N_max/A = 2P/A.
Compute σ_max = 16000 N / 300 mm² = 53.3333 N/mm² = 53.3333 MPa.

Compute maximum axial strain as ε_max = σ_max/E.
Compute ε_max = 53.3333 MPa / 200000 MPa = 2.66667×10^-4.

Report the dynamic amplification factor for stress and displacement for a suddenly applied load as DAF = 2 relative to the corresponding static values.
