Use small-deformation, linear-elastic Euler–Bernoulli beam theory (plane sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis).
Assume the beam is prismatic with constant second moment of area I along the region of interest.
Assume bending is about the neutral axis associated with the provided second moment of area I = 1,200,000 mm^4.
Assume pure bending at the section(s) where curvature is evaluated (shear deformation neglected for curvature-from-moment relation).
Use consistent MPa-based units: take E in MPa (N/mm^2), I in mm^4, bending moment M in N·mm, curvature κ in 1/mm, and radius of curvature ρ in mm.
Relate bending moment to curvature by κ = M/(E I), with sign following the chosen moment/curvature convention.
Compute radius of curvature as ρ = 1/|κ| = (E I)/|M|.
If M varies along the beam coordinate x, use κ(x) = M(x)/(E I) and ρ(x) = (E I)/|M(x)|.
If only a moment magnitude is given, report curvature magnitude as |κ| = |M|/(E I) and radius magnitude as ρ = (E I)/|M|.
Treat I as sufficient for κ and ρ; cross-section shape and dimensions are not required beyond the provided I.
