Assume a uniform cylindrical tension specimen with initial diameter d0 = 12.5 mm, initial cross-sectional area A0 = (π d0^2)/4, and initial gage length L0 = 50 mm.
Assume uniaxial, quasi-static axial loading with a single load point P = 15000 N producing an elongation ΔL = 0.15 mm over the gage length.
Assume small deformation so engineering strain equals axial strain: εeng = ΔL/L0.
Compute engineering strain: εeng = 0.15/50 = 0.003.
Assume engineering stress equals nominal stress based on initial area: σeng = P/A0.
Compute A0: A0 = (π/4)(12.5 mm)^2 = 122.72 mm^2 = 1.2272×10^-4 m^2.
Compute engineering stress: σeng = 15000/(1.2272×10^-4) = 1.222×10^8 Pa = 122.2 MPa.
Assume linear-elastic isotropic Hooke’s law in uniaxial form: σ = E ε.
Determine Young’s modulus from the slope at the given point: E = σeng/εeng.
Compute E: E = (122.2 MPa)/(0.003) = 40.7 GPa.
Relevant response quantities for this case are engineering stress σeng = 122.2 MPa, engineering strain εeng = 0.003, and Young’s modulus E = 40.7 GPa.
Assume no necking, no plasticity, and no change in cross-sectional area is needed for engineering measures at this load point.
