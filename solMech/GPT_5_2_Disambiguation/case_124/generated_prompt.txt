Assume small deformation, linear-elastic material behavior.
Assume Euler–Bernoulli beam theory applies (plane sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis after bending).
Assume the beam is prismatic with a rectangular cross-section of width b = 40 mm and height h = 100 mm.
Assume bending is about the centroidal horizontal axis (z-axis) with y measured from the neutral axis (centroid), positive upward.
Assume the internal bending moment at the section of interest is M = +4,000,000 N·mm, sagging, and treat M as constant over that cross-section for stress evaluation.
Neglect shear deformation effects on normal bending stress (i.e., compute only σx due to bending moment).
Use the centroidal second moment of area about the z-axis: I = b h^3 / 12.
Compute I numerically: I = (40 mm)(100 mm)^3 / 12 = 3.333333×10^6 mm^4.
Define the arbitrary fiber location by its y-coordinate from the neutral axis, with valid range −h/2 ≤ y ≤ +h/2 (i.e., −50 mm ≤ y ≤ +50 mm).
Compute the longitudinal bending normal stress at an arbitrary fiber location y using the flexure formula: σx(y) = −(M y) / I.
Use the stated sign convention: for sagging M > 0, fibers with y > 0 (top) are in compression (σx < 0) and fibers with y < 0 (bottom) are in tension (σx > 0).
Compute the extreme-fiber stresses at y = ±h/2 as σx,top = −M(h/2)/I and σx,bottom = +M(h/2)/I.
Compute the extreme-fiber magnitude numerically: |σx,max| = M(h/2)/I = (4.0×10^6 N·mm)(50 mm)/(3.333333×10^6 mm^4) = 60 N/mm^2 = 60 MPa.
Report bending stress at any requested fiber location by substituting its y value into σx(y) = −(M y)/I, yielding stress in N/mm^2 (MPa).
