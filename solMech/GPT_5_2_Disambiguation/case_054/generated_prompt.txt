Assume a straight prismatic bar of length L loaded only by an axial end force P, with no body forces and no bending.
Assume small deformation, linear elasticity, homogeneous isotropic material with constant Young’s modulus E.
Assume the axial force is constant along the bar: N(x)=P (tension positive).
Use the given linearly varying area A(x)=A0(1+k x/L), with x measured from the left end.
Compute axial stress as σ(x)=N(x)/A(x)=P/[A0(1+k x/L)].
Compute axial strain as ε(x)=σ(x)/E=P/[E A0(1+k x/L)].
Relate strain to displacement by ε(x)=du/dx.
Compute axial displacement field (taking u(0)=0 as reference) as u(x)=∫0x P/[E A0(1+k ξ/L)] dξ.
Evaluate the integral to obtain u(x)=(P L)/(E A0 k) ln(1+k x/L).
Compute total elongation as δ=u(L)=(P L)/(E A0 k) ln(1+k).
Compute axial compliance as δ/P=(L)/(E A0 k) ln(1+k).
Compute axial stiffness as K=P/δ=(E A0 k)/(L ln(1+k)).
Compute maximum stress at the minimum area end: σmax=σ(0)=P/A0.
Compute minimum stress at the maximum area end: σmin=σ(L)=P/[A0(1+k)].
Use consistent N–mm–MPa units: P in N, L and x in mm, A in mm^2, E in MPa (N/mm^2), giving u and δ in mm.
