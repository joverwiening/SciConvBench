Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior and Euler–Bernoulli beam theory (plane sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis after bending).
Assume pure bending at the section of interest (the given internal bending moment M governs the normal stress; shear effects on normal stress are neglected).
Use a prismatic rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm, with y measured from the neutral axis, positive upward.
Use the sign convention positive bending moment M = sagging.
Compute the second moment of area about the neutral (centroidal) axis for bending as I = b h^3 / 12.
For the given section, I = (50 mm)(120 mm)^3 / 12 = 7,200,000 mm^4.
Use the flexure formula for normal bending stress at an arbitrary fiber location y: σ(y) = -(M y) / I.
Use M = 4,000,000 N·mm.
At the neutral axis y = 0: σ(0) = 0 N/mm^2.
At the top fiber y = +h/2 = +60 mm: σ_top = -(4,000,000)(60) / 7,200,000 = -33.33 N/mm^2 (=-33.33 MPa), compressive.
At the bottom fiber y = -h/2 = -60 mm: σ_bot = -(4,000,000)(-60) / 7,200,000 = +33.33 N/mm^2 (=+33.33 MPa), tensile.
State that for sagging moment with y positive upward, the top fibers are in compression and the bottom fibers are in tension under this convention.
