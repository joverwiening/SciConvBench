Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
Model the member as a short prismatic bar with a constant rectangular cross section.
Take the cross-sectional dimensions as b = 40 mm and h = 120 mm.
Evaluate stresses at the cross section where the internal resultants are P = 30,000 N (tension) and V = 12,000 N (transverse shear).
Assume the bending moment at the section is M = 0.
Assume Saint-Venant type stress resultants so that average (section-averaged) stresses are acceptable response quantities.
Assume the transverse shear force V acts parallel to the width direction b, and compute average shear stress on the cross section associated with that shear resultant.
Compute the cross-sectional area as A = b h.
Compute the average normal stress at the section as sigma_avg = P / A (tension taken as positive).
Compute the average shear stress at the section as tau_avg = V / A (magnitude), with direction consistent with the applied shear resultant.
Report sigma_avg and tau_avg in consistent units (e.g., N/mm^2 = MPa).
With the given numbers, A = (40 mm)(120 mm) = 4800 mm^2.
With the given numbers, sigma_avg = 30,000 N / 4800 mm^2 = 6.25 MPa (tension).
With the given numbers, tau_avg = 12,000 N / 4800 mm^2 = 2.50 MPa.
