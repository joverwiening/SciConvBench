Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
Model the member as a prismatic, straight beam with a rectangular cross-section of width b and height h.
Assume homogeneous, isotropic material behavior.
Assume elementary beam theory stress recovery for transverse shear stress using the shear formula τ = VQ/(I b).
Do not include Timoshenko shear deformation or any shear correction factor beyond the rectangle maximum-shear factor 1.5.
Assume the transverse shear force resultant at the section is V (units consistent with MPa stress output, e.g., N with mm geometry).
Define cross-sectional area A = b h.
Define second moment of area about the neutral axis I = b h^3 / 12.
Define the through-depth coordinate y measured from the neutral axis, with y = 0 at mid-depth and y = ±h/2 at the outer fibers.
Define b(y) = b for a rectangle (constant width through the depth).
Define Q(y) as the first moment of area of the portion of the cross-section above (or below) the level y about the neutral axis.
For a rectangular section, use Q(y) = b (h^2/8 − y^2/2) for −h/2 ≤ y ≤ h/2.
Compute the transverse shear stress distribution as τ(y) = V Q(y) / (I b).
For a rectangular section, simplify to τ(y) = (3V/(2A)) (1 − 4y^2/h^2).
State boundary behavior: τ(±h/2) = 0 at the top and bottom surfaces.
State maximum transverse shear stress occurs at the neutral axis: τ_max = τ(0) = 3V/(2A) = 1.5 V/A.
Report response quantities relevant to transverse shear stress as V, τ(y), and τ_max, with τ in MPa when V and geometry are provided in consistent units.
