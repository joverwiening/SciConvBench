Assume a straight, prismatic, solid circular shaft with constant diameter d = 35 mm and length L = 1200 mm.
Assume small deformation, linear elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior with shear modulus G = 77,519.4 N/mm^2.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion applies (plane cross-sections remain plane and circular; warping effects neglected).
Assume the shaft is fixed against rotation at x = 0 so θ(0) = 0.
Apply concentrated torques at x = 800 mm with T2 = −250,000 N·mm and at x = 1200 mm with T1 = +200,000 N·mm, with the given sign convention.
Compute the polar second moment of area for a solid circular section as J = (π d^4)/32.
Compute the internal torque distribution T(x) from torque equilibrium using a piecewise-constant function with jumps at applied torque locations.
Use the convention that the internal torque at a section equals the algebraic sum of external torques acting on the free-end side of the cut.
For 0 ≤ x < 800 mm, set T(x) = T1 + T2.
For 800 mm ≤ x ≤ 1200 mm, set T(x) = T1.
Compute the reaction torque at the fixed end as Tr = −(T1 + T2) to satisfy global torque equilibrium.
Compute the maximum torsional shear stress at radius r as τ(r, x) = T(x) r / J.
Compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface as τmax(x) = T(x) (d/2) / J.
Compute the angle-of-twist rate as dθ/dx = T(x) / (G J).
Compute the twist distribution as θ(x) = ∫0→x [T(ξ)/(GJ)] dξ with θ(0) = 0, evaluated piecewise over the intervals [0, 800] and [800, 1200].
Compute the relative twist between any two stations xa and xb as θ(xb) − θ(xa) = ∫xa→xb [T(ξ)/(GJ)] dξ.
Report as relevant response quantities the internal torque diagram T(x), reaction torque Tr, torsional shear stress distribution τ(r, x) and τmax(x), twist distribution θ(x), and total twist at the free end θ(L).
