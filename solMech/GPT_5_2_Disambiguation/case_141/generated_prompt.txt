Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
Model a solid circular shaft with diameter d = 35 mm.
Evaluate stresses at the critical section on the outer surface where bending stress is maximum.
Apply a bending moment M = 3,000,000 N·mm and a torque T = 500,000 N·mm simultaneously at that section.
Assume no other applied moments or loads at the section beyond M and T.
Assume plane stress at the outer surface point of interest (free surface), so σz = 0 and τxz = τyz = 0 for a shaft axis taken as z.
Define the longitudinal (axial) stress from bending as σ = σb acting along the shaft axis at the outer fiber.
Define the torsional shear stress as τ = τt acting on the surface in the tangential direction associated with torque about the shaft axis.
Use outer radius c = d/2.
Use second moment of area for a solid circle I = (π d^4)/64.
Use polar moment of inertia for a solid circle J = (π d^4)/32.
Compute bending normal stress magnitude at the outer surface as σb = M c / I = 32 M / (π d^3).
Compute torsional shear stress magnitude at the outer surface as τt = T c / J = 16 T / (π d^3).
Form the in-plane stress state at the surface point as σx = σb, σy = 0, τxy = τt (with x aligned to the shaft axis and y aligned circumferentially at the point).
Compute principal stresses for plane stress as σ1,2 = (σx + σy)/2 ± sqrt(((σx − σy)/2)^2 + τxy^2), with σ3 = 0 at the free surface.
Compute maximum in-plane shear stress as τmax,in-plane = sqrt(((σx − σy)/2)^2 + τxy^2).
Compute the principal plane angle (from the x-axis to the σ1 plane) as tan(2θp) = 2 τxy / (σx − σy).
Compute von Mises equivalent stress for plane stress as σv = sqrt(σx^2 − σx σy + σy^2 + 3 τxy^2), which reduces here to σv = sqrt(σb^2 + 3 τt^2).
Compute Tresca equivalent stress as σTresca = max(|σ1 − σ2|, |σ2 − σ3|, |σ3 − σ1|).
If strains are additionally desired despite not being required, assume isotropic linear elasticity with representative steel properties E = 200 GPa and ν = 0.30.
If strains are computed under plane stress, use εx = (1/E)(σx − ν σy), εy = (1/E)(σy − ν σx), and γxy = τxy/G with G = E/(2(1+ν)).
If principal strains are computed from strains, use ε1,2 = (εx + εy)/2 ± sqrt(((εx − εy)/2)^2 + (γxy/2)^2), with ε3 = −(ν/E)(σx + σy) under plane stress.
