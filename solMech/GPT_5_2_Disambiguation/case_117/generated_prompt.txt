Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.
Assume a prismatic, straight beam with a rectangular cross-section of width b = 60 mm and height h = 80 mm.
Assume the internal transverse shear force at the section is V = +16,000 N (use magnitude for stress).
Use consistent N–mm units so that stress is obtained in N/mm^2 = MPa.
Neglect shear stress variation across the width and use beam theory transverse shear formulas for a rectangular section.
Compute cross-sectional area as A = b h.
Compute second moment of area about the centroidal (neutral) axis as I = b h^3 / 12.
Define y measured from the neutral axis, positive upward, with the top surface at y = +h/2 and bottom surface at y = −h/2.
Determine the transverse shear stress distribution through the depth using τ(y) = V Q(y) / (I b), where Q(y) is the first moment of area of the portion of the cross-section above the level y about the neutral axis.
For a rectangle using “area above y”, compute A′(y) = b (h/2 − y) for −h/2 ≤ y ≤ +h/2.
For that area, compute its centroid location from the neutral axis as ȳ′(y) = (y + h/2)/2.
Compute Q(y) = A′(y) ȳ′(y).
Evaluate the maximum transverse shear stress at the neutral axis y = 0 using τ_max = 1.5 V / A.
Evaluate the transverse shear stress at y = +26.667 mm using τ(y) = V Q(y) / (I b) with Q(y) computed from the area above that level.
Report τ_max and τ(y = +26.667 mm) in MPa.
