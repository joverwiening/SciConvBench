Assume small deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior in shear.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion of a prismatic solid circular shaft with no warping restraint effects.
Use a solid circular shaft with diameter d = 30 mm (radius R = 15 mm) and length L = 1000 mm.
Use shear modulus G = 25939.8 MPa = 25939.8 N/mm².
Apply a constant internal torque T = 450000 N·mm along 0 ≤ x ≤ L.
Apply boundary conditions φ(0) = 0 at the fixed end and torque T applied at x = L, so the end-to-end twist is φ(L) − φ(0).

Use the polar second moment of area for a solid circular section: J = (π d^4)/32 = (π R^4)/2.
Compute J numerically: J = (π (30 mm)^4)/32 = 79577.47 mm^4.

Use the torsional strain energy density relation integrated over the length: U = ∫0^L [T(x)^2/(2 G J)] dx.
With constant torque T(x) = T, the total strain energy is U = T^2 L/(2 G J).

Apply Castigliano’s theorem for rotation at the point of application of torque: θ = ∂U/∂T.
Compute twist (end rotation) from Castigliano: θ = ∂/∂T [T^2 L/(2 G J)] = (T L)/(G J).
Compute numerically: θ = (450000 N·mm × 1000 mm)/(25939.8 N/mm² × 79577.47 mm^4) = 0.2182 rad.
Report twist in degrees if desired: θ = 0.2182 rad × (180/π) = 12.50°.

Compute the torsional shear stress distribution: τ(r) = T r/J for 0 ≤ r ≤ R.
Compute maximum shear stress at the outer surface: τmax = T R/J.
Compute numerically: τmax = (450000 N·mm × 15 mm)/79577.47 mm^4 = 84.83 MPa.

Compute the angle of twist per unit length (rate of twist): dφ/dx = T/(G J).
Compute numerically: dφ/dx = 450000/(25939.8 × 79577.47) = 2.182×10^-4 rad/mm = 0.2182 rad/m.

Compute the strain energy numerically: U = T^2 L/(2 G J) = (450000^2 × 1000)/(2 × 25939.8 × 79577.47) = 49090 N·mm = 49.09 J.
