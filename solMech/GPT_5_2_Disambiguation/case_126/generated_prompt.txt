Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.
Assume the shaft is a straight, prismatic, solid circular shaft with constant diameter d = 30 mm and length L = 1200 mm.
Assume the shaft material is homogeneous and isotropic with shear modulus G = 39,179.1 MPa = 39,179.1 N/mm^2.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion of a circular shaft applies (plane cross-sections remain plane and rotate as rigid disks; warping is not restrained).
Assume the applied loading is a pure torque T = 400,000 N·mm about the shaft axis (no bending moment, no axial force).
Compute the polar second moment of area for a solid circular shaft as J = (π d^4)/32.
Compute the torsional shear stress distribution as τ(r) = (T r)/J for 0 ≤ r ≤ d/2.
Compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface as τ_max = τ(d/2) = (T (d/2))/J = (16 T)/(π d^3).
Compute the angle of twist over the length as θ = (T L)/(J G).
Compute the twist rate as dθ/dx = T/(J G).
Report τ_max in MPa (since 1 MPa = 1 N/mm^2) and θ in radians (optionally also in degrees using θ_deg = θ·180/π).
