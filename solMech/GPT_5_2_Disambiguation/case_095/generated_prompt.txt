Model the member as a prismatic Euler–Bernoulli cantilever beam of length L = 1800 mm with constant flexural rigidity EI = 2.4×10^11 N·mm^2.
Use small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior and small rotations.
Assume plane sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis (Euler–Bernoulli kinematics) and neglect shear deformation and rotary inertia.
Take x measured from the fixed end (x = 0) to the free end (x = L).
Apply a concentrated transverse load P = 2000 N downward at the free end x = L.
Apply no end moment at the free end, i.e., M(L) = 0 N·mm.
Use the sign convention: downward deflection v(x) is negative, positive bending moment produces concave-up curvature, and the governing relation is EI v''(x) = M(x).
Use cantilever boundary conditions v(0) = 0 and v'(0) = 0.
Use free-end conditions V(L) = -P and M(L) = 0, where V(x) is internal shear force and M(x) is internal bending moment.
For 0 ≤ x ≤ L, the internal shear is V(x) = -P.
For 0 ≤ x ≤ L, the internal bending moment is M(x) = -P (L - x).
The curvature is v''(x) = M(x)/EI = -P (L - x)/EI.
The slope is v'(x) = -P (L x - x^2/2)/EI.
The deflection is v(x) = -P (L x^2/2 - x^3/6)/EI.
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end and equals v(L) = -P L^3/(3 EI).
The maximum slope occurs at the free end and equals v'(L) = -P L^2/(2 EI).
Compute the free-end deflection magnitude: |v(L)| = (2000)(1800^3)/(3·2.4×10^11) mm = 0.0162 mm, with v(L) = -0.0162 mm (downward).
Compute the free-end slope: v'(L) = -(2000)(1800^2)/(2·2.4×10^11) rad = -1.35×10^-5 rad.
Report relevant response quantities for this case as V(x), M(x), v''(x), v'(x), v(x), and the extrema at x = L.
