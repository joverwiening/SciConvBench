Model the member as a vertical, prismatic, linearly elastic bar undergoing small deformation under axial loading only.
Use bar length L = 3500 mm = 3.5 m.
Use constant cross-sectional area A = 200 mm^2 = 2.0e-4 m^2.
Use Young’s modulus E = 69000 MPa = 6.9e10 Pa.
Use mass density ρ = 7850 kg/m^3 and gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s^2, so specific weight γ = ρg = 77008.5 N/m^3.
Assume the bar hangs from a rigid support at the top end and is free at the bottom end, with no external axial loads other than self-weight.
Define the axial coordinate x measured downward from the top support, with x = 0 at the top and x = L at the bottom.
Assume uniaxial stress and strain with plane sections remaining plane, so σ(x) = N(x)/A and ε(x) = du/dx.
Use linear elasticity σ(x) = E ε(x).
Model self-weight as a distributed axial body force per unit length w = γA (constant for a prismatic bar).
Write axial force equilibrium as dN/dx + w = 0.
Apply the free-end boundary condition N(L) = 0.
Solve for internal axial force N(x) = w(L − x) = γA(L − x).
Compute axial stress distribution σ(x) = N(x)/A = γ(L − x), with maximum at the top σ(0) = γL and zero at the bottom σ(L) = 0.
Compute axial strain distribution ε(x) = σ(x)/E = γ(L − x)/E.
Compute displacement by integrating du/dx = ε(x) with boundary condition u(0) = 0, giving u(x) = (γ/E)(Lx − x^2/2).
Report elongation of the bar as the bottom displacement δ = u(L) = γL^2/(2E).
Evaluate δ numerically: δ = (77008.5)(3.5^2)/(2·6.9e10) m = 6.83e-6 m = 0.00683 mm.
Optionally report the total weight W = γAL and the top axial force N(0) = W for consistency checks.
