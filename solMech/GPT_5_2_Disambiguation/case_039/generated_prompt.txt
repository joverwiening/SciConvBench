Assume small strains and small deformations.
Assume linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior.
Use AISI 1020 steel with E = 200,000 MPa and ν = 0.30.
Model the member as a straight, prismatic, solid circular rod.
Use initial diameter d0 = 20 mm and initial radius r0 = 10 mm.
Assume the only applied load is a centric axial tensile force P = 35,000 N.
Assume lateral load = 0, so bending and transverse shear effects are neglected.
Assume Saint-Venant conditions away from the loaded ends so the stress state is uniform uniaxial tension in the gauge region.
Compute initial cross-sectional area as A0 = (π/4) d0^2.
Compute axial (engineering) normal stress as σz = P / A0.
Set transverse normal stresses to zero for uniaxial tension: σx = 0 and σy = 0.
Compute axial (engineering) strain as εz = σz / E.
Compute lateral strains from Poisson effect as εx = −ν εz and εy = −ν εz.
Relate lateral strain to diameter change using engineering strain: εd = Δd / d0, and for an isotropic circular rod under uniaxial tension take εd = εx = εy.
Compute diameter change as Δd = εd d0 = (−ν εz) d0.
Compute radius change as Δr = Δd / 2.
Compute the deformed diameter as d = d0 + Δd and deformed radius as r = r0 + Δr.
Optionally compute volumetric strain under uniaxial stress as εv = εx + εy + εz = (1 − 2ν) εz.
Report as relevant response quantities for Poisson effect under uniaxial tension: σz, εz, εx, εy, Δd, Δr, and (if desired) εv.
