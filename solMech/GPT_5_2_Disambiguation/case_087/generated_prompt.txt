Assume a standard round tension specimen with initial gage length L0 = 50 mm and initial diameter d0 = 12 mm.
Assume the final gage length after fracture is measured by fitting the broken pieces together to reconstruct the original gage section, giving Lf = 66 mm.
Assume the minimum diameter at the neck after fracture is df = 9.6 mm.
Assume the cross-section is circular both initially and at the minimum neck section.
Assume deformation is uniform over the gage length for the purpose of percent elongation (engineering ductility measure).
Assume reduction of area is evaluated at the minimum necked section at fracture (localized deformation measure).
Compute initial cross-sectional area as A0 = (π/4) d0^2.
Compute final minimum cross-sectional area as Af = (π/4) df^2.
Define percent elongation as %EL = ((Lf − L0) / L0) × 100%.
Using the given values, %EL = ((66 − 50) / 50) × 100% = 32%.
Define percent reduction of area as %RA = ((A0 − Af) / A0) × 100%.
Using circular areas, %RA = (1 − (df/d0)^2) × 100%.
Using the given values, %RA = (1 − (9.6/12)^2) × 100% = (1 − 0.64) × 100% = 36%.
