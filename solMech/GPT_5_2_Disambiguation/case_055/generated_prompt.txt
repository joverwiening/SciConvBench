Assume a straight prismatic bar in the sense of 1D axial deformation (no bending), with small strain and linear elasticity.
Assume the axial force is constant along the bar, N(x)=P, because there are no distributed axial loads and the only applied load is an end tensile force.
Assume the bar is statically determinate with one end fixed and the other end pulled by P, so the total elongation equals the end displacement.
Use x measured from the left end, with 0≤x≤L.
Use the given linearly varying area A(x)=A0(1+kx/L), with A0=200 mm^2, k=0.6, L=1200 mm.
Use the given constant tensile load P=20000 N.
Use the given Young’s modulus E=69000 MPa = 69000 N/mm^2.
Compute the axial stress distribution as σ(x)=N(x)/A(x)=P/[A0(1+kx/L)].
Compute the axial strain distribution as ε(x)=σ(x)/E=P/[E A0(1+kx/L)].
Relate strain to displacement by ε(x)=du/dx.
Compute the axial displacement field (taking u(0)=0 at the fixed left end) as u(x)=∫0^x P/[E A0(1+kξ/L)] dξ.
Evaluate the integral to obtain u(x)=(P L)/(E A0 k) ln(1+kx/L).
Compute the total elongation as δ=u(L)=(P L)/(E A0 k) ln(1+k).
Numerically, δ=(20000·1200)/(69000·200·0.6) ln(1.6) mm ≈ 1.36 mm.
Compute the end displacement at any x using u(x)=(20000·1200)/(69000·200·0.6) ln(1+0.6x/1200) mm.
Compute the maximum stress at the minimum area end x=0 as σmax=σ(0)=P/A0=20000/200=100 MPa.
Compute the minimum stress at the maximum area end x=L as σmin=σ(L)=P/[A0(1+k)]=20000/320=62.5 MPa.
Compute the internal axial force as N(x)=P=20000 N everywhere.
Compute the axial stiffness in an equivalent sense as Keq=P/δ ≈ 20000/1.36 ≈ 14.7 kN/mm.
