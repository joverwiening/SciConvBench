Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic axial response of a uniform prismatic rod.
Model the rod as a 1D axial spring with stiffness k = AE/L.
Use a vertical rod with top end fixed and bottom end fitted with a collar that is struck by a falling weight.
Use dropped-weight impact with weight W = 500 N released from height h = 200 mm above the collar.
Assume the weight engages the collar and remains engaged through maximum extension, with no rebound modeling.
Assume no pre-applied constant axial load prior to impact, i.e., P0 = 0.
Neglect the rod self-weight and any damping, plasticity, stress waves, and local contact compliance.
Use rod geometry L = 1200 mm, d = 30 mm, A = πd^2/4 = 706.86 mm^2.
Use Young’s modulus E = 200,000 MPa (N/mm^2).
Let δmax be the maximum axial extension of the rod during impact.
Relate axial force to extension by P = kδ where k = AE/L.
Compute k = (200,000 N/mm^2)(706.86 mm^2)/(1200 mm) = 117,810 N/mm.
Apply energy conservation at maximum extension: work by weight equals strain energy in rod, W(h + δmax) = (1/2)Pmax δmax.
Substitute Pmax = kδmax into the energy equation to obtain W(h + δmax) = (1/2)kδmax^2.
Solve for δmax from (1/2)kδmax^2 − Wδmax − Wh = 0, taking the positive root: δmax = [W + sqrt(W^2 + 2kWh)]/k.
Use consistent units N and mm so that Wh is in N·mm.
Compute δmax = [500 + sqrt(500^2 + 2(117,810)(500)(200))]/117,810 = 0.920 mm.
Compute maximum axial force during impact: Pmax = kδmax = (117,810 N/mm)(0.920 mm) = 108,400 N (≈ 108 kN).
Compute maximum normal stress: σmax = Pmax/A = 108,400 N / 706.86 mm^2 = 153.4 MPa.
