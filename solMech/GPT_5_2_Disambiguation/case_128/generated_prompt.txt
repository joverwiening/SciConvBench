Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, isotropic material behavior.
Assume a prismatic solid circular shaft of constant diameter d = 50 mm and length L = 600 mm.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion with free (unrestrained) warping and no stress concentrations.
Assume the shaft is subjected to a constant applied torque T = 500,000 N·mm about its longitudinal axis.
Use shear modulus G = 77,519.4 MPa = 77,519.4 N/mm².
Neglect axial force, bending, transverse shear force, and body forces unless explicitly added later.
Take the torsional response quantities of interest as the shear stress distribution τ(r), the maximum shear stress τmax, and the angle of twist θ over length L.

Compute the polar second moment of area for a solid circular shaft as J = (π d^4)/32.
Compute the shear stress distribution under torsion as τ(r) = (T r)/J for 0 ≤ r ≤ d/2.
Compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface as τmax = (T (d/2))/J = (16 T)/(π d^3).
Compute the twist rate as dθ/dx = T/(G J).
Compute the total angle of twist over the length as θ = (T L)/(G J).

For the given values, use J = (π (50 mm)^4)/32 = 613,592.3 mm^4.
For the given values, use τmax = (16·500,000 N·mm)/(π·(50 mm)^3) = 20.37 MPa.
For the given values, use θ = (500,000 N·mm · 600 mm)/(77,519.4 N/mm² · 613,592.3 mm^4) = 0.00631 rad = 0.361°.
