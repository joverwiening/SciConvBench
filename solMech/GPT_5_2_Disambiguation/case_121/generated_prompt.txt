Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior and plane sections remain plane (Euler–Bernoulli).
Treat the member as a short column so buckling and second-order (P–Δ) effects are neglected.
Use a prismatic rectangular cross-section with width b = 120 mm and height h = 200 mm.
Take the centroid at the section center and the member axis along x.
Apply an axial compressive load P = −40,000 N (tension positive convention).
Apply the load with eccentricity e = 25 mm from the centroid along the width direction b, producing bending about the centroidal z-axis.
Neglect shear stress and torsion; consider only normal stress from axial force plus bending moment.
Compute cross-sectional area as A = b h.
Compute the eccentric bending moment magnitude about the z-axis as Mz = P e (retain sign consistent with P and the chosen y-axis direction).
Define y as the coordinate along the width b measured from the centroid, positive toward the eccentricity direction.
Use the centroidal second moment of area about the z-axis as Iz = (h b^3) / 12.
Compute the normal stress distribution as σx(y) = P/A − (Mz y)/Iz.
Evaluate the extreme-edge stresses along the width at y = +b/2 (near edge toward the load) and y = −b/2 (far edge away from the load).
Report σnear = σx(+b/2) and σfar = σx(−b/2) with sign (tension positive, compression negative).
Determine whether any portion of the cross-section is in tension by checking whether max(σnear, σfar) > 0 (equivalently whether the minimum compressive stress becomes tensile at one edge).
Optionally report the “no-tension” condition as |e| ≤ b/6 for a rectangular section under compressive P, and use it to support the yes/no tension conclusion.
