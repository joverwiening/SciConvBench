Assume a straight, prismatic rod of length L and constant cross-sectional area A made of homogeneous, isotropic, linear-elastic AISI steel.
Assume small strains and small displacements.
Assume 1D axial behavior (Saint-Venant), so stress and strain are uniform over each cross-section and vary only along x.
Assume uniform temperature change along the rod: ΔT = +65°C everywhere.
Assume fully rigid, unyielding end supports with axial displacement boundary conditions u(0)=0 and u(L)=0, implying total axial elongation ΔL = 0.
Assume no externally applied axial mechanical load; any axial force is a support reaction induced by restraint.
Assume material properties are constant over the temperature change, using Young’s modulus E and coefficient of thermal expansion α for the chosen AISI grade.

Define total axial strain as ε = du/dx.
Define thermal strain (free expansion strain) as ε_th = α ΔT.
Define mechanical (elastic) strain as ε_mech = ε − ε_th.
Use linear elasticity: σ = E ε_mech = E(ε − αΔT).
Use axial force relation: N = σ A.

Apply full restraint compatibility: ΔL = ∫0^L ε dx = 0.
With uniform fields, ε is constant, so ε = 0.
Therefore ε_mech = −αΔT.
Therefore induced axial stress is σ = E(−αΔT) = −E α ΔT (compressive for ΔT>0).
Therefore induced axial reaction force magnitude is |N| = A E α ΔT, with N = −A E α ΔT (compressive sign convention).
The thermal strain quantity of interest under full restraint is ε_th = αΔT (the strain the rod would undergo if free), while the actual total strain is ε = 0 and the elastic strain is ε_mech = −αΔT.
