Assume a straight prismatic bar of length L with linearly varying cross-sectional area A(x) loaded in uniaxial tension.
Use small deformation, linear elasticity, homogeneous isotropic material with constant Young’s modulus E.
Assume Saint-Venant/uniaxial stress state so that the internal axial force is N(x)=P (constant) and bending/shear are negligible.
Assume quasi-static loading and neglect body forces and temperature effects.
Given A(x)=A0(1+k x/L), with A0=200 mm^2, k=1.0, L=500 mm, x measured from the left end.
Given end tensile force P=12,000 N, and E=200,000 MPa = 200,000 N/mm^2.

Internal force relation: N(x)=P.
Axial stress distribution: σ(x)=N(x)/A(x)=P/[A0(1+k x/L)].
Axial strain distribution: ε(x)=σ(x)/E=P/[E A0(1+k x/L)].
Axial displacement field (taking u(0)=0): u(x)=∫0^x ε(ξ)dξ = (P/(E A0)) ∫0^x 1/(1+k ξ/L) dξ.
Closed form displacement: u(x)=(P L/(E A0 k)) ln(1+k x/L).
Total axial elongation: δ=u(L)=(P L/(E A0 k)) ln(1+k).

Numerical stress at left end: σ(0)=P/A0=12,000/200=60 MPa.
Numerical stress at right end: σ(L)=P/A(L)=12,000/400=30 MPa.
Numerical total elongation: δ=(12,000·500)/(200,000·200·1) ln(2) mm = 0.15 ln(2) mm ≈ 0.10397 mm.
Numerical displacement at position x: u(x)=0.15 ln(1+x/500) mm (since k=1.0).
