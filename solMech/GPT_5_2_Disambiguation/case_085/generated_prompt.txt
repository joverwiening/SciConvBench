Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.
Assume the beam is prismatic with a homogeneous, isotropic material.
Assume Euler–Bernoulli beam theory applies (plane sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis; shear deformation neglected).
Assume the stress state at the critical section is governed by the flexure formula (normal stress from bending).
Assume the axial force at the critical section is N = 0 because it is not specified and the request is to focus on the pure-bending flexure-formula response.
Use a rectangular cross section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.
Take the centroidal axes such that bending is about the centroidal z-axis and the bending stress varies linearly with the distance y from the neutral axis (y measured along the section depth).
Compute the second moment of area about the z-axis as I_z = b h^3 / 12.
Compute I_z = (50)(100^3)/12 mm^4 = 4.1667×10^6 mm^4.
Compute the distance to the extreme fibers as c = h/2 = 50 mm.
Use the flexure formula for normal stress due to bending: σ_x(y) = − M_z y / I_z.
Compute the extreme-fiber bending stress magnitude as σ_max = M_z c / I_z.
With M_z = 3.0×10^6 N·mm, compute σ_max = (3.0×10^6)(50)/(4.1667×10^6) N/mm^2 = 36 N/mm^2 = 36 MPa.
State the stress distribution as linear across the depth: σ_x(y) varies from +36 MPa at one extreme fiber to −36 MPa at the opposite extreme fiber (sign depending on the chosen y-direction and moment sign convention).
Compute the section modulus about the z-axis as S_z = I_z / c = b h^2 / 6.
Compute S_z = (50)(100^2)/6 mm^3 = 8.3333×10^4 mm^3.
Express the extreme-fiber stress equivalently as σ_max = M_z / S_z = 3.0×10^6 / 8.3333×10^4 N/mm^2 = 36 MPa.
If an axial force N is later specified, use combined normal stress: σ_x(y) = N/A − M_z y / I_z.
Compute the cross-sectional area for combined loading use as A = b h = 5000 mm^2.
