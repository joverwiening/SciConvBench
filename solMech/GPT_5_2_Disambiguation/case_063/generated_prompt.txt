Assume a straight, prismatic rod with constant cross-sectional area A and constant material properties.
Assume small deformation, linear elasticity, and 1D uniaxial stress/strain in the axial direction.
Assume uniform temperature change along the rod: ΔT = +35 °C.
Assume both ends are rigidly fixed against axial translation so the total axial elongation is zero: ΔL = 0, equivalently average axial strain εtotal = ΔL/L = 0.
Assume no externally applied axial mechanical force or prescribed axial displacement other than the full restraint.
Use the additive strain decomposition: εtotal = εmech + εth.
Use the free thermal strain relation: εth = α ΔT.
Compute thermal strain: εth = (1.0×10^-5 /°C)(35 °C) = 3.5×10^-4.
Apply the restraint condition εtotal = 0 to get mechanical strain: εmech = -εth = -3.5×10^-4.
Use Hooke’s law for uniaxial stress: σ = E εmech.
Compute induced axial stress: σ = (200,000 MPa)(-3.5×10^-4) = -70 MPa.
Interpret the sign: the induced stress is compressive for a positive temperature increase under full axial restraint.
Relate internal axial force to stress if needed: N = σ A, so N = (-70 MPa)A with A expressed in mm^2 to obtain N in newtons.
