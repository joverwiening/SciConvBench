Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic Euler–Bernoulli beam theory (plane sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis after bending).
Assume the beam is prismatic with a rectangular cross-section of width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.
Assume bending is about the centroidal z-axis so that the normal stress varies linearly with the y coordinate through the height.
Assume the y coordinate is measured from the neutral axis (centroid), positive upward, with y ∈ [−h/2, +h/2] = [−50 mm, +50 mm].
Assume the internal bending moment at the section of interest is constant and equal to M = +3,000,000 N·mm, with positive sign defined as sagging.
Compute the second moment of area about the centroidal z-axis as I_z = b h^3 / 12.
For the given dimensions, use I_z = (50 mm)(100 mm)^3 / 12 = 4.1667×10^6 mm^4.
Determine the bending (normal) stress at an arbitrary fiber location y using σ_x(y) = −(M y) / I_z.
Use the sign convention implied by the formula: for sagging M > 0, fibers with y > 0 (top) are in compression (σ_x < 0) and fibers with y < 0 (bottom) are in tension (σ_x > 0).
Optionally report extreme-fiber stresses using y = ±h/2: σ_top = −M(h/2)/I_z and σ_bottom = +M(h/2)/I_z.
For the given values, σ_top = −(3.0×10^6 N·mm)(50 mm)/(4.1667×10^6 mm^4) = −36 MPa and σ_bottom = +36 MPa.
State that the stress distribution is linear in y and independent of elastic modulus E for pure bending stress under the stated assumptions.
