Assume a straight, vertical, prismatic bar of length L = 3500 mm and constant cross-sectional area A = 200 mm^2.
Assume linear-elastic, small-strain, uniaxial stress with Young’s modulus E = 69000 MPa.
Assume the bar material mass density is ρ = 8900 kg/m^3 and gravitational acceleration is g = 9.81 m/s^2, giving unit weight γ = ρg = 87309 N/m^3.
Assume the bar hangs from a rigid support at the top end.
Define the axial coordinate x measured downward from the top, with x ∈ [0, L].
Impose the essential boundary condition u(0) = 0.
Assume the bottom end is traction-free, so the axial force satisfies N(L) = 0.
Model self-weight as a distributed axial body force per unit length w = γA acting downward.
Use axial equilibrium dN/dx + w = 0.
Solve equilibrium to obtain the internal axial force N(x) = w(L − x) = γA(L − x).
Compute axial stress as σ(x) = N(x)/A = γ(L − x).
Relate strain to displacement by ε(x) = du/dx.
Use Hooke’s law in 1D: σ(x) = E ε(x) = E du/dx.
Combine to obtain the governing differential relation du/dx = γ(L − x)/E.
Integrate with u(0) = 0 to obtain the displacement field u(x) = (γ/E)(Lx − x^2/2).
Compute the total elongation (downward displacement of the bottom end) as δ = u(L) = (γ L^2)/(2E).
Report additional relevant response quantities as needed: maximum axial force Nmax = N(0) = γAL, maximum stress σmax = σ(0) = γL, and stress distribution σ(x) = γ(L − x).
