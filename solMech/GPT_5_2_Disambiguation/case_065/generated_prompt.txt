Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior.
Assume a straight, prismatic, solid circular shaft with diameter d = 18 mm and length L = 400 mm.
Assume pure torsion only with applied torque T = 500,000 N·mm and no transverse shear force.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion applies (plane cross-sections remain plane and rotate as rigid disks; warping effects are neglected for a circular shaft).
Use shear modulus G = 39,179.1 MPa = 39,179.1 N/mm^2.
Use consistent units N and mm throughout so stresses are in MPa and angles are in radians.

Compute the polar second moment of area for a solid circular shaft as J = (π d^4)/32.
Compute the outer radius as c = d/2.

Compute the torsional shear stress distribution as τ(r) = (T r)/J for 0 ≤ r ≤ c.
Compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface as τ_max = (T c)/J = (16 T)/(π d^3).

Compute the twist rate as dθ/dx = T/(G J).
Compute the total angle of twist over length L as θ = (T L)/(G J).

Report τ_max in MPa and θ in radians (optionally also in degrees using θ_deg = θ·180/π).
