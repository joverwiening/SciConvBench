Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior.
Assume a straight, prismatic, solid circular shaft of constant diameter d = 35 mm and length L = 400 mm.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion of a circular shaft (no warping restraint effects), with plane cross-sections remaining plane and rotating as rigid discs.
Assume loading is a constant applied torque only, T = 250,000 N·mm, with no transverse shear forces and no bending.
Use shear modulus G = 77,519.4 MPa = 77,519.4 N/mm².
Use polar second moment of area for a solid circular section J = (π d^4)/32.
Compute J = (π (35 mm)^4)/32 = 147,262.1556 mm^4.
Use torsion shear stress distribution τ(r) = (T r)/J for 0 ≤ r ≤ d/2.
Use maximum shear stress at the outer surface τ_max = (T (d/2))/J = (16 T)/(π d^3).
Compute τ_max = (250,000 N·mm × 17.5 mm) / 147,262.1556 mm^4 = 29.709 N/mm² = 29.709 MPa.
Use angle of twist relation θ = (T L)/(J G) (θ in radians).
Compute θ = (250,000 N·mm × 400 mm) / (147,262.1556 mm^4 × 77,519.4 N/mm²) = 0.008759 rad.
Compute θ in degrees as θ = 0.008759 × (180/π) = 0.5019°.
