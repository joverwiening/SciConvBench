Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic torsion of a prismatic shaft.
Use a solid circular shaft with polar moment of inertia J = (π d^4)/32.
Neglect any shear loading beyond torsion for sizing (no transverse shear force and no shear-stress allowable governs).
Given torque T = 1,000,000 N·mm.
Given shaft length L = 1500 mm.
Given shear modulus G = 25,939.8 MPa = 25,939.8 N/mm^2.
Given allowable twist φ_allow = 0.75° over the full length L.
Convert allowable twist to radians: φ_allow(rad) = 0.75·π/180 = 0.01308997 rad.
Use the torsion twist relation φ = TL/(JG).
Impose the design constraint TL/(JG) ≤ φ_allow.
Solve for required polar moment: J_req = TL/(G φ_allow).
Compute J_req = (1,000,000·1500)/(25,939.8·0.01308997) = 4,418,000 mm^4 (approximately).
Relate J to diameter: J = (π d^4)/32.
Solve for diameter: d_req = [32 J_req/π]^(1/4).
Compute d_req = [32·4,418,000/π]^(1/4) = 81.9 mm (approximately).
Select a design diameter d ≥ 81.9 mm to satisfy the angle-of-twist limit (e.g., choose 82 mm or the next standard size above this value).
