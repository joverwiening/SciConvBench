Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

Assume linear elastic material behavior.

The shaft is a hollow circular cylinder made of structural steel.

The outer diameter is do = 50 mm and the inner diameter is di = 42 mm, giving a wall thickness of t = 4 mm.

The shaft length is L = 1200 mm.

A constant torque T = 450000 N·mm is applied along the shaft axis.

Compute the polar moment of inertia using the exact hollow-circle formula: J = π(do^4 − di^4) / 32.

Substitute do = 50 mm and di = 42 mm into that formula and evaluate J numerically.

Use the shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa.

For part (i), compute the maximum shear stress at the outer surface using the torsion formula τ_max = T · (do/2) / J.

Also compute the shear stress using the thin-walled closed-tube shear-flow relation as a cross-check: define the mean radius as r_m = (do + di) / 4 (i.e., the radius to the wall midline = (50+42)/4 = 23 mm), compute the area enclosed by the median line as A_m = π · r_m^2, compute the shear flow q = T / (2 · A_m), and compute the wall shear stress as τ_thin = q / t.

Report both τ_max values (exact and thin-wall) and note the percentage difference between them.

For part (ii), compute the angle of twist using the exact formula φ_exact = T · L / (G · J), expressed in radians, and then convert to degrees.

Also compute the angle of twist using the thin-walled formula: the perimeter of the median line is s = 2π · r_m, the thin-wall torsional constant is J_thin = 4 · A_m^2 / (s / t) = 4 · A_m^2 · t / s, and φ_thin = T · L / (G · J_thin).

Report both φ values (exact and thin-wall) in radians and in degrees, and note the percentage difference.

Present all intermediate values (J, A_m, q, τ, φ) with at least four significant figures.
