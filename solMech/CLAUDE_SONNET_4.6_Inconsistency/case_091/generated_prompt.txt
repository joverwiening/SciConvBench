Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

Assume Euler–Bernoulli beam theory.

The beam is simply supported with span L = 3500 mm.

Material is structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The second moment of area is constant at I = 3000000 mm^4.

Compute the flexural rigidity EI = 200000 × 3000000 = 600000000000 N·mm^2.

The beam carries two loads simultaneously: a uniform distributed load w = 0.3 N/mm over the full span, and a downward point load P = 1200 N at midspan.

For the distributed load contribution, replace the uniform load with its single resultant force W = w × L = 0.3 × 3500 = 1050 N acting as a point load at midspan.

Treat this replaced resultant as a midspan point load and use the standard midspan point-load deflection formula: delta = PL^3 / (48EI).

Compute the midspan deflection due to the equivalent resultant of the distributed load: delta_1 = (1050 × 3500^3) / (48 × 600000000000).

Show the numerical evaluation of delta_1 step by step, including 3500^3 and the full numerator and denominator before dividing.

Compute the midspan deflection due to the actual point load P = 1200 N at midspan using the same formula: delta_2 = (1200 × 3500^3) / (48 × 600000000000).

Show the numerical evaluation of delta_2 step by step in the same manner.

Apply superposition by adding the two individual deflections: delta_total = delta_1 + delta_2.

State each intermediate result (delta_1, delta_2) explicitly in mm before summing.

State the final combined midspan deflection delta_total in mm.
