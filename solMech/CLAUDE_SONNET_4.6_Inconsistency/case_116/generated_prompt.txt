Use N, mm, and MPa units throughout.

The beam has a rectangular cross section with width b = 60 mm and height h = 80 mm.

The internal shear force at the section is V = 16000 N.

Part (a): Compute the maximum transverse shear stress using τ_max = 1.5 × V/A.

Compute the cross-sectional area as A = b × h = 60 × 80 = 4800 mm².

Compute τ_max = 1.5 × 16000 / 4800 = 5.0 MPa.

The maximum transverse shear stress is τ_max = 5.0 MPa, occurring at the neutral axis.

Part (b): Compute the shear stress at y = 26.667 mm from the neutral axis using τ = VQ / (I × b).

Compute the second moment of area as I = b × h³ / 12 = 60 × 80³ / 12 = 2,560,000 mm⁴.

The area above the level y = 26.667 mm extends from y = 26.667 mm to y = h/2 = 40 mm.

The height of that strip is (40 − 26.667) = 13.333 mm.

Compute Q as the first moment of that area about the neutral axis: Q = b × (40 − y) × (y + (40 − y)/2) = 60 × 13.333 × (26.667 + 6.6665) = 60 × 13.333 × 33.3335 = 26,666.5 mm³.

Alternatively, use the standard formula Q = (b/2) × ((h/2)² − y²) = (60/2) × (1600 − 711.129) = 30 × 888.871 = 26,666.1 mm³, which rounds to Q ≈ 26,667 mm³.

Compute τ = V × Q / (I × b) = 16000 × 26667 / (2,560,000 × 60).

The numerator is 16000 × 26667 = 426,672,000 N·mm³.

The denominator is 2,560,000 × 60 = 153,600,000 mm⁴·mm.

τ = 426,672,000 / 153,600,000 ≈ 2.778 MPa.

The shear stress at y = 26.667 mm from the neutral axis is τ ≈ 2.78 MPa.
