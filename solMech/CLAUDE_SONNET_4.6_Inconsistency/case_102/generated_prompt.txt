Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.
The bar is vertical, prismatic, and linearly elastic.
Bar length is L = 3500 mm.
Cross-sectional area is A = 200 mm^2.
Modulus of elasticity is E = 69000 MPa.
Material density is ρ = 7850 kg/m^3, which must be converted to N/mm^3 as follows: ρ = 7850 kg/m^3 × 9.81 m/s^2 = 76984.5 N/m^3, then convert to N/mm^3 by dividing by 10^9, giving ρg = 7.69845 × 10^-5 N/mm^3.
Gravitational acceleration is g = 9.81 m/s^2, already incorporated in the unit-converted weight density above.
Ignore all external loads; only the bar's self-weight acts.
Compute the total weight of the bar as W = ρg × A × L, using the unit-converted ρg in N/mm^3, A in mm^2, and L in mm, so that W is in Newtons.
Numerically, W = 7.69845 × 10^-5 N/mm^3 × 200 mm^2 × 3500 mm.
Evaluate W = 7.69845 × 10^-5 × 200 × 3500 = 53.889 N.
Replace the distributed self-weight by a single concentrated load equal to W acting at the free (bottom) end of the bar.
Compute the elongation using the concentrated-load formula δ = WL / (AE).
Numerically, δ = 53.889 N × 3500 mm / (200 mm^2 × 69000 MPa).
Evaluate the denominator: 200 × 69000 = 13,800,000 N.
Evaluate the numerator: 53.889 × 3500 = 188,611.5 N·mm.
Compute δ = 188,611.5 / 13,800,000 = 0.01367 mm.
Report the final elongation as δ ≈ 0.01367 mm.
No other deformation sources, boundary conditions, or load cases are considered.
