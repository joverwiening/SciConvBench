Q: The standard flexure formula σ = My/I gives a stress that varies linearly with y, reaching maximum magnitude at the extreme fibers and zero at the neutral axis.

Your instruction says to "treat the pure-bending normal stress as uniform over the section depth, so the stress at the neutral axis has the same sign as at the extreme fibers."

These two statements directly contradict each other: the flexure formula produces a linear (not uniform) distribution, and the neutral axis stress is always zero by definition.

Which do you want to use?

Option A: Apply the standard flexure formula σ = My/I correctly (linear distribution, zero stress at neutral axis, maximum stress at extreme fibers).

Option B: Use a uniform stress distribution over the full cross-section, meaning σ = M / (A · something), which is not the flexure formula.
A: Use Option A: the standard flexure formula σ = My/I with linear distribution. Ignore the contradictory sentence about uniform stress.
