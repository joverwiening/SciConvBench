Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

The beam has a prismatic rectangular cross section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.

The internal bending moment at the critical section is M = 3 000 000 N·mm, acting about the centroidal z-axis and causing bending in the vertical plane.

Compute the second moment of area of the rectangular section about the centroidal z-axis using I = b·h³/12.

I = 50·100³/12 = 4 166 666.67 mm⁴.

The distance from the neutral axis to the extreme fiber is c = h/2 = 50 mm.

Apply the flexure formula σ = M·y/I to find the maximum bending normal stress, which occurs at the extreme fibers where y = ±c.

σ_max = M·c/I = 3 000 000·50 / 4 166 666.67 = 36 MPa.

The stress distribution is linear: zero at the neutral axis and ±36 MPa at the extreme fibers.

For a sagging moment (positive M, concave upward), the convention in the flexure formula places the top fiber at y = +c in compression and the bottom fiber at y = −c in tension, or vice versa depending on sign convention; with the standard beam sign convention where sagging is positive, the top fiber is in compression (σ = −36 MPa) and the bottom fiber is in tension (σ = +36 MPa).

The maximum bending normal stress magnitude is 36 MPa.
