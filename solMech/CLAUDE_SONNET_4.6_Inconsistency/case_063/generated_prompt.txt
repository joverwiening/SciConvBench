Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

The beam cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.

The internal bending moment at the section is M = 3,000,000 N·mm, and it is a sagging moment (positive bending).

The y-axis is measured from the neutral axis (centroid of the section), positive upward.

Compute the second moment of area of the rectangular section about the neutral axis using I = b·h³/12.

I = 50 × 100³ / 12 = 4,166,666.67 mm⁴.

Apply the standard linear bending stress formula σ = M·y / I at each required point.

Compute the bending stress at y = +25 mm (25 mm above the neutral axis).
σ = 3,000,000 × 25 / 4,166,666.67 = 18.0 MPa (tensile, positive).

Compute the bending stress at the top fiber, y = +50 mm.
σ = 3,000,000 × 50 / 4,166,666.67 = 36.0 MPa (tensile, positive).

Compute the bending stress at the bottom fiber, y = −50 mm.
σ = 3,000,000 × (−50) / 4,166,666.67 = −36.0 MPa (compressive, negative).

For a sagging moment, the top fiber is in tension and the bottom fiber is in compression, consistent with the signs above.
