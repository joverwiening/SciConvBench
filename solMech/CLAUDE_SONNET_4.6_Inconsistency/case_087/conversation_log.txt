Q: I need to flag a critical contradiction in your problem before proceeding.

You state that the same axial force P = 25000 N passes through both segments, which is correct for a statically determinate bar in series. However, you then instruct to "take the normal stress to be identical in the two segments" and to "enforce a common elongation before combining stiffnesses."

Identical stress in both segments is only possible if both segments have the same cross-sectional area, which is not the case here (d1 = 12 mm vs d2 = 30 mm). Enforcing a common elongation and combining stiffnesses is the procedure for segments in parallel, not in series.

Which configuration do you actually want analyzed?

Option A: Segments in series (the standard stepped bar). The same force P passes through both, stresses differ because areas differ, and total elongation is the sum of the two individual elongations.

Option B: Segments in parallel (both segments share the same elongation and together carry the total load P). This is where you combine stiffnesses and enforce a common deformation.
