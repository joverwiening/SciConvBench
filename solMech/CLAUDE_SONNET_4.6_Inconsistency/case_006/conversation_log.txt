Q: I need to clarify one critical contradiction before proceeding.

You stated: "For the auxiliary system, use a unit moment at the point of interest to generate m(x), since the desired response is a deflection."

This is physically incorrect. A unit moment in the auxiliary system corresponds — by the unit-load theorem — to finding a **rotation**, not a deflection. To find a **deflection** at a point, the auxiliary system must apply a **unit point load** (unit force) at that point in the direction of the desired deflection.

Which auxiliary loading do you want to use?

Option A: Unit downward point load at x = 1200 mm (correct for computing deflection).
Option B: Unit moment at x = 1200 mm (correct for computing rotation, not deflection).
A: The complete requirement says to use a unit load applied at the point and direction of the desired deflection — so use Option A: a unit downward point load at x = 1200 mm.
