Q: The problem states "assume plane strain in the two transverse directions so that the lateral strain is zero," but then asks to compute a lateral (diametral) strain and a change in diameter Δd.

If the lateral strain is truly zero (plane strain constraint), then Δd = 0 by definition, making parts (iii) and (iv) trivial and the Poisson's ratio irrelevant.

Should I treat this as a standard uniaxial stress problem (free lateral contraction via Poisson's ratio), ignoring the plane strain statement, or do you want the plane strain condition enforced (in which case lateral strain and Δd are both zero)?
A: Ignore the plane strain statement — it is not in the complete requirement. Treat this as a standard uniaxial stress problem where lateral strain is computed using Poisson's ratio: ε_lateral = −ν · ε_axial.
