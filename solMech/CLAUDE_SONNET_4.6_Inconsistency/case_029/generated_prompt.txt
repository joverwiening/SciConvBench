Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

The rod is a solid circular cross-section with initial diameter d0 = 20 mm and length L = 1000 mm.
The applied axial tensile load is P = 25000 N, acting through the centroid.
Material properties are E = 105000 MPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.34.
Assume linear elastic, isotropic behavior under uniaxial stress (no lateral constraint).

Step 1: Compute the cross-sectional area.
A = π · (d0/2)² = π · (10)² = 100π mm².

Step 2: Compute the average axial normal stress.
σ_axial = P / A = 25000 / (100π) MPa.
σ_axial = 250/π ≈ 79.577 MPa (tensile).

Step 3: Compute the axial strain.
ε_axial = σ_axial / E = (250/π) / 105000.
ε_axial ≈ 79.577 / 105000 ≈ 7.579 × 10⁻⁴ (dimensionless, elongation).

Step 4: Compute the lateral (diametral) strain.
ε_lateral = −ν · ε_axial = −0.34 · 7.579 × 10⁻⁴.
ε_lateral ≈ −2.577 × 10⁻⁴ (dimensionless, contraction).

Step 5: Compute the change in diameter.
Δd = ε_lateral · d0 = −2.577 × 10⁻⁴ · 20 mm.
Δd ≈ −5.154 × 10⁻³ mm (negative sign indicates diameter decreases).

Summary of results:
(i) Average axial normal stress σ_axial ≈ 79.577 MPa (tensile).
(ii) Axial strain ε_axial ≈ 7.579 × 10⁻⁴ (elongation).
(iii) Lateral strain ε_lateral ≈ −2.577 × 10⁻⁴ (contraction).
(iv) Change in diameter Δd ≈ −0.005154 mm (diameter decreases by approximately 5.154 × 10⁻³ mm).
