Solve a uniaxial tension problem for a prismatic circular steel rod using consistent N–mm–MPa units and small-deformation, linear-elastic assumptions.

Use Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.
Use yield strength Sy = 250 MPa.
The rod is straight, prismatic, and has a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 12 mm.
The gage length is L = 800 mm.
An axial tensile force P = 30000 N is applied through the centroid of the cross-section.

Step 1: Compute the cross-sectional area.
Use A = π × d² / 4 with d = 12 mm.
A = π × (12)² / 4 = 113.097 mm².

Step 2: Compute the average normal stress.
Use σ_avg = P / A.
σ_avg = 30000 N / 113.097 mm² = 265.26 MPa.

Step 3: Compute the elastic normal strain.
Use ε = σ_avg / E.
ε = 265.26 MPa / 200000 MPa = 1.3263 × 10⁻³ (dimensionless).

Step 4: Compute the axial elongation of the gage length.
Use δ = ε × L.
δ = 1.3263 × 10⁻³ × 800 mm = 1.061 mm.

Step 5: Compute the factor of safety against yielding.
Use n = Sy / σ_avg, where Sy is the yield strength and σ_avg is the computed working stress.
n = 250 MPa / 265.26 MPa = 0.943.

State the interpretation of the factor of safety result.
Because n = 0.943 is less than 1.0, the applied stress of 265.26 MPa exceeds the yield strength of 250 MPa.
The rod has yielded under the given load; the design is not safe at this load level with this diameter.
The linear-elastic solution is reported as requested, but the stress state is beyond the elastic limit, so the strain and elongation values are not physically valid for the actual deformed rod.
