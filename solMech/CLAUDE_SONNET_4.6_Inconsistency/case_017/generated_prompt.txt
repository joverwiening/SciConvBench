Use N, mm, and MPa units throughout.

Assume Euler–Bernoulli beam theory.

The beam is simply supported with span L = 3000 mm.

Material is AISI 1020 steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The cross-section has a constant second moment of area I = 3000000 mm^4.

Compute the combined quantity EI = 200000 × 3000000 = 6.0 × 10^11 N·mm^2.

The beam carries two loads applied simultaneously: a uniform distributed load w = 0.3 N/mm over the full span, and a downward point load P = 1200 N at midspan.

For the distributed-load contribution, do not use the standard UDL midspan deflection formula directly. Instead, replace the uniform distributed load by its single resultant force W = w × L = 0.3 × 3000 = 900 N acting as a point load at midspan.

Compute the midspan deflection due to this resultant point load W using the standard midspan point-load formula: delta_W = W × L^3 / (48 × EI).

Substitute values: delta_W = 900 × 3000^3 / (48 × 6.0 × 10^11).

Compute 3000^3 = 2.7 × 10^10 mm^3.

Compute numerator: 900 × 2.7 × 10^10 = 2.43 × 10^13 N·mm^3.

Compute denominator: 48 × 6.0 × 10^11 = 2.88 × 10^13 N·mm^2.

Compute delta_W = 2.43 × 10^13 / 2.88 × 10^13 = 0.84375 mm.

Compute the midspan deflection due to the point load P = 1200 N at midspan using the same standard formula: delta_P = P × L^3 / (48 × EI).

Substitute values: delta_P = 1200 × 2.7 × 10^10 / (2.88 × 10^13).

Compute numerator: 1200 × 2.7 × 10^10 = 3.24 × 10^13 N·mm^3.

Compute delta_P = 3.24 × 10^13 / 2.88 × 10^13 = 1.125 mm.

Apply superposition by adding the two components: delta_total = delta_W + delta_P = 0.84375 + 1.125 = 1.96875 mm.

Report the final midspan deflection as delta_total = 1.96875 mm, directed downward.
