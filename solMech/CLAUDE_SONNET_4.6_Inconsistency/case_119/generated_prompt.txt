The task is to compute the axial elongation of a tapered bar using two methods and deliver a full step-by-step derivation plus the final numerical result for each.

Units are N, mm, and MPa throughout.

Given data:
L = 500 mm
P = 12000 N
E = 200000 MPa
A(x) = A0 · (1 + k · x/L), with A0 = 200 mm^2 and k = 1.0

Method 1: Exact evaluation of the integral δ = ∫[0 to L] P / (A(x) · E) dx

Step 1: Substitute A(x) into the integrand.
A(x) = 200 · (1 + 1.0 · x/500) = 200 · (1 + x/500)

The integrand becomes P / (A(x) · E) = 12000 / (200 · (1 + x/500) · 200000).

Simplify the constant: 12000 / (200 · 200000) = 12000 / 40000000 = 3.0 × 10^-4 mm^-1.

So the integrand = 3.0 × 10^-4 / (1 + x/500).

Step 2: Evaluate the integral.
δ = ∫[0 to 500] 3.0 × 10^-4 / (1 + x/500) dx

Let u = 1 + x/500, then du = dx/500, so dx = 500 du.
When x = 0, u = 1. When x = 500, u = 2.

δ = 3.0 × 10^-4 · 500 · ∫[1 to 2] (1/u) du
δ = 0.15 · [ln(u)] from 1 to 2
δ = 0.15 · (ln 2 − ln 1)
δ = 0.15 · ln(2)
δ = 0.15 · 0.693147
δ = 0.103972 mm

The exact elongation is δ_exact ≈ 0.1040 mm.

Method 2: Arithmetic-mean area approximation

Step 1: Compute the area at each end.
A(0) = 200 · (1 + 0) = 200 mm^2
A(L) = 200 · (1 + 1.0) = 400 mm^2

Step 2: Compute the arithmetic mean area.
A_mean = (A(0) + A(L)) / 2 = (200 + 400) / 2 = 300 mm^2

Step 3: Apply the standard axial deformation formula treating A_mean as constant.
δ = P · L / (A_mean · E)
δ = 12000 · 500 / (300 · 200000)
δ = 6000000 / 60000000
δ = 0.100000 mm

The arithmetic-mean-area elongation is δ_approx = 0.1000 mm.

Comparison:
The exact integral gives δ_exact ≈ 0.1040 mm.
The arithmetic-mean-area approximation gives δ_approx = 0.1000 mm.
The difference is 0.0040 mm, which is approximately 3.8% below the exact value.
The arithmetic-mean area overestimates the effective stiffness of the bar because the harmonic mean of the area, not the arithmetic mean, is the correct equivalent for this integral, causing the approximation to underestimate elongation slightly.
