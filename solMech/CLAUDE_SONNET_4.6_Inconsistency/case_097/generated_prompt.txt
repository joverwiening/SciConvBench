Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

The beam is a cantilever of length L = 1500 mm, fixed at x = 0 and free at x = L.

The material is AISI 1020 steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The second moment of area is constant at I = 2500000 mm^4.

A downward point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end (x = L).

Step 1: Write the bending moment M(x).
For a cantilever with a tip load P, the bending moment at position x measured from the fixed end is M(x) = P * (L - x).
However, per the problem instruction, replace M(x) with the constant peak value M = P * L over the full span before differentiating.
Therefore use M(x) = P * L for all x in [0, L].

Step 2: Write the strain energy U.
U = integral from 0 to L of M(x)^2 / (2 * E * I) dx.
Substitute M(x) = P * L.
U = integral from 0 to L of (P * L)^2 / (2 * E * I) dx.
Since (P * L)^2 / (2 * E * I) is constant with respect to x, the integral becomes:
U = (P * L)^2 / (2 * E * I) * L.
U = P^2 * L^3 / (2 * E * I).

Step 3: Differentiate U with respect to P to get the deflection delta at the free end (Castigliano's theorem).
delta = dU/dP = d/dP [ P^2 * L^3 / (2 * E * I) ].
delta = 2 * P * L^3 / (2 * E * I).
delta = P * L^3 / (E * I).

Step 4: Substitute numerical values.
P = 500 N.
L = 1500 mm, so L^3 = 1500^3 = 3375000000 mm^3.
E = 200000 MPa = 200000 N/mm^2.
I = 2500000 mm^4.
E * I = 200000 * 2500000 = 500000000000 N*mm^2.

delta = (500 * 3375000000) / 500000000000.
delta = 1687500000000 / 500000000000.
delta = 3.375 mm.

The vertical deflection at the free end is delta = 3.375 mm (downward).

Note: This result is three times larger than the classical Castigliano result of P*L^3/(3*E*I) = 1.125 mm, because the problem instructs replacing the true moment distribution M(x) = P*(L-x) with the constant peak value M = P*L before integrating, which inflates the strain energy by a factor of 3.
