Final Specification:

The problem is a uniaxial tension problem for a prismatic circular rod.
Use consistent N, mm, and MPa units throughout all calculations.
Apply small-deformation and linear-elastic assumptions.
Use Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa for 6061-T6 aluminum.
Use yield strength Sy = 275 MPa for 6061-T6 aluminum.
The rod is straight and prismatic with a solid circular cross-section.
The rod diameter is 20 mm.
The gage length is 800 mm.
An axial tensile force of 15000 N is applied through the centroid of the cross-section.
There is no bending, shear, or torsion; the loading is purely axial.

Step 1: Compute the cross-sectional area.
Use A = pi times (d/2) squared, where d = 20 mm.
A = pi times (10 mm) squared = 100 pi mm squared, approximately 314.159 mm squared.

Step 2: Compute the average normal stress.
Use sigma_avg = F divided by A, where F = 15000 N.
sigma_avg = 15000 N divided by 314.159 mm squared, approximately 47.746 MPa.
The stress is tensile and uniform across the cross-section.

Step 3: Compute the elastic normal strain.
Use epsilon = sigma_avg divided by E.
epsilon = 47.746 MPa divided by 69000 MPa, approximately 6.919 times 10 to the power of negative 4 (dimensionless).

Step 4: Compute the axial elongation of the gage length.
Use delta = epsilon times L, where L = 800 mm.
delta = 6.919 times 10 to the power of negative 4 times 800 mm, approximately 0.5535 mm.
Equivalently, delta = F times L divided by (A times E) = 15000 times 800 divided by (314.159 times 69000), approximately 0.5535 mm.

Step 5: Compute the factor of safety against yielding.
Use the definition n = Sy divided by sigma_avg.
n = 275 MPa divided by 47.746 MPa, approximately 5.76.
This means the applied stress is about 5.76 times smaller than the yield strength, so the rod remains well within the elastic range.

Disregard the alternative definition n = sigma_avg divided by Sy mentioned in the problem statement, as that ratio is less than one and does not represent a factor of safety.
The correct and physically meaningful factor of safety is n = Sy divided by sigma_avg, which gives n approximately equal to 5.76.
