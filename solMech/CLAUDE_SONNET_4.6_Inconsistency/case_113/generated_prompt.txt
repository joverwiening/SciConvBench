Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

Assume linear elastic material behavior.

The shaft is a hollow circular cylinder made of structural steel.

The outer diameter is do = 50 mm and the inner diameter is di = 44 mm, giving a wall thickness of t = 3 mm.

The shaft length is L = 800 mm.

The applied torque is T = 600000 N·mm, constant along the entire length.

Compute the polar moment of inertia using the exact hollow-circle formula: J = π(do^4 − di^4) / 32.

Substitute do = 50 mm and di = 44 mm into that formula and evaluate J numerically.

Compute the maximum shear stress using the standard torsion formula τ_max = T · (do/2) / J, where do/2 is the outer radius c = 25 mm.

This gives the shear stress at the outer surface, which is the maximum value.

Also compute the shear stress using the thin-walled closed-tube (Bredt) shear-flow approach as a parallel check.

For the thin-walled approach, define the mean radius as r_m = (do + di) / 4 = (50 + 44) / 4 = 23.5 mm.

Define the area enclosed by the median line as A_m = π · r_m^2.

Compute the shear flow as q = T / (2 · A_m).

Compute the thin-walled shear stress as τ_thin = q / t, where t = 3 mm.

Report both τ_max (from the exact formula) and τ_thin (from the Bredt formula) and note the percentage difference between them.

Compute the angle of twist using φ = T · L / (J · G).

Use G = 77519.4 MPa.

Substitute T = 600000 N·mm, L = 800 mm, J from the exact formula, and G = 77519.4 MPa.

Report φ in radians and also convert it to degrees.

Present all intermediate values: J in mm^4, c in mm, A_m in mm^2, q in N/mm, τ_max in MPa, τ_thin in MPa, and φ in both radians and degrees.
