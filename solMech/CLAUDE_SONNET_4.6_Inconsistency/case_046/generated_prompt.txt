Solve a mechanics of materials problem using consistent N–mm–MPa units throughout.
Assume small deformation and linear-elastic material behavior.
Use Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa for structural steel.
The rod is straight, prismatic, and has a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 25 mm.
The gage length is L = 800 mm.
An axial tensile force P = 20000 N is applied through the centroid of the cross-section.
The yield strength of the material is S_y = 250 MPa.

Step 1: Compute the cross-sectional area.
A = π × d² / 4 = π × (25)² / 4 mm².

Step 2: Compute the average normal stress.
σ_avg = P / A in MPa.

Step 3: Compute the elastic normal strain.
ε = σ_avg / E (dimensionless).

Step 4: Compute the axial elongation of the gage length.
δ = ε × L in mm.

Step 5: Compute the factor of safety against yielding.
n = S_y / σ_avg.

Report the cross-sectional area in mm², the average normal stress in MPa, the elastic normal strain as a dimensionless value, the axial elongation in mm, and the factor of safety as a dimensionless ratio.
