Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

Assume linear elastic material behavior.

The shaft is a hollow circular cylinder made of brass.

The outer diameter is do = 40 mm and the inner diameter is di = 30 mm, giving a wall thickness of t = 5 mm.

The shaft length is L = 1000 mm.

A constant torque T = 300000 N·mm is applied along the shaft axis.

Compute the polar moment of inertia using the exact formula J = π(do^4 − di^4)/32.

Use the shear modulus G = 39179.1 MPa.

For the maximum shear stress, apply the thin-walled closed-section shear-flow formula: shear flow q = T / (2 · A_m), where A_m is the area enclosed by the median (mean) wall centerline.

The mean diameter is d_m = (do + di) / 2 = 35 mm, so the mean radius is r_m = 17.5 mm.

Compute A_m = π · r_m^2 = π · (17.5)^2 mm^2.

Compute shear flow q = T / (2 · A_m).

Compute the shear stress at the outer surface as τ = q / t, where t = 5 mm.

For the angle of twist, use the standard torsion formula φ = T · L / (J · G).

Compute J = π · (40^4 − 30^4) / 32 in mm^4.

Compute φ = T · L / (J · G) in radians.

Also express φ in degrees by multiplying by (180 / π).

Report the following results clearly:
- The mean enclosed area A_m in mm^2.
- The shear flow q in N/mm.
- The maximum shear stress τ at the outer surface in MPa.
- The polar moment of inertia J in mm^4.
- The angle of twist φ in both radians and degrees.
