Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

Apply Euler–Bernoulli beam theory.

The beam is simply supported with span L = 4500 mm.

The boundary conditions are: deflection v(0) = 0, deflection v(L) = 0, bending moment M(0) = 0, bending moment M(L) = 0.

The end slopes are free (not constrained); the earlier statement about zero end slopes is disregarded.

A uniformly distributed load w = 0.3 N/mm acts downward over the full span.

The beam material is structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The second moment of area is I = 2000000 mm^4.

The flexural rigidity is EI = 200000 × 2000000 = 4.0 × 10^11 N·mm².

Use the standard Euler–Bernoulli closed-form solution for a simply supported beam under full-span UDL.

The deflection at position x along the beam is v(x) = (w × x / (24 × E × I)) × (L^3 − 2 × L × x^2 + x^3).

Determine the location of maximum deflection by symmetry; for a symmetric UDL on a simply supported beam the maximum deflection occurs at midspan x = L/2 = 2250 mm.

Compute the maximum deflection by substituting x = L/2 into the deflection equation, which gives v_max = (5 × w × L^4) / (384 × E × I).

Substitute values: v_max = (5 × 0.3 × 4500^4) / (384 × 4.0 × 10^11).

Compute the slope at x = 0 using the derivative of the deflection equation: v'(x) = (w / (24 × E × I)) × (L^3 − 6 × L × x^2 + 4 × x^3).

Substitute x = 0 to get the slope at the left support: v'(0) = (w × L^3) / (24 × E × I).

Substitute values: v'(0) = (0.3 × 4500^3) / (24 × 4.0 × 10^11).

Report the maximum deflection in mm (positive value taken as downward), its location in mm from the left support, and the slope at x = 0 in radians (the sign convention is that a positive slope indicates the beam deflects downward moving left to right at that point).
