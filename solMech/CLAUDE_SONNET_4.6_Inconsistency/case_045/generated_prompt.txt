Final Specification:

The problem is a uniaxial tension problem for a straight prismatic circular rod.
Use consistent N, mm, and MPa units throughout all calculations.
Apply small-deformation and linear-elastic assumptions.
Use Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa for 6061-T6 aluminum.
Use yield strength Sy = 275 MPa for 6061-T6 aluminum.
The rod has a circular cross-section with diameter d = 12 mm.
The gage length is L = 800 mm.
The applied axial tensile force is P = 15000 N, applied through the centroid of the cross-section.

Step 1: Compute the cross-sectional area.
Calculate the cross-sectional area as A = pi times (d/2) squared = pi times (6 mm) squared.
A = 113.097 mm squared.

Step 2: Compute the average normal stress.
Calculate stress as sigma_avg = P divided by A = 15000 N divided by 113.097 mm squared.
sigma_avg = 132.63 MPa.
This is a tensile stress, so it is positive.

Step 3: Compute the elastic normal strain.
Calculate strain as epsilon = sigma_avg divided by E = 132.63 MPa divided by 69000 MPa.
epsilon = 0.001922 mm/mm (dimensionless).
This is approximately 1.922 times 10 to the power of negative 3.

Step 4: Compute the axial elongation of the gage length.
Calculate elongation as delta = epsilon times L = 0.001922 times 800 mm.
delta = 1.537 mm.

Step 5: Compute the factor of safety against yielding.
Use the definition n = Sy divided by sigma_avg = 275 MPa divided by 132.63 MPa.
n = 2.07.
This means the applied stress is approximately 2.07 times below the yield strength, so the rod remains safely in the elastic range.

Note on the contradiction in the problem statement: The problem first states n = sigma_avg divided by Sy (working-to-yield ratio), then restates it as n = Sy divided by sigma_avg. The correct and standard engineering definition of factor of safety against yielding is n = Sy divided by sigma_avg, which gives a value greater than 1 when the part is safe. This definition is used here. The inverse ratio sigma_avg divided by Sy = 0.482 represents the utilization ratio, not the factor of safety.
