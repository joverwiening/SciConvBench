Q: I need to flag a direct contradiction in your specification before proceeding.

You state that the rod is placed between two rigid, unyielding supports so that total change in length is constrained to zero, AND you also state that the actual axial strain equals the free thermal strain and the thermal stress vanishes.

These two statements are physically contradictory. A rod fully restrained against thermal expansion by rigid supports cannot have zero thermal stress. The zero-deformation constraint is precisely what generates the compressive stress.

Which condition should govern the solution: the rigid supports that enforce zero total deformation, or the assumption that stress vanishes?
A: The rigid supports enforcing zero total deformation govern the solution. Ignore the contradictory sentence about stress vanishing — that was an error in the problem statement. The rod is fully restrained, so solve it the standard way: thermal stress develops and is compressive.
