Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

Model a stepped circular bar made of 6061-T6 aluminum, fixed at its left end and loaded in tension at its right end by an axial force P = 20000 N.

Segment 1 is the left segment with length L1 = 500 mm and diameter d1 = 30 mm.
Segment 2 is the right segment with length L2 = 600 mm and diameter d2 = 25 mm.
Use elastic modulus E = 69000 MPa for both segments.
Assume linear-elastic behavior throughout.

Compute the cross-sectional area of each segment using A = π·d²/4.
Compute A1 = π·(30)²/4.
Compute A2 = π·(25)²/4.

For part (i), compute the normal stress in each segment using σ = P/A.
Compute σ1 = P/A1 = 20000/A1.
Compute σ2 = P/A2 = 20000/A2.
Report σ1 and σ2 separately in MPa, noting that they differ because the cross-sectional areas differ.

For part (ii), compute the elongation of each segment using δ = P·L/(A·E).
Compute δ1 = P·L1/(A1·E) = 20000·500/(A1·69000).
Compute δ2 = P·L2/(A2·E) = 20000·600/(A2·69000).
Compute the total elongation as δ_total = δ1 + δ2.
Report δ_total in mm.

Do not apply any parallel-system or common-elongation constraint; the two segments are in series and each elongates independently under the same axial force P.
