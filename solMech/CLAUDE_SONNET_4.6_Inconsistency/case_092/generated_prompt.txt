Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.
Apply Euler–Bernoulli beam theory assuming small deflections and linear elastic material behaviour.
The beam is a cantilever of length L = 1800 mm, fixed at x = 0 and free at x = L.
The material is structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The constant second moment of area is I = 1200000 mm^4.
A downward point load P = 2000 N is applied at the free end (x = L).
No external couple is applied anywhere on the beam.
Use the linearly varying bending moment M(x) = P(L − x) over the full span.
Apply the fixed-end boundary conditions: slope = 0 and deflection = 0 at x = 0.
Compute the free-end slope using the formula theta = PL^2 / (2EI).
Substitute P = 2000, L = 1800, E = 200000, I = 1200000 into the slope formula and evaluate numerically.
Report the free-end slope in radians and also convert it to degrees.
Compute the free-end deflection using the formula delta = PL^3 / (3EI).
Substitute the same values into the deflection formula and evaluate numerically.
Report the free-end deflection in mm, with the sign convention that a downward deflection is positive.
Present the two results clearly labelled as (i) free-end slope and (ii) free-end deflection.
