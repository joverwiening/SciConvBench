Q: I need to clarify a critical contradiction before proceeding.

For a statically determinate shaft with one fixed end (A) and a single applied torque at the free end (C), the internal torque in both segments is simply equal to the applied torque T — that is the direct result of equilibrium, with no stiffness distribution needed.

The instruction to "distribute the applied torque in proportion to torsional stiffnesses" applies to a statically INdeterminate shaft — for example, one that is fixed at BOTH ends A and C, with the torque applied at the intermediate point B.

Which configuration is intended: fixed at A only with torque at C (statically determinate), or fixed at both A and C with torque applied at the intermediate point B (statically indeterminate)?
A: The configuration is fixed at A only with torque applied at C (statically determinate). The same internal torque T acts through both segments. Ignore the instruction about distributing torque in proportion to stiffnesses — that was an error in the problem statement.
