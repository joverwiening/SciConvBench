Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

The bar is prismatic with length L = 800 mm and cross-sectional area A = 300 mm².
The material is linear elastic with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.
A tensile load P = 8000 N is applied suddenly (as a step load, not gradually ramped).

Model the bar as a linear axial spring with stiffness k = EA/L.
Compute k = (200000 × 300) / 800 = 75000 N/mm.

Apply the energy method for a suddenly applied constant load.
Set the work done by the load equal to the strain energy stored in the bar at maximum deflection.
Work done by the suddenly applied load = P × δ_max, where δ_max is the maximum axial deformation.
Strain energy stored in the bar = (1/2) × k × δ_max².
Set P × δ_max = (1/2) × k × δ_max².
Solve for δ_max = 2P/k.
Compute δ_max = 2 × 8000 / 75000 = 0.21333 mm.

The maximum dynamic stress is σ_max = E × (δ_max / L).
Compute σ_max = 200000 × (0.21333 / 800) = 53.33 MPa.

Verify this equals 2 × (P/A) = 2 × (8000 / 300) = 53.33 MPa.
Confirm that the dynamic amplification factor for a suddenly applied constant load is exactly 2.
Report the maximum stress in the bar as 53.33 MPa.
