Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

The shaft is solid and circular with diameter d = 25 mm.

The applied axial tensile force is P = 15000 N.

The applied torque is T = 400000 N·mm.

Part (a): Compute the cross-sectional area A = π·d²/4. Compute the average axial stress σ = P/A in MPa.

Part (b): Compute the polar moment of inertia J = π·d⁴/32. Take the outer surface radius c = d/2 = 12.5 mm. Compute the torsional shear stress τ = T·c/J in MPa.

Part (c): Set up a plane stress state at the outer surface with σx = σ (axial stress from part a), σy = 0, and τxy = τ (torsional shear stress from part b).

Compute the two principal stresses using σ1,2 = (σx + σy)/2 ± √[((σx − σy)/2)² + τxy²].

Compute the maximum in-plane shear stress using τmax = √[((σx − σy)/2)² + τxy²].

Report all intermediate values (A, J, c, σ, τ) and all final results (σ1, σ2, τmax) in MPa, rounded to two decimal places.
