Q: I need to clarify a contradiction before proceeding.

You have described a statically determinate bar fixed only at A (left end), with forces applied at B, C, and D. For that system, the internal forces in each segment are found by standard section cuts, and the displacement at D is found by summing the axial deformations segment by segment. This is the correct and physically standard approach.

However, you then instruct: "treat the internal axial force as uniform along the full length and compute one stress level from the net applied load." That instruction contradicts the segmented analysis, because the internal force is NOT uniform — it changes at each loaded point (B and C), giving three different internal forces in the three segments.

Which approach do you want used?

Option 1: Correct segmented analysis — find the internal force in each of the three segments separately by equilibrium (section cuts), compute three stress values, and sum the three deformations to get displacement at D.

Option 2: Single net-force approach — sum all applied forces into one net resultant, compute one uniform stress, and use the total length for one deformation value (this is physically incorrect for this loading but matches your last instruction).
A: Use Option 1: the correct segmented analysis. Find the internal force in each of the three segments separately, compute three stress values, and sum the three deformations to get the displacement at D. Ignore the contradictory instruction about treating the force as uniform.
