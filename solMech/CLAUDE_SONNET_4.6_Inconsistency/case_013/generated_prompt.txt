Use N, mm, and MPa (N/mm²) throughout all calculations.

Assume Euler–Bernoulli beam theory applies.

The beam is simply supported with span L = 3000 mm.

Material is structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.

The second moment of area is constant at I = 2000000 mm⁴.

Compute the combined quantity EI = 200000 × 2000000 = 4.0 × 10¹¹ N·mm².

The beam carries two loads simultaneously: a uniform distributed load w = 0.3 N/mm over the full span, and a downward point load P = 3000 N at midspan.

Use superposition: treat each load contribution separately, then sum the midspan deflections.

For the distributed load contribution, do NOT use the standard UDL formula (5wL⁴/384EI). Instead, replace the uniform load by its single resultant force W = wL acting at midspan.

Compute the resultant W = 0.3 × 3000 = 900 N acting as a point load at midspan.

For a simply supported beam with a single point load Q at midspan, the midspan deflection formula is δ = QL³ / (48EI).

Apply this formula for the distributed load contribution using Q = W = 900 N.

Compute δ₁ = WL³ / (48EI) = 900 × 3000³ / (48 × 4.0 × 10¹¹).

Evaluate the numerator: 900 × 27000000000 = 2.43 × 10¹³ N·mm³.

Evaluate the denominator: 48 × 4.0 × 10¹¹ = 1.92 × 10¹³ N·mm².

Compute δ₁ = 2.43 × 10¹³ / 1.92 × 10¹³ = 1.265625 mm.

Apply the same midspan point load formula for the actual point load P = 3000 N.

Compute δ₂ = PL³ / (48EI) = 3000 × 3000³ / (48 × 4.0 × 10¹¹).

Evaluate the numerator: 3000 × 27000000000 = 8.1 × 10¹³ N·mm³.

Compute δ₂ = 8.1 × 10¹³ / 1.92 × 10¹³ = 4.21875 mm.

Apply superposition: total midspan deflection δ_total = δ₁ + δ₂.

Compute δ_total = 1.265625 + 4.21875 = 5.484375 mm.

Report δ₁, δ₂, and δ_total separately in the final answer, each clearly labeled, with δ_total rounded to three decimal places as 5.484 mm downward.
