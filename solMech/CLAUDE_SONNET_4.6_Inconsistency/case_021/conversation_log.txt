Q: I need to clarify a critical contradiction before proceeding.

When end A is fixed and torque T is applied at end C with no intermediate loads, the shaft is statically determinate. The internal torque in both segments is simply T = 300,000 N·mm — there is nothing to distribute. Torsional stiffness is irrelevant for finding the internal torque in a statically determinate series shaft.

The instruction to "distribute torque in proportion to torsional stiffnesses" applies to a statically INdeterminate shaft — for example, one that is fixed at BOTH ends A and C, with the torque applied at the intermediate point B.

These two problem descriptions are mutually contradictory. Which configuration do you intend?

Option 1: Fixed at A only, torque at C — statically determinate, internal torque = T in both segments, straightforward stress and twist calculation.

Option 2: Fixed at both A and C, torque T applied at the intermediate point B — statically indeterminate, torque is distributed by stiffness ratio.

Which configuration should be used?
A: Use Option 1: fixed at A only, torque applied at end C. The same internal torque T = 300,000 N·mm acts through both segments. Ignore the instruction about distributing torque by stiffness — that was an error in the problem statement.
