Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

The rod is made of AISI 1020 steel and is prismatic with length L = 1200 mm and diameter d = 35 mm.

The rod is placed between two rigid, unyielding supports, meaning the total axial deformation is constrained to be exactly zero.

The rod experiences a uniform temperature increase of ΔT = 65 °C.

Use E = 200000 MPa and α = 1e-05 per °C.

Assume linear elastic behavior throughout.

Because the supports are rigid and allow zero deformation, the mechanical (elastic) strain must exactly cancel the free thermal strain.

The free thermal strain is ε_thermal = α × ΔT = (1e-05) × 65 = 6.5e-04 (dimensionless).

The mechanical strain is equal in magnitude and opposite in sign to the free thermal strain: ε_mechanical = −6.5e-04.

Calculate the thermal stress as σ = E × ε_mechanical = 200000 × (−6.5e-04) = −130 MPa.

The negative sign indicates the stress is compressive, because the supports prevent the rod from expanding freely.

Report the thermal stress as 130 MPa, compressive.

Calculate the cross-sectional area as A = π × (d/2)² = π × (17.5)² = π × 306.25 ≈ 962.11 mm².

Calculate the axial force as F = σ × A = 130 × 962.11 ≈ 125074 N, compressive.

Report the axial force as approximately 125074 N (≈ 125.07 kN), compressive.

Indicate clearly in the final answer that the stress is compressive and the rod is being compressed by the supports resisting thermal expansion.
