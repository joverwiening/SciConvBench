Use N, mm, and MPa as the unit system throughout.

The beam cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.

The internal bending moment at the section is M = 4,000,000 N·mm, and it is a sagging moment (positive bending).

Define y as measured from the neutral axis (centroid of the section), positive upward.

Compute the second moment of area of the rectangular section about the neutral axis using I = b·h³/12.

Apply the standard linear bending stress formula σ = M·y / I at each point of interest.

Compute the bending stress σ at y = 0 mm (neutral axis); the result must be 0 MPa.

Compute the bending stress σ at the top fiber, where y = +60 mm (half of h = 120 mm above the neutral axis).

Compute the bending stress σ at the bottom fiber, where y = −60 mm (half of h = 120 mm below the neutral axis).

For a sagging moment, the top fiber stress must be compressive (negative) and the bottom fiber stress must be tensile (positive).

Report all three stress values in MPa, showing the sign (positive for tension, negative for compression).
