The bar is assumed to be fixed at the left end (x = 0) and free at the right end (x = L) where the tensile load P is applied.
The cross-sectional area varies linearly according to A(x) = A0·(1 + k·x/L) where A0 = 200 mm², k = 0.6, L = 1200 mm.
The material is 6061-T6 aluminum with elastic modulus E = 69000 MPa.
A constant tensile load P = 20000 N is applied at the free end.
Small-deformation theory applies with linear-elastic material behavior.
The governing differential equation is d/dx[EA(x)·du/dx] = 0 where u(x) is the axial displacement.
The axial stress is σ(x) = P/A(x) = P/[A0·(1 + k·x/L)].
The axial strain is ε(x) = σ(x)/E = P/[E·A0·(1 + k·x/L)].
The boundary conditions are u(0) = 0 and EA(L)·du/dx|x=L = P.
The axial displacement is u(x) = (P·L)/(E·A0·k)·ln(1 + k·x/L).
The maximum displacement occurs at x = L and equals u(L) = (P·L)/(E·A0·k)·ln(1 + k).
The maximum stress occurs at x = 0 where the area is smallest and equals σ_max = P/A0.
