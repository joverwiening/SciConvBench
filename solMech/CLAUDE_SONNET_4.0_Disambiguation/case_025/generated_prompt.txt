The cantilever beam analysis uses Castigliano's theorem to determine the end deflection under the applied load.
The beam is fixed at x = 0 and free at x = L = 1500 mm.
A downward point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end (x = L).
The beam has constant second moment of area I = 2500000 mm^4.
The material is 6061-T6 aluminum with elastic modulus E = 69000 MPa.
Small-deformation and linear-elastic behavior are assumed.
The cross-sectional shape is assumed to be rectangular or I-beam with the given moment of inertia.
Shear deformation effects are neglected (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
The beam weight is neglected compared to the applied load.
The bending moment at position x from the fixed end is M(x) = P(L-x).
The strain energy in the beam is U = ∫[0 to L] M(x)²/(2EI) dx.
Substituting the moment expression: U = ∫[0 to L] P²(L-x)²/(2EI) dx.
Evaluating the integral: U = P²L³/(6EI).
Castigliano's theorem states that the deflection δ = ∂U/∂P.
Taking the partial derivative: δ = ∂/∂P [P²L³/(6EI)] = PL³/(3EI).
Substituting the given values: δ = (500 N)(1500 mm)³/[3(69000 MPa)(2500000 mm^4)].
Converting units: δ = (500)(1500)³/[3(69000)(2500000)] mm = 3.26 mm.
The cantilever end deflection under the end load is 3.26 mm downward.
