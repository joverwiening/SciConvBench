The beam is a rectangular cross-section beam with width b = 40 mm and height h = 80 mm.
The beam length is assumed to be L = 1000 mm (25 times the height, following standard beam theory assumptions).
The material is assumed to be structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.
The loading is assumed to be a simply supported beam with a concentrated load at mid-span.
At the section of interest, the internal shear force is V = 25000 N.
Small-deformation and linear-elastic behavior are assumed.
Plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies for bending).
The transverse shear stress distribution follows the parabolic profile for rectangular cross-sections.
The maximum transverse shear stress occurs at the neutral axis (center of the cross-section).
The transverse shear stress at any height y from the neutral axis is given by τ = (VQ)/(Ib), where Q is the first moment of area above level y, I is the second moment of area, and b is the width.
For a rectangular cross-section, the maximum shear stress is τ_max = 1.5V/(bh) = 1.5 × 25000/(40 × 80) = 11.72 MPa.
The shear stress is zero at the top and bottom fibers of the beam.
The relevant response quantities are the shear stress distribution τ(y) and the maximum shear stress τ_max.
