The tension specimen is a ductile metal with circular cross-section.
Initial gage length L0 = 50 mm.
Initial diameter d0 = 12 mm.
Final gage length Lf = 66 mm (measured by fitting broken pieces together).
Final diameter at neck df = 9.6 mm.
The analysis assumes the specimen fails in a ductile manner with necking.
The percent elongation is calculated as: %EL = ((Lf - L0)/L0) × 100 = ((66 - 50)/50) × 100 = 32%.
The initial cross-sectional area is A0 = π(d0/2)² = π(6)² = 113.1 mm².
The final cross-sectional area at neck is Af = π(df/2)² = π(4.8)² = 72.4 mm².
The reduction of area is calculated as: %RA = ((A0 - Af)/A0) × 100 = ((113.1 - 72.4)/113.1) × 100 = 36.0%.
The percent elongation measures the specimen's ability to deform plastically in the longitudinal direction.
The reduction of area measures the specimen's ability to deform plastically in the transverse direction during necking.
Both measures quantify the ductility of the material.
Higher values of percent elongation and reduction of area indicate greater ductility.
