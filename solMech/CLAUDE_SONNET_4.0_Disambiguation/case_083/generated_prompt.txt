The shaft is made of steel with elastic modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.
The shaft length is assumed to be L = 200 mm (8 times the diameter for typical shaft proportions).
The shaft has diameter d = 25 mm and radius r = 12.5 mm.
A tensile axial force P = 25000 N is applied along the shaft axis.
A torque T = 300000 N·mm is applied about the shaft axis.
Small deformation theory applies with linear elastic material behavior.
The shaft is assumed to be homogeneous and isotropic.
End effects are neglected and Saint-Venant's principle applies.
The shaft is assumed to be straight and prismatic.
Calculate the polar moment of inertia J = πd⁴/32 = π(25)⁴/32 mm⁴.
Calculate the cross-sectional area A = πd²/4 = π(25)²/4 mm².
Determine the normal stress due to axial load: σ_axial = P/A.
Determine the maximum shear stress due to torsion: τ_max = Tr/J at the outer surface.
The stress state at the outer surface combines normal stress σ_axial and shear stress τ_max.
Calculate principal stresses using σ₁,₂ = (σ_axial/2) ± √((σ_axial/2)² + τ_max²).
Calculate maximum shear stress: τ_max_principal = (σ₁ - σ₂)/2.
Determine normal strains: ε_axial = σ_axial/E and ε_circumferential = -νσ_axial/E.
Determine shear strain: γ_max = τ_max/G where G = E/(2(1+ν)).
