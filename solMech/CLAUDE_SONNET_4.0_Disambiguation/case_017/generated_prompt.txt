The beam is simply supported at both ends with pinned connections allowing rotation but preventing vertical displacement.
The beam length is L = 3000 mm.
The beam has moment of inertia I = 3000000 mm^4 and elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
A uniformly distributed load w = 0.3 N/mm acts downward over the entire span.
A point load P = 1200 N acts downward at the midspan location x = L/2 = 1500 mm.
The analysis uses small-deformation theory with linear-elastic material behavior.
The Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies with the assumption that plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis.
Shear deformation is neglected compared to bending deformation.
The beam deflection is determined by superposition of the deflections from each load case.
For the uniformly distributed load case, the maximum deflection occurs at midspan and equals 5wL^4/(384EI).
For the point load at midspan case, the maximum deflection occurs at midspan and equals PL^3/(48EI).
The total deflection at any point x is the sum of deflections from both load cases.
The maximum total deflection occurs at midspan and equals 5wL^4/(384EI) + PL^3/(48EI).
The governing differential equation is EI(d^4v/dx^4) = q(x) where v is deflection and q(x) is the distributed load intensity.
Boundary conditions are v(0) = 0, v(L) = 0, M(0) = 0, and M(L) = 0 where M is the bending moment.
