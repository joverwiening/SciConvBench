The shaft has diameter d = 25 mm and length L = 1000 mm.
The shaft is made of AISI 1020 steel with shear modulus G = 76923.1 MPa.
A constant torque T = 350000 N·mm is applied along the entire length.
The analysis uses small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics theory.
The shaft is assumed to be homogeneous and isotropic.
The shaft is assumed to be prismatic with constant circular cross-section.
End effects and stress concentrations are neglected.
The polar moment of inertia is J = πd⁴/32 = π(25)⁴/32 = 38349.5 mm⁴.
The strain energy stored in the shaft is U = T²L/(2GJ).
Substituting values: U = (350000)²(1000)/(2×76923.1×38349.5) = 20.77 N·mm.
Using Castigliano's theorem, the twist angle is φ = ∂U/∂T = TL/(GJ).
The twist angle is φ = (350000)(1000)/(76923.1×38349.5) = 0.1186 radians.
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface: τ_max = Td/(2J) = 350000×25/(2×38349.5) = 114.6 MPa.
The shear strain at the outer surface is γ_max = τ_max/G = 114.6/76923.1 = 0.00149 radians.
The angle of twist per unit length is φ/L = T/(GJ) = 0.0001186 rad/mm.
