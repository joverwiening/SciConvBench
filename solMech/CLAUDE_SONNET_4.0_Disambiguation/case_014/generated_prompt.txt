The cantilever beam has length L = 1200 mm and is fixed at one end with a free end at the other.
A downward point load P = 800 N is applied at the free end of the beam.
The beam is made of structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200,000 MPa.
The beam has a constant second moment of area I = 2,500,000 mm^4.
The beam follows Euler-Bernoulli beam theory assumptions including plane sections remain plane and small deflections.
The beam material behaves as linear elastic with no yielding.
Shear deformation is neglected compared to bending deformation.
The beam cross-section and material properties are uniform along the length.
The governing differential equation is EI d^4v/dx^4 = q(x) where v is deflection and q(x) is distributed load.
For this point load case, the moment equation is M(x) = -P(L-x) for 0 ≤ x ≤ L.
The boundary conditions are v(0) = 0, dv/dx(0) = 0, d^2v/dx^2(L) = 0, and d^3v/dx^3(L) = -P/EI.
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end and equals PL^3/(3EI).
The maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed end and equals PL.
The maximum bending stress occurs at the fixed end at the extreme fiber and equals PL*c/I where c is the distance from neutral axis to extreme fiber.
The deflection at any point x from the fixed end is v(x) = P/(6EI) * x^2 * (3L - x).
The slope at any point x from the fixed end is dv/dx = P/(2EI) * x * (2L - x).
