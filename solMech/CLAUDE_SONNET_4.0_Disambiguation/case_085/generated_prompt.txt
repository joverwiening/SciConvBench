The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The beam length is assumed to be L = 1000 mm (10 times the height for typical beam proportions).
An internal bending moment M = 3000000 N·mm acts at the critical section.
The beam material follows small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior with elastic modulus E.
Plane sections remain plane after deformation (Bernoulli-Euler beam theory).
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Shear deformations are neglected compared to bending deformations.
The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section.
The second moment of area about the neutral axis is I = bh³/12 = 50 × 100³/12 = 4166667 mm⁴.
The maximum distance from the neutral axis to the extreme fiber is c = h/2 = 50 mm.
The flexural stress distribution is σ = -My/I where y is the distance from the neutral axis.
The maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fibers: σ_max = Mc/I = 3000000 × 50/4166667 = 36 MPa.
The stress varies linearly across the cross-section height.
Tensile stress occurs in fibers below the neutral axis and compressive stress occurs in fibers above the neutral axis.
The curvature of the beam is κ = M/(EI).
The deflection can be determined using appropriate boundary conditions and integration of the moment-curvature relationship.
