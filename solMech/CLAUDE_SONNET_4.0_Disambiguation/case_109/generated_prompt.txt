The beam is simply supported at both ends with pinned connections allowing rotation but preventing vertical displacement.
The beam has length L = 2500 mm, elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa, and constant moment of inertia I = 3000000 mm^4.
A downward point load P = 1000 N is applied at the midspan (x = L/2 = 1250 mm from either support).
The beam material follows Hooke's law with linear elastic behavior under small deformations.
The beam cross-section remains plane during bending (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies).
Shear deformations are neglected compared to bending deformations.
The beam weight is neglected compared to the applied load.
Use Castigliano's theorem to determine the midspan deflection by taking the partial derivative of strain energy with respect to the applied load P.
The bending moment for 0 ≤ x ≤ L/2 is M(x) = Px/2.
The bending moment for L/2 ≤ x ≤ L is M(x) = P(L-x)/2.
The total strain energy is U = (1/2EI) ∫[0 to L/2] (Px/2)² dx + (1/2EI) ∫[L/2 to L] (P(L-x)/2)² dx.
The midspan deflection is δ = ∂U/∂P = PL³/(48EI).
Substituting the given values: δ = (1000 N)(2500 mm)³/(48 × 200000 MPa × 3000000 mm^4) = 0.0434 mm.
