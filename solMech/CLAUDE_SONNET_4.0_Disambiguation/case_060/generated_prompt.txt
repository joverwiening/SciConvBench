The column is made of steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The column length is L = 2000 mm.
The column has fixed-fixed end conditions with effective length factor K = 0.5.
The second moment of area is I = 800000 mm^4.
The cross-sectional shape is assumed to be circular for simplicity in practical applications.
Small-deformation linear-elastic behavior is assumed.
The material remains in the elastic range throughout the analysis.
Initial geometric imperfections are neglected.
The column is perfectly straight and homogeneous.
The load is applied concentrically along the column axis.
Calculate the effective length as L_eff = K × L = 0.5 × 2000 = 1000 mm.
Apply the Euler buckling formula P_cr = π²EI/L_eff².
Substitute values: P_cr = π² × 200000 × 800000 / (1000)².
Calculate the critical buckling load P_cr = 1579.14 × 10⁶ N = 1579.14 kN.
For buckling evaluation, assume a typical applied load of P_applied = 0.7 × P_cr = 1105.4 kN as a reasonable design load.
Calculate the buckling safety factor as SF = P_cr / P_applied = 1.43.
Verify that SF > 1.0 indicates the column is stable under the assumed applied load.
The governing equation is the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation: EI d⁴w/dx⁴ + P d²w/dx² = 0.
The critical load represents the minimum load at which buckling occurs.
For loads P < P_cr, the column remains stable in its straight configuration.
For loads P ≥ P_cr, the column becomes unstable and will buckle.
