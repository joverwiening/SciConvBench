The circular shaft has diameter d = 45 mm and is subjected to combined bending moment M = 2000000 N·mm and torque T = 500000 N·mm.
The analysis is performed at the outer surface of the shaft at the location of maximum bending stress.
The shaft material follows small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.
The shaft cross-section remains plane during bending.
Shear deformation due to bending is neglected compared to that due to torsion.
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Calculate the section modulus for bending as Z = πd³/32.
Calculate the polar section modulus for torsion as Zp = πd³/16.
The normal stress due to bending is σ = M/Z.
The shear stress due to torsion is τ = T/Zp.
At the critical point, the stress state consists of normal stress σ in the axial direction and shear stress τ in the circumferential-axial plane.
The principal stresses are calculated using σ₁,₂ = σ/2 ± √((σ/2)² + τ²).
The maximum shear stress is τmax = (σ₁ - σ₂)/2.
The von Mises equivalent stress is σeq = √(σ₁² - σ₁σ₂ + σ₂²).
For strain analysis, use Hooke's law with the material's elastic modulus E and Poisson's ratio ν.
The principal strains follow the same transformation as stresses but with elastic relationships.
