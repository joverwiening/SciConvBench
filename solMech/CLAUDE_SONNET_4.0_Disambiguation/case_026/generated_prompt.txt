The cantilever beam is fixed at one end and free at the other end.
The beam has length L = 1500 mm.
The beam has constant second moment of area I = 800000 mm^4.
The beam material is AISI 1020 steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
A point load P = 500 N is applied vertically downward at the free end.
The cross-sectional shape is assumed to be rectangular for simplicity.
Small-deformation theory applies with linear-elastic material behavior.
The beam undergoes bending deformation only, with negligible shear deformation.
The beam weight is neglected compared to the applied load.
The coordinate system has x-axis along the beam length from fixed end to free end.
The bending moment at distance x from the fixed end is M(x) = P(L-x).
The strain energy in the beam is U = ∫[0 to L] M(x)²/(2EI) dx.
Using Castigliano's theorem, the deflection at the free end is δ = ∂U/∂P.
Substituting the bending moment expression: δ = ∫[0 to L] M(x) × (∂M/∂P)/(EI) dx.
Since ∂M/∂P = (L-x), the deflection becomes δ = ∫[0 to L] P(L-x)²/(EI) dx.
Evaluating the integral: δ = P/(EI) × ∫[0 to L] (L-x)² dx = P/(EI) × [L³/3].
The final expression for cantilever end deflection is δ = PL³/(3EI).
Substituting values: δ = (500 N × (1500 mm)³)/(3 × 200000 MPa × 800000 mm⁴).
Converting units: δ = (500 × 1500³)/(3 × 200000 × 800000) = 3.515625 mm.
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end and equals 3.52 mm downward.
