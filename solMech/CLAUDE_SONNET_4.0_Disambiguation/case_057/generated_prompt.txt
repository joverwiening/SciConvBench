The column is a fixed-fixed Euler buckling column with length L = 3000 mm.
The material has elastic modulus E = 69000 MPa.
The second moment of area is I = 2500000 mm^4.
Assume the cross-section is uniform along the length.
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the column is initially straight and perfectly aligned.
Assume small deformations and linear elastic behavior.
The effective length factor for fixed-fixed end conditions is K = 0.5.
The effective length is Le = K × L = 0.5 × 3000 = 1500 mm.
The Euler critical buckling load is Pcr = π²EI/Le² = π² × 69000 × 2500000 / (1500)² N.
Calculate Pcr = π² × 172500000000 / 2250000 = 756637 N = 756.6 kN.
For buckling instability check, compare applied load P to critical load Pcr.
If P < Pcr, the column is stable.
If P ≥ Pcr, buckling instability occurs.
The critical stress is σcr = Pcr/A where A is the cross-sectional area.
The slenderness ratio is λ = Le/r where r is the radius of gyration.
For a rectangular cross-section assumption, use r = √(I/A) to determine geometric properties.
The governing differential equation is EI(d⁴w/dx⁴) + P(d²w/dx²) = 0 where w is lateral displacement.
The boundary conditions for fixed-fixed ends are w(0) = w(L) = 0 and dw/dx(0) = dw/dx(L) = 0.
