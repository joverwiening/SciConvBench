The beam is a simply supported steel beam with pinned supports at both ends.
The beam has a length L = 3000 mm and constant second moment of area I = 3000000 mm^4.
The beam material is structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
A downward point load P = 2000 N is applied at the midspan of the beam.
The beam cross-section is assumed to be rectangular with reasonable proportions consistent with the given second moment of area.
Small deformation theory applies with linear elastic material behavior.
The beam follows Euler-Bernoulli beam theory assumptions.
The governing differential equation is EI d^4w/dx^4 = q(x) where w is deflection and q(x) is the distributed load.
For the point load at midspan, the reaction forces at each support are P/2 = 1000 N upward.
The bending moment at any point x from the left support is M(x) = (P/2)x for 0 ≤ x ≤ L/2.
By symmetry, the maximum bending moment occurs at midspan and equals M_max = PL/4 = 1500000 N·mm.
Using Castigliano's theorem, the midspan deflection is calculated as δ = ∂U/∂P where U is the strain energy.
The strain energy is U = ∫(M^2)/(2EI) dx integrated over the beam length.
The midspan deflection equals δ = PL^3/(48EI) = (2000 × 3000^3)/(48 × 200000 × 3000000) = 1.875 mm.
The maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fibers at midspan.
All deformations remain within the elastic range and small deformation assumptions are valid.
