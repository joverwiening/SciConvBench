The beam is simply supported with supports at x = 0 and x = L = 1800 mm.
A single point load P = 2000 N acts downward at x = 900 mm.
The beam material is AISI 1020 steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The beam cross-section has second moment of area I = 3500000 mm^4.
Small-deformation theory applies with linear-elastic material behavior.
Euler-Bernoulli beam theory governs the bending behavior.
The governing differential equation is EI d^4v/dx^4 = q(x) where v is deflection and q(x) is distributed load.
For point loads, use EI d^2v/dx^2 = M(x) where M(x) is the bending moment.
The point load creates a discontinuity in shear force and bending moment at x = 900 mm.
Support reactions are determined from equilibrium: vertical force sum equals zero and moment sum about any point equals zero.
The left reaction force is R_A = P(L-a)/L = 2000(1800-900)/1800 = 1000 N upward.
The right reaction force is R_B = Pa/L = 2000(900)/1800 = 1000 N upward.
The bending moment for 0 ≤ x ≤ 900 mm is M(x) = R_A × x = 1000x N·mm.
The bending moment for 900 mm ≤ x ≤ 1800 mm is M(x) = R_A × x - P(x-900) = 1000x - 2000(x-900) N·mm.
For unit-load method deflection calculation, apply a unit load at the desired deflection location and determine the corresponding moment diagram.
The deflection at any point is calculated using the virtual work principle: δ = ∫(M × m)/(EI) dx over the beam length.
Here M is the moment from actual loading and m is the moment from unit load at the deflection point.
The integration is performed separately over each segment where the moment expressions are valid.
