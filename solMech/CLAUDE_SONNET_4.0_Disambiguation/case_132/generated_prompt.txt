The beam has a span length of 3000 mm (reasonable assumption for typical structural applications).
The beam experiences a maximum bending moment of M_max = 3000000 N·mm.
The loading configuration that creates this maximum moment is assumed to be a uniformly distributed load over the entire span.
The beam material has an allowable bending stress of σ_allow = 90 MPa.
The rectangular cross-section has a width of b = 40 mm.
The beam height h is to be determined based on the allowable bending stress criterion.
The beam material follows linear-elastic behavior with small deformations.
The beam is homogeneous and isotropic.
Plane sections remain plane after bending (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies).
The maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fiber of the cross-section where the maximum bending moment occurs.
For a rectangular cross-section, the section modulus is S = bh²/6.
The bending stress formula is σ = M/S = 6M/(bh²).
The design criterion is σ_max ≤ σ_allow, which gives 6M_max/(bh²) ≤ 90 MPa.
Solving for the required height: h ≥ √(6M_max/(b × σ_allow)).
Substituting values: h ≥ √(6 × 3000000/(40 × 90)) = √(18000000/3600) = √5000 = 70.7 mm.
The minimum required beam height is h_min = 70.7 mm.
For practical design, round up to the nearest standard dimension or add a safety factor as appropriate.
