The hollow circular shaft has outer radius ro = 30 mm and inner radius ri = 24 mm with length L = 800 mm.
The shaft is subjected to an applied torque T = 450000 N·mm.
The material follows small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.
The shaft is assumed to be homogeneous and isotropic.
Cross-sections remain plane and undistorted during deformation (plane sections remain plane).
The material is assumed to have a shear modulus G (to be specified based on material properties).
The governing equation for torsion is T = GJ(dθ/dx) where J is the polar moment of inertia.
For a hollow circular cross-section, J = π(ro⁴ - ri⁴)/2 = π(30⁴ - 24⁴)/2 mm⁴.
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer radius and is given by τmax = T·ro/J.
The minimum shear stress in the material occurs at the inner radius and is given by τmin = T·ri/J.
The angle of twist over the length L is φ = TL/(GJ).
The shear strain at any radius r is γ = rφ/L = Tr/(GJ).
The shear stress distribution varies linearly with radius from the inner surface to the outer surface.
At any radius r (where ri ≤ r ≤ ro), the shear stress is τ = Tr/J.
