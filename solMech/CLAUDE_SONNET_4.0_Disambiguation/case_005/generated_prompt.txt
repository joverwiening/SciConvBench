The prismatic bar has a constant circular cross-sectional area A = 200 mm² and total length L = 1800 mm divided into three segments: AB = 700 mm, BC = 600 mm, and CD = 500 mm.
Point loads are applied at B (18000 N rightward), C (-8000 N leftward), and D (20000 N rightward).
The left end A is fixed with displacement u_A = 0, and the right end D is free.
The material follows Hooke's law with stress-strain relation σ = Eε where E is Young's modulus.
The strain-displacement relation is ε = du/dx for small deformations.
The equilibrium equation is dN/dx + f = 0 where N is the axial force and f is the distributed load per unit length.
For this problem with point loads only, f = 0 between load application points.
The axial force N is constant within each segment and changes abruptly at point load locations.
In segment AB: N_AB = 0 N (no loads applied yet).
In segment BC: N_BC = 18000 N (after load at B).
In segment CD: N_CD = 18000 - 8000 = 10000 N (after loads at B and C).
The stress in each segment is σ = N/A.
The displacement field is obtained by integrating ε = σ/E = N/(EA) with boundary condition u_A = 0.
The relevant response quantities are axial force distribution N(x), stress distribution σ(x), strain distribution ε(x), and displacement field u(x).
The maximum stress occurs in segment BC with σ_max = 18000/200 = 90 MPa.
The total elongation is the displacement at the free end D.
