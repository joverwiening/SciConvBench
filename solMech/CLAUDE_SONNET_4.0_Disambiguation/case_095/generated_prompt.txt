The cantilever beam has length L = 1800 mm and is fixed at one end with the other end free.
A downward point load P = 2000 N is applied at the free end of the beam.
The beam is made of structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The beam has a constant second moment of area I = 1200000 mm^4.
Small-deformation theory applies with linear-elastic material behavior.
Euler-Bernoulli beam theory is used, assuming plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis.
Shear deformation is neglected compared to bending deformation.
The beam is prismatic with constant cross-sectional properties along its length.
The coordinate system has x measured from the fixed end toward the free end, with y positive upward.
The governing differential equation is EI d^4v/dx^4 = q(x), where v(x) is the transverse deflection and q(x) is the distributed load.
For this point load case, the moment equation is EI d^2v/dx^2 = M(x), where M(x) = -P(L-x) for 0 ≤ x ≤ L.
The boundary conditions at the fixed end (x = 0) are v(0) = 0 and dv/dx(0) = 0.
The boundary conditions at the free end (x = L) are d^2v/dx^2(L) = 0 and d^3v/dx^3(L) = -P/EI.
The deflection solution is v(x) = (P/(6EI))(3Lx^2 - x^3).
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end and equals v_max = PL^3/(3EI).
The slope at any point is dv/dx = (P/(2EI))(3Lx - x^2).
The maximum slope occurs at the free end and equals θ_max = PL^2/(2EI).
The bending moment at any point is M(x) = -P(L-x).
The maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed end and equals M_max = -PL.
The shear force is constant along the beam and equals V = -P.
