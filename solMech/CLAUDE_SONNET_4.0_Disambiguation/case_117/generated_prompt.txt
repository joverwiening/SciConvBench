The beam has rectangular cross-section with width b = 60 mm and height h = 80 mm.
The beam length is assumed to be L = 1200 mm (15 times the height for typical beam proportions).
The loading is assumed to be a concentrated load P = 16000 N applied at mid-span of a simply supported beam.
The material is assumed to be structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.
The analysis focuses on transverse shear stress distribution at the section where internal shear force V = 16000 N.
Small deformation theory applies with linear elastic material behavior.
Plane sections remain plane after deformation (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
The transverse shear stress varies parabolically across the beam height according to τ = VQ/(Ib).
The first moment of area Q = b(h²/4 - y²)/2 where y is measured from the neutral axis.
The second moment of area I = bh³/12 = 60 × 80³/12 = 2.56 × 10⁶ mm⁴.
Maximum shear stress occurs at the neutral axis: τ_max = 3V/(2bh) = 3 × 16000/(2 × 60 × 80) = 5.0 MPa.
Shear stress is zero at the top and bottom fibers of the beam.
The shear stress distribution is τ(y) = (3V/2bh)[1 - (2y/h)²] where -h/2 ≤ y ≤ h/2.
