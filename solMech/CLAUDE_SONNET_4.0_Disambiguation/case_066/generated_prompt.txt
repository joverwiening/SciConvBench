The shaft is a solid circular shaft with diameter d = 45 mm and length L = 1000 mm.
The shaft is made of a linear elastic material with shear modulus G = 76923.1 MPa.
The shaft is subjected to an applied torque T = 400,000 N·mm.
Small deformation theory applies.
The shaft behaves as a linear elastic material under torsional loading.
Cross-sections remain plane and do not warp during twisting.
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface of the shaft.
The maximum shear stress is calculated using τ_max = T·c/J where c is the outer radius and J is the polar moment of inertia.
For a solid circular shaft, J = π·d⁴/32.
The polar moment of inertia J = π·(45)⁴/32 = 201,061.9 mm⁴.
The outer radius c = d/2 = 22.5 mm.
The maximum shear stress τ_max = (400,000 × 22.5)/201,061.9 = 44.7 MPa.
The angle of twist is calculated using φ = T·L/(G·J).
The angle of twist φ = (400,000 × 1000)/(76923.1 × 201,061.9) = 0.0259 radians.
The angle of twist in degrees is φ = 0.0259 × 180/π = 1.48 degrees.
The shear stress varies linearly from zero at the center to maximum at the outer surface.
At any radius r from the center, the shear stress is τ(r) = T·r/J.
