The analysis determines stress distribution and deformation in a short rectangular column under eccentric axial loading.

The rectangular cross-section has width b = 120 mm and height h = 200 mm.
A compressive axial load P = 40000 N is applied with eccentricity e = 25 mm from the centroid along the width direction.
The column length is assumed to be L = 600 mm (3 times the height, typical for short column classification).
The material follows small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior with assumed properties: E = 200 GPa and ν = 0.3.
The column is assumed to have fixed-fixed end conditions for boundary constraints.
The eccentric load creates combined axial compression and bending about the weak axis.
The direct stress from axial load is σ_axial = P/A = P/(b×h).
The bending moment about the weak axis is M = P×e.
The bending stress varies linearly across the width as σ_bending = ±M×y/I_y where I_y = b×h³/12.
The total normal stress is σ_total = σ_axial + σ_bending = P/(b×h) ± (P×e×y)/(b×h³/12).
Maximum compressive stress occurs at y = h/2 on the side toward the eccentricity.
Maximum tensile stress (or minimum compressive stress) occurs at y = -h/2 on the side away from the eccentricity.
The neutral axis location is determined by setting σ_total = 0.
Axial deformation is δ = PL/(EA) where A = b×h.
Lateral deflection follows beam bending theory with the applied moment M = P×e.
Check that maximum stress does not exceed material allowable limits.
Verify that the section remains in compression (no tension) if required by design criteria.
