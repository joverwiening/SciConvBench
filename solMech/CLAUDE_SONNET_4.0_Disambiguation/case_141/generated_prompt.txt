The circular shaft has diameter d = 35 mm and is subjected to combined bending moment M = 3000000 N·mm and torque T = 500000 N·mm.
The analysis is performed at the outer surface of the shaft at the location of maximum bending stress.
The material follows small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior with isotropic properties.
The shaft cross-section remains plane during bending (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies).
Shear deformation due to bending is neglected compared to bending deformation.
The shaft material is homogeneous and the loads are applied statically.
Calculate the section modulus Z = πd³/32 for bending stress analysis.
Calculate the polar moment of inertia J = πd⁴/32 for torsional stress analysis.
Determine the maximum bending stress σ_b = M/Z at the outer fiber.
Determine the maximum shear stress τ = T·r/J at the outer surface where r = d/2.
At the critical point, the normal stress σ_x = σ_b acts in the axial direction.
At the critical point, the shear stress τ_xy = τ acts in the circumferential-axial plane.
The stress state is plane stress with σ_y = 0 and σ_z = 0.
Calculate principal stresses using σ_1,2 = (σ_x ± √(σ_x² + 4τ_xy²))/2.
Calculate maximum shear stress τ_max = (σ_1 - σ_2)/2.
Determine the orientation of principal stress directions using tan(2θ) = 2τ_xy/σ_x.
Calculate von Mises equivalent stress σ_eq = √(σ_x² + 3τ_xy²).
For strain analysis, apply Hooke's law with material properties E (Young's modulus) and ν (Poisson's ratio).
Calculate principal strains from principal stresses using the plane stress constitutive relations.
