The beam is simply supported at both ends with pinned connections allowing rotation but preventing vertical displacement.
The beam has length L = 3000 mm, moment of inertia I = 2000000 mm^4, and Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.
Two loads act on the beam: a uniformly distributed load w = 0.3 N/mm over the full span and a point load P = 3000 N downward at midspan (x = L/2 = 1500 mm).
The analysis uses small-deformation theory with linear-elastic material behavior.
The Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies with assumptions of plane sections remaining plane and negligible shear deformation.
The governing differential equation is EI(d^4v/dx^4) = q(x) where v is deflection and q(x) is the distributed load.
Boundary conditions are v(0) = 0, v(L) = 0, M(0) = 0, and M(L) = 0.
The deflection is determined by superposition of two cases: Case 1 with uniformly distributed load w only, and Case 2 with point load P only.
For Case 1, the maximum deflection at midspan is v1_max = 5wL^4/(384EI).
For Case 2, the maximum deflection at midspan is v2_max = PL^3/(48EI).
The total deflection at any point is v_total(x) = v1(x) + v2(x).
The maximum total deflection occurs at midspan and equals v_max = v1_max + v2_max.
Present the deflection equations for both cases and calculate the numerical values for maximum deflection.
Verify that the superposition principle is valid since the system remains linear-elastic.
