The problem involves analyzing the dynamic response of a vertical steel rod subjected to impact loading from a dropped weight.

Given parameters: Weight W = 800 N, drop height h = 150 mm, rod length L = 1500 mm, rod diameter d = 16 mm, circular cross-sectional area A = π d²/4 = π(16)²/4 = 201.06 mm², material is AISI 1020 steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa.

Assume no additional static axial load beyond the impact loading.

Assume the rod mass is negligible compared to the dropped weight.

Assume the impact occurs at the free end of the rod with the other end fixed.

Assume the dropped weight sticks to the rod after impact (perfectly inelastic collision).

Assume small deformations and linear elastic material behavior throughout the impact event.

The velocity of the weight just before impact is v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.15) = 1.715 m/s.

The equivalent static load that produces the same maximum stress as the dynamic impact is P_eq = W(1 + √(1 + 2h/δ_st)) where δ_st is the static deflection under weight W.

The static deflection is δ_st = WL/(AE) = 800 × 1.5/(201.06 × 10⁻⁶ × 200 × 10⁹) = 2.985 × 10⁻⁵ m.

The dynamic amplification factor is DAF = 1 + √(1 + 2h/δ_st) = 1 + √(1 + 2 × 0.15/(2.985 × 10⁻⁵)) = 101.0.

The maximum dynamic stress is σ_max = (W × DAF)/A = (800 × 101.0)/(201.06 × 10⁻⁶) = 402.0 MPa.

The maximum dynamic deflection is δ_max = DAF × δ_st = 101.0 × 2.985 × 10⁻⁵ = 3.014 × 10⁻³ m.

The governing equation for axial vibration is ∂²u/∂t² = (E/ρ)(∂²u/∂x²) where u(x,t) is displacement, but since rod mass is neglected, use energy methods for impact analysis.

Verify that maximum stress does not exceed material yield strength for AISI 1020 steel.
