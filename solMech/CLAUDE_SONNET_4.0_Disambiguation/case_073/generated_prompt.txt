The shaft is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 30 mm and length L = 1000 mm.
The shaft is made of 6061-T6 aluminum with shear modulus G = 25939.8 MPa.
A constant torque T = 450000 N·mm is applied along the entire length of the shaft.
The analysis uses small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics theory.
The shaft behaves as a linearly elastic material under torsional loading.
The cross-section remains plane and circular during deformation.
Warping of the cross-section is neglected.
The polar moment of inertia is J = πd⁴/32 = π(0.03)⁴/32 = 7.952 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴.
The strain energy stored in the shaft is U = T²L/(2GJ).
Using Castigliano's theorem, the angle of twist is φ = ∂U/∂T = TL/(GJ).
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface: τ_max = Td/(2J).
The shear strain at the outer surface is γ_max = τ_max/G.
The angle of twist per unit length is φ/L = T/(GJ).
Substituting values: φ = (450000 × 10⁻³ N·m)(1.0 m)/[(25939.8 × 10⁶ Pa)(7.952 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴)] = 0.218 radians.
The maximum shear stress is τ_max = (450000 × 10⁻³ N·m)(0.015 m)/(7.952 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴) = 84.85 × 10⁶ Pa = 84.85 MPa.
