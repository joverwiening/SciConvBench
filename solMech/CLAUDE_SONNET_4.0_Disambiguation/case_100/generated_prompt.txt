The cantilever beam analysis uses Castigliano's theorem to determine the end deflection under the applied point load.

The beam is fixed at one end and free at the other end.

A downward point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end of the cantilever beam.

The beam has length L = 1500 mm and constant moment of inertia I = 2500000 mm^4.

The beam material is AISI 1020 steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.

Small-deformation theory applies with linear-elastic material behavior.

The bending moment at distance x from the fixed end is M(x) = P(L-x).

The strain energy in the beam is U = ∫[0 to L] M(x)²/(2EI) dx.

Castigliano's theorem states that the deflection at the point of load application equals ∂U/∂P.

The partial derivative ∂M/∂P = (L-x).

The end deflection is δ = ∫[0 to L] M(x) × (∂M/∂P)/(EI) dx.

Substituting gives δ = ∫[0 to L] P(L-x)(L-x)/(EI) dx = P∫[0 to L] (L-x)²/(EI) dx.

Evaluating the integral yields δ = PL³/(3EI).

The numerical result is δ = (500 N × (1500 mm)³)/(3 × 200000 MPa × 2500000 mm^4).

Converting units consistently gives δ = 1.125 mm downward at the free end.
