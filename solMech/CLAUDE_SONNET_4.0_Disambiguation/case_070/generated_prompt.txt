The beam is made of AISI 1020 steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The beam has a second moment of area I = 2500000 mm^4 about the neutral axis.
A bending moment M = 3000000 N·mm is applied to the beam.
The cross-sectional shape is assumed to be rectangular for analysis purposes.
The bending moment is assumed to cause positive curvature (compression on top fiber, tension on bottom fiber).
Small-deformation theory applies with linear-elastic material behavior.
Plane sections remain plane after bending (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Shear deformation is neglected compared to bending deformation.
The governing relation for curvature is κ = M/(EI) where κ is curvature, M is bending moment, E is elastic modulus, and I is second moment of area.
The curvature κ = 3000000/(200000 × 2500000) = 6.0 × 10^-6 mm^-1.
The radius of curvature ρ = 1/κ = 1/(6.0 × 10^-6) = 166667 mm.
The maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fibers and is given by σ_max = Mc/I where c is the distance from neutral axis to extreme fiber.
