The problem involves determining the elongation of a vertical bar due to its own self-weight under small-deformation, linear-elastic conditions.

Given parameters: Length L = 3500 mm, cross-sectional area A = 200 mm², material density ρ = 8900 kg/m³, elastic modulus E = 69000 MPa.

Additional assumptions: The bar is homogeneous with uniform cross-section, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s², the bar is fixed at the top end, and deformations remain small.

The weight per unit length is w = ρgA = 17.458 N/mm.

The axial stress at distance x from the free bottom end is σ(x) = ρgx.

The axial strain at distance x from the free bottom end is ε(x) = ρgx/E.

The total elongation is calculated by integrating strain over the length: δ = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x)dx = ∫₀ᴸ (ρgx/E)dx = ρgL²/(2E).

Substituting values: δ = (8900 × 9.81 × (3.5)²)/(2 × 69000) = 7.74 × 10⁻³ m = 7.74 mm.

The maximum stress occurs at the fixed top end: σₘₐₓ = ρgL = 305.9 kPa.

The maximum strain occurs at the fixed top end: εₘₐₓ = ρgL/E = 4.43 × 10⁻⁶.
