The specification for the rectangular beam bending analysis is as follows:

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The moment is applied about the beam's minor (weak) axis.
The applied moment magnitude is M = 4,000,000 N·mm (sagging).
Use Euler-Bernoulli beam theory assumptions.
Calculate bending stress using the flexure formula: σ = (M * y) / I.
Compute the moment of inertia for the rectangular cross-section: I = (b * h³) / 12.
Determine stress at arbitrary fiber locations by substituting y-coordinate into the flexure formula.
Stress will vary linearly from compression at one extreme fiber to tension at the opposite extreme fiber.
The neutral axis will be located at the centroid of the cross-section.
Maximum bending stress will occur at the extreme fibers (y = ±h/2).
Neglect shear deformation effects due to the assumed beam theory.
