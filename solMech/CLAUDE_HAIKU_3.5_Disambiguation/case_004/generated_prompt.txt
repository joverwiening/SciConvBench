For AISI 1020 steel, I will use the standard engineering assumption of ν = 0.3 (Poisson's ratio).


The specification for the axially loaded prismatic bar is as follows:

Geometry:
The prismatic bar has a total length of 1500 mm, divided into three segments:
Segment AB = 500 mm
Segment BC = 600 mm
Segment CD = 400 mm
Cross-sectional area = 300 mm^2 (rectangular cross-section)

Material Properties:
Material: AISI 1020 Steel
Young's Modulus (E) = 200,000 MPa
Poisson's Ratio (ν) = 0.3

Loading Conditions:
Point load at B: 18,000 N (positive direction, to the right)
Point load at C: -12,000 N (negative direction, to the left)
Point load at D: 16,000 N (positive direction, to the right)

Analysis Assumptions:
Small-deformation linear-elastic solid mechanics
Axial loading only
Uniform cross-sectional area
Homogeneous material properties

Governing Relations:
Normal stress (σ) = Axial force (F) / Cross-sectional area (A)
Axial strain (ε) = Normal stress (σ) / Young's Modulus (E)
Axial displacement (u) = Strain (ε) * Original length (L)
