Determine the bending stress distribution for a rectangular beam with the following specifications:

Beam cross-section: Rectangular
Width (b) = 50 mm
Height (h) = 120 mm
Applied Moment (M) = 4,000,000 N·mm about the minor axis (sagging)

Assumptions:
Small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics model
Plane sections remain plane (Bernoulli-Navier hypothesis)
Homogeneous and isotropic material
Elastic deformation regime

Governing Relations:
Calculate bending stress using the flexure formula: σ = (M * y) / I
Where:
- σ is the normal stress at a fiber location
- M is the applied moment
- y is the distance from the neutral axis
- I is the moment of inertia about the minor axis

Moment of Inertia (I) = (b * h³) / 12 = (50 * 120³) / 12 = 720,000 mm⁴

Neutral axis located at the centroid of the cross-section (h/2 from either extreme fiber)

Maximum bending stress will occur at the extreme fibers (±h/2 from neutral axis)
