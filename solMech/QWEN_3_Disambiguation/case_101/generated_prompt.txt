Use linear-elastic small-deformation solid mechanics theory.  
Model the beam as a cantilever beam fixed at one end and free at the other.  
Apply a uniformly distributed transverse load of 1.5 N/mm acting downward along the entire length of the beam.  
Set the beam length to 1000 mm.  
Set the elastic modulus (E) of the material to 200000 MPa.  
Set the cross-sectional moment of inertia (I) to 2000000 mm⁴.  
Use Euler-Bernoulli beam theory to compute deflection.  
Assume no axial deformation or shear deformation effects.  
The governing differential equation for beam deflection is: E * I * (d⁴w/dx⁴) = q, where q = 1.5 N/mm.  
The boundary conditions are: at the fixed end (x = 0), deflection w = 0 and slope dw/dx = 0.  
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end (x = 1000 mm).  
Compute the deflection using the analytical formula: δ_max = (q * L⁴) / (8 * E * I).
