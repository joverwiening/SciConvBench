Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The cantilever beam has a length of 1500 mm.  
The beam is subjected to a uniformly distributed transverse load of 1.5 N/mm acting downward.  
The only applied load is the uniform transverse load; no other distributed loads are present.  
The cross-sectional moment of inertia (I) of the beam is 1,500,000 mm⁴.  
The elastic modulus (E) of the beam material is 200,000 MPa.  
The beam is fixed at one end and free at the other.  
The governing equation for beam deflection is the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation.  
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end of the cantilever.  
The maximum deflection is calculated using the formula: δ_max = (w * L^4) / (8 * E * I), where w is the distributed load, L is the beam length, E is the elastic modulus, and I is the moment of inertia.  
Deflections are small and within the linear-elastic range.  
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.  
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after deformation.  
Axial and shear deformations are negligible.  
The response quantity of interest is the free-end deflection under the given loading.
