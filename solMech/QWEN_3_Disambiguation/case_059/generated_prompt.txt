Assume linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics.
The column is prismatic with constant cross-sectional area and homogeneous material.
The material behavior follows Hooke's law with constant Young's modulus E.
The column is perfectly straight and loading is concentric, with no initial imperfections or lateral loads.
The end conditions are pinned–pinned, allowing rotation but no translational movement.
The Euler critical buckling load governs the onset of elastic instability.
The governing differential equation for buckling is EI(d²y/dx²) + P y = 0, where y is the lateral deflection.
The general solution satisfies the boundary conditions y(0) = 0 and y(L) = 0.
The critical buckling load is given by P_cr = (π² E I) / L².
The response quantity of interest is the Euler critical buckling load P_cr.
A buckling/instability check compares the applied compressive load P to P_cr.
Failure is indicated if P ≥ P_cr, as elastic instability occurs at this threshold.
The second moment of area I is defined about the minimum stiffness axis of the cross-section.
The length L is the effective length between supports, equal to the actual length for pinned–pinned conditions.
