Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The shaft is composed of two segments: AB and BC.  
Segment AB has a diameter d1 = 25 mm and length L1 = 300 mm.  
Segment BC has a diameter d2 = 30 mm and length L2 = 700 mm.  
A torque T = 450000 N·mm is applied at end C.  
End A is fixed and does not rotate or translate.  
The shaft is made of structural steel with a shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa.  
There are no distributed torques or additional shear forces acting on the shaft.  
The torque is applied statically and remains constant over time.  
The cross-sections of the shaft remain plane and circular during deformation (no warping considered).  
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.  
The response quantities of interest are the maximum shear stress in each segment and the total angle of twist at end C.  
The maximum shear stress in a segment is calculated using τ_max = (T × r) / J, where r is the outer radius and J is the polar moment of inertia.  
The polar moment of inertia for a solid circular section is J = (π × d⁴) / 32.  
The angle of twist for each segment is φ = (T × L) / (G × J).  
The total twist at end C is the sum of the twists of segments AB and BC.  
Segment AB carries the full applied torque T.  
Segment BC carries the full applied torque T.  
The maximum shear stress occurs in the segment with the smallest diameter (AB).  
The total twist at C is calculated with respect to the fixed end A.
