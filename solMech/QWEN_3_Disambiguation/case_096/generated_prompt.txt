Use linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics with Euler-Bernoulli beam theory.
Assume the beam is homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic with constant cross-section.
Assume no axial deformation and negligible shear deformation.
Assume the only load is a downward point load of 500 N at the free end of the cantilever beam.
Assume no applied moment at the free end.
The length of the cantilever beam is 800 mm.
The cross-sectional moment of inertia is 800,000 mm⁴.
The Young's modulus of the material (6061-T6 aluminum) is 69,000 MPa.
The governing differential equation for beam deflection is $ EI \frac{d^4w}{dx^4} = 0 $ for $ 0 < x < L $.
The boundary conditions at the fixed end (x = 0) are zero deflection and zero slope.
The boundary conditions at the free end (x = L) are a shear force equal to the applied load and zero moment.
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end and is given by $ \delta = \frac{PL^3}{3EI} $.
The maximum slope at the free end is given by $ \theta = \frac{PL^2}{2EI} $.
Compute deflection and slope at the free end using the given parameters.
