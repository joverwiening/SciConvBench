Use linear-elastic material behavior with small deformations.  
Assume the bar is prismatic in shape with length L = 500 mm.  
The cross-sectional area varies linearly as A(x) = A₀·(1 + k·x/L), where A₀ = 200 mm², k = 1.0.  
At x = 0, the cross-sectional area is 200 mm²; at x = L, it is 400 mm².  
The left end of the bar (x = 0) is fixed and fully restrained.  
A constant axial tensile force P = 12000 N is applied at the right end (x = L).  
The load is point-applied at the free end and acts along the axial direction.  
The material has a constant Young’s modulus E; assume E = 200 GPa unless otherwise specified.  
The axial deformation δ is computed by integrating the strain over the length: δ = ∫₀ᴸ [P / (E·A(x))] dx.  
The stress at any position x is given by σ(x) = P / A(x).  
The strain at any position x is ε(x) = σ(x) / E = P / (E·A(x)).  
Compute the total elongation by evaluating δ = P/(E·A₀) · ∫₀ᴸ dx / (1 + k·x/L).  
Use analytical integration to determine the exact axial deformation.
