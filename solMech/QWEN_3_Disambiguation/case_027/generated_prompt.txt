Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The bar is subjected to impact loading due to a dropped weight and a constant axial load.  
The dropped weight has a magnitude of 800 N.  
The weight is dropped from a height of 150 mm.  
The velocity of the weight at impact is 1715 mm/s, calculated from v = √(2gh) with g = 9.81 m/s² and h = 0.15 m.  
The rod is made of AISI 1020 steel.  
The rod has a circular cross-section with diameter 16 mm.  
The cross-sectional area of the rod is 201.06 mm².  
The length of the rod is 1500 mm.  
The rod is axially loaded and oriented vertically.  
The impact occurs at the free end of the rod.  
The rod is fixed at the opposite end.  
The material behavior is linear-elastic with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa for AISI 1020 steel.  
Poisson’s ratio is 0.29 for AISI 1020 steel.  
The dynamic impact is modeled using the conservation of energy and momentum.  
The maximum axial stress due to impact is calculated using the impact load magnification factor.  
The static deflection under the weight is used to compute the impact factor.  
The total axial force during impact is the sum of the static axial load and the dynamic impact force.  
The governing equation for maximum stress is σ_max = (W + F_dynamic) / A, where F_dynamic = W × √(1 + 2h/δ_st).  
The static deflection δ_st = (W × L) / (A × E).  
The maximum strain is ε_max = σ_max / E.  
The maximum displacement during impact is approximately the static deflection multiplied by the impact factor.  
Damping and energy loss during impact are neglected.  
Wave propagation effects are neglected.  
The axial load is co-linear with the rod axis.  
Gravity acts along the axis of the rod.  
The rod mass is negligible compared to the impact mass.
