The structural member is a rectangular prismatic bar with width b = 60 mm and height h = 100 mm.  
The cross-sectional area is A = b × h = 6000 mm².  
The material is assumed to be structural steel with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.3.  
The member is subjected to an axial tensile force P = 40000 N, producing a uniform normal stress σ_x = P/A.  
The member is subjected to a transverse shear force V = 16000 N acting parallel to the width (b-direction), assumed uniformly distributed over the cross-section.  
The average shear stress is τ_xy = V/A.  
Plane stress conditions are assumed, with non-zero stresses σ_x and τ_xy, and all other stress components zero.  
The normal stress σ_x is calculated as 6.67 MPa.  
The average shear stress τ_xy is calculated as 2.67 MPa.  
Principal stresses are determined using the formula: σ_1,2 = (σ_x / 2) ± √((σ_x / 2)^2 + τ_xy^2).  
The maximum in-plane shear stress is calculated as τ_max = √((σ_x / 2)^2 + τ_xy^2).  
Principal strains are calculated using Hooke’s law for plane stress: ε_1 = (σ_1 − ν σ_2)/E and ε_2 = (σ_2 − ν σ_1)/E.  
Out-of-plane strain ε_z is calculated as ε_z = −ν (σ_x)/E.  
All deformations are assumed to be within the linear-elastic range.  
Small deformation theory is applied.  
Stress and strain states are uniform across the member length.
