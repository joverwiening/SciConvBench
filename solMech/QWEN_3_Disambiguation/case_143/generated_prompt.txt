Assume the material is structural steel with Young's modulus of 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio of 0.3.  
The shaft has a diameter of 25 mm.  
The applied torque is 400000 N·mm.  
The applied axial load is 15000 N.  
The material behavior is linear-elastic and small deformations are assumed.  
The stress state is analyzed at the outer surface of the shaft where maximum stresses occur.  
The axial stress is calculated using σ_axial = F / A, where F is the axial load and A is the cross-sectional area.  
The shear stress due to torsion is calculated using τ_torsion = T * r / J, where T is the torque, r is the outer radius, and J is the polar moment of inertia.  
The cross-sectional area A is π * (d/2)².  
The polar moment of inertia J is π * (d/2)⁴ / 2.  
The principal stresses are determined from the plane stress transformation equations using σ_axial and τ_torsion.  
The maximum in-plane shear stress is calculated using τ_max = √((σ_axial/2)² + τ_torsion²).  
The principal strains are calculated using Hooke’s law for plane stress: ε₁ = (σ₁ - ν * σ₂) / E and ε₂ = (σ₂ - ν * σ₁) / E.  
The analysis assumes no stress concentrations and uniform load distribution across the cross-section.
