Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
Assume an Euler-Bernoulli beam model.  
The beam is simply supported with a span of 3500 mm.  
The beam is subjected to a uniformly distributed transverse load of 0.3 N/mm applied over the full span.  
The beam is subjected to a downward point load of 1200 N applied at midspan (1750 mm from either support).  
Determine the deflection using the method of superposition.  
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.  
The cross-section is symmetric and prismatic along the span.  
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after deformation.  
Axial and shear deformations are negligible.  
The governing differential equation for beam deflection is $ \frac{d^2}{dx^2}\left(EI \frac{d^2w}{dx^2}\right) = q(x) $.  
The total deflection is the sum of deflections due to the distributed load and the point load acting separately.  
The flexural rigidity $ EI $ is constant along the span.  
Boundary conditions are: deflection is zero at both supports.  
Continuity of deflection and slope is maintained at all points along the span.
