Use linear-elastic small-deformation solid mechanics.  
Assume plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).  
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic.  
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.  
The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section, located at mid-height.  
The second moment of area (moment of inertia) about the neutral axis is I = (b × h³)/12.  
The internal bending moment is M = 4,000,000 N·mm, positive and sagging, causing compression on the top fiber and tension on the bottom fiber.  
Bending stress at a fiber located at a distance y from the neutral axis is given by σ = (M × y) / I.  
The distance y is measured positive upward from the neutral axis.  
Compute bending stress at any arbitrary fiber location using the above relation.
