Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
Assume an Euler-Bernoulli beam model.  
The beam is simply supported with a span of 3000 mm.  
The beam is subjected to a uniformly distributed transverse load of 0.3 N/mm acting downward over the full span.  
The beam is also subjected to a point load of 1200 N acting downward at midspan (1500 mm from either support).  
Determine the deflection of the beam using the method of superposition.  
Use the principle of superposition to compute total deflection as the sum of deflections due to the distributed load and the point load acting separately.  
For the uniformly distributed load, use the standard deflection formula for a simply supported beam under full-span uniform load.  
For the point load, use the standard deflection formula for a simply supported beam with a midspan point load.  
Assume the beam has constant flexural rigidity (EI) along its length.  
Deflection is to be expressed as a function of position along the beam or evaluated at critical locations such as midspan.  
Neglect the weight of the beam unless already included in the loads.  
All loads act in the transverse vertical plane.
