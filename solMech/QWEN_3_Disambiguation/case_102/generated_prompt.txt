Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The rod is made of AISI 1020 steel with a diameter of 25 mm, giving a cross-sectional area of 490.87 mm².
The length of the rod is 800 mm.
The rod is initially unloaded and oriented vertically, fixed at the bottom end.
A weight of 1500 N (mass ≈ 152.9 kg) is dropped from a height of 200 mm onto the top of the rod, causing axial impact loading.
The impact is perfectly plastic, and the weight remains in contact with the rod after impact.
The response is one-dimensional axial deformation along the rod’s length.
Neglect wave propagation effects and assume uniform stress distribution during impact.
Use conservation of energy to equate the potential energy of the falling mass to the strain energy stored in the rod.
The maximum axial force, stress, and deflection in the rod are to be determined.
The elastic modulus of AISI 1020 steel is 200 GPa.
The acceleration due to gravity is 9.81 m/s².
The maximum dynamic stress and deflection occur at the point of peak strain energy storage.
The governing equation is: mgh = (1/2) * (F_max² * L) / (A * E).
Solve for F_max, the maximum axial force in the rod.
Compute maximum stress as σ_max = F_max / A.
Compute maximum deflection as δ_max = (F_max * L) / (A * E).
