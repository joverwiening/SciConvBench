Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics theory.
Assume the beam is an Euler-Bernoulli beam.
The beam is simply supported with a length of 3000 mm.
A point load of 1500 N is applied at the midspan.
There is no shear loading applied to the beam.
The cross-sectional moment of inertia (I) is 3,500,000 mm⁴.
The elastic modulus (E) of the beam material is 200,000 MPa.
The flexural rigidity (EI) is calculated as the product of E and I.
The deflection at midspan is calculated using the formula: (P * L³) / (48 * E * I), where P is the point load, L is the beam length, E is the elastic modulus, and I is the moment of inertia.
The slope at each support is calculated using the formula: (P * L²) / (16 * E * I).
All units are consistent (forces in N, lengths in mm, E in MPa, I in mm⁴, resulting in deflection in mm and slope in radians).
