Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The circular shaft has a diameter of 45 mm.  
The applied bending moment is 2,000,000 N·mm, acting such that it induces maximum bending stress at the outer surface, perpendicular to the shaft axis.  
The applied torsional moment is 500,000 N·mm.  
The material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic.  
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the shaft axis after deformation.  
Deformations are small, and shear effects due to bending are negligible.  
The shaft is prismatic and solid with a circular cross-section.  
The bending moment causes uniaxial normal stress at the outer surface.  
The torsional moment causes pure shear stress at the outer surface.  
The maximum bending stress is calculated using σ_bending = (M * c) / I, where M is the bending moment, c is the outer radius, and I is the area moment of inertia.  
The maximum shear stress due to torsion is calculated using τ_torsion = (T * c) / J, where T is the torsional moment, c is the outer radius, and J is the polar moment of inertia.  
For a solid circular shaft, I = (π * d⁴) / 64 and J = (π * d⁴) / 32, where d is the diameter.  
The outer radius c = d / 2.  
At the critical point on the surface, the state of stress is biaxial: normal stress σ_x = σ_bending and shear stress τ_xy = τ_torsion.  
Principal stresses are determined using σ_1, σ_2 = (σ_x / 2) ± √((σ_x / 2)² + τ_xy²).  
The maximum in-plane shear stress is τ_max = √((σ_x / 2)² + τ_xy²).  
Principal strains are calculated using Hooke’s law for plane stress: ε_1 = (σ_1 - ν * σ_2) / E, ε_2 = (σ_2 - ν * σ_1) / E, assuming material modulus E and Poisson’s ratio ν.  
The material properties E and ν are required for strain computation; if not provided, use typical values for steel (E = 200 GPa, ν = 0.3).  
The critical response quantities include maximum bending stress, maximum shear stress due to torsion, principal stresses, maximum shear stress, and principal strains.  
Stress concentrations are neglected.  
The analysis is static and no dynamic or fatigue effects are considered.
