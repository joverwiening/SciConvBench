Use linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics.  
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and behaves elastically under loading.  
Assume the shaft is prismatic with a constant hollow circular cross-section along its length.  
The outer radius of the shaft is 30 mm.  
The inner radius of the shaft is 22 mm.  
The length of the shaft is 1200 mm.  
A torque of 600,000 N·mm is applied about the shaft’s longitudinal axis.  
The shear modulus of the material is required and assumed to be known.  
The polar moment of inertia is calculated as J = (π/2) * (ro⁴ - ri⁴), where ro = 30 mm and ri = 22 mm.  
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface and is given by τ_max = T * ro / J.  
The angle of twist at the free end relative to the fixed end is given by θ = (T * L) / (G * J), assuming one end is fixed.  
The governing equilibrium equation is dT/dz = 0 for torsion in the absence of distributed torque.  
The constitutive relation is τ = G * γ, where τ is shear stress and γ is shear strain.  
The kinematic relation is γ = r * dθ/dz, where r is the radial distance from the center.
