Use linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics.  
Assume homogeneous, isotropic material behavior.  
Assume the axial load is applied uniformly along the axis of the specimen.  
The initial diameter of the specimen is 12.5 mm, so the initial cross-sectional area is A₀ = π/4 × (12.5)² ≈ 122.72 mm².  
The initial gauge length is L₀ = 50 mm.  
The applied axial load is 12,000 N.  
Engineering stress is calculated as σ_eng = F / A₀.  
Engineering strain is defined as ε_eng = ΔL / L₀, where ΔL is the change in gauge length.  
The elastic modulus E is the slope of the linear-elastic portion of the engineering stress–strain curve.  
The governing relation for axial deformation under load is σ_eng = E × ε_eng, assuming uniaxial stress state.  
Compute engineering stress: σ_eng = 12,000 N / 122.72 mm² ≈ 97.78 MPa.  
Report the engineering stress–strain response up to the applied load.  
The modulus E must be provided or determined from experimental data or material properties.
