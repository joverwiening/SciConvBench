Use a diameter of 40 mm and a total length of 1500 mm for the shaft.  
Fix the shaft at x = 0.  
Apply a torque T1 = 300,000 N·mm at the free end (x = 1500 mm) in the positive direction.  
Apply a torque T2 = -150,000 N·mm at x = 750 mm in the negative direction.  
Use the material 6061-T6 aluminum with shear modulus G = 25,939.8 MPa.  
Use N–mm–MPa units throughout the analysis.  
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.  
Assume the shaft is prismatic and made of a homogeneous, isotropic material.  
Assume torsional loading produces pure shear stress and angular deformation governed by the torsion formula.  
The shear stress at any cross-section is given by τ = (T × r) / J, where T is the net internal torque, r is the radial distance from the center, and J is the polar moment of inertia.  
The polar moment of inertia J is calculated as J = π × d⁴ / 32.  
The angle of twist at any section relative to the fixed end is given by θ = (T × L) / (G × J), using superposition for multiple torques.  
The net internal torque in each segment (0–750 mm and 750–1500 mm) is determined by free-body analysis.  
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface (r = d/2).  
The angular deflection at the free end is the sum of the twists in each segment due to T1 and T2.
