Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The shaft is a solid circular bar with diameter 35 mm.  
The shaft is made of AISI 1020 steel with shear modulus G = 76923.1 MPa.  
The applied torque is 53,050 N·mm.  
The rotational speed is 1800 rpm, corresponding to angular velocity ω = 188.496 rad/s.  
Power is related to torque and angular velocity by P = T × ω.  
The polar moment of inertia J is calculated as J = π × (d/2)^4 / 2 = π × (17.5)^4 / 2 = 147,262.5 mm⁴.  
The maximum shear stress due to torsion is τ_max = T × c / J, where c = d/2 = 17.5 mm.  
The maximum shear stress is τ_max = (53,050 N·mm) × (17.5 mm) / 147,262.5 mm⁴ ≈ 6.30 MPa.  
The angle of twist per unit length is θ/L = T / (G × J).  
The torsional rigidity is G × J = 76923.1 MPa × 147,262.5 mm⁴ ≈ 1.133 × 10^10 N·mm².  
The angle of twist per meter length is θ = (53,050 N·mm) / (1.133 × 10^10 N·mm²) × (1000 mm/m) ≈ 0.00468 radians/m ≈ 0.268 degrees/m.  
Perform torsion strength check by comparing τ_max to the allowable shear stress of AISI 1020 steel.  
Perform stiffness check by evaluating whether the angle of twist is within acceptable limits for the application.  
Assume no axial or bending loads are present; only pure torsion and shear are considered.  
Shear loading is assumed to be negligible compared to torsional shear stress unless specified otherwise.
