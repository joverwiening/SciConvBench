Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The stepped circular bar consists of two segments in series under uniaxial tension.  
Segment 1 has length L1 = 400 mm and diameter d1 = 16 mm.  
Segment 2 has length L2 = 800 mm and diameter d2 = 40 mm.  
The applied tensile load P = 35000 N is constant across both segments.  
The material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linear-elastic with Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa (assumed typical steel unless otherwise specified).  
Stress in each segment is calculated as σ = P / A, where A is the cross-sectional area of the segment.  
Cross-sectional area A1 = π(d1/2)² = π(8 mm)².  
Cross-sectional area A2 = π(d2/2)² = π(20 mm)².  
Axial stress in Segment 1 is σ1 = P / A1.  
Axial stress in Segment 2 is σ2 = P / A2.  
Total elongation is the sum of elongations in each segment: Δ = Δ1 + Δ2.  
Elongation in each segment is Δi = (P × Li) / (Ai × E), for i = 1, 2.  
The governing equations are based on uniaxial stress assumption and compatibility of displacement (equal force, additive deformation).  
Stress concentration effects at the step are neglected (assumed gradual transition or no geometric singularity).  
The load is applied axially and statically, with no bending or shear components.
