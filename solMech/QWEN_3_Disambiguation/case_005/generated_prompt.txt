Use a prismatic bar with a constant cross-sectional area of 200 mm².  
Use a material with a Young's modulus of 200000 MPa.  
The bar has a total length of 1800 mm, divided into segments AB = 700 mm, BC = 600 mm, and CD = 500 mm.  
Apply axial point loads as follows: 18000 N at point B (700 mm from A), -8000 N at point C (1300 mm from A), and 20000 N at point D (1800 mm from A).  
Define positive axial direction to the right.  
Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behavior.  
Assume the bar is fixed at end A.  
Compute axial force distribution in each segment by summing applied loads from the fixed end.  
Use the formula for axial deformation: δ = (P × L) / (A × E) for each segment, where P is the internal axial force, L is the segment length, A is the cross-sectional area, and E is Young's modulus.  
Sum deformations of all segments to obtain total elongation of the bar.  
Report internal axial forces in segments AB, BC, and CD.  
Report axial deformations in segments AB, BC, and CD.  
Report total axial deformation of the bar from A to D.
