Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The beam is simply supported with a length of 3500 mm.
The beam is subjected to a uniformly distributed transverse load of 0.5 N/mm acting downward over the entire span.
The elastic modulus (E) of the beam material is 200,000 MPa.
The cross-sectional moment of inertia (I) is 1,500,000 mm⁴.
The governing differential equation for beam deflection is E * I * (d⁴v/dx⁴) = w, where v is the transverse deflection, x is the axial coordinate, and w is the distributed load intensity.
The reaction forces at the supports are each equal to (w * L) / 2, where w = 0.5 N/mm and L = 3500 mm.
The slope is obtained by integrating the moment equation divided by E * I.
The deflection is obtained by applying the boundary conditions: v = 0 at x = 0 and x = L.
The maximum deflection occurs at the midspan and is given by (5 * w * L⁴) / (384 * E * I).
The slope at the supports is given by (w * L³) / (24 * E * I) and is equal in magnitude but opposite in sign at each end.
