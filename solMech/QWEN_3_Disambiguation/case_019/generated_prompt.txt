Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The shaft is a solid circular cylinder with a diameter of 30 mm and a total length of 1200 mm.  
The shaft is fixed at x = 0 mm, preventing all displacement and rotation at that end.  
A torque T1 = 200,000 N·mm is applied at x = 1200 mm in the positive (counterclockwise) direction.  
A torque T2 = -200,000 N·mm is applied at x = 400 mm in the negative (clockwise) direction.  
The material is AISI 1020 steel with a shear modulus G = 76,923.1 MPa.  
All loads are static and applied about the shaft’s longitudinal (x) axis.  
The shaft is prismatic and homogeneous along its length.  
Deformations are purely due to torsion; no axial or transverse loads are present.  
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the axis during deformation.  
The polar moment of inertia J is constant and calculated as J = πd⁴/32, where d = 30 mm.  
The torsional rigidity is GJ.  
The angle of twist at any point x is computed using the stepped-torque method with superposition.  
The internal torque is piecewise constant: T(x) = 0 for 0 ≤ x < 400 mm, T(x) = -200,000 N·mm for 400 ≤ x < 1200 mm, and T(x) = 0 for x = 1200 mm due to equilibrium.  
The maximum shear stress occurs where the internal torque is maximum and is calculated using τ_max = T·r/J, where r = 15 mm.  
The angle of twist at any location x relative to the fixed end is given by the integral of T(x)/(GJ) dx from 0 to x.  
The net applied torque is zero, so the shaft is in static equilibrium.  
The response quantities include the internal torque distribution, maximum shear stress, and angular deflection along the shaft.  
Use N–mm–MPa units throughout all calculations.
