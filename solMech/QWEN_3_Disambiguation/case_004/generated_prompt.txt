Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The prismatic bar has a constant cross-sectional area of 300 mm².  
The material's Young's modulus is 200000 MPa.  
The total length of the bar is 1500 mm.  
The left end A is fixed, meaning zero displacement at A.  
Point loads are applied as follows:  
A force of 18,000 N is applied at point B, located 500 mm from A, in the positive (rightward) axial direction.  
A force of -12,000 N is applied at point C, located 1100 mm from A, in the negative (leftward) axial direction.  
A force of 16,000 N is applied at point D, located 1500 mm from A, in the positive (rightward) axial direction.  
The bar is divided into segments AB, BC, and CD based on the locations of the applied loads.  
Axial force in each segment is determined by summing the forces to the left of the segment.  
The axial stress in each segment is calculated as the axial force divided by the cross-sectional area.  
The axial strain in each segment is calculated as the axial stress divided by Young's modulus.  
The elongation of each segment is calculated as the axial strain multiplied by the segment length.  
The displacement at each point is calculated cumulatively from the fixed end A, summing the elongations of preceding segments.  
Governing equations:  
Axial force: N(x) = sum of external forces to the left of x.  
Axial stress: σ(x) = N(x) / A.  
Axial strain: ε(x) = σ(x) / E.  
Elongation of segment: ΔL = ε(x) × L_segment.  
Displacement at a point: u(x) = sum of elongations from A to x.  
All deformations are positive in extension, and the coordinate system is aligned with the bar from A to D.
