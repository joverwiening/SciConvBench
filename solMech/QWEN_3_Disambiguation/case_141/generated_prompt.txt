Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The circular shaft has a diameter of 35 mm.  
A bending moment of 3,000,000 N·mm is applied to the shaft.  
A torsional moment of 500,000 N·mm is applied to the shaft.  
The material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic.  
Plane stress conditions are assumed at the critical point on the surface of the shaft.  
The critical location for stress analysis is at the outer surface of the shaft where maximum bending and torsional stresses occur.  
The bending stress is calculated using σ_bending = (M * c) / I, where M is the bending moment, c is the radius, I is the second moment of area.  
The torsional shear stress is calculated using τ_torsion = (T * c) / J, where T is the torsional moment, c is the radius, J is the polar moment of inertia.  
For a solid circular shaft, I = (π * d⁴) / 64 and J = (π * d⁴) / 32.  
The radius c = d / 2 = 17.5 mm.  
The normal stress due to bending acts along the axial direction.  
The shear stress due to torsion acts in the circumferential-axial plane.  
Principal stresses are determined using σ₁,₂ = (σ_x / 2) ± √((σ_x / 2)² + τ_xy²), where σ_x is the bending stress and τ_xy is the torsional shear stress.  
Maximum in-plane shear stress is calculated as τ_max = √((σ_x / 2)² + τ_xy²).  
Principal strains are calculated using Hooke’s law and the plane stress transformation equations.  
Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.3 and Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa are assumed unless otherwise specified.  
The analysis focuses on combined bending and torsion at the critical section of the shaft.
