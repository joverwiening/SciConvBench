Assume the material is structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.  
Assume plane stress conditions apply due to the prismatic geometry and loading configuration.  
The cross-section of interest is remote from stress concentrations and load application points.  
The axial tension load P = 40000 N acts uniformly over the cross-sectional area.  
The shear force V = 12000 N acts parallel to the width and is assumed to produce average transverse shear stress.  
The cross-sectional area A = b × h = 50 mm × 80 mm = 4000 mm².  
The average normal stress due to axial load is σ_avg = P / A = 40000 N / 4000 mm² = 10 MPa.  
The average shear stress due to shear force is τ_avg = V / A = 12000 N / 4000 mm² = 3 MPa.  
Assume uniform stress distribution for normal stress; use average shear stress for simplicity.  
The in-plane normal stress component in the loading direction is σ_x = 10 MPa.  
The in-plane shear stress component is τ_xy = 3 MPa.  
The other in-plane normal stress component is σ_y = 0 (plane stress condition).  
The principal stresses are calculated using the formula: σ_1,2 = (σ_x + σ_y)/2 ± √[((σ_x − σ_y)/2)² + τ_xy²].  
Substituting values: σ_1,2 = (10 + 0)/2 ± √[((10 − 0)/2)² + 3²] = 5 ± √[25 + 9] = 5 ± √34.  
Thus, σ_1 = 5 + √34 ≈ 10.83 MPa, σ_2 = 5 − √34 ≈ −0.83 MPa.  
The maximum in-plane shear stress is τ_max = √[((σ_x − σ_y)/2)² + τ_xy²] = √[25 + 9] = √34 ≈ 5.83 MPa.  
The principal strains are calculated using Hooke’s law for plane stress: ε_1 = (σ_1 − ν σ_2)/E, ε_2 = (σ_2 − ν σ_1)/E.  
Substituting values: ε_1 = (10.83 − 0.3×(−0.83)) / 200000 ≈ (10.83 + 0.249) / 200000 ≈ 5.54 × 10⁻⁵.  
ε_2 = (−0.83 − 0.3×10.83) / 200000 ≈ (−0.83 − 3.249) / 200000 ≈ −2.04 × 10⁻⁵.  
Out-of-plane strain ε_z is non-zero due to Poisson effect but not a principal in-plane strain.  
The state of stress is fully described by σ_x = 10 MPa, σ_y = 0, τ_xy = 3 MPa.  
The state of strain is derived from stress using material properties and plane stress assumptions.  
All calculations assume linear elasticity, homogeneous isotropic material, and small deformations.
