Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics theory.  
Assume Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies.  
The beam is a cantilever with fixed support at the left end and free at the right end.  
The length of the beam is 800 mm.  
A downward point load of 2500 N is applied at the free end.  
No moment is applied at the free end.  
The cross-sectional moment of inertia (I) is 1,600,000 mm⁴.  
The elastic modulus (E) of 6061-T6 aluminum is 69,000 MPa.  
The flexural rigidity (EI) is constant along the beam.  
The governing differential equation for beam deflection is: E I d⁴v/dx⁴ = 0 for 0 < x < L.  
The shear force is constant and equal to -2500 N along the beam.  
The bending moment varies linearly: M(x) = -2500 (L - x) N·mm.  
The slope of the deflection curve is zero at the fixed end (x = 0).  
The deflection is zero at the fixed end (x = 0).  
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end (x = L).  
The maximum deflection is given by: v_max = (P L³) / (3 E I).  
The maximum deflection is 1.92 mm downward.  
The maximum slope at the free end is: θ_max = (P L²) / (2 E I).  
All deformations are within the elastic limit.  
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis.  
Axial and shear deformations are negligible.
