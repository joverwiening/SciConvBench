The shaft is a solid circular bar with a diameter of 35 mm and a total length of 1200 mm.
The shaft is fixed at the left end (x = 0), preventing rotation at that support.
A torque T1 = 200000 N·mm is applied at the free end (x = 1200 mm) in the positive (counterclockwise) direction.
A torque T2 = -250000 N·mm is applied at x = 800 mm in the negative (clockwise) direction.
The material is structural steel with a shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa.
The analysis assumes linear-elastic behavior and small deformations.
The torsional response is governed by the equation for angle of twist: φ(x) = ∫(T(x)/(G·J)) dx, where J is the polar moment of inertia.
The polar moment of inertia J is calculated as J = π·(d/2)^4/2 = π·(35/2)^4/2 mm⁴.
The internal torque distribution T(x) is piecewise constant, determined by free-body diagrams between the applied torques.
The reaction torque at the fixed end is equal to the sum of applied torques: T_reaction = - (T1 + T2) = 50000 N·mm.
The maximum shear stress occurs where the internal torque is largest in magnitude and is calculated using τ_max = T·r/J, where r = d/2.
The angle of twist at any location is calculated by integrating the twist rate over each segment with constant torque.
The analysis assumes uniform cross-section and homogeneous material properties along the shaft length.
The sign convention follows the right-hand rule, with positive torque causing counterclockwise rotation when viewed from the right.
