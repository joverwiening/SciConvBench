Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics theory.  
The cantilever beam has a length of 1500 mm.  
The beam is subjected to a downward point load of 500 N at the free end.  
There is no applied bending moment at the free end.  
The cross-sectional moment of inertia is 2,500,000 mm⁴.  
The elastic modulus (Young's modulus) of the material is 69,000 MPa.  
Use Castigliano’s theorem to determine the vertical deflection at the free end of the cantilever beam.  
The deflection is due solely to the applied point load.  
The beam is fixed at the support end and free at the loaded end.  
Neglect the effects of shear deformation and axial load.  
The governing equation for strain energy in bending is U = ∫(M²/(2EI)) dx from 0 to L.  
The bending moment at a distance x from the fixed end is M(x) = -P(L - x), where P = 500 N.  
The deflection at the free end is obtained by taking the partial derivative of the strain energy with respect to the point load.  
The theoretical deflection at the free end is δ = PL³/(3EI).
