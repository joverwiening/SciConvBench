Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.  
The bar has a linearly varying cross-sectional area given by A(x) = A₀·(1 + k·x/L), where A₀ = 200 mm², k = 0.6, and L = 1200 mm.  
The axial load is a constant tensile force P = 20000 N applied at the free end, with the other end fixed.  
The material is homogeneous and isotropic with constant Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa.  
The axial deformation is computed under the assumption of uniaxial stress state and negligible lateral effects.  
The governing differential equation for axial displacement u(x) is d/dx[E·A(x)·du/dx] = 0.  
The boundary conditions are u(0) = 0 (fixed end) and E·A(L)·du/dx|ₓ₌ₗ = P (force equilibrium at free end).  
The axial stress σ(x) is given by σ(x) = P / A(x).  
The axial strain ε(x) is given by ε(x) = σ(x) / E.  
The displacement u(x) is obtained by integrating strain: u(x) = ∫₀ˣ ε(ξ) dξ.  
The total elongation ΔL is u(L) = ∫₀ᴸ [P / (E·A(ξ))] dξ.  
Evaluate the integral analytically or numerically using the given A(x).  
Report the displacement field u(x), total elongation ΔL, stress distribution σ(x), and strain distribution ε(x).
