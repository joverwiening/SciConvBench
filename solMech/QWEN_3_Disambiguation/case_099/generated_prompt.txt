Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The beam is a cantilever with a length of 2000 mm.
The only applied load is a uniformly distributed transverse load of magnitude 1.0 N/mm acting downward.
The cross-sectional moment of inertia (I) of the beam is 1,500,000 mm⁴.
The Young's modulus (E) of the beam material is 200,000 MPa.
The governing differential equation for beam deflection is E * I * (d⁴w/dx⁴) = q(x), where w is the transverse deflection, x is the axial coordinate, and q(x) = 1.0 N/mm is the uniform load.
The boundary conditions are w(0) = 0 and dw/dx(0) = 0 at the fixed end (x = 0).
The deflection along the beam is given by w(x) = (q * x²) / (24 * E * I) * (6 * L² - 4 * L * x + x²), where L = 2000 mm.
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end (x = L) and is w(L) = (q * L⁴) / (8 * E * I).
Calculate the deflection at any point x along the beam using the given formula.
Report the maximum deflection at the free end.
