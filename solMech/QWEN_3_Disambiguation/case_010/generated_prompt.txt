The composite bar consists of two parallel bars connected to two rigid plates at both ends.  
An axial tensile force of 30,000 N is applied to the top rigid plate, with the bottom plate fixed.  
Bar 1 is made of structural steel with a cross-sectional area A₁ = 150 mm² and Young's modulus E₁ = 200,000 MPa.  
Bar 2 is made of cast iron with a cross-sectional area A₂ = 200 mm² and Young's modulus E₂ = 110,000 MPa.  
The deformation is small and within the linear-elastic range.  
The two bars share the applied load in proportion to their axial stiffnesses.  
The total applied force is the sum of the forces carried by each bar.  
The elongation of both bars is equal due to compatibility enforced by the rigid end plates.  
The force in each bar is calculated using the stiffness ratio based on (E₁A₁) and (E₂A₂).  
The stress in each bar is the force divided by its cross-sectional area.  
The strain in each bar is the same and equal to the elongation divided by the original length.  
The governing equations are:  
F₁ + F₂ = 30,000 N (force equilibrium)  
Δ₁ = Δ₂ → (F₁L)/(E₁A₁) = (F₂L)/(E₂A₂) (compatibility of deformation)  
Stresses: σ₁ = F₁/A₁, σ₂ = F₂/A₂  
Strains: ε = Δ/L = σ₁/E₁ = σ₂/E₂
