Assume the beam has a rectangular cross-section, as this is a common and analytically tractable shape in basic beam theory.

  
Use small-deformation theory.  
Assume linear-elastic material behavior.  
Assume the beam has a rectangular cross-section.  
The material follows Hooke’s law with constant Young’s modulus.  
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis.  
The beam is prismatic with constant cross-section along its length.  
Bending moment is applied about the principal axis of the cross-section.  
Curvature is uniform over the length of interest.  
The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section.  
The second moment of area is calculated about the neutral axis.  
The bending moment M is related to curvature κ by κ = M / (E * I).  
The radius of curvature ρ is the reciprocal of curvature: ρ = 1 / κ.  
Curvature κ is in units of 1/m.  
Radius of curvature ρ is in meters.  
Bending moment M is in N·m.  
Young’s modulus E is in Pa.  
Second moment of area I is in m⁴.
