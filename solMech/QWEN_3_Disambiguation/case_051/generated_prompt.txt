Use the standard formula for transverse shear stress in a rectangular beam cross-section.  
Assume linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics with plane sections remaining plane.  
Assume no axial load is present and bending occurs about the principal centroidal axis.  
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 50 mm and height h = 80 mm.  
The internal shear force at the section of interest is V = 20000 N.  
Calculate the transverse shear stress at a point located y = 35 mm from the neutral axis, with y positive upward.  
The neutral axis is located at the centroid of the cross-section.  
The moment of inertia I of the cross-section is calculated as I = (b * h^3) / 12.  
The first moment of area Q about the neutral axis for the area above y = 35 mm is computed as Q = b * (h/2 - y) * [(h/2 + y)/2 + (h/2 - y)/2].  
Simplify Q as Q = b * (h/2 - y) * (h/4 + y/2).  
Use the transverse shear stress formula τ = (V * Q) / (I * b).  
Ensure all units are consistent: convert mm to meters for stress in Pa, or maintain mm and report stress in MPa.  
The transverse shear stress distribution is parabolic, with maximum at the neutral axis and zero at the top and bottom surfaces.  
Report the shear stress value at y = 35 mm.
