Use structural steel as the material for the beam.

Young's modulus is 200 GPa.

Poisson's ratio is 0.3.

The beam is a rectangular prismatic member with width b = 40 mm and height h = 120 mm.

The axial tension load P = 40,000 N acts along the longitudinal axis of the beam.

The shear load V = 8,000 N acts vertically, parallel to the height of the cross-section.

Stresses are uniformly distributed across the cross-section.

Plane stress conditions are assumed, with non-zero stress components in the plane defined by the axial and vertical directions.

The average normal stress due to axial load is calculated as P divided by the cross-sectional area.

The average shear stress due to transverse load is calculated as V divided by the cross-sectional area.

The in-plane normal stress component is aligned with the axial direction.

The in-plane shear stress component arises from the vertical shear force.

Principal stresses are determined from the plane stress state using the stress transformation equations.

The maximum and minimum principal stresses are computed based on the normal and shear stress components.

Principal angles are calculated to determine the orientation of the principal planes.

Engineering strain is computed using Hooke's law under plane stress conditions.

The longitudinal strain is derived from the normal stress divided by Young's modulus.

The transverse strain is computed considering Poisson's effect.

Shear strain is calculated as the shear stress divided by the shear modulus.

Shear modulus is derived from Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio.

The state of stress is two-dimensional with zero out-of-plane stress components.

The analysis assumes linear-elastic behavior and small deformations.
