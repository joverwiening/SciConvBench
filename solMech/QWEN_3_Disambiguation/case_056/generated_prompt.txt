Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior for the bar.  
The left end of the bar at x = 0 is fixed, implying zero displacement at x = 0.  
The cross-sectional area varies linearly as A(x) = A₀·(1 + k·x/L), where A₀ = 250 mm², k = 0.8, and L = 800 mm.  
A constant tensile force P = 30000 N is applied at the right end of the bar at x = L.  
The material is homogeneous and isotropic with constant Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa.  
The axial deformation δ(x) is governed by the differential equation: d/dx [E·A(x)·du(x)/dx] = 0.  
The boundary conditions are: u(0) = 0 and E·A(L)·du(L)/dx = P.  
The axial strain is ε(x) = du(x)/dx.  
The axial stress is σ(x) = E·ε(x).  
The internal axial force is N(x) = σ(x)·A(x).  
The displacement field u(x) is obtained by integrating the strain field: u(x) = ∫₀ˣ ε(ξ) dξ.  
The total elongation of the bar is δ = u(L).  
All deformations are elastic and within the proportional limit.  
The bar is prismatic in shape with area variation only along the axial direction.  
Self-weight of the bar is negligible compared to the applied load.
