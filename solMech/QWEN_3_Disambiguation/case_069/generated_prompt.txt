Assume the beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b and height h, as this is a common and analytically tractable shape for bending problems.

  
Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
Assume the beam is made of a homogeneous, isotropic material.  
Assume the beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b and height h.  
Assume plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).  
The bending moment M is applied about the neutral axis.  
The material obeys Hooke’s law with Young’s modulus E.  
The curvature κ is related to the bending moment by κ = M / (E * I).  
The second moment of area I for the rectangular cross-section is I = (b * h³) / 12.  
The radius of curvature ρ is the reciprocal of curvature: ρ = 1 / κ.  
Curvature κ and radius of curvature ρ are valid within the elastic limit of the material.  
Deformations are small, so the radius of curvature is much larger than the beam length.
