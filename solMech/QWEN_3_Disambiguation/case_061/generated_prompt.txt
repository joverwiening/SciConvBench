Assume the column is made of structural steel with a Young's modulus of 200 GPa and a Poisson's ratio of 0.3.  

  
Perform analysis under small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics assumptions.  
The rectangular column has a height h = 120 mm (vertical dimension) and width b = 60 mm (horizontal dimension).  
The column is oriented vertically, with loading applied parallel to the height.  
The eccentric axial load is a compressive force of magnitude P = 60,000 N.  
The load is offset from the centroid by an eccentricity e = 20 mm in the width direction (horizontal).  
The eccentricity causes bending about the centroidal z-axis.  
The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 60 mm and height h = 120 mm.  
The moment of inertia about the z-axis is calculated as I_z = (b * h^3) / 12.  
The maximum bending moment due to eccentricity is M = P * e.  
The axial stress due to compression is uniform and calculated as σ_axial = -P / (b * h).  
The bending stress varies linearly across the width and is calculated as σ_bending = -M * y / I_z, where y is the distance from the centroid along the width.  
The total normal stress is the sum of axial and bending stresses: σ_total = σ_axial + σ_bending.  
The material is structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3.  
Plane sections remain plane; strain varies linearly with distance from the neutral axis.  
The maximum tensile and compressive stresses occur at the extreme fibers of the cross-section.  
The neutral axis is determined by setting the total normal stress to zero and solving for the location.  
Use standard sign conventions: compression is negative, tension is positive.
