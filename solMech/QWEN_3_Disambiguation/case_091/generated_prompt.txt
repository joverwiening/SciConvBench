The prismatic bar has a constant cross-sectional area of 500 mm².  
The material has a Young's modulus of 200000 MPa.  
The total length of the bar is 1200 mm, with end A fixed.  
Point B is located 300 mm from A, point C is located 900 mm from A, and point D is located 1200 mm from A.  
A point load of 18,000 N is applied at B in the positive axial direction (to the right).  
A point load of -12,000 N is applied at C in the negative axial direction (to the left).  
A point load of 10,000 N is applied at D in the positive axial direction (to the right).  
The problem is analyzed under small-deformation, linear-elastic assumptions.  
Axial force, displacement, and stress are piecewise-constant or piecewise-linear functions between load application points.  
The governing equation for axial deformation is σ = Eε, where σ is axial stress, E is Young's modulus, and ε is axial strain.  
The axial force N(x) at any section is the sum of all external forces to the left of the section.  
Displacement u(x) is computed from u(x) = ∫₀ˣ (N(ξ)/(EA)) dξ, with u(0) = 0 due to fixed end at A.  
Stress is computed as σ(x) = N(x)/A.  
Strain is computed as ε(x) = σ(x)/E.  
The analysis assumes no body forces or thermal effects.  
The bar is prismatic and homogeneous, with properties constant along its length.
