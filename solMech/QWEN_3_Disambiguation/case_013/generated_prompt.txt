Use Euler-Bernoulli beam theory with small-deformation and linear-elastic assumptions.  
Assume the beam is prismatic with constant flexural rigidity EI.  
The beam is simply supported at both ends with a total span of 3000 mm.  
A uniformly distributed transverse load of 0.3 N/mm acts downward over the entire span.  
A point load of 3000 N acts downward at midspan (1500 mm from each support).  
Determine the total deflection at any point along the beam using the principle of superposition.  
Compute deflection due to the uniformly distributed load separately using standard beam deflection formulas.  
Compute deflection due to the point load separately using standard beam deflection formulas.  
Sum the deflections from both load cases to obtain the total deflection.  
The governing equation for beam deflection is the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation: d²/dx²(EI d²w/dx²) = q(x).  
Assume no axial load or thermal effects.  
Deflection is measured positive downward.
