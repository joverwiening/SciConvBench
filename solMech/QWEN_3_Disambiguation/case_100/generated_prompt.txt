Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The cantilever beam has a length of 1500 mm.  
The beam is subjected to a downward point load of 500 N at the free end.  
There is no applied bending moment at the free end.  
The cross-sectional moment of inertia is 2,500,000 mm⁴.  
The elastic modulus of the material is 200,000 MPa.  
Use Castigliano’s theorem to determine the vertical deflection at the free end of the cantilever beam.  
The deflection is calculated as the partial derivative of the strain energy with respect to the applied point load.  
The strain energy in the beam is due to bending only.  
The bending moment at a distance x from the fixed end is M(x) = P × (L − x), where P = 500 N and L = 1500 mm.  
The strain energy U = ∫₀ᴸ [M(x)² / (2EI)] dx from 0 to L.  
The deflection δ = ∂U/∂P = ∫₀ᴸ [M(x)/EI] × (∂M(x)/∂P) dx.  
∂M(x)/∂P = (L − x).  
Thus, δ = (1/(EI)) ∫₀ᴸ M(x)(L − x) dx.  
Substitute M(x) = P(L − x) to obtain δ = (P/(EI)) ∫₀ᴸ (L − x)² dx.  
Evaluate the integral to get δ = (P L³) / (3 E I).  
Use consistent units: convert all lengths to mm and forces to N.  
The deflection is downward.
