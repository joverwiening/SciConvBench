Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics theory.  
Assume the bar is prismatic in shape with length L = 800 mm.  
The cross-sectional area varies linearly as A(x) = A₀·(1 + k·x/L), where A₀ = 250 mm², k = 0.6, and x is the position along the bar from x = 0 to x = L.  
The bar is subjected to a constant axial tensile force of 12000 N applied at one end, with the other end fixed.  
The material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic with constant Young’s modulus E (value to be specified if needed).  
Axial deformation is computed under the assumption of uniaxial stress state.  
The axial stress at any point x is given by σ(x) = F / A(x), where F = 12000 N.  
The axial strain at any point x is given by ε(x) = σ(x) / E.  
The total axial elongation ΔL is obtained by integrating strain over the length: ΔL = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x) dx = (F / E) ∫₀ᴸ dx / A(x).  
Substitute A(x) = A₀·(1 + k·x/L) into the integral to compute ΔL.  
The integral evaluates to ΔL = (F / (E·A₀)) · (L / k) · ln(1 + k).  
Use this expression to determine the total deformation when E is known.
