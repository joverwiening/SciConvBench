Use a solid circular shaft with a diameter of 25 mm and a length of 1000 mm.  
Apply a torque of 350000 N·mm about the shaft’s longitudinal axis.  
Assume the material is linear-elastic with isotropic behavior and constant shear modulus G.  
Assume small deformations and no axial or transverse loading.  
Use Castigliano’s theorem to compute the angle of twist at the free end or point of interest.  
The strain energy due to torsion is given by U = ∫₀ᴸ (T² / (2 G J)) dx, where T is the internal torque, G is the shear modulus, J is the polar moment of inertia, and L is the shaft length.  
For a solid circular shaft, J = π d⁴ / 32, where d is the diameter.  
The angle of twist θ is obtained as the partial derivative of strain energy with respect to the applied torque: θ = ∂U/∂T.  
Since torque is constant along the length, θ = (T L) / (G J).  
Report the twist angle in radians.  
Assume G = 79 GPa for steel unless otherwise specified.
