Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The shaft is composed of two segments: AB and BC.  
Segment AB has a diameter of 30 mm and a length of 400 mm.  
Segment BC has a diameter of 30 mm and a length of 700 mm.  
The entire shaft is circular in cross-section and made of a homogeneous, isotropic material.  
The shear modulus G of the material is 77519.4 MPa.  
A torque of 600000 N·mm is applied to the shaft.  
The shaft is subjected only to pure torsion with no axial or bending loads.  
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface of each segment.  
The polar moment of inertia J is calculated as πd⁴/32 for each segment.  
The shear stress τ in each segment is calculated as τ = (T × r) / J, where T is the torque, r is the radius, and J is the polar moment of inertia.  
The angle of twist φ for each segment is calculated as φ = (T × L) / (G × J), where L is the length of the segment.  
The total twist of the shaft is the sum of the twists of segments AB and BC.  
Since both segments have the same diameter, the shear stress is uniform across both.  
The maximum shear stress in the shaft is 113.18 MPa.  
The total twist of the shaft is 0.0142 radians.
