Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The beam has a rectangular cross section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 100 mm.  
The internal bending moment is M = 4,000,000 N·mm, positive in the sagging direction, causing compression above the neutral axis and tension below.  
The material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic.  
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after deformation.  
The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross section.  
The bending stress varies linearly with distance from the neutral axis.  
The maximum compressive stress occurs at the top fiber, and the maximum tensile stress occurs at the bottom fiber.  
The moment of inertia of the cross section about the neutral axis is I = (b * h^3) / 12.  
The bending stress at a fiber located at a distance y from the neutral axis is given by σ = (M * y) / I.  
Use y = 0 at the neutral axis, with positive y upward.  
Compute I using b = 40 mm and h = 100 mm.  
Compute bending stress σ at any arbitrary fiber location y using M = 4,000,000 N·mm and the calculated I.
