Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The beam has a rectangular cross section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 80 mm.  
The internal shear force at the section of interest is V = 25,000 N.  
The transverse shear stress is to be calculated at a point located y = 20 mm from the neutral axis, with y positive upward.  
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.  
Plane sections remain plane, and shear stress is assumed to be uniformly distributed across the width of the beam.  
The neutral axis coincides with the centroidal axis of the cross section.  
The second moment of area (moment of inertia) about the neutral axis is I = (b * h³) / 12.  
The first moment of area Q about the neutral axis for a rectangular section at distance y from the neutral axis is Q = (b / 2) * ((h² / 4) - y²).  
The transverse shear stress τ at a given y is calculated using the formula τ = (V * Q) / (I * b).  
Use V = 25,000 N, b = 0.04 m, h = 0.08 m, y = 0.02 m, and consistent SI units for computation.
