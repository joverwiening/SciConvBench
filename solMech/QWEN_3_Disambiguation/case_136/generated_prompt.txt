Assume the beam has a rectangular cross-section made of structural steel, with a width of 100 mm and a height of 200 mm. The material is isotropic and homogeneous, with a Young’s modulus of 200 GPa and Poisson’s ratio of 0.3. Plane sections remain plane, and shear deformation is negligible. The beam is loaded within the elastic limit, and small deformation theory applies.

The beam is simply supported at both ends A and B, with a span of 6000 mm.

A single downward concentrated vertical load of 20000 N is applied at a point 2000 mm from support A.

The reaction force at support A is calculated by taking moments about support B and equating to zero.

The reaction force at support B is calculated by enforcing vertical force equilibrium.

The internal shear force at any section of the beam is determined by summing the vertical forces to the left or right of the section.

The internal bending moment at any section of the beam is determined by summing the moments about the section from forces on one side.

The shear force is constant between A and the load point, then drops by 20000 N at the load point.

The bending moment varies linearly from zero at A to a maximum at the load point, then decreases linearly to zero at B.

The maximum bending moment occurs at the location of the applied load.

The governing equations are based on static equilibrium: sum of forces in the vertical direction equals zero, and sum of moments about any point equals zero.
