Use linear-elastic small-deformation solid mechanics.
Assume the beam is homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic with constant cross-sectional area.
Assume the material follows Hooke's law with constant Young's modulus.
Assume the beam is initially straight and unstressed.
Assume small deflections such that slope and curvature approximations are valid.
The beam is simply supported at both ends: pinned support at x = 0 and roller support at x = L.
The length of the beam is 4500 mm.
A uniformly distributed transverse load of 0.3 N/mm acts downward (negative y-direction) along the entire span.
The load is statically equivalent to a total force of 1350 N applied at the midspan.
The governing differential equation for beam deflection is: d²/dx²(EI d²v/dx²) = w, where w = 0.3 N/mm.
The bending moment is given by: M(x) = (wLx)/2 - (wx²)/2.
The shear force is given by: V(x) = (wL)/2 - wx.
The slope is obtained by integrating the moment equation: dv/dx = (1/EI) ∫ M(x) dx + C1.
The deflection is obtained by double integration: v(x) = (1/EI) ∫∫ M(x) dx dx + C1x + C2.
Apply boundary conditions: v(0) = 0 and v(L) = 0 to solve for constants C1 and C2.
The maximum deflection occurs at midspan and is given by: v_max = (5wL⁴)/(384EI).
The maximum slope occurs at the supports and is given by: θ_max = (wL³)/(24EI).
Deflection and slope are to be expressed as functions of position x along the beam.
