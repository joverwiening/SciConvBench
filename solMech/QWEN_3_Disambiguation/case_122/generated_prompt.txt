The cross-section is square with width b = 100 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The eccentric axial load of 90,000 N is applied parallel to the column axis.
The eccentricity e = 15 mm is measured along the width direction (x-direction).
The load produces bending about the centroidal z-axis, which is perpendicular to the width.
The load acts in the plane of symmetry along the height (y-direction is vertical, z-axis is out-of-plane).
The material behavior is linear-elastic and small deformations are assumed.
The axial stress due to the centric component of the load is uniformly distributed and calculated as σ_axial = P / A.
The bending stress due to eccentricity is linearly distributed and calculated as σ_bending = (M * y) / I, where M = P * e, y is the distance from the neutral axis, and I is the second moment of area about the z-axis.
The total axial stress at any point in the cross-section is the superposition of axial and bending stresses: σ_total = σ_axial + σ_bending.
The second moment of area for the square cross-section about the z-axis is I_z = (b * h^3) / 12.
The cross-sectional area A = b * h.
The maximum tensile and compressive stresses occur at the extreme fibers along the width direction.
The neutral axis remains coincident with the centroidal z-axis under combined axial and bending loading.
