Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The beam is simply supported with a span length of 2500 mm.  
The only applied load is a uniformly distributed transverse load of magnitude 0.7 N/mm acting downward over the entire span.  
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.  
The cross-section is prismatic and symmetric about the bending plane.  
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after deformation.  
The deflection is governed by the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation.  
The flexural rigidity (EI) is constant along the span.  
The maximum deflection occurs at the midspan and is calculated using the formula: (5 * w * L^4) / (384 * E * I), where w = 0.7 N/mm and L = 2500 mm.  
The slope at each support is calculated using the formula: (w * L^3) / (24 * E * I).  
The maximum slope occurs at the supports and is equal in magnitude but opposite in sign.  
Deflection is measured positive downward.  
All deformations are within the linear-elastic range.
