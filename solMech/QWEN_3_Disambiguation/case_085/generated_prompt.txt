Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The beam is prismatic with a rectangular cross section of width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.
Bending occurs about the centroidal z-axis, which is horizontal and perpendicular to the height, resulting in flexure in the vertical plane.
The material is linear-elastic and isotropic, with no need for specific material properties.
The flexure formula is applicable: σ = - (M y) / I_zz, where σ is the normal stress, M is the bending moment, y is the vertical coordinate from the neutral axis, and I_zz is the second moment of area about the z-axis.
The second moment of area I_zz is calculated as (b h^3) / 12.
Compression, bending, and tension loadings are applied, and the resulting normal stress distribution is linear across the height of the cross section.
The maximum tensile and compressive stresses occur at the top and bottom fibers, respectively.
The neutral axis coincides with the centroidal z-axis, located at mid-height of the cross section.
The analysis is limited to elastic response; plastic deformation is not considered.
Superposition is used to combine stresses from axial loading and bending.
