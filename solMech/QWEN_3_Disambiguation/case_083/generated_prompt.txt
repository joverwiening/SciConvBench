Use a linear-elastic, isotropic material model with Young's modulus of 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio of 0.3.  
Assume small deformations and static loading conditions.  
The shaft is made of a homogeneous and continuous material.  
The cross-section of the shaft is perfectly circular and uniform along its length.  
The applied loads are steady and act along the geometric axis for axial load and about the longitudinal axis for torque.  
The response quantities of interest are axial stress, shear stress due to torsion, total stress state, principal stresses, principal strains, and equivalent von Mises stress.  
Axial stress is calculated as force divided by cross-sectional area.  
Shear stress due to torsion is calculated using the torsion formula: shear stress = (torque × radius) / polar moment of inertia.  
The stress state at a point on the surface is described by a 2D plane stress condition with axial stress in the longitudinal direction and shear stress due to torsion.  
Principal stresses are computed from the stress transformation equations for plane stress.  
Principal strains are derived from Hooke’s law using the principal stresses and material properties.  
Von Mises stress is calculated from the principal stresses to assess yielding under combined loading.  
The governing equations include equilibrium, Hooke’s law, and kinematic relations for small deformations.
