Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
The circular shaft has a diameter of 30 mm.  
The applied bending moment is 4,000,000 N·mm, acting perpendicular to the shaft axis, inducing maximum bending stress at the outer surface.  
The applied torque is 400,000 N·mm, acting about the longitudinal axis of the shaft, inducing torsional shear stress at the outer surface.  
The critical section is assumed to be at the location of maximum combined loading, where both bending and torsional stresses are evaluated.  
The material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic.  
Poisson’s ratio is assumed to be 0.3 if needed for strain calculations.  
The bending stress is calculated using σ_b = (M * c) / I, where M is the bending moment, c is the outer radius, and I is the area moment of inertia.  
The torsional shear stress is calculated using τ_t = (T * c) / J, where T is the torque, c is the outer radius, and J is the polar moment of inertia.  
For a solid circular shaft, I = (π * d⁴) / 64 and J = (π * d⁴) / 32, with d = 30 mm.  
The outer radius c = d / 2 = 15 mm.  
The state of stress at the critical point on the outer surface is biaxial, with normal stress due to bending and shear stress due to torsion.  
Principal stresses are calculated using σ₁,₂ = (σ_x / 2) ± √((σ_x / 2)² + τ_xy²), where σ_x is the bending stress and τ_xy is the torsional shear stress.  
Maximum in-plane shear stress is calculated using τ_max = √((σ_x / 2)² + τ_xy²).  
Principal strains are calculated using Hooke’s law for plane stress: ε₁ = (σ₁ - ν * σ₂) / E and ε₂ = (σ₂ - ν * σ₁) / E, assuming Young’s modulus E is provided or known.  
Young’s modulus E is assumed to be 200 GPa if not specified.  
Stress concentration factors are assumed to be negligible.  
The analysis assumes no axial load or transverse shear effects.  
The response quantities include bending stress, torsional shear stress, principal stresses, maximum shear stress, and principal strains.
