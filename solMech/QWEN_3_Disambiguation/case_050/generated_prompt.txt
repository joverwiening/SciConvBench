Use a linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics model.  
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and behaves elastically under loading.  
Assume the shaft is prismatic and the cross-section remains plane and undistorted during deformation (Saint-Venant's assumption).  
The outer radius of the shaft is 20 mm.  
The inner radius of the shaft is 15 mm.  
The applied torque is 300000 N·mm.  
The shear modulus of the material is required and will be denoted as G.  
Calculate the polar moment of inertia J using the formula J = (π/2) * (ro⁴ - ri⁴), where ro is the outer radius and ri is the inner radius.  
Compute the maximum shear stress τ_max at the outer surface using τ_max = (T * ro) / J, where T is the applied torque.  
Compute the minimum shear stress τ_min at the inner surface using τ_min = (T * ri) / J.  
Calculate the angle of twist θ over a length L using θ = (T * L) / (G * J), where L is the length of the shaft.  
Report the maximum shear stress, minimum shear stress, and angle of twist per unit length if L is not specified.
