Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
Assume the beam is made of a homogeneous, isotropic material.  
Assume plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after bending.  
The maximum bending moment is 6,000,000 N·mm.  
The allowable bending stress is 90 MPa.  
The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width 30 mm.  
The required section modulus is calculated as S = M_max / σ_allowable.  
Calculate S = 6,000,000 N·mm / 90 MPa = 66,666.67 mm³.  
For a rectangular cross-section, S = (b * h²) / 6, where b is width and h is height.  
Solve for h: h = sqrt(6 * S / b).  
Substitute values: h = sqrt(6 * 66,666.67 / 30) = sqrt(13,333.33) ≈ 115.47 mm.  
Design the rectangular beam with a minimum height of 115.5 mm.  
Round the height to the nearest practical engineering increment, resulting in 116 mm.  
The final beam cross-section is 30 mm wide by 116 mm high.
