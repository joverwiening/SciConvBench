Use small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.  
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic.  
Assume plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after deformation.  
The rectangular column has height h = 200 mm, width b = 120 mm, and is oriented vertically.  
The column is subjected to a compressive axial force P = 40,000 N applied parallel to the column axis.  
The load is applied with an eccentricity e = 25 mm from the centroid along the width direction (horizontal plane).  
Eccentricity causes bending about the centroidal z-axis, where the z-axis is perpendicular to the height and width in the cross-section.  
The cross-section is rectangular with area A = b × h.  
The moment of inertia about the z-axis is I_z = (b × h³) / 12.  
The bending moment due to eccentricity is M = P × e.  
The axial stress due to direct compression is σ_axial = -P / A (negative indicating compression).  
The bending stress distribution is σ_bending = - (M × y) / I_z, where y is the distance from the neutral axis (z-axis) in the height direction.  
The total normal stress at any point in the cross-section is σ_total = σ_axial + σ_bending.  
The maximum tensile stress occurs at the fiber farthest from the neutral axis on the tension side.  
The maximum compressive stress occurs at the fiber farthest from the neutral axis on the compression side.  
The neutral axis is the line of zero longitudinal stress in the cross-section under combined loading.  
The location of the neutral axis shifts due to eccentric loading.  
The section remains fully in compression if the eccentricity is within the kern (e ≤ h/6 for rectangular section under uniaxial bending).  
Stress distribution is linear across the height of the section.  
Displacements are small and do not affect the equilibrium configuration.  
The governing equations are based on Euler-Bernoulli beam theory and Saint-Venant’s principle for stress distribution.
