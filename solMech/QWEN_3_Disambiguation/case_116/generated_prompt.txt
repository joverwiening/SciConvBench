Use a linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics model for the hollow circular shaft.
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and behaves elastically under loading.
Assume the shaft is prismatic with a constant cross-section along its length.
The outer radius of the shaft is 25 mm.
The inner radius of the shaft is 22 mm.
The applied torque is 600,000 N·mm.
The cross-section is subjected to pure torsion with no axial or bending loads.
The shear stress varies linearly with radial distance from the center of the shaft.
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface of the shaft.
The minimum shear stress (non-zero) occurs at the inner surface.
The polar moment of inertia J is calculated as (π/2)*(ro⁴ - ri⁴), where ro = 25 mm and ri = 22 mm.
The shear stress at any radius r is given by τ = (T * r) / J, where T is the applied torque.
The maximum shear stress is computed at r = 25 mm.
The torsional stiffness is proportional to J and the material shear modulus G.
The angle of twist per unit length is given by θ/L = T / (G * J).
The material shear modulus G is required to compute the angle of twist.
The response quantities of interest are the maximum shear stress, the shear stress distribution, and the angle of twist per unit length.
