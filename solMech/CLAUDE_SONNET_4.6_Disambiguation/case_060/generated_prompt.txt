The column is a prismatic, straight, homogeneous member under pure axial compressive load.
Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behaviour throughout (Euler buckling theory applies).
Assume the load is applied exactly along the centroidal axis (no eccentricity, no initial imperfection).
Assume the cross-section is uniform along the entire length L.
The column length is L = 2000 mm.
The cross-sectional second moment of area is I = 800000 mm⁴.
Young's modulus is E = 200000 MPa (N/mm²).
The end condition is fixed-fixed, giving an effective length factor K = 0.5.
Compute the effective length as Le = K × L = 0.5 × 2000 = 1000 mm.
The governing relation for the Euler critical buckling load is P_cr = (π² × E × I) / Le².
Substitute values: P_cr = (π² × 200000 × 800000) / (1000²).
Compute the numerator: π² × 200000 × 800000 = 9.8696 × 1.6 × 10¹¹ = 1.57914 × 10¹² N·mm².
Compute the denominator: 1000² = 1.0 × 10⁶ mm².
Therefore P_cr = 1.57914 × 10¹² / 1.0 × 10⁶ = 1,579,137 N ≈ 1579.1 kN.
The Euler critical buckling load for this fixed-fixed column is P_cr ≈ 1579.1 kN.
For the buckling/instability check, define the demand-to-capacity ratio as UCR = P_applied / P_cr.
If P_applied < P_cr then UCR < 1.0 and the column is stable (no buckling).
If P_applied ≥ P_cr then UCR ≥ 1.0 and buckling is predicted; the design is inadequate.
No applied load magnitude was specified, so the check is expressed as: the column is safe provided P_applied < 1579.1 kN.
Note that Euler buckling theory is valid only when the critical stress σ_cr = P_cr / A remains below the material proportional limit; verify this once the cross-sectional area A is known.
Note that real columns may buckle at lower loads due to imperfections, eccentricity, or residual stresses; apply an appropriate safety factor or use a column curve (e.g., EN 1993 or AISC) for design purposes.
