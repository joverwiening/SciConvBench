The shaft is a solid circular cross-section subjected to pure torque only; no transverse shear force is applied.

Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behavior throughout.

Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.

Assume the shaft is prismatic (constant cross-section) along its entire length.

Assume torque is applied at the ends and the shaft is fixed at one end (for angle of twist calculation).

Use N, mm, and MPa (N/mm²) as the unit system throughout.

Given values are: diameter d = 18 mm, length L = 400 mm, torque T = 500,000 N·mm, shear modulus G = 39,179.1 MPa.

Compute the radius: r = d / 2 = 18 / 2 = 9 mm.

Compute the polar moment of inertia: J = π·d⁴ / 32 = π·(18)⁴ / 32 = π·104,976 / 32 = 10,306.0 mm⁴.

The governing relation for shear stress due to torsion is τ = T·r / J.

Compute maximum shear stress: τ_max = 500,000 · 9 / 10,306.0 = 4,500,000 / 10,306.0 = 436.6 MPa.

The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface of the shaft.

The shear stress distribution is linear from zero at the centroidal axis to τ_max at the outer radius.

The governing relation for angle of twist is φ = T·L / (G·J).

Compute angle of twist: φ = 500,000 · 400 / (39,179.1 · 10,306.0) = 200,000,000 / 403,876,746 = 0.4952 radians.

Convert to degrees: φ = 0.4952 · (180 / π) = 28.37 degrees.

State the assumption that plane cross-sections remain plane after twisting (Navier–Bernoulli torsion assumption for circular shafts).

State that warping is zero for circular cross-sections, so this assumption is exact for this geometry.
