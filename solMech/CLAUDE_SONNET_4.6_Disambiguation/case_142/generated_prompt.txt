The specimen has a circular cross-section with initial diameter d0 = 12.5 mm and gauge length L0 = 50 mm.
The applied axial load is P = 15000 N.
The measured elongation over the gauge length is ΔL = 0.15 mm.
Assume small deformation and linear-elastic material behavior throughout.
Assume the load is purely uniaxial, applied along the specimen axis, with no bending or torsion.
Assume the cross-section is uniform over the gauge length.
Assume the deformation is homogeneous over the gauge length.
All quantities are evaluated at the initial (undeformed) geometry, consistent with engineering (nominal) definitions.

Governing relations and computed results follow.

Compute the initial cross-sectional area A0 from the initial diameter d0.
A0 = π × (d0 / 2)² = π × (12.5 / 2)² mm²
A0 = π × (6.25)² = π × 39.0625 ≈ 122.72 mm²

Engineering stress σ is defined as the applied load divided by the original cross-sectional area.
σ = P / A0
σ = 15000 N / 122.72 mm²
σ ≈ 122.23 MPa (N/mm²)

Engineering strain ε is defined as the change in gauge length divided by the original gauge length.
ε = ΔL / L0
ε = 0.15 mm / 50 mm
ε = 0.003 (dimensionless), or 0.30 %

Young's modulus E is defined as the ratio of engineering stress to engineering strain in the linear-elastic regime.
E = σ / ε
E = 122.23 MPa / 0.003
E ≈ 40,743 MPa ≈ 40.74 GPa

Note: this value of E is substantially lower than the ~200 GPa typical of steel, which suggests the specimen material is not steel; it is consistent with materials such as aluminum alloys (~70 GPa) only if the numbers are taken at face value, and even lower, suggesting a polymer, composite, or soft metal, or that the measured elongation is larger than expected for a metal.
If the specimen is intended to be a metal such as steel or aluminum, the measured ΔL = 0.15 mm should be verified, as it may include machine compliance or grip slip beyond the true gauge-length deformation.
No further assumption about material identity is made; E is reported directly from the given data.

Summary of results:
Initial cross-sectional area A0 ≈ 122.72 mm²
Engineering stress σ ≈ 122.23 MPa
Engineering strain ε = 0.003 (0.30 %)
Young's modulus E ≈ 40.74 GPa (computed from the stress–strain slope using the single data point)
