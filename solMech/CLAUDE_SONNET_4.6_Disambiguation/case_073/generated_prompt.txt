The shaft is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 30 mm and length L = 1000 mm.
The shear modulus is G = 25939.8 MPa (6061-T6 aluminum).
A torque T = 450000 N·mm is applied at the free end; the opposite end is fixed (cantilever torsion configuration).
Assume small deformation and linear-elastic material behavior throughout.
Assume the shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section along its entire length).
Assume the torque is the only load applied; no bending, axial, or shear forces are present.
Assume Saint-Venant torsion theory applies (warping effects are neglected).

Governing Relations:

Polar moment of inertia for a solid circular cross-section:
J = π d^4 / 32

Compute J:
J = π (30)^4 / 32 = π (810000) / 32 = 79521.6 mm^4

The internal torque T(x) is constant along the shaft length:
T(x) = T = 450000 N·mm for all x in [0, L]

The strain energy stored in the shaft due to torsion is:
U = integral from 0 to L of [T(x)^2 / (2 G J)] dx

Since T(x) is constant:
U = T^2 L / (2 G J)

By Castigliano's Second Theorem, the angle of twist at the free end (the point and direction of the applied torque T) is:
φ = dU / dT = T L / (G J)

This is identical to the direct integration result, confirming consistency.

Numerical Evaluation:

J = π (30)^4 / 32 = 79521.56 mm^4

G J = 25939.8 × 79521.56 = 2,063,000,000 N·mm^2 (approximately 2.063 × 10^9 N·mm^2)

φ = T L / (G J) = (450000 × 1000) / (2.063 × 10^9)

φ = 4.5 × 10^8 / 2.063 × 10^9 = 0.2181 radians

Convert to degrees: φ = 0.2181 × (180 / π) = 12.49 degrees

Maximum shear stress at the outer surface:
τ_max = T r / J, where r = d/2 = 15 mm
τ_max = 450000 × 15 / 79521.56 = 84.88 MPa

Strain energy stored:
U = T^2 L / (2 G J) = (450000)^2 × 1000 / (2 × 2.063 × 10^9)
U = 2.025 × 10^11 / 4.126 × 10^9 = 49.08 N·mm (approximately 49.1 mJ)

Additional Assumption Stated:
Warping of the cross-section is fully restrained only at the fixed end but is otherwise free; for a circular cross-section, warping is identically zero, so no warping correction is needed.
