The problem is a linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics problem in MPa (N and mm units throughout.

The beam has a solid rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 80 mm.

The internal transverse shear force acting on the cross-section is V = 20000 N.

Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.

Assume the beam is a straight prismatic member (constant cross-section along its length).

Assume Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies, but the shear stress distribution is evaluated using the Jourawski (shear formula) approach.

Assume shear stresses on a horizontal plane are uniform across the width b at any given height y (valid when b is not large relative to h).

Assume no torsion, no axial force, and no out-of-plane loading are present.

The coordinate y is measured from the centroidal (neutral) axis of the cross-section, ranging from -h/2 = -40 mm to +h/2 = +40 mm.

Compute the second moment of area of the cross-section about the neutral axis using I = b h^3 / 12 = 50 x 80^3 / 12 = 2133333 mm^4.

The first moment of area Q(y) of the portion of the cross-section above height y about the neutral axis is Q(y) = b x (h/2 - y) x (y + (h/2 - y)/2) = (b/2) x (h^2/4 - y^2).

Evaluate Q(y) as Q(y) = (50/2) x (1600 - y^2) = 25 x (1600 - y^2) mm^3, where y is in mm.

The transverse shear stress at height y is given by the shear formula tau(y) = V Q(y) / (I b).

Substitute values: tau(y) = 20000 x 25 x (1600 - y^2) / (2133333 x 50) MPa.

Simplify the constant: tau(y) = (500000 x (1600 - y^2)) / 106666650 = 0.004688 x (1600 - y^2) MPa, where y is in mm.

The shear stress distribution is parabolic, varying with y^2, and is symmetric about the neutral axis.

The shear stress is zero at the top and bottom free surfaces where y = +40 mm and y = -40 mm respectively.

The maximum shear stress occurs at the neutral axis where y = 0.

Compute the maximum shear stress: tau_max = V Q_max / (I b) where Q_max = 25 x 1600 = 40000 mm^3.

tau_max = 20000 x 40000 / (2133333 x 50) = 800000000 / 106666650 = 7.5 MPa.

Confirm using the standard rectangular section formula: tau_max = (3/2) x V / (b h) = 1.5 x 20000 / (50 x 80) = 30000 / 4000 = 7.5 MPa.

The maximum shear stress is 7.5 MPa and acts at the neutral axis (y = 0).

The shear stress at any height y (in mm) is tau(y) = 0.004688 x (1600 - y^2) MPa.

The shear stress acts on the horizontal plane at height y and, by complementary shear, also acts on the vertical cross-sectional plane in the transverse direction.
