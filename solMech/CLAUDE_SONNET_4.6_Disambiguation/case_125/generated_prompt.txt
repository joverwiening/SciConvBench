The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.
The applied bending moment is M = 4,000,000 N·mm, acting as a sagging (positive) moment.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior throughout.
Assume the beam material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the cross-section is symmetric about both the horizontal and vertical centroidal axes.
Assume Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies: plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after bending.
Assume the neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section.
The centroid is located at h/2 = 60 mm from either extreme fiber.

Compute the second moment of area (moment of inertia) about the neutral axis:
I = (b * h^3) / 12
I = (50 * 120^3) / 12
I = (50 * 1,728,000) / 12
I = 86,400,000 / 12
I = 7,200,000 mm^4

The governing relation for bending stress at a fiber located at distance y from the neutral axis is:
sigma(y) = M * y / I

Define y as positive upward from the neutral axis, ranging from -60 mm (bottom fiber) to +60 mm (top fiber).

For a sagging moment, the top fibers are in compression (negative stress) and the bottom fibers are in tension (positive stress).
Apply the sign convention: sagging moment M is positive, y positive upward, compression is negative stress.

Bending stress at the top extreme fiber (y = +60 mm):
sigma_top = 4,000,000 * 60 / 7,200,000
sigma_top = 240,000,000 / 7,200,000
sigma_top = +33.33 N/mm^2 (but this fiber is in compression for sagging, so apply sign: sigma_top = -33.33 MPa)

Bending stress at the bottom extreme fiber (y = -60 mm):
sigma_bottom = 4,000,000 * (-60) / 7,200,000
sigma_bottom = -240,000,000 / 7,200,000
sigma_bottom = -33.33 N/mm^2 (tension at bottom for sagging, so with consistent sign: sigma_bottom = +33.33 MPa)

Clarification on sign convention used: with sagging moment defined as positive and y measured upward from the neutral axis, sigma(y) = -M * y / I places compression at the top and tension at the bottom, consistent with sagging behavior.
Use the corrected governing relation: sigma(y) = -M * y / I for sagging positive convention.

Bending stress at an arbitrary fiber at distance y from the neutral axis:
sigma(y) = -M * y / I = -(4,000,000 * y) / 7,200,000 = -0.5556 * y MPa (with y in mm)

At y = +60 mm (top): sigma = -33.33 MPa (compression).
At y = 0 mm (neutral axis): sigma = 0 MPa.
At y = -60 mm (bottom): sigma = +33.33 MPa (tension).
At any arbitrary y between -60 mm and +60 mm, substitute into sigma(y) = -0.5556 * y MPa.

The bending stress distribution is linear across the cross-section height.
The maximum magnitude of bending stress is 33.33 MPa, occurring at the extreme fibers.
The neutral axis carries zero bending stress.
