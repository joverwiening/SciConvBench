The shaft is a solid circular cross-section subjected to pure torque only; no transverse shear force is applied.

Assume small deformations and linear-elastic, isotropic material behavior throughout.

Assume the shaft is prismatic (constant cross-section) along its entire length.

Assume the material is homogeneous and the torque is applied along the centroidal axis.

Assume warping is unrestrained and Saint-Venant torsion theory applies.

Use the following given values: diameter d = 35 mm, radius c = d/2 = 17.5 mm, length L = 400 mm, torque T = 250,000 N·mm, shear modulus G = 77,519.4 MPa.

Compute the polar moment of inertia J for a solid circular cross-section using J = π·d⁴/32.

J = π·(35)⁴/32 = π·1,500,625/32 = 147,323.5 mm⁴ (approximately).

Compute the maximum shear stress using the torsion formula τ_max = T·c/J.

τ_max = 250,000 × 17.5 / 147,323.5 = 4,375,000 / 147,323.5 = 29.7 MPa (approximately).

The maximum shear stress τ_max occurs at the outer surface of the shaft (r = c = 17.5 mm).

The shear stress distribution is linear from zero at the centroidal axis to τ_max at the outer radius, given by τ(r) = T·r/J.

Compute the angle of twist using φ = T·L/(G·J).

φ = 250,000 × 400 / (77,519.4 × 147,323.5) = 100,000,000 / 11,418,753,090 = 0.008757 radians (approximately).

Convert to degrees: φ = 0.008757 × (180/π) = 0.5016 degrees (approximately).

The angle of twist φ is measured between the two ends of the shaft.

Report all results in consistent N–mm–MPa units; stress is in MPa and angle of twist is in radians (with degree equivalent stated).
