Design a solid circular shaft diameter based on an angle-of-twist limit under pure torsion.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section) along its full length.
Assume the torque T is constant along the full shaft length (no distributed torque).
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume transverse shear stresses due to any transverse shear force are secondary and do not govern this design check; the design is driven solely by the angle-of-twist limit.

Given values are as follows.
Applied torque: T = 1,000,000 N·mm.
Shear modulus: G = 25,939.8 MPa (N/mm²).
Allowable angle of twist: φ_allow = 0.75° = 0.75 × π/180 = 0.013090 radians.
Shaft length: L = 1,500 mm.

The governing relation for angle of twist of a solid circular shaft is φ = T·L / (G·J).
The polar second moment of area for a solid circular cross-section of diameter d is J = π·d⁴ / 32.
Substituting gives φ = T·L·32 / (G·π·d⁴).

Rearrange to solve for the minimum required diameter d.
Set φ equal to φ_allow and solve for d.
d⁴ = (32·T·L) / (G·π·φ_allow).
d = [ (32·T·L) / (G·π·φ_allow) ]^(1/4).

Substitute numerical values as follows.
Numerator: 32 × 1,000,000 × 1,500 = 4.800 × 10¹⁰ N·mm³.
Denominator: 25,939.8 × π × 0.013090 = 25,939.8 × 0.041123 = 1,067.07 N/mm² · rad (dimensionally N/mm²).
d⁴ = 4.800 × 10¹⁰ / 1,067.07 = 4.4984 × 10⁷ mm⁴.
d = (4.4984 × 10⁷)^(1/4) mm.

Compute step by step.
(4.4984 × 10⁷)^(1/2) = 6,707.0 mm².
(6,707.0)^(1/2) = 81.9 mm.
Therefore d_min ≈ 81.9 mm.

Round up to the nearest standard size or use d = 82 mm as the minimum acceptable diameter.

The required shaft diameter to satisfy the angle-of-twist limit of 0.75° over 1,500 mm is d_min ≈ 81.9 mm; select d ≥ 82 mm in practice.

After selecting d, verify the resulting angle of twist using φ = 32·T·L / (G·π·d⁴) and confirm φ ≤ 0.013090 rad (0.75°).
Also check shear stress using τ_max = 16·T / (π·d³) and compare against the material allowable shear stress as a separate strength check.
