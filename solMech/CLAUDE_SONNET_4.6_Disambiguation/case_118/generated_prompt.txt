The beam is a straight, prismatic member with a solid rectangular cross-section.
The width is b = 40 mm and the height is h = 80 mm.
The internal transverse shear force at the cross-section of interest is V = 25 000 N.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, isotropic, homogeneous material behavior.
Assume the beam is long relative to its cross-section so that Euler-Bernoulli / Saint-Venant shear-stress theory applies.
Assume the shear stress is uniformly distributed across the width b at any given height y (valid for b << h or accepted as the standard engineering approximation for rectangular sections).
Neglect warping restraint and out-of-plane effects.

Governing relation — shear formula (Zhuravsky / shear-flow formula):

tau(y) = V * Q(y) / (I * b)

where:
  V  = internal shear force = 25 000 N
  I  = second moment of area of the full cross-section about the neutral axis = b * h^3 / 12
  Q(y) = first moment of area of the portion of the cross-section above (or below) height y, taken about the neutral axis
  b  = width of the cross-section at the cut height y = 40 mm (constant for a rectangle)
  y  = distance measured from the neutral axis, ranging from -h/2 to +h/2

Step 1 — Compute I.
I = b * h^3 / 12 = 40 * 80^3 / 12 = 40 * 512 000 / 12 = 1 706 667 mm^4 (= 1.7067 × 10^6 mm^4)

Step 2 — Express Q(y) for a rectangle.
The area above height y is A'= b * (h/2 - y).
Its centroid is at distance y_bar = (h/2 + y) / 2 from the neutral axis.
Q(y) = A' * y_bar = b * (h/2 - y) * (h/2 + y) / 2 = (b / 2) * ((h/2)^2 - y^2)

Step 3 — Substitute into the shear formula.
tau(y) = V * (b/2) * ((h/2)^2 - y^2) / (I * b)
tau(y) = V * ((h/2)^2 - y^2) / (2 * I)

Step 4 — Compute the maximum shear stress (occurs at y = 0, the neutral axis).
Q_max = (b/2) * (h/2)^2 = (40/2) * (40)^2 = 20 * 1600 = 32 000 mm^3
tau_max = V * Q_max / (I * b) = 25 000 * 32 000 / (1 706 667 * 40)
tau_max = 800 000 000 / 68 266 680 = 11.72 MPa

Step 5 — Compute the shear stress at the top and bottom surfaces (y = ±h/2 = ±40 mm).
Q = 0 at the free surfaces, therefore tau = 0 MPa at y = ±40 mm.

Step 6 — Parabolic distribution summary.
tau(y) = 11.72 * (1 - (y/40)^2) MPa, with y in mm, valid for -40 mm ≤ y ≤ 40 mm.
The distribution is parabolic, symmetric about the neutral axis, zero at the top and bottom surfaces, and maximum at the neutral axis.

Additional assumption stated: the shear stress on any horizontal plane acts parallel to the shear force V (i.e., in the transverse direction), and by complementary shear, an equal shear stress acts on the vertical plane at the same point.
