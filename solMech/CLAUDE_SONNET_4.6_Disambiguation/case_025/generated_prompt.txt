The beam is a cantilever of length L = 1500 mm, fixed at one end and free at the other.
A downward point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end.
The beam material is linear-elastic with elastic modulus E = 69000 MPa.
The second moment of area of the cross-section is I = 2500000 mm⁴.
Assume small deformations throughout.
Assume the beam is prismatic, meaning E and I are constant along the entire length.
Assume the beam is initially straight and horizontal.
Neglect shear deformation; only bending strain energy is considered (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
Neglect self-weight; the only load is the applied point load P.

Governing relation using Castigliano's Second Theorem:
The deflection at the point of load application in the direction of the load equals the partial derivative of the total strain energy U with respect to that load.
Deflection delta = dU/dP.

The bending moment at a section located at distance x from the free end is M(x) = P * x.
The total strain energy stored in the beam due to bending is U = integral from 0 to L of [M(x)^2 / (2EI)] dx.
Substituting M(x) = P * x gives U = integral from 0 to L of [P^2 * x^2 / (2EI)] dx.
Evaluating the integral gives U = P^2 * L^3 / (6EI).

Applying Castigliano's theorem: delta = dU/dP = P * L^3 / (3EI).

Substituting numerical values:
Numerator: P * L^3 = 500 * (1500)^3 = 500 * 3375000000 = 1.6875 x 10^12 N·mm^3.
Denominator: 3 * E * I = 3 * 69000 * 2500000 = 5.175 x 10^11 N·mm^2.
delta = 1.6875 x 10^12 / 5.175 x 10^11 = 3.26 mm (downward).

The free-end deflection of the cantilever under the 500 N downward point load is delta = P * L^3 / (3EI) = 3.26 mm downward.
