Perform an Euler critical buckling load analysis for a column under axial compressive load.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the column is perfectly straight, homogeneous, and prismatic (constant cross-section along its length).
Assume the compressive load is applied concentrically (no eccentricity).
Assume no residual stresses or imperfections.

Column properties are as follows.
Length: L = 3000 mm.
Young's modulus: E = 69000 MPa.
Second moment of area: I = 2500000 mm⁴ (given directly).
Material: 6061-T6 aluminum.
End conditions: fixed-fixed, with effective length factor K = 0.5.

Compute the effective length as Le = K × L = 0.5 × 3000 = 1500 mm.

Apply the Euler critical buckling load formula: Pcr = (π² × E × I) / Le².
Substitute values: Pcr = (π² × 69000 × 2500000) / (1500²).
Compute the denominator: 1500² = 2250000 mm².
Compute the numerator: π² × 69000 × 2500000 = 9.8696 × 172500000000 = 1.7025 × 10¹² N·mm².
Compute Pcr = 1.7025 × 10¹² / 2250000 = 756,667 N, approximately 756.7 kN.

For the buckling/instability check, compare the applied compressive load P to Pcr.
The buckling check is: P ≤ Pcr / SF, where SF is the safety factor specified by the applicable design code or project requirement.
If no safety factor is specified, state the raw Pcr = 756.7 kN as the critical threshold.
If P < Pcr, the column is stable against Euler buckling.
If P ≥ Pcr, the column buckles and the design is inadequate.

Note that Euler buckling governs only when the column is slender enough that elastic buckling precedes material yielding.
Verify this by checking that the critical stress σcr = Pcr / A does not exceed the yield strength of 6061-T6 aluminum (276 MPa).
If σcr exceeds the yield strength, inelastic buckling governs and the Euler formula is not valid; a Johnson or tangent-modulus approach would be required instead.
Cross-sectional area A is needed to perform this yield check; obtain A from the actual cross-section geometry.
