The problem is a linear-elastic beam bending problem solved in a consistent N–mm–MPa unit system.

Assume small deformations and rotations throughout.
Assume the beam is prismatic (constant cross-section along its length).
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic (Hooke's law applies).
Assume the bending moment M is constant at the cross-section of interest (pure bending condition).
Assume the neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section.
Assume plane sections remain plane after bending (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).

Given values are: M = 3,000,000 N·mm, E = 200,000 MPa (N/mm²), I = 2,500,000 mm⁴.

Compute the flexural rigidity as EI = E × I = 200,000 × 2,500,000 = 500,000,000,000 N·mm² (5.0 × 10¹¹ N·mm²).

The governing relation from Euler-Bernoulli beam theory relating bending moment to curvature is M = EI × κ, where κ is the curvature in units of mm⁻¹.

Rearrange to solve for curvature: κ = M / (EI).

Substitute values: κ = 3,000,000 / 500,000,000,000 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁶ mm⁻¹.

The radius of curvature ρ is the reciprocal of curvature: ρ = 1 / κ.

Substitute: ρ = 1 / (6.0 × 10⁻⁶) = 166,666.67 mm.

Convert radius of curvature to metres for physical context: ρ = 166,666.67 mm = 166.67 m.

Summary of results: curvature κ = 6.0 × 10⁻⁶ mm⁻¹, radius of curvature ρ = 166,666.67 mm (≈ 166.67 m).

The small-deformation assumption is confirmed valid because κ is very small (much less than 1 mm⁻¹) and the resulting radius of curvature is very large relative to any practical beam length.
