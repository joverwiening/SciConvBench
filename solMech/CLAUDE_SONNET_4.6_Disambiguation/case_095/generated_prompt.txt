Model a cantilever Euler-Bernoulli beam with a downward point load at the free end.

Geometry and Loading
The beam has length L, constant cross-section, and is fixed (clamped) at one end and free at the other.
Apply a single downward point load P = 2000 N at the free end.
There is no applied moment anywhere on the beam.

Material and Section Properties
Assume linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material with Young's modulus E.
Assume a constant second moment of area I about the bending axis along the full length.
EI is the flexural rigidity and is constant throughout.

Assumptions
Small deformations and small rotations (small-deflection theory applies).
Plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after bending (Euler-Bernoulli hypothesis).
Shear deformation is neglected.
Self-weight of the beam is neglected.
The beam is initially straight and unstressed.

Coordinate System
Let x run from 0 at the fixed end to L at the free end.
Let v(x) be the transverse deflection, positive downward.

Governing Differential Equation
The Euler-Bernoulli bending equation is: EI * d²v/dx² = M(x), where M(x) is the internal bending moment at position x.

Bending Moment Distribution
Taking a free-body diagram from the free end, the internal bending moment at position x is: M(x) = -P * (L - x).
At the fixed end (x = 0): M = -P * L (maximum magnitude).
At the free end (x = L): M = 0.

Boundary Conditions
At the fixed end (x = 0): deflection v(0) = 0 (no displacement).
At the fixed end (x = 0): slope dv/dx(0) = 0 (no rotation).

Integration for Slope
Integrate EI * d²v/dx² = -P*(L - x) once to get: EI * dv/dx = -P*(L*x - x²/2) + C1.
Apply dv/dx(0) = 0, giving C1 = 0.
Slope at position x: dv/dx = [-P / (EI)] * (L*x - x²/2).

Integration for Deflection
Integrate once more: EI * v(x) = -P*(L*x²/2 - x³/6) + C2.
Apply v(0) = 0, giving C2 = 0.
Deflection at position x: v(x) = [-P / (EI)] * (L*x²/2 - x³/6).

Key Response Quantities
Maximum deflection occurs at the free end (x = L): v_max = P*L³ / (3*EI).
Substituting P = 2000 N: v_max = (2000 * L³) / (3 * E * I).
Maximum slope (rotation) occurs at the free end (x = L): theta_max = dv/dx at x=L = P*L² / (2*EI).
Substituting P = 2000 N: theta_max = (2000 * L²) / (2 * E * I) = 1000 * L² / (E * I).
Maximum bending moment occurs at the fixed end (x = 0): M_max = P * L = 2000 * L (in N·m, with L in metres).
Maximum shear force is constant along the entire beam: V = P = 2000 N.

To obtain numerical values for v_max and theta_max, provide the beam length L (in metres), Young's modulus E (in Pa), and second moment of area I (in m⁴).
