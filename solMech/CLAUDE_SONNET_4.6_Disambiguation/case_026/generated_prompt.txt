The beam is a cantilever of length L = 1500 mm, fixed at one end and free at the other.
A downward point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end.
The beam material is AISI 1020 steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa (N/mm²).
The second moment of area of the cross-section is I = 800000 mm⁴.
Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behaviour throughout.
Assume the beam is prismatic (uniform cross-section and material properties along the entire length).
Assume the beam is initially straight and horizontal.
Neglect self-weight, shear deformation, and any axial or torsional effects.
Assume Euler-Bernoulli beam theory applies (plane sections remain plane, shear strains are negligible).

Apply Castigliano's Second Theorem to find the vertical deflection at the free end under the point load P.

Castigliano's Second Theorem states that the deflection at the point of load application in the direction of the load equals the partial derivative of the total strain energy U with respect to that load.

delta = dU/dP

The total strain energy stored in the beam due to bending is:

U = integral from 0 to L of [M(x)² / (2EI)] dx

Take the coordinate x measured from the fixed end toward the free end.
The bending moment at position x due to the downward load P at the free end is:

M(x) = P * (L - x)

This is the sagging/hogging moment at section x; the moment varies linearly from P*L at the fixed end to zero at the free end.

The partial derivative of M with respect to P is:

dM/dP = (L - x)

Apply Castigliano's theorem in differentiated form:

delta = integral from 0 to L of [M(x) / (EI)] * [dM/dP] dx

Substitute M(x) and dM/dP:

delta = integral from 0 to L of [P*(L - x)² / (EI)] dx

Since E, I, and P are constants along the beam, factor them out:

delta = (P / EI) * integral from 0 to L of (L - x)² dx

Evaluate the integral by substituting u = L - x, du = -dx, limits from L to 0 becoming 0 to L:

integral from 0 to L of (L - x)² dx = [u³/3] from 0 to L = L³/3

Therefore the governing relation for the free-end deflection is:

delta = P * L³ / (3 * E * I)

Substitute the numerical values:

delta = (500 * 1500³) / (3 * 200000 * 800000)

Compute the numerator: 500 * 1500³ = 500 * 3375000000 = 1687500000000 N·mm³

Compute the denominator: 3 * 200000 * 800000 = 480000000000 N·mm²

delta = 1687500000000 / 480000000000 = 3.515625 mm

The vertical deflection at the free end of the cantilever is delta = 3.52 mm (downward), rounded to three significant figures.

The deflection is positive downward, consistent with the direction of the applied load.
The result confirms that the free-end deflection depends linearly on P and cubically on L, and inversely on the flexural rigidity EI.
