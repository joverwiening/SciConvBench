Design a solid circular shaft diameter based on an angle-of-twist limit.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section) along its full length.
Assume the torque T is constant along the full shaft length (pure torsion, no variation with position).
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the shaft is simply supported in torsion (one end fixed, one end free or torque-loaded).

Given values are as follows.
Applied torque: T = 600,000 N·mm.
Shaft length: L = 800 mm.
Shear modulus: G = 76,923.1 MPa.
Allowable angle of twist: φ_allow = 1.5° = 1.5 × (π/180) = 0.02618 radians.

The governing relation for angle of twist in a circular shaft is φ = T·L / (G·J).
The polar moment of inertia for a solid circular cross-section is J = π·d⁴ / 32.
Substituting gives φ = T·L·32 / (G·π·d⁴).

Rearrange to solve for the required diameter d.
Set φ equal to φ_allow to find the minimum required diameter.
d⁴ = (32·T·L) / (G·π·φ_allow).
Substitute values: d⁴ = (32 × 600,000 × 800) / (76,923.1 × π × 0.02618).
Compute numerator: 32 × 600,000 × 800 = 15,360,000,000 N·mm³.
Compute denominator: 76,923.1 × π × 0.02618 = 76,923.1 × 0.08222 = 6,324.6 N/mm² (dimensionless product yields mm⁴ units when combined).
Compute d⁴ = 15,360,000,000 / 6,324.6 = 2,428,900 mm⁴ (approximately).
Compute d = (2,428,900)^(1/4).
Take square root of 2,428,900 = 1558.5 mm².
Take square root of 1558.5 = 39.48 mm.
Therefore d_required ≈ 39.5 mm.

Round up to the nearest standard size or use d ≥ 39.5 mm to satisfy the angle-of-twist limit.

Report the final result as: the minimum solid shaft diameter required to limit the angle of twist to 1.5° over 800 mm under a torque of 600,000 N·mm with G = 76,923.1 MPa is d ≈ 39.5 mm.

State the following additional assumptions in the design record.
Stress concentration factors are neglected; this is a preliminary sizing calculation.
Yielding and strength limits are not checked here; a separate strength check against allowable shear stress should be performed using τ = T·c / J where c = d/2.
Shaft self-weight and any bending loads are not considered in this torsion-only design.
