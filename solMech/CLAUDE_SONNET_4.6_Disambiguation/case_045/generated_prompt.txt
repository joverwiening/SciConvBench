A straight prismatic circular rod is made of 6061-T6 aluminum.
The material has elastic modulus E = 69,000 MPa and yield strength Sy = 275 MPa.
The rod has a circular cross-section with diameter d = 12 mm and length L = 800 mm.
An axial tensile load P = 15,000 N is applied along the centroidal axis.

Assume small deformation and linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the load is static, monotonic, and centrally applied with no eccentricity.
Assume the rod is prismatic (uniform cross-section along its entire length).
Assume no stress concentrations, no buckling, and no temperature effects.
Assume both ends are free to deform axially (no end constraint beyond equilibrium).

Compute the cross-sectional area as A = π × (d/2)² = π × (6 mm)² = 113.097 mm².

Compute the average normal stress as σ = P / A = 15,000 N / 113.097 mm² = 132.63 MPa (tensile).

Compute the axial strain as ε = σ / E = 132.63 MPa / 69,000 MPa = 1.9221 × 10⁻³ (dimensionless).

Compute the total elongation as δ = ε × L = 1.9221 × 10⁻³ × 800 mm = 1.538 mm.

Compute the factor of safety with respect to yielding as FS = Sy / σ = 275 MPa / 132.63 MPa = 2.07.

The rod does not yield under the applied load since σ < Sy.
The factor of safety of 2.07 indicates the rod can sustain approximately twice the applied load before the onset of yielding.
No failure by fracture is evaluated since the material is ductile; yielding is the governing failure criterion.
