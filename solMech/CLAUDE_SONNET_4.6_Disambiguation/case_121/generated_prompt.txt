The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 120 mm and depth h = 200 mm.
The applied load is P = 40 000 N, compressive, acting at an eccentricity e = 25 mm measured along the b-direction (i.e., offset from the centroid in the x-direction, causing bending about the centroidal z-axis).
Use N, mm, and MPa (N/mm²) throughout.
Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behaviour (Hooke's law).
Assume the column is short enough that buckling and second-order (P-delta) effects are negligible.
Assume the cross-section is homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic.
Assume plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli / Navier bending assumption).
Treat compressive normal stress as negative and tensile normal stress as positive.

Compute the cross-sectional area.
A = b × h = 120 × 200 = 24 000 mm².

Compute the second moment of area about the centroidal z-axis (the axis about which the eccentricity causes bending).
I_z = (b × h³) / 12 = (120 × 200³) / 12 = 80 000 000 mm⁴ = 8.0 × 10⁷ mm⁴.

Resolve the eccentric load into a concentric axial force plus a bending moment.
The concentric axial force is P = 40 000 N (compressive).
The bending moment about the centroidal z-axis is M_z = P × e = 40 000 × 25 = 1 000 000 N·mm = 1.0 × 10⁶ N·mm.

Write the governing stress distribution (Navier's formula).
σ(x, y) = −P/A ± (M_z × x) / I_z, where x is the coordinate measured from the centroid in the width direction, ranging from −b/2 = −60 mm to +b/2 = +60 mm.
The sign of the bending term depends on which side of the centroid is being evaluated: positive x is on the same side as the eccentricity.

Compute the uniform axial (direct) stress.
σ_axial = −P / A = −40 000 / 24 000 = −1.667 N/mm² (compressive).

Compute the bending stress at the extreme fibres (x = ±60 mm).
σ_bending = ± (M_z × x_max) / I_z = ± (1 000 000 × 60) / 80 000 000 = ±0.750 N/mm².

Compute the combined stress at each extreme fibre.
At x = +60 mm (same side as eccentricity): σ = −1.667 + 0.750 = −0.917 N/mm² (compressive).
At x = −60 mm (opposite side): σ = −1.667 − 0.750 = −2.417 N/mm² (compressive).

Check the kern condition to confirm whether tension develops anywhere in the section.
The kern half-width for a rectangle about the z-axis is k_x = b/6 = 120/6 = 20 mm.
The eccentricity e = 25 mm exceeds k_x = 20 mm, so the load falls outside the kern.
This means the bending stress is large enough to overcome the direct compressive stress on the less-compressed side; however, because the bending stress magnitude (0.750 MPa) is less than the direct stress magnitude (1.667 MPa), the net stress at x = +60 mm is still compressive (−0.917 MPa).
Re-check: tension would require σ_bending > σ_axial in magnitude, i.e., (M_z × x_max)/I_z > P/A, i.e., 0.750 > 1.667, which is false.
Therefore no tensile stress develops anywhere in the section despite the load being outside the kern.

Locate the neutral axis (line of zero stress).
Set σ = 0: −P/A + (M_z × x_NA)/I_z = 0.
x_NA = (P/A) × (I_z / M_z) = 1.667 × (80 000 000 / 1 000 000) = 1.667 × 80 = 133.3 mm from the centroid.
Since 133.3 mm > 60 mm (half the section width), the neutral axis lies outside the cross-section, confirming the entire section is in compression.

Summary of key results.
Cross-sectional area A = 24 000 mm².
Second moment of area I_z = 8.0 × 10⁷ mm⁴.
Direct (axial) stress σ_axial = −1.667 MPa (uniform, compressive).
Bending stress at extreme fibres σ_bending = ±0.750 MPa.
Maximum compressive stress = −2.417 MPa at x = −60 mm (side away from eccentricity).
Minimum compressive stress = −0.917 MPa at x = +60 mm (side toward eccentricity).
No tensile stress exists in the section.
The neutral axis is located 133.3 mm from the centroid, outside the section boundary.
