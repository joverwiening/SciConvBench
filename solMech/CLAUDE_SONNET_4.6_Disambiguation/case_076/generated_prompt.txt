The beam is a simply supported beam of span length L, with a single concentrated vertical load P acting downward at the midpoint x = L/2.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.

Assume the beam is prismatic with a uniform cross-section along its entire length.

Assume the beam is straight and initially stress-free.

Assume the concentrated load P is applied at exactly x = L/2, the geometric midpoint of the span.

Since the only applied load is the single concentrated vertical load P, there is no axial (tension) loading and no applied external moment in this problem; those load types are absent and their effects are zero.

The left support (at x = 0) is a pin providing a vertical reaction; the right support (at x = L) is a roller providing a vertical reaction.

Apply global vertical equilibrium: the sum of vertical reactions equals P.

Apply moment equilibrium about the left support: the right reaction R_B = P * (L/2) / L = P/2.

By symmetry, the left reaction R_A = P/2.

Confirm: R_A + R_B = P/2 + P/2 = P. Equilibrium is satisfied.

There is no horizontal reaction because there is no horizontal applied force.

Use the method of sections to determine internal shear force V(x) and bending moment M(x) at any cross-section.

Define x as the coordinate measured from the left support toward the right support, with 0 <= x <= L.

For the left segment, 0 < x < L/2:
Cut the beam at position x and consider the left free body.
V(x) = R_A = +P/2 (constant, positive by the convention that upward force on the left face is positive shear).
M(x) = R_A * x = (P/2) * x (linearly increasing from 0 at x=0 to P*L/4 at x = L/2).

For the right segment, L/2 < x < L:
Cut the beam at position x and consider the left free body.
V(x) = R_A - P = P/2 - P = -P/2 (constant, negative shear).
M(x) = R_A * x - P * (x - L/2) = (P/2)*x - P*x + P*L/2 = -(P/2)*x + P*L/2 = (P/2)*(L - x) (linearly decreasing from P*L/4 at x = L/2 to 0 at x = L).

At x = L/2, the shear force has a jump discontinuity of magnitude P, from +P/2 to -P/2, coinciding with the point of load application.

The maximum bending moment occurs at x = L/2 and equals M_max = P*L/4.

The bending moment diagram is triangular, rising linearly from zero at the left support to P*L/4 at midspan, then falling linearly back to zero at the right support.

The shear force diagram is a positive rectangle of value +P/2 from x=0 to x=L/2, then a negative rectangle of value -P/2 from x=L/2 to x=L.

The governing differential relations between load intensity q(x), shear V(x), and moment M(x) are:
dV/dx = -q(x), where q(x) = 0 everywhere except at x = L/2 where a concentrated load P is represented by a Dirac delta: q(x) = P * delta(x - L/2).
dM/dx = V(x) at all sections where no concentrated moment is applied.

No axial force resultant exists anywhere in the beam under this loading.

No applied external moment exists at any point along the beam under this loading.
