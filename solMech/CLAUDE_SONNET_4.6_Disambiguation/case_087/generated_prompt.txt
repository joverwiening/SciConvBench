The specimen is a ductile metal under uniaxial tension loading.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior up to yielding, with ductility measures computed from post-fracture geometry.
Assume the cross-section is circular throughout, so areas are computed from diameters using A = π/4 · d².

Given values:
Initial gauge length L₀ = 50 mm.
Initial diameter d₀ = 12 mm.
Final gauge length Lf = 66 mm.
Final minimum diameter at the neck df = 9.6 mm.

Compute the initial cross-sectional area:
A₀ = π/4 · d₀² = π/4 · (12)² = π/4 · 144 = 113.097 mm².

Compute the final cross-sectional area at the neck:
Af = π/4 · df² = π/4 · (9.6)² = π/4 · 92.16 = 72.382 mm².

Governing relation for Percent Elongation (%EL):
%EL = [(Lf − L₀) / L₀] × 100.
%EL = [(66 − 50) / 50] × 100 = [16 / 50] × 100 = 32%.

Governing relation for Percent Reduction of Area (%RA):
%RA = [(A₀ − Af) / A₀] × 100.
%RA = [(113.097 − 72.382) / 113.097] × 100 = [40.715 / 113.097] × 100 = 36%.

The percent elongation of the specimen is 32%.
The percent reduction of area of the specimen is 36%.
Both values are consistent with ductile metal behavior, where %RA typically exceeds %EL when significant necking occurs, and here %RA (36%) is slightly greater than %EL (32%), confirming localized necking dominated the final fracture stage.
