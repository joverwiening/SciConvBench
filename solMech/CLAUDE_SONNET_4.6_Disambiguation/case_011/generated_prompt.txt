Perform a column buckling and yielding analysis for a solid circular column under axial compressive load.

Use linear-elastic, small-deformation solid mechanics throughout.

Material is structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200,000 MPa and yield strength Sy = 250 MPa.

Cross-section is solid circular with diameter d = 25 mm.

Column length is L = 2000 mm.

Effective length factor is K = 0.7, giving an effective length Le = K × L = 0.7 × 2000 = 1400 mm.

Compute the cross-sectional area: A = π × d² / 4.

Compute the second moment of area: I = π × d⁴ / 64.

Compute the radius of gyration: r = sqrt(I / A), which for a solid circle equals d / 4.

Compute the slenderness ratio: SR = Le / r.

Compute the Euler critical buckling load: P_cr = π² × E × I / Le².

Compute the Euler critical buckling stress: σ_cr = P_cr / A, equivalently σ_cr = π² × E / SR².

Compute the yield load: P_y = Sy × A.

Determine the transition slenderness ratio that separates Euler (long column) behavior from yielding (short column) behavior: SR_transition = π × sqrt(E / Sy).

If SR > SR_transition, the column is a long column and Euler buckling governs before yielding.

If SR ≤ SR_transition, the column is a short column and yielding governs before Euler buckling.

Classify the column based on the computed SR versus SR_transition.

State which failure mode governs and identify the controlling critical load (the lesser of P_cr and P_y).

Perform a buckling instability check: compute the factor of safety against Euler buckling as FS_buckling = P_cr / P_applied, where P_applied is the applied compressive load.

Since no specific applied load is given, present P_cr and P_y as the key results and note that an applied load P must satisfy P < P_cr (buckling check) and P < P_y (yielding check) simultaneously.

State all intermediate computed values: A, I, r, Le, SR, SR_transition, P_cr, σ_cr, and P_y.

Assume the column is perfectly straight, concentrically loaded, and free of residual stresses.

Assume no local buckling or material nonlinearity.
