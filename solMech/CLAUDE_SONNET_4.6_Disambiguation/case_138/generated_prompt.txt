The prismatic bar has a rectangular cross section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 120 mm.

The cross-sectional area is A = b × h = 40 × 120 = 4800 mm².

The internal resultants at the cross section are: axial tensile force P = 30000 N and transverse shear force V = 12000 N acting parallel to the width b.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.

Assume the bar is prismatic and homogeneous, so cross-sectional properties are constant along the length.

Assume the cross section is far enough from any load application point or geometric discontinuity that Saint-Venant's principle applies and the stress distributions are fully developed.

Assume no bending moment or torque acts at the section (only P and V are present as internal resultants).

The average normal stress on the cross section due to the axial force P is defined as σ_avg = P / A.

Substituting: σ_avg = 30000 N / 4800 mm² = 6.25 MPa (tensile).

This average normal stress is uniform over the entire cross-sectional area in the average sense.

The average shear stress on the cross section due to the transverse shear force V is defined as τ_avg = V / A.

Substituting: τ_avg = 12000 N / 4800 mm² = 2.50 MPa.

The direction of τ_avg is parallel to the width b, consistent with the direction of V.

Note that the actual shear stress distribution over a rectangular section is parabolic, not uniform; τ_avg represents the force-resultant-equivalent uniform value, not the local peak.

The peak shear stress for a rectangular section occurs at the neutral axis and equals τ_max = (3/2) × (V / A) = 1.5 × 2.50 = 3.75 MPa, stated here for reference but not the primary requested quantity.

Summary of governing relations:

σ_avg = P / A = 30000 / 4800 = 6.25 MPa (tension, normal to the section).

τ_avg = V / A = 12000 / 4800 = 2.50 MPa (parallel to b, tangential to the section).
