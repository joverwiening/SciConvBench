The specimen is a circular cylindrical rod under uniaxial tension.
The analysis assumes small deformation and linear-elastic material behavior (Hookean solid).
The specimen geometry is defined by an initial diameter d0 = 12.5 mm and an initial gauge length L0 = 50 mm.
The applied axial load is P = 12000 N.
The measured elongation under load is ΔL = 0.12 mm.
The deformed gauge length is L = L0 + ΔL = 50.12 mm.

Assumption 1: The cross-section remains circular and uniform along the gauge length throughout loading.
Assumption 2: The load is applied concentrically along the centroidal axis, producing pure uniaxial stress with no bending or torsion.
Assumption 3: The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assumption 4: Strains are small enough that engineering (nominal) measures are valid and indistinguishable from true measures at this load level.
Assumption 5: The elongation ΔL is measured over the full gauge length L0 and is uniform.

Governing Relations:

Compute the initial cross-sectional area.
A0 = π × (d0/2)² = π × (6.25 mm)² = π × 39.0625 mm²
A0 = 122.718 mm²

Compute engineering (nominal) axial stress.
σ = P / A0
σ = 12000 N / 122.718 mm²
σ = 97.79 MPa

Compute engineering (nominal) axial strain.
ε = ΔL / L0
ε = 0.12 mm / 50 mm
ε = 0.0024 (dimensionless), equivalently 2.4 × 10⁻³ or 0.24 %

Compute the elastic modulus (Young's modulus) from the linear-elastic constitutive relation σ = E × ε.
E = σ / ε
E = 97.79 MPa / 0.0024
E = 40,746 MPa ≈ 40.75 GPa

Summary of results:

Young's modulus E = 40.75 GPa

Note: The computed modulus of approximately 40.75 GPa is well below the typical value for structural steel (≈ 200 GPa) or aluminum (≈ 70 GPa), which suggests the specimen material may be a polymer, soft metal, or composite, or that the measured elongation is larger than typical for metals at this load. If the material is known to be steel or aluminum, the elongation value or load should be verified experimentally, as the data imply a much more compliant material.
