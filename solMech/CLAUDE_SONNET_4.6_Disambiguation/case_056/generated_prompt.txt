The bar is a straight, prismatic-axis member with a continuously varying circular (or arbitrary) cross-section.
Assume small deformations and linear-elastic, homogeneous, isotropic material behavior throughout.
Assume the bar is statically determinate: one end is fixed and the axial load P is applied at the free end (or equivalently, P is constant along the entire length).
Assume no body forces, no temperature change, and no lateral loads.
Assume plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli bar assumption for axial loading).

Given quantities are: A0 = 250 mm², k = 0.8, L = 800 mm, P = 30000 N, E = 200000 MPa.
The cross-sectional area varies as A(x) = A0·(1 + k·x/L), where x is measured from the left (loaded or fixed) end.

Governing relation 1 — Axial stress at position x:
σ(x) = P / A(x) = P / [A0·(1 + k·x/L)]

Governing relation 2 — Axial strain at position x:
ε(x) = σ(x) / E = P / [E·A0·(1 + k·x/L)]

Governing relation 3 — Differential axial deformation:
du = ε(x) dx = P dx / [E·A0·(1 + k·x/L)]

Governing relation 4 — Total axial deformation (integrate from x = 0 to x = L):
δ = ∫₀ᴸ P / [E·A0·(1 + k·x/L)] dx

Evaluate the integral by substitution u = 1 + k·x/L, du = k/(L) dx, so dx = L/(k) du:
δ = (P·L) / (E·A0·k) · ∫₁^(1+k) du/u = (P·L) / (E·A0·k) · ln(1 + k)

Substitute numerical values:
δ = (30000 × 800) / (200000 × 250 × 0.8) · ln(1 + 0.8)
δ = (24000000) / (40000000) · ln(1.8)
δ = 0.6 · ln(1.8)
ln(1.8) = 0.5878
δ = 0.6 × 0.5878 = 0.3527 mm

Compute stress at the left end (x = 0):
σ(0) = P / A0 = 30000 / 250 = 120 MPa

Compute stress at the right end (x = L):
A(L) = A0·(1 + k) = 250 × 1.8 = 450 mm²
σ(L) = 30000 / 450 = 66.67 MPa

Compute strain at the left end (x = 0):
ε(0) = σ(0) / E = 120 / 200000 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁴

Compute strain at the right end (x = L):
ε(L) = σ(L) / E = 66.67 / 200000 = 3.333 × 10⁻⁴

Summary of results:
Total axial deformation δ = 0.3527 mm (elongation).
Axial stress varies from 120 MPa at x = 0 to 66.67 MPa at x = L.
Axial strain varies from 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ at x = 0 to 3.333 × 10⁻⁴ at x = L.
The stress and strain distributions are hyperbolically decreasing from left to right as the area increases.
