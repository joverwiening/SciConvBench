The task is a small-deformation, linear-elastic, isotropic solid mechanics problem focused on the Poisson effect under uniaxial tension for a circular rod.

The rod has a circular cross-section with diameter d0 = 25 mm.
Compute the cross-sectional area as A = π × d0² / 4.
The rod has length L = 1200 mm.
The elastic modulus is E = 200000 MPa.
Poisson's ratio is ν = 0.29.
The rod is subjected to an axial tensile load P applied along its longitudinal axis (x-axis).
No lateral (transverse) load is active for the Poisson-effect case; the lateral load mentioned in the problem statement is set aside and only uniaxial tension is considered here.
Assume the rod is homogeneous, isotropic, and behaves in a linear-elastic manner throughout.
Assume small deformations and small strains.
Assume the cross-section remains plane and perpendicular to the axis after deformation (Euler-Bernoulli / Saint-Venant uniaxial assumption).
Assume no buckling, no body forces, and no temperature effects.

Compute the cross-sectional area: A = π × (25)² / 4 in mm².
Define the axial stress: σ_x = P / A, where P is the applied axial tensile force in N, giving σ_x in MPa.
State that all other direct stress components are zero: σ_y = 0, σ_z = 0.
State that all shear stress components are zero: τ_xy = τ_yz = τ_xz = 0.
Apply the generalized Hooke's law for an isotropic material to obtain the three direct strains.
Compute the axial (longitudinal) strain: ε_x = σ_x / E.
Compute the lateral strain in the y-direction: ε_y = -ν × σ_x / E.
Compute the lateral strain in the z-direction: ε_z = -ν × σ_x / E.
Note that ε_y = ε_z by symmetry of the circular cross-section under uniaxial load.
State all shear strains are zero: γ_xy = γ_yz = γ_xz = 0.
Compute the axial elongation of the rod: δ_x = ε_x × L.
Compute the change in diameter: Δd = ε_y × d0 (this value is negative for tension, indicating lateral contraction).
Compute the new diameter after deformation: d_final = d0 + Δd = d0 × (1 + ε_y).
Compute the volumetric strain (dilatation): e = ε_x + ε_y + ε_z = ε_x × (1 - 2ν).
Express all results in terms of the applied load P, and also present numerical values once P is specified.
Present the governing relations in the following order: area calculation, axial stress, axial strain, lateral strain, axial elongation, lateral contraction, and volumetric strain.
State explicitly that the lateral contraction is a direct consequence of the Poisson effect and that the ratio of lateral strain to axial strain equals -ν = -0.29.
