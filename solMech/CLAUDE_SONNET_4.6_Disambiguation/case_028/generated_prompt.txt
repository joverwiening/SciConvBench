The rod has length L = 1200 mm, circular cross-section with diameter d = 30 mm, cross-sectional area A = π(30)²/4 = 706.86 mm², and elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
The rod is vertical and fixed at one end; the dropped weight W = 500 N is released from height h = 200 mm above the free (top) end of the rod and strikes it axially.
There is no separate static axial preload; the only loading is the impact.
Neglect the mass of the rod (massless bar assumption).
Assume the weight and rod remain in contact after impact (no rebound during the loading phase).
Assume the impact is perfectly plastic in the sense that the striker does not bounce — the energy method (equivalent static load approach) is used.
Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behavior throughout; no yielding occurs.
Assume the cross-section is uniform along the entire length.

Governing Relations:

Compute the static deflection of the rod under the weight W applied as a static axial load.
δ_st = WL / (AE)
δ_st = (500 × 1200) / (706.86 × 200000)
δ_st = 600000 / 141372000
δ_st = 0.004244 mm

Compute the impact factor K_d using the energy method.
K_d = 1 + sqrt(1 + 2h / δ_st)
K_d = 1 + sqrt(1 + 2 × 200 / 0.004244)
K_d = 1 + sqrt(1 + 94249.8)
K_d = 1 + sqrt(94250.8)
K_d = 1 + 306.99
K_d ≈ 307.99

Compute the maximum dynamic deflection.
δ_max = K_d × δ_st
δ_max = 307.99 × 0.004244
δ_max ≈ 1.307 mm

Compute the equivalent dynamic (impact) force.
P_d = K_d × W
P_d = 307.99 × 500
P_d ≈ 154000 N = 154 kN

Compute the maximum dynamic axial stress.
σ_max = P_d / A
σ_max = 154000 / 706.86
σ_max ≈ 217.9 MPa

Compute the maximum dynamic axial strain.
ε_max = σ_max / E
ε_max = 217.9 / 200000
ε_max ≈ 1.089 × 10⁻³

State the governing energy balance used.
The potential energy of the falling weight equals the strain energy stored in the rod at maximum deflection: W(h + δ_max) = (1/2)(AE/L)(δ_max)².
This leads to the quadratic: (AE/L)(δ_max)² − 2W(δ_max) − 2Wh = 0, whose positive root gives δ_max, consistent with the impact factor formula above.

Additional assumptions stated:
The contact between the weight and the rod top is rigid (no local contact deformation).
The rod is fixed rigidly at its base with no support compliance.
The weight falls concentrically along the rod axis so no bending is induced.
Dynamic effects such as stress wave propagation are neglected; a quasi-static energy approach is used.
