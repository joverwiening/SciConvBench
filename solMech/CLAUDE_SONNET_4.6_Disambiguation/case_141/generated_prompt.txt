The shaft has a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 35 mm and radius r = 17.5 mm.
The analysis is restricted to the critical cross-section only; shaft length is not required.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, isotropic, homogeneous material behavior throughout.
Assume no axial force and no transverse shear force act at the critical section.
The applied bending moment is M = 3,000,000 N·mm.
The applied torque is T = 500,000 N·mm.
No additional moment beyond M and T is specified; treat the problem as combined bending plus torsion only.

Cross-section properties:
Compute the second moment of area for bending: I = π d⁴ / 64.
With d = 35 mm, I = π (35)⁴ / 64 = π (1,500,625) / 64 ≈ 73,662 mm⁴.
Compute the polar moment of area for torsion: J = π d⁴ / 32 = 2I.
With d = 35 mm, J = π (35)⁴ / 32 ≈ 147,324 mm⁴.

Bending stress:
The bending normal stress at a fiber located at distance y from the neutral axis is σ_x = M y / I.
The maximum bending normal stress occurs at the outermost fiber, y = r = 17.5 mm.
Compute σ_x_max = M r / I = 3,000,000 × 17.5 / 73,662 ≈ 712.9 MPa.
This stress is tensile on one side of the neutral axis and compressive on the other.
At the neutral axis, the bending normal stress is zero.

Torsional shear stress:
The torsional shear stress at radius ρ from the centroidal axis is τ = T ρ / J.
The maximum torsional shear stress occurs at the outer surface, ρ = r = 17.5 mm.
Compute τ_max = T r / J = 500,000 × 17.5 / 147,324 ≈ 59.4 MPa.
The torsional shear stress is uniform around the circumference at any given radius.

Critical point identification:
The critical point is at the outermost fiber on the side where bending produces maximum tensile normal stress.
At this point the state of stress is: normal stress σ_x = +712.9 MPa, shear stress τ = 59.4 MPa, and σ_y = 0.

Principal stress calculation:
Use the plane-stress principal stress formula: σ_1,2 = (σ_x / 2) ± sqrt((σ_x / 2)² + τ²).
Compute σ_x / 2 = 356.45 MPa.
Compute (σ_x / 2)² = 127,056.6 MPa².
Compute τ² = 3,528.4 MPa².
Compute sqrt(127,056.6 + 3,528.4) = sqrt(130,585) ≈ 361.4 MPa.
Principal stress σ_1 = 356.45 + 361.4 ≈ 717.8 MPa (tensile).
Principal stress σ_2 = 356.45 − 361.4 ≈ −4.9 MPa (compressive).
The third principal stress σ_3 = 0 (free surface, plane stress).

Maximum in-plane shear stress:
τ_max_inplane = sqrt((σ_x / 2)² + τ²) ≈ 361.4 MPa.
The absolute maximum shear stress is τ_abs_max = (σ_1 − σ_2) / 2 = (717.8 − (−4.9)) / 2 ≈ 361.4 MPa.

Principal angle:
The angle of the principal plane from the x-axis is θ_p = 0.5 arctan(2τ / σ_x) = 0.5 arctan(2 × 59.4 / 712.9) = 0.5 arctan(0.1666) ≈ 0.5 × 9.46° ≈ 4.73°.

Von Mises equivalent stress:
Compute σ_vm = sqrt(σ_1² − σ_1 σ_2 + σ_2²).
σ_vm = sqrt((717.8)² − (717.8)(−4.9) + (−4.9)²) = sqrt(515,237 + 3,517 + 24) ≈ sqrt(518,778) ≈ 720.3 MPa.
Alternatively, σ_vm = sqrt(σ_x² + 3τ²) = sqrt(712.9² + 3 × 59.4²) = sqrt(508,226 + 10,585) ≈ sqrt(518,811) ≈ 720.3 MPa.

Principal strains (assuming Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3 and elastic modulus E as material parameters):
ε_1 = (1/E)(σ_1 − ν σ_2) = (1/E)(717.8 − 0.3 × (−4.9)) = (1/E)(717.8 + 1.47) = 719.3/E.
ε_2 = (1/E)(σ_2 − ν σ_1) = (1/E)(−4.9 − 0.3 × 717.8) = (1/E)(−4.9 − 215.3) = −220.2/E.
ε_3 = (−ν/E)(σ_1 + σ_2) = (−0.3/E)(717.8 − 4.9) = −212.97/E.
Substitute the actual value of E in MPa to obtain numerical strain values.

Additional assumptions stated:
The cross-section remains plane after deformation (Euler-Bernoulli bending assumption).
Saint-Venant's torsion theory applies; warping is neglected for a solid circular section.
The material is isotropic and homogeneous with constant E and ν.
Stress concentrations, residual stresses, and geometric imperfections are neglected.
The critical-section stresses are computed at the point of maximum combined effect, which is the outermost fiber on the tension side of bending.
