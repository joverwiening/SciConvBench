The rod is a vertical, axially loaded member fixed at the top end, with a collar at the bottom end onto which a weight is dropped.

Geometry: length L = 800 mm, diameter d = 25 mm, cross-sectional area A = π(25)²/4 = 490.874 mm².

Material: AISI 1020 steel, elastic modulus E = 200 000 MPa (N/mm²).

Loading: a weight W = 1500 N is dropped from a height h = 200 mm onto the collar at the free (bottom) end of the rod, causing axial tension (stretching).

Neglect the mass of the rod itself (static weight of rod ignored).

Assume small deformations and linear-elastic behaviour throughout.

Assume the collar and the dropped weight remain in contact after impact (no rebound) and that all kinetic energy at impact is absorbed elastically by the rod.

Assume no energy is lost to heat, sound, or plastic deformation (perfectly elastic impact).

Assume the load is applied suddenly and uniformly over the cross-section (one-dimensional stress state, uniaxial).

Step 1 — Static deformation under W treated as a static load.
Compute the static elongation: δ_st = WL / (AE).
δ_st = (1500 × 800) / (490.874 × 200 000).
δ_st = 1 200 000 / 98 174 800 = 0.01222 mm.

Step 2 — Impact factor.
Using energy conservation, the equivalent static load that produces the same maximum deformation as the impact is found from:
(1/2) k δ_max² = W (h + δ_max), where k = AE/L is the axial stiffness of the rod.
k = (490.874 × 200 000) / 800 = 122 718.5 N/mm.
This gives the quadratic: (k/2) δ_max² − W δ_max − Wh = 0.
Solve: δ_max = (W/k) + sqrt((W/k)² + 2Wh/k).
δ_max = δ_st + sqrt(δ_st² + 2Wh/k).
Numerically: δ_st = 0.01222 mm, 2Wh/k = (2 × 1500 × 200) / 122 718.5 = 4.8897 mm².
sqrt(0.01222² + 4.8897) = sqrt(0.0001493 + 4.8897) = sqrt(4.8898) = 2.2113 mm.
δ_max = 0.01222 + 2.2113 = 2.2235 mm.

Step 3 — Impact factor n.
n = δ_max / δ_st = 2.2235 / 0.01222 = 181.9 (approximately).
Alternatively, n = 1 + sqrt(1 + 2h/δ_st).

Step 4 — Maximum dynamic (equivalent static) force.
P_max = k × δ_max = 122 718.5 × 2.2235 = 272 870 N ≈ 272.9 kN.
Equivalently, P_max = n × W = 181.9 × 1500 ≈ 272 850 N.

Step 5 — Maximum dynamic stress.
σ_max = P_max / A = 272 870 / 490.874 = 555.9 MPa.

Step 6 — Check against material yield strength.
AISI 1020 steel has a yield strength of approximately 210 MPa (hot-rolled) to 380 MPa (cold-drawn).
σ_max = 555.9 MPa exceeds the yield strength in both conditions.
Therefore the small-deformation linear-elastic assumption is violated; the rod would yield under this impact.
The elastic solution gives an upper-bound estimate of stress; a plastic or energy-absorption analysis would be required for a physically accurate result.

Summary of governing relations used:
Static stiffness: k = AE/L.
Static deflection: δ_st = W/k = WL/(AE).
Energy balance: W(h + δ_max) = (1/2) k δ_max².
Maximum deflection: δ_max = δ_st + sqrt(δ_st² + 2Wh/k).
Impact factor: n = δ_max / δ_st.
Maximum dynamic force: P_max = n W.
Maximum dynamic stress: σ_max = P_max / A.
