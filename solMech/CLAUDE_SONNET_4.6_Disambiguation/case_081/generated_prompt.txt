The shaft has a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 30 mm and radius r = 15 mm.
The analysis is restricted to the critical cross-section only; shaft length is not required.
Applied loads at the critical cross-section are: bending moment M = 4,000,000 N·mm and torque T = 400,000 N·mm.
No axial force, transverse shear force, or pressure loading is present.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, isotropic, homogeneous material behavior.
Assume the shaft material is steel with E = 200,000 MPa and ν = 0.3 unless a different material is specified; state this as an assumption.
Assume the cross-section remains plane and perpendicular to the shaft axis (Euler-Bernoulli / Saint-Venant assumptions apply).
Neglect stress concentrations, residual stresses, and temperature effects.
The critical point is located on the outer surface of the shaft at the position of maximum bending stress (top or bottom fiber), where bending stress is maximum and torsional shear stress is also maximum.

Compute the second moment of area for bending: I = π d^4 / 64.
With d = 30 mm, I = π (30)^4 / 64 = 39,760.78 mm^4.

Compute the polar moment of area for torsion: J = π d^4 / 32.
With d = 30 mm, J = π (30)^4 / 32 = 79,521.56 mm^4.

Compute the maximum bending (normal) stress at the outer fiber: σ = M r / I.
σ = 4,000,000 × 15 / 39,760.78 = 1,509.55 MPa.

Compute the maximum torsional shear stress at the outer surface: τ = T r / J.
τ = 400,000 × 15 / 79,521.56 = 75.48 MPa.

At the critical point the stress state is: normal stress σ_x = 1,509.55 MPa, shear stress τ_xy = 75.48 MPa, and σ_y = 0.

Compute the principal stresses using σ_1,2 = (σ_x / 2) ± sqrt((σ_x / 2)^2 + τ_xy^2).
σ_1 = 754.78 + sqrt(754.78^2 + 75.48^2) = 754.78 + sqrt(569,772.45 + 5,697.23) = 754.78 + sqrt(575,469.68) = 754.78 + 758.53 = 1,513.31 MPa.
σ_2 = 754.78 − 758.53 = −3.75 MPa.

Compute the maximum in-plane shear stress: τ_max = sqrt((σ_x / 2)^2 + τ_xy^2) = 758.53 MPa.

Compute the principal angle: θ_p = 0.5 arctan(2 τ_xy / σ_x) = 0.5 arctan(150.96 / 1,509.55) = 0.5 arctan(0.09934) ≈ 2.84 degrees.

Compute the von Mises (equivalent) stress: σ_vm = sqrt(σ_1^2 − σ_1 σ_2 + σ_2^2) = sqrt(1,513.31^2 − (1,513.31)(−3.75) + (−3.75)^2).
σ_vm = sqrt(2,290,107.76 + 5,674.91 + 14.06) = sqrt(2,295,796.73) ≈ 1,515.52 MPa.

Compute the principal strains using ε_1 = (1/E)(σ_1 − ν σ_2) and ε_2 = (1/E)(σ_2 − ν σ_1).
ε_1 = (1/200,000)(1,513.31 − 0.3 × (−3.75)) = (1,513.31 + 1.125) / 200,000 = 1,514.435 / 200,000 = 7.572 × 10^-3.
ε_2 = (1/200,000)(−3.75 − 0.3 × 1,513.31) = (−3.75 − 453.99) / 200,000 = −457.74 / 200,000 = −2.289 × 10^-3.

State the governing relations used: σ = M c / I (flexure formula), τ = T c / J (torsion formula), principal stress formula from 2D Mohr's circle, von Mises criterion σ_vm = sqrt(σ_1^2 − σ_1 σ_2 + σ_2^2), and generalized Hooke's law for plane stress.

Note: the bending stress value of approximately 1,510 MPa far exceeds the yield strength of typical steel (250–700 MPa), so in practice this shaft would yield and a plastic or fatigue analysis would be required; the results here are valid only within the stated linear-elastic assumption.
