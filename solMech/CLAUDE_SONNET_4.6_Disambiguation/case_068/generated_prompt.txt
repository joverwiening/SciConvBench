The shaft is a solid circular cross-section subjected to pure torsion only; there is no transverse shear force.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior throughout.
Assume the shaft is homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic (constant cross-section along its length).
Assume the torque T is applied about the longitudinal axis and is constant along the full length L.
Assume warping is unrestrained and Saint-Venant torsion theory applies.

Given values:
Torque T = 500,000 N·mm
Diameter d = 30 mm, outer radius c = 15 mm
Length L = 800 mm
Shear modulus G = 25,939.8 MPa

Compute the polar moment of inertia J for a solid circular cross-section using J = (pi / 32) * d^4.
J = (pi / 32) * (30)^4 = (pi / 32) * 810,000 = 79,521.56 mm^4.

Governing relation for maximum shear stress: tau_max = T * c / J.
tau_max = 500,000 * 15 / 79,521.56 = 7,500,000 / 79,521.56 = 94.31 MPa.
This maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface of the shaft.
The shear stress varies linearly from zero at the centroidal axis to tau_max at the outer radius.
At any interior radius r, the shear stress is tau(r) = T * r / J.

Governing relation for angle of twist: phi = T * L / (G * J).
phi = 500,000 * 800 / (25,939.8 * 79,521.56).
Numerator = 400,000,000 N·mm^2.
Denominator = 25,939.8 * 79,521.56 = 2,062,500,000 N·mm^2 (approximately).
phi = 400,000,000 / 2,062,500,000 = 0.19394 radians.
Convert to degrees: phi = 0.19394 * (180 / pi) = 11.11 degrees.

Additional assumptions stated:
The material remains within the elastic limit so Hooke's law in shear applies: tau = G * gamma.
Plane cross-sections remain plane after twisting (valid for circular cross-sections under Saint-Venant torsion).
The ends of the shaft are free to warp; no axial constraint is imposed.
Self-weight and any body forces are neglected.
