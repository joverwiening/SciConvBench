A straight prismatic circular rod is subjected to a uniaxial tensile axial load.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics throughout.
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and ductile (yielding governs failure).
Assume the load P is applied concentrically along the centroidal axis, so bending is absent.
Assume the cross-section is uniform along the entire length (prismatic rod).
Assume stress concentrations are neglected (average stress is used).
Assume the rod is in static equilibrium.

Given values:
Axial load P = 30000 N (tensile).
Rod diameter d = 12 mm.
Rod length L = 800 mm.
Yield strength σ_y = 250 MPa.
Elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.

Step 1 — Cross-sectional area:
Compute the cross-sectional area as A = π × d² / 4.
A = π × (12)² / 4 = π × 144 / 4 = 36π ≈ 113.10 mm².

Step 2 — Average normal stress:
Apply the axial stress formula σ = P / A.
σ = 30000 N / 113.10 mm² ≈ 265.3 MPa (tensile).

Step 3 — Axial strain:
Apply Hooke's Law: ε = σ / E.
ε = 265.3 MPa / 200000 MPa ≈ 1.326 × 10⁻³ (dimensionless).

Step 4 — Elongation:
Compute elongation as δ = ε × L, equivalently δ = P × L / (A × E).
δ = 1.326 × 10⁻³ × 800 mm ≈ 1.061 mm.

Step 5 — Factor of safety with respect to yielding:
FS = σ_y / σ = 250 MPa / 265.3 MPa ≈ 0.943.

Step 6 — Interpretation of factor of safety:
Since FS ≈ 0.943 < 1.0, the applied stress (265.3 MPa) exceeds the yield strength (250 MPa).
The rod has yielded under the given load; the design is unsafe under this loading condition.
To achieve a minimum FS = 1.0, the applied stress must not exceed 250 MPa, requiring a minimum area of A_min = P / σ_y = 30000 / 250 = 120 mm², corresponding to a minimum diameter of d_min = √(4 × 120 / π) ≈ 12.36 mm.

Summary of results:
Cross-sectional area A ≈ 113.10 mm².
Average normal stress σ ≈ 265.3 MPa (tensile).
Axial strain ε ≈ 1.326 × 10⁻³.
Elongation δ ≈ 1.061 mm.
Factor of safety FS ≈ 0.943 — rod has yielded; design is inadequate.
