The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 40 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The applied bending moment is M = 4,000,000 N·mm, acting as a sagging (positive) moment.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
Assume the beam is homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic along its length.
Assume the cross-section is symmetric about both the horizontal and vertical centroidal axes.
The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section, located at h/2 = 50 mm from either extreme fiber.
Define y as the distance measured from the neutral axis, positive upward, ranging from -50 mm to +50 mm.

Compute the second moment of area (moment of inertia) about the neutral axis:
I = (b * h^3) / 12 = (40 * 100^3) / 12 = 3,333,333.33 mm^4.

The governing relation for bending stress at any fiber location y is the flexure formula:
sigma(y) = M * y / I.

For a sagging moment (positive M convention), the top fiber is in compression and the bottom fiber is in tension.

Evaluate bending stress at the top fiber (y = +50 mm):
sigma_top = 4,000,000 * 50 / 3,333,333.33 = +60 N/mm^2 (but this is the top fiber under sagging, so it is compressive; apply sign: sigma_top = -60 MPa in compression).

Evaluate bending stress at the bottom fiber (y = -50 mm):
sigma_bottom = 4,000,000 * (-50) / 3,333,333.33 = -60 N/mm^2 magnitude, but under sagging the bottom fiber is in tension: sigma_bottom = +60 MPa in tension.

For an arbitrary fiber at distance y from the neutral axis:
sigma(y) = (4,000,000 / 3,333,333.33) * y = 1.2 * y MPa, where y is in mm.

The stress varies linearly from -60 MPa at the top (y = +50 mm, compression) to zero at the neutral axis (y = 0) to +60 MPa at the bottom (y = -50 mm, tension).

The maximum bending stress magnitude (occurring at the extreme fibers) is:
sigma_max = M * c / I, where c = h/2 = 50 mm, giving sigma_max = 60 MPa.

The section modulus is S = I / c = 3,333,333.33 / 50 = 66,666.67 mm^3, and sigma_max = M / S = 4,000,000 / 66,666.67 = 60 MPa.

No shear force is specified, so transverse shear stress is not computed here.
No axial force is specified, so no axial (membrane) stress is superimposed.
