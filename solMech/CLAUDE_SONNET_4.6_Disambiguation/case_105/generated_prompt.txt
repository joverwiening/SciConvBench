The bar is vertical, fixed at the top, and free at the bottom.
The bar has length L = 3500 mm, cross-sectional area A = 200 mm², Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa (N/mm²), mass density ρ = 7850 kg/m³, and gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².
Assume small deformation and linear-elastic material behaviour throughout.
Assume the cross-section is uniform (constant A) along the entire length.
Assume the self-weight is the only applied load (no external axial force at the free end).
Neglect any lateral (Poisson) effects and buckling; treat this as a pure axial problem.

Convert density to N/mm³ units as follows: ρ = 7850 kg/m³ = 7850 × 10⁻⁹ kg/mm³; multiply by g = 9810 mm/s² to get the weight density γ = 7850 × 10⁻⁹ × 9810 = 7.7009 × 10⁻⁵ N/mm³.

Define the coordinate x measured downward from the fixed top end, so x = 0 at the top and x = L at the free bottom.

The axial (tensile-positive) internal force at position x is the weight of the bar below that cross-section.
N(x) = γ · A · (L − x), in Newtons.

At x = 0 (top, maximum internal force): N(0) = γ · A · L = 7.7009 × 10⁻⁵ × 200 × 3500 = 53.906 N.
At x = L (bottom, free end): N(L) = 0 N.

The axial stress at position x is σ(x) = N(x) / A = γ · (L − x), in MPa.
At x = 0: σ(0) = 7.7009 × 10⁻⁵ × 3500 = 0.26953 MPa (maximum, tensile).
At x = L: σ(L) = 0 MPa.

The axial strain at position x is ε(x) = σ(x) / E = γ · (L − x) / E (dimensionless).
At x = 0: ε(0) = 0.26953 / 69000 = 3.906 × 10⁻⁶.
At x = L: ε(L) = 0.

The differential elongation of an infinitesimal slice dx is dδ = ε(x) dx = [γ(L − x) / E] dx.

Integrate from x = 0 to x = L to obtain the total elongation of the bar.
δ = ∫₀ᴸ [γ(L − x) / E] dx = (γ / E) · [Lx − x²/2] evaluated from 0 to L = (γ / E) · (L² − L²/2) = γ L² / (2E).

Substitute numerical values: δ = (7.7009 × 10⁻⁵ × 3500²) / (2 × 69000).
Numerator: 7.7009 × 10⁻⁵ × 12,250,000 = 943.86 N/mm.
Denominator: 138,000 N/mm².
δ = 943.86 / 138,000 = 6.840 × 10⁻³ mm.

The total elongation of the bar due to self-weight is δ ≈ 0.00684 mm (6.84 × 10⁻³ mm).

Note this equals half the elongation that would result from applying the entire self-weight (W = γAL = 53.906 N) as a concentrated end load, because the self-weight is distributed and the average load over the length is W/2.
