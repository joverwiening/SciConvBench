The column is a prismatic, straight, homogeneous member under pure axial compressive load.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behaviour (Euler buckling theory applies).
Assume the cross-section is uniform along the full length L.
Assume the load is applied concentrically (no eccentricity, no initial imperfection).
Assume the material remains elastic at the critical load (Euler theory is valid; no inelastic buckling).

Given data:
Length L = 2000 mm.
Moment of inertia I = 1,200,000 mm⁴.
Young's modulus E = 200,000 MPa (N/mm²).
End condition: Fixed-pinned with effective length factor K = 0.7.

Compute the effective length:
Le = K × L = 0.7 × 2000 = 1400 mm.

Governing relation — Euler critical buckling load:
P_cr = (π² × E × I) / Le²

Substitute values:
P_cr = (π² × 200,000 × 1,200,000) / (1400²)
P_cr = (9.8696 × 200,000 × 1,200,000) / 1,960,000
P_cr = (2,368,704,000,000) / 1,960,000
P_cr = 1,208,522 N
P_cr ≈ 1208.5 kN

Buckling/instability check:
The applied compressive load P must satisfy P ≤ P_cr for stability.
The buckling utilisation ratio is defined as η = P / P_cr.
If η ≤ 1.0, the column is stable; if η > 1.0, the column buckles.
No applied load magnitude was specified, so the check is expressed in terms of the ratio η = P / 1208.5 kN.

Additional assumptions stated:
Lateral loads and moments are absent; the column carries axial compression only.
Torsional and lateral-torsional buckling modes are not considered here; only flexural (Euler) buckling is evaluated.
Self-weight of the column is neglected.
The value K = 0.7 is accepted as given for the fixed-pinned condition (theoretical value is 0.6999 ≈ 0.7).
