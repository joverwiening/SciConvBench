The material is 6061-T6 aluminum alloy.
Use the following material properties: Young's modulus E = 68.9 GPa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, yield strength 276 MPa.
The rod has a circular cross-section with diameter d₀ = 40 mm and length L = 500 mm.
The cross-sectional area is A = π(d₀/2)² = π(0.020)² ≈ 1.2566 × 10⁻³ m².
The problem focuses solely on the Poisson effect under uniaxial tension; the lateral load is not active for this case.
Apply an axial tensile load P along the longitudinal axis (x-axis) of the rod.
Assume the rod is homogeneous, isotropic, and obeys Hooke's law throughout.
Assume small deformations so that geometry changes do not affect equilibrium.
Assume the cross-section remains plane and the stress state is uniaxial: σ_x = P/A, σ_y = σ_z = 0, all shear stresses = 0.

Governing relations:

The axial (longitudinal) normal stress is σ_x = P / A.
The axial (longitudinal) normal strain is ε_x = σ_x / E = P / (A · E).
The lateral (transverse) normal strains due to the Poisson effect are ε_y = ε_z = −ν · ε_x = −ν · σ_x / E.
The axial elongation of the rod is δ_x = ε_x · L = P · L / (A · E).
The change in diameter is Δd = ε_y · d₀ = −ν · (P / (A · E)) · d₀.
The negative sign on Δd indicates the diameter decreases under tensile load.
The volumetric strain (dilatation) is e = ε_x + ε_y + ε_z = ε_x (1 − 2ν).

Response quantities in terms of applied load P:

σ_x = P / (1.2566 × 10⁻³) [Pa], with P in Newtons.
ε_x = σ_x / (68.9 × 10⁹).
ε_y = ε_z = −0.33 · ε_x.
δ_x = P · 0.500 / (1.2566 × 10⁻³ · 68.9 × 10⁹) = P · 5.766 × 10⁻⁹ [m/N].
Δd = −0.33 · (P / (1.2566 × 10⁻³ · 68.9 × 10⁹)) · 0.040 [m].
e = ε_x · (1 − 2 · 0.33) = 0.34 · ε_x.

Yield check: ensure σ_x = P/A ≤ 276 MPa; maximum allowable load is P_max = 276 × 10⁶ × 1.2566 × 10⁻³ ≈ 346.8 kN before yielding invalidates the linear-elastic assumption.
