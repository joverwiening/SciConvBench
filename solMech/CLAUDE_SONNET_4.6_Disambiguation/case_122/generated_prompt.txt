The cross-section is a solid rectangle with width b = 100 mm and height h = 100 mm.

The applied load is P = 90 000 N, acting in compression (axial direction, taken as negative by the compressive-positive sign convention where compressive stress is negative).

The load is applied with a single eccentricity e = 15 mm measured along the width direction (x-direction), producing bending about the centroidal z-axis only.

There is no eccentricity in the h-direction, no additional applied bending moments, and no applied tension load.

Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behaviour throughout.

Assume the column is short enough that buckling and second-order (P-delta) effects are neglected.

Assume the cross-section is homogeneous and isotropic.

Assume the Euler-Bernoulli (plane-sections-remain-plane) hypothesis applies.

Compute the cross-sectional area: A = b × h = 100 × 100 = 10 000 mm².

Compute the second moment of area about the centroidal z-axis: I_z = b × h³ / 12 = 100 × 100³ / 12 = 8 333 333 mm⁴.

Resolve the eccentric load into an equivalent centroidal axial force P = 90 000 N (compressive) plus a bending moment M_z = P × e = 90 000 × 15 = 1 350 000 N·mm, acting about the centroidal z-axis.

The direct (uniform) axial stress is σ_axial = −P / A = −90 000 / 10 000 = −9 N/mm² (compressive, uniform over the section).

The bending stress distribution is σ_bending(x) = −M_z × x / I_z, where x is measured from the centroid, ranging from −50 mm to +50 mm.

The sign convention for bending stress follows: the eccentricity is in the positive x-direction, so the moment M_z causes compression on the positive-x face and tension on the negative-x face (using the standard flexure formula with the moment sense established by the eccentric load direction).

The combined normal stress at any point x on the cross-section is σ(x) = −P/A − (M_z × x) / I_z.

Evaluate stress at the extreme fibre on the same side as the eccentricity (x = +50 mm): σ = −9 − (1 350 000 × 50) / 8 333 333 = −9 − 8.1 = −17.1 N/mm² (compressive).

Evaluate stress at the extreme fibre on the opposite side (x = −50 mm): σ = −9 − (1 350 000 × (−50)) / 8 333 333 = −9 + 8.1 = −0.9 N/mm² (compressive).

The entire cross-section remains in compression because the maximum bending stress magnitude (8.1 N/mm²) is less than the direct compressive stress (9 N/mm²).

Locate the neutral axis (zero-stress line) by setting σ(x) = 0: x_na = −(P/A) × (I_z / M_z) = −9 × (8 333 333 / 1 350 000) = −55.6 mm.

Because x_na = −55.6 mm lies outside the cross-section boundary (which extends only to x = −50 mm), this confirms no tensile stress exists anywhere on the section.

The kern (core) of the rectangular section is the region within which the load must be applied to avoid tensile stress; for a rectangle it extends ±b/6 in the x-direction and ±h/6 in the y-direction, giving ±16.67 mm in each direction.

The eccentricity e = 15 mm is within the kern limit of 16.67 mm, which is consistent with the finding that the entire section is in compression.

The maximum compressive stress is 17.1 N/mm², located at the extreme fibre on the side toward which the load is eccentric (x = +50 mm).

The minimum compressive stress is 0.9 N/mm², located at the extreme fibre on the opposite side (x = −50 mm).
