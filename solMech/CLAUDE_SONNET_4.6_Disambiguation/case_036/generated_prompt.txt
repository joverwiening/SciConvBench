The task is to design a solid circular cross-section column for buckling safety and evaluate the factor of safety against buckling.

Material is structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200,000 MPa.

Applied compressive load is P = 30,000 N.

Column length is L = 1,800 mm.

Both ends are pinned, giving an effective length factor K = 1.0, so the effective length Le = K × L = 1.0 × 1,800 = 1,800 mm.

The required factor of safety against buckling is n = 2.0.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.

Assume the column is perfectly straight and centrally loaded (ideal Euler column).

Assume the column is slender enough that elastic buckling governs (Euler buckling), and verify this assumption after finding the diameter.

Assume uniform cross-section along the full length.

The yield strength of structural steel is taken as 250 MPa for slenderness verification purposes; state this assumption explicitly in the solution.

The governing relation is the Euler critical buckling load: Pcr = (pi^2 × E × I) / Le^2.

For a solid circular cross-section of diameter d, the second moment of area is I = pi × d^4 / 64.

Substituting: Pcr = (pi^2 × E × pi × d^4 / 64) / Le^2 = (pi^3 × E × d^4) / (64 × Le^2).

The design requirement is Pcr >= n × P, so set Pcr = n × P = 2.0 × 30,000 = 60,000 N.

Solve for the minimum required diameter d from: d^4 = (64 × Le^2 × n × P) / (pi^3 × E).

Substitute values: d^4 = (64 × 1,800^2 × 60,000) / (pi^3 × 200,000).

Compute numerator: 64 × 3,240,000 × 60,000 = 64 × 1.944 × 10^11 = 1.24416 × 10^13.

Compute denominator: pi^3 × 200,000 = 31.006 × 200,000 = 6,201,200 approximately.

Compute d^4 = 1.24416 × 10^13 / 6,201,200 = 2,006,300 mm^4 approximately.

Compute d = (2,006,300)^(1/4): square root of 2,006,300 ≈ 1,416.4, square root of 1,416.4 ≈ 37.6 mm.

Round up to the next practical size: select d = 38 mm as the minimum design diameter.

After selecting d, compute the actual I = pi × 38^4 / 64 = pi × 2,085,136 / 64 = 102,354 mm^4 approximately.

Compute the actual Pcr = (pi^2 × 200,000 × 102,354) / 1,800^2 = (1,973,921 × 102,354) / 3,240,000 ≈ 62,300 N approximately.

Evaluate the actual factor of safety: n_actual = Pcr / P = 62,300 / 30,000 ≈ 2.08, which exceeds the required n = 2.0, so the design is acceptable.

Verify that Euler buckling governs by computing the slenderness ratio: radius of gyration r = d/4 = 38/4 = 9.5 mm; slenderness ratio = Le / r = 1,800 / 9.5 ≈ 189.5.

The transition slenderness ratio for structural steel (250 MPa yield) is approximately sqrt(2 × pi^2 × E / Fy) ≈ sqrt(2 × pi^2 × 200,000 / 250) ≈ 126; since 189.5 > 126, elastic Euler buckling governs and the assumption is confirmed valid.

Present the final design diameter as d = 38 mm with an actual factor of safety of approximately 2.08 against elastic buckling.

State all assumptions: ideal straight column, concentric axial load, uniform cross-section, linear-elastic material, Euler buckling governs (confirmed by slenderness check), yield strength assumed 250 MPa for verification only.
