The shaft is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 20 mm and length L = 1200 mm.
The material is 6061-T6 aluminum with shear modulus G = 25939.8 MPa.
All units are N, mm, and MPa throughout.
There is no transverse shear force; loading is pure torsion only.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior.
Assume the shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section along its entire length).
Assume the torque is steady and uniformly applied (no stress concentrations, no dynamic effects).

Step 1: Convert power and rotational speed to torque.

Power is given as P = 5 kW = 5000 W = 5,000,000 N·mm/s.
Rotational speed is n = 1200 rpm.
Convert rotational speed to radians per second: omega = 2 * pi * n / 60 = 2 * pi * 1200 / 60 = 125.664 rad/s.
Apply the power–torque relation: T = P / omega.
T = 5,000,000 / 125.664 = 39,788.7 N·mm.

Step 2: Compute the polar moment of inertia of the solid circular cross-section.

The polar moment of inertia is J = pi * d^4 / 32.
J = pi * (20)^4 / 32 = pi * 160,000 / 32 = 15,707.96 mm^4.

Step 3: Compute the maximum shear stress due to torsion.

The torsion formula is tau_max = T * c / J, where c = d / 2 = 10 mm is the outer radius.
tau_max = 39,788.7 * 10 / 15,707.96 = 25.33 MPa.
This maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface of the shaft.

Step 4: Compute the angle of twist.

The angle of twist formula is phi = T * L / (G * J).
phi = 39,788.7 * 1200 / (25939.8 * 15,707.96).
Numerator: 39,788.7 * 1200 = 47,746,440 N·mm^2.
Denominator: 25939.8 * 15,707.96 = 407,468,000 N·mm^2 (approximately).
phi = 47,746,440 / 407,468,000 = 0.11718 radians.
Convert to degrees: phi = 0.11718 * (180 / pi) = 6.713 degrees.

Summary of governing relations used:

omega = 2 * pi * n / 60 (rad/s), converts rpm to angular velocity.
T = P / omega, converts power and angular velocity to torque.
J = pi * d^4 / 32, polar moment of inertia for a solid circular shaft.
tau_max = T * c / J, maximum shear stress at the outer fiber (torsion formula, Bredt–Saint-Venant).
phi = T * L / (G * J), angle of twist over length L (linear-elastic torsion).

Additional assumptions stated:

The shaft is statically determinate and the full torque T is transmitted uniformly along the length L.
No stress concentrations, keyways, fillets, or geometric discontinuities are present.
The material is homogeneous and isotropic.
The cross-section does not warp in a manner that violates the Saint-Venant torsion assumption (circular sections do not warp, so this is exactly satisfied).
Self-weight and bending effects are neglected.
