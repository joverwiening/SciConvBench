The rod is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d0 = 30 mm and length L = 500 mm.
The material is isotropic and linear-elastic with elastic modulus E = 105000 MPa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.34.
Assume small deformations throughout.
Assume the rod is homogeneous and the cross-section is uniform along its entire length.
Assume the axial load P = 25000 N is applied as a pure uniaxial tensile force acting through the centroid of the cross-section, with no eccentricity.
Assume no lateral load is present; the problem is purely uniaxial tension.
Assume no bending, shear, or torsion is induced.
Assume both ends allow free lateral deformation (no lateral constraint), so the Poisson effect is unrestrained.

Compute the cross-sectional area A = π × (d0/2)² using d0 = 30 mm.
Compute the axial stress σ_x = P / A.
Compute the axial strain ε_x = σ_x / E.
Compute the lateral strain ε_y = ε_z = −ν × ε_x using the Poisson effect relation.
Compute the axial elongation δ_L = ε_x × L.
Compute the lateral deformation (change in diameter) δ_d = ε_y × d0, which will be a contraction (negative value) due to the Poisson effect.
Report σ_x (axial stress), ε_x (axial strain), ε_y = ε_z (lateral strains), δ_L (axial elongation), and δ_d (diametral change) as the relevant response quantities.
State clearly that the volumetric strain is ε_vol = ε_x + ε_y + ε_z = ε_x (1 − 2ν) as a supplementary result.
Use consistent units of N and mm throughout, giving stresses in MPa and deformations in mm.
