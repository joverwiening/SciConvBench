The problem is a vertical bar hanging under its own self-weight, analyzed under small-deformation linear-elastic solid mechanics.

The bar is uniform with constant cross-sectional area A = 200 mm², length L = 3500 mm = 3.5 m, Young's modulus E = 69000 MPa = 69000 N/mm², mass density ρ = 8900 kg/m³ = 8.9 × 10⁻⁶ kg/mm³, and gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².

Assume the bar is fixed at the top and free at the bottom.
Assume the bar is prismatic (uniform cross-section throughout).
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume self-weight is the only load (no external axial force applied).
Assume the deformation is small so linear elasticity applies throughout.

Define a coordinate x measured from the free bottom end upward to the fixed top end, so x ranges from 0 (bottom) to L (top).

The weight per unit length (force per unit length) is w = ρ × g × A.
Compute w: ρ = 8.9 × 10⁻⁶ kg/mm³, g = 9810 mm/s², A = 200 mm².
w = 8.9 × 10⁻⁶ × 9810 × 200 = 17.4618 N/mm.

At a cross-section located at distance x from the free bottom end, the internal axial force N(x) equals the weight of the bar below that section.
N(x) = w × x.

The axial stress at position x is σ(x) = N(x) / A = w × x / A.

The axial strain at position x is ε(x) = σ(x) / E = w × x / (A × E).

The total elongation δ is obtained by integrating the strain over the full length.
δ = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x) dx = ∫₀ᴸ [w × x / (A × E)] dx = w × L² / (2 × A × E).

This result is equivalent to δ = W × L / (2 × A × E), where W = w × L is the total weight of the bar, confirming the known result that self-weight elongation equals half of what a concentrated load equal to the total weight would produce.

Compute the numerator: w × L² = 17.4618 × (3500)² = 17.4618 × 12,250,000 = 213,907,050 N·mm.

Compute the denominator: 2 × A × E = 2 × 200 × 69000 = 27,600,000 N.

Compute δ = 213,907,050 / 27,600,000 = 7.75 mm.

The total elongation of the vertical bar due to self-weight is δ ≈ 7.75 mm.
