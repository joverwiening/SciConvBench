The structural member is a rectangular adhesive layer with plan area A = 4000 mm² and thickness t = 20 mm.
The applied lateral (shear) load is V = 12000 N.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the shear stress is uniformly distributed over the entire area A (average shear stress assumption).
Assume the adhesive layer is bonded to rigid substrates on both faces, so all shear deformation occurs within the layer of thickness t.
A shear modulus G for the adhesive material must be known or specified to compute shear strain and deformation; treat G as a given material parameter in the governing relations below.

Governing relation 1 — Average shear stress:
τ = V / A
τ = 12000 N / 4000 mm²
τ = 3 MPa (N/mm²)

Governing relation 2 — Shear constitutive law (Hooke's law in shear):
τ = G · γ
Therefore the shear strain is:
γ = τ / G
γ = 3 / G (with G in MPa, γ is dimensionless in radians)

Governing relation 3 — Shear deformation (lateral displacement) of the layer:
δ = γ · t
δ = (τ / G) · t
δ = (3 / G) · 20
δ = 60 / G mm (with G in MPa)

Additional assumptions stated:
The stress state is pure shear; normal stresses through the thickness are neglected.
The layer thickness t = 20 mm is small compared to the lateral dimensions, justifying the uniform shear stress assumption.
No peel (tensile through-thickness) forces are present.
The adhesive remains within its linear-elastic range, so G is constant.
