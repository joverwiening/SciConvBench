Determine the Euler critical buckling load for a column with pinned-pinned end conditions.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the column is perfectly straight, prismatic, and axially loaded with no eccentricity.
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume buckling occurs in the plane corresponding to the given moment of inertia I.

Given properties are: length L = 2500 mm, second moment of area I = 1800000 mm⁴, Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa, and effective length factor K = 1.0 for pinned-pinned end conditions.

Compute the effective length as Le = K × L = 1.0 × 2500 = 2500 mm.

Apply the Euler critical buckling load formula: P_cr = (π² × E × I) / Le².

Substitute values: P_cr = (π² × 200000 × 1800000) / (2500²).

Compute the denominator: 2500² = 6250000 mm².

Compute the numerator: π² × 200000 × 1800000 = 9.8696 × 3.6 × 10¹¹ = 3.55306 × 10¹² N·mm².

Compute P_cr = 3.55306 × 10¹² / 6250000 = 568489 N, approximately 568.5 kN.

Report P_cr ≈ 568.5 kN as the Euler critical buckling load.

Perform the buckling/instability check by comparing the applied compressive load P to P_cr.

The instability check criterion is: P ≤ P_cr / SF, where SF is the factor of safety against buckling (specify SF as required by the applicable design code or project requirement; a common value is SF = 2.0 to 3.0 for columns).

If no applied load is specified, state that the column can sustain a maximum axial compressive load of 568.5 kN before the onset of elastic buckling.

If the applied load P exceeds P_cr, the column is predicted to buckle elastically and is inadequate.

Note that this result is valid only if the column fails by elastic buckling before yielding; verify that the compressive stress at P_cr, given by σ = P_cr / A, does not exceed the material yield strength F_y (cross-sectional area A must be provided to complete this check).
