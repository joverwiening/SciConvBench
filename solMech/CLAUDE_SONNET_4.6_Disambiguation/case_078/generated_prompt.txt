Design a solid circular shaft diameter based on an angle-of-twist limit under pure torsion.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section) along its full length.
Assume the torque T is constant along the full shaft length (no distributed torque).
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the shaft is simply supported or fixed-free such that the full length L contributes to the total angle of twist.

Given values are: applied torque T = 800,000 N·mm, shear modulus G = 77,519.4 MPa, shaft length L = 1,200 mm, allowable angle of twist φ_allow = 1.0° = π/180 radians = 0.017453 rad.

The governing relation for angle of twist of a solid circular shaft is φ = TL / (GJ).

The polar moment of inertia for a solid circular cross-section of diameter d is J = πd⁴ / 32.

Substitute J into the angle-of-twist equation to get φ = 32TL / (Gπd⁴).

Rearrange to solve for the required diameter: d⁴ = 32TL / (Gπφ_allow).

Substitute numerical values: d⁴ = (32 × 800,000 × 1,200) / (77,519.4 × π × 0.017453).

Compute the numerator: 32 × 800,000 × 1,200 = 30,720,000,000 N·mm³.

Compute the denominator: 77,519.4 × π × 0.017453 = 77,519.4 × 0.054831 = 4,250.0 MPa·rad (N/mm²·rad).

Compute d⁴ = 30,720,000,000 / 4,250.0 = 7,228,235 mm⁴.

Compute d = (7,228,235)^(1/4).

First compute the square root: √7,228,235 ≈ 2688.5 mm².
Then compute the square root again: √2688.5 ≈ 51.85 mm.

The minimum required shaft diameter based on the angle-of-twist limit is d_min ≈ 51.85 mm.

Round up to the nearest standard size to ensure the angle-of-twist limit is not exceeded.

Select d = 52 mm as the minimum practical diameter (verify final φ with this diameter).

Verification check: J = π(52)⁴ / 32 = π × 7,311,616 / 32 = 718,688 mm⁴.
Compute φ = TL / (GJ) = (800,000 × 1,200) / (77,519.4 × 718,688) = 960,000,000 / 55,706,400,000 = 0.01723 rad = 0.987°.
Since 0.987° < 1.0°, d = 52 mm satisfies the angle-of-twist limit.
