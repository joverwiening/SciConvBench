The beam is prismatic with a rectangular cross section of width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The only internal load at the critical section is a bending moment M = 3,000,000 N·mm applied about the centroidal z-axis.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
Assume the cross section is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the neutral axis coincides with the centroidal z-axis, located at h/2 = 50 mm from either outer fiber.
Assume plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis after bending.

Compute the second moment of area (moment of inertia) about the centroidal z-axis using I = (b * h^3) / 12.
I = (50 * 100^3) / 12 = 4,166,667 mm^4.

Apply the flexure formula: sigma = M * y / I, where y is the distance from the neutral axis to the point of interest.
The maximum bending stress occurs at the outermost fibers, at y = +h/2 = +50 mm (tension fiber) and y = -h/2 = -50 mm (compression fiber).

Compute the maximum tensile bending stress: sigma_max_tension = M * (h/2) / I = 3,000,000 * 50 / 4,166,667 = 36 MPa (tensile).
Compute the maximum compressive bending stress: sigma_max_compression = -M * (h/2) / I = -3,000,000 * 50 / 4,166,667 = -36 MPa (compressive).

The bending stress distribution is linear through the cross-section height, varying from -36 MPa at the bottom fiber to +36 MPa at the top fiber (assuming M is positive per the right-hand rule about the z-axis).
The bending stress is zero at the neutral axis (y = 0).

The section modulus S = I / (h/2) = 4,166,667 / 50 = 83,333 mm^3.
The maximum bending stress can also be expressed as sigma_max = M / S = 3,000,000 / 83,333 = 36 MPa.
