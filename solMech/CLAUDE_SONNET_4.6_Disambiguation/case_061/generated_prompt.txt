The cross-section is rectangular with width b = 60 mm and depth h = 120 mm.
The applied load is a compressive axial force P = 60 000 N acting at an eccentricity e = 20 mm measured along the b-direction (i.e., along the width), so bending is about the centroidal z-axis (the axis parallel to h).
Treat the column as a short column so that buckling and second-order (P-delta) effects are ignored.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behaviour throughout.
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume plane sections remain plane (Euler-Bernoulli / Navier bending assumption).
Take compressive normal stress as negative and tensile normal stress as positive.

Step 1 – Cross-sectional properties.
Cross-sectional area: A = b × h = 60 × 120 = 7 200 mm².
Second moment of area about the centroidal z-axis (axis parallel to h, bending driven by eccentricity along b): I_z = h × b³ / 12 = 120 × 60³ / 12 = 2 160 000 mm⁴.
Distance from centroidal z-axis to the extreme fibres in the b-direction: c = b / 2 = 30 mm.

Step 2 – Equivalent centroidal loading.
The eccentric compressive load P at eccentricity e is statically equivalent to a centroidal compressive force P plus a bending moment M = P × e about the centroidal z-axis.
M = 60 000 × 20 = 1 200 000 N·mm = 1 200 N·m.

Step 3 – Governing stress formula (Navier's formula).
The normal stress at any point located at coordinate y measured from the centroidal z-axis (y positive toward the side where the load is offset) is:
σ(y) = −P/A ± M × y / I_z.
Using sign convention: compressive axial term is −P/A; bending adds +M·y/I_z on the side toward the eccentricity and −M·y/I_z on the opposite side.
σ(y) = −P/A + M·y / I_z.

Step 4 – Uniform axial stress component.
σ_axial = −P / A = −60 000 / 7 200 = −8.333 MPa (compressive, uniform over the section).

Step 5 – Bending stress at extreme fibres.
σ_bending = ±M × c / I_z = ±1 200 000 × 30 / 2 160 000 = ±16.667 MPa.

Step 6 – Combined stress at extreme fibres.
At the fibre on the side toward the eccentricity (y = +30 mm):
σ_max = −8.333 + 16.667 = +8.333 MPa (tensile).
At the fibre on the opposite side (y = −30 mm):
σ_min = −8.333 − 16.667 = −25.000 MPa (compressive).

Step 7 – Neutral axis location.
The neutral axis is where σ(y) = 0: y_NA = (P/A) × (I_z / M) = 8.333 × (2 160 000 / 1 200 000) = +15.0 mm from the centroid, toward the eccentric-load side.
Because |y_NA| = 15 mm < c = 30 mm, the neutral axis lies within the cross-section, meaning part of the section is in tension and part in compression.

Step 8 – Kern (core) of the section.
The load must lie within the kern to keep the entire section in compression.
Kern half-dimension in the b-direction: e_kern = b / 6 = 60 / 6 = 10 mm.
The applied eccentricity e = 20 mm exceeds 10 mm, which confirms that tensile stress develops on one side, consistent with Step 6.

Step 9 – Summary of results.
Axial stress component: −8.33 MPa (uniform compression).
Maximum bending stress: ±16.67 MPa.
Maximum compressive stress (at y = −30 mm): −25.00 MPa.
Maximum tensile stress (at y = +30 mm): +8.33 MPa.
Neutral axis location: 15 mm from centroid toward the load eccentricity side.
The eccentricity (20 mm) exceeds the kern limit (10 mm), so tensile stress exists on the far face.
