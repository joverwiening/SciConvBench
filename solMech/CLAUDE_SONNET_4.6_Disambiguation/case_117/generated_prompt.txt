The beam is a straight, prismatic member with a solid rectangular cross-section.
The width is b = 60 mm and the height is h = 80 mm.
The internal transverse shear force at the cross-section of interest is V = 16000 N.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior throughout.
Assume the beam material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the cross-section remains plane and the beam is long relative to its cross-sectional dimensions (Euler-Bernoulli / Saint-Venant shear stress assumptions apply).
Assume the shear stress is uniformly distributed across the width b at any given height y from the neutral axis (valid for rectangular sections where b is constant).

Governing relation for transverse shear stress (Shear Formula):

The shear stress at a distance y from the neutral axis is given by the shear formula: tau(y) = V * Q(y) / (I * b), where V is the internal shear force, Q(y) is the first moment of area of the portion of the cross-section above (or below) the cut at y, I is the second moment of area of the full cross-section about the neutral axis, and b is the width of the cross-section at the cut location y.

Compute the second moment of area I:
I = b * h^3 / 12 = 60 * 80^3 / 12 = 2,560,000 mm^4.

Define y as measured from the neutral axis, ranging from -h/2 = -40 mm to +h/2 = +40 mm.

Compute Q(y) for a rectangular section:
Q(y) = b * (h/2 - y) * ((h/2 + y) / 2) = (b / 2) * ((h/2)^2 - y^2).
Q(y) = (60 / 2) * (1600 - y^2) = 30 * (1600 - y^2) mm^3, with y in mm.

Substitute into the shear formula:
tau(y) = V * Q(y) / (I * b) = 16000 * 30 * (1600 - y^2) / (2,560,000 * 60).
tau(y) = 480,000 * (1600 - y^2) / 153,600,000.
tau(y) = (1 / 320) * (1600 - y^2) MPa, with y in mm.

The shear stress distribution is parabolic over the height of the cross-section.

The shear stress is zero at the top and bottom surfaces: tau(y = ±40 mm) = (1/320) * (1600 - 1600) = 0 MPa.

The shear stress is maximum at the neutral axis: tau(y = 0) = (1/320) * 1600 = 5 MPa.

The maximum shear stress can also be expressed using the standard rectangular-section result: tau_max = (3/2) * (V / (b * h)) = 1.5 * (16000 / (60 * 80)) = 1.5 * 3.333 = 5 MPa, which confirms the result.

Additional assumption: the applied transverse load acts through the shear center of the rectangular section (centroid for doubly symmetric sections), so no torsion is induced.
Additional assumption: the normal stress distribution due to bending does not affect the shear stress calculation at this cross-section under the stated formula.
Units throughout: forces in N, lengths in mm, stresses in MPa (N/mm^2).
