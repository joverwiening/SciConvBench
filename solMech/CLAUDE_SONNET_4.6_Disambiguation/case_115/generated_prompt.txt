The problem is a hollow circular shaft under pure torsion, analyzed within the small-deformation, linear-elastic framework.

Geometry:
Outer diameter do = 60 mm, so outer radius ro = 30 mm.
Inner diameter di = 44 mm, so inner radius ri = 22 mm.
Length L = 1200 mm.

Loading:
Applied torque T = 600,000 N·mm (pure torsion, no transverse shear force).

Additional Assumptions:
The shaft material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linear-elastic.
The shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section along its length).
The torque is applied along the longitudinal axis of the shaft.
Plane cross-sections remain plane after twisting (Saint-Venant torsion theory applies).
Use standard steel properties: shear modulus G = 80,000 N/mm² (80 GPa), unless the user specifies otherwise.
Both ends are assumed to allow free warping (no warping restraint), so Saint-Venant torsion theory is fully applicable.

Governing Relations:

Polar moment of inertia:
J = (π / 32) × (do⁴ − di⁴)
J = (π / 32) × (60⁴ − 44⁴) mm⁴

Shear stress at any radial position r:
τ(r) = T × r / J

Maximum shear stress occurs at the outer radius ro = 30 mm:
τ_max = T × ro / J

Minimum shear stress occurs at the inner radius ri = 22 mm:
τ_min = T × ri / J

Angle of twist over the full length L:
φ = T × L / (G × J)   [result in radians]

The shear stress distribution is linear in r, varying from τ_min at the inner surface to τ_max at the outer surface.
