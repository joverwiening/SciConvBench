The beam is simply supported with span L = 1800 mm.
A single point load P = 2000 N acts vertically downward at midspan, x = 900 mm from the left support.
There is no distributed load and no applied moment anywhere on the beam.
Assume small deformation and linear elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the beam has uniform cross-section with constant flexural rigidity EI along its entire length.
Assume the beam is prismatic, homogeneous, and isotropic.
Assume shear deformations are negligible; bending dominates the deflection response.
Use the unit-load method (also called the virtual work method or Mohr's integral) to compute the deflection at an arbitrary point x = a along the beam.

Set up the real bending moment diagram M(x) due to P = 2000 N at x = 900 mm.
For the real system, the left reaction is R_A = P(L - 900)/L = 2000 × 900/1800 = 1000 N and the right reaction is R_B = 1000 N, because the load is at midspan.
For 0 ≤ x ≤ 900 mm, M(x) = R_A · x = 1000x.
For 900 mm ≤ x ≤ 1800 mm, M(x) = R_A · x − P(x − 900) = 1000x − 2000(x − 900) = −1000x + 1800000.

Apply a virtual unit load of magnitude 1 (dimensionless, treated as 1 N) downward at the arbitrary point x = a where deflection is sought.
The virtual left reaction is r_A = (L − a)/L = (1800 − a)/1800.
The virtual right reaction is r_B = a/L = a/1800.

For 0 ≤ x ≤ a, the virtual bending moment is m(x) = r_A · x = x(1800 − a)/1800.
For a ≤ x ≤ 1800, the virtual bending moment is m(x) = r_B · (L − x) = (a/1800)(1800 − x).

Apply the unit-load theorem: deflection at x = a equals (1/EI) times the integral over the full span of M(x) · m(x) dx.
Split the integral at both x = a and x = 900 mm because both M(x) and m(x) have kinks at those points.

Case 1: the arbitrary point a is less than 900 mm (load to the right of the desired deflection point).
Integral = integral from 0 to a of [1000x · x(1800 − a)/1800] dx
         + integral from a to 900 of [1000x · (a/1800)(1800 − x)] dx
         + integral from 900 to 1800 of [(−1000x + 1800000) · (a/1800)(1800 − x)] dx.

Evaluate the first segment (0 to a): (1000(1800 − a)/1800) · integral of x² dx from 0 to a = (1000(1800 − a)/1800) · a³/3.
Evaluate the second segment (a to 900): (1000a/1800) · integral of x(1800 − x) dx from a to 900.
The antiderivative of x(1800 − x) is 900x² − x³/3.
Second segment = (1000a/1800) · [(900 · 900² − 900³/3) − (900a² − a³/3)].
Evaluate the third segment (900 to 1800): (a/1800) · integral of (−1000x + 1800000)(1800 − x) dx from 900 to 1800.
Expand (−1000x + 1800000)(1800 − x) = −1800000x + 1000x² + 3240000000 − 1800000x = 1000x² − 3600000x + 3240000000.
Antiderivative: 1000x³/3 − 1800000x² + 3240000000x.
Evaluate from 900 to 1800 and multiply by a/1800.

The deflection at x = a (for a ≤ 900 mm) is delta(a) = (1/EI) × [sum of the three segment results above].

Case 2: the arbitrary point a equals 900 mm (midspan deflection).
By symmetry, the result simplifies to delta(midspan) = PL³/(48EI).
Substituting: delta(midspan) = 2000 × 1800³ / (48 × EI).
Compute 1800³ = 5.832 × 10⁹ mm³.
delta(midspan) = (2000 × 5.832 × 10⁹) / (48 × EI) = 1.1664 × 10¹³ / (48 × EI) = 2.43 × 10¹¹ / EI mm (with EI in N·mm²).

Case 3: the arbitrary point a is greater than 900 mm, use symmetry with Case 1 by substituting a' = L − a = 1800 − a and the result is identical to Case 1 evaluated at a'.

State the final governing relation for the unit-load method: delta(a) = integral from 0 to L of M(x) · m(x) / EI dx, where M(x) is the real moment and m(x) is the virtual moment due to a unit load at x = a.
The numerical value of delta(a) requires a specified value of EI in N·mm² to produce a deflection in mm.
If EI is not given, the result is expressed as delta(a) = [numerical coefficient] / EI mm.
