The shaft is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 50 mm and length L = 600 mm.
The applied loading is a pure torque T = 500000 N·mm (no transverse shear force).
The shear modulus is G = 77519.4 MPa.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section along its entire length).
Assume one end is fixed and the torque is applied at the free end (cantilever torsion configuration).
Assume the torque is applied about the longitudinal axis of the shaft.

Compute the polar moment of inertia J for a solid circular cross-section using J = π·d⁴/32.
With d = 50 mm, J = π·(50)⁴/32 = π·6250000/32 = 613592.3 mm⁴ (use this value in all subsequent calculations).

Compute the maximum shear stress using the torsion formula τ_max = T·c/J, where c = d/2 = 25 mm is the outer radius.
With T = 500000 N·mm, c = 25 mm, J = 613592.3 mm⁴: τ_max = 500000·25/613592.3 = 20.37 MPa.
The maximum shear stress τ_max = 20.37 MPa occurs at the outer surface of the shaft.
The shear stress at any interior radius r is τ(r) = T·r/J, varying linearly from zero at the centroidal axis to τ_max at r = c.

Compute the angle of twist using φ = T·L/(G·J).
With T = 500000 N·mm, L = 600 mm, G = 77519.4 MPa, J = 613592.3 mm⁴: φ = 500000·600/(77519.4·613592.3).
The denominator G·J = 77519.4 × 613592.3 = 4.7565 × 10¹⁰ N·mm².
φ = 300000000/47565000000 = 0.006307 radians = 0.3614 degrees.

The governing relation for shear stress is τ = T·r/J (Navier torsion formula).
The governing relation for angle of twist is φ = T·L/(G·J).
Both relations are valid under the Saint-Venant torsion theory for circular prismatic shafts.
No warping occurs in a circular cross-section under torsion; plane cross-sections remain plane.
No bending, axial, or transverse shear effects are present since loading is pure torque only.
