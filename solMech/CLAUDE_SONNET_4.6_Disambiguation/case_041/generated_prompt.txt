The task is to compute the Euler critical buckling load for a column with fixed-free end conditions and perform a buckling check.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the column is perfectly straight, prismatic, and centrally loaded with no eccentricity.
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume no residual stresses, imperfections, or lateral loads are present.
The column is treated as an ideal Euler column, so only elastic buckling is considered.

Given values are: L = 1000 mm, E = 200000 MPa, I = 1200000 mm⁴, and effective length factor K = 2.0 for fixed-free (cantilever) end conditions.

Compute the effective length as Le = K × L = 2.0 × 1000 = 2000 mm.

The governing relation for the Euler critical buckling load is: P_cr = (π² × E × I) / Le².

Substitute values: P_cr = (π² × 200000 × 1200000) / (2000²).

Compute the numerator: π² × 200000 × 1200000 = 9.8696 × 2.4 × 10¹¹ = 2.36870 × 10¹² N·mm².

Compute the denominator: 2000² = 4,000,000 mm².

Compute P_cr = 2.36870 × 10¹² / 4.0 × 10⁶ = 592,176 N, which is approximately 592.2 kN.

The Euler critical buckling load for this fixed-free column is P_cr ≈ 592.2 kN.

For the buckling check, compare the applied compressive load P_applied against P_cr.
The buckling safety factor is defined as SF = P_cr / P_applied.
If P_applied is not specified, the check cannot be numerically completed, but the criterion is: the column is safe against buckling if P_applied < P_cr, i.e., SF > 1.0.
A commonly required minimum safety factor against buckling in practice is SF ≥ 2.0 to 3.0, depending on the design code and application.

Additional assumption: buckling is the governing failure mode, and material yielding does not occur before the critical buckling load is reached (i.e., the column is slender enough for Euler buckling to apply).
To verify this, confirm that the compressive stress at P_cr, given by σ = P_cr / A, does not exceed the material yield strength.
If the cross-sectional area A is known, compute σ_cr = 592,176 / A and compare to the yield strength; if σ_cr exceeds yield, inelastic buckling methods (e.g., tangent modulus) would be required instead.
