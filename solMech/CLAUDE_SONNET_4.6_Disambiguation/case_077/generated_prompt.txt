The prismatic bar has a rectangular cross section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 120 mm.

The cross-sectional area is A = b × h = 50 mm × 120 mm = 6000 mm².

The internal resultants at the section of interest are: axial tensile force P = 40000 N and transverse shear force V = 15000 N acting parallel to the width b.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.

Assume the bar is prismatic, meaning the cross section is constant along its length.

Assume the section is sufficiently far from any load application points or geometric discontinuities so that Saint-Venant's principle applies and stress distributions are as given by elementary strength-of-materials formulas.

Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.

The average normal stress at the section due to the axial tensile force P is computed as:
σ_avg = P / A

Substituting: σ_avg = 40000 N / 6000 mm² = 6.67 MPa (tensile).

The average shear stress at the section due to the transverse shear force V is computed as:
τ_avg = V / A

Substituting: τ_avg = 15000 N / 6000 mm² = 2.50 MPa.

Note that the average shear stress τ_avg = V/A is a uniform approximation over the entire cross section. The actual shear stress distribution for a rectangular section under transverse shear is parabolic, with a maximum value at the neutral axis of τ_max = (3/2) × (V/A) = 1.5 × 2.50 MPa = 3.75 MPa, and zero at the top and bottom edges. The average value τ_avg = 2.50 MPa is the resultant-consistent mean.

The governing relation for average normal stress is σ_avg = P / A, valid when P is a pure axial resultant with no bending moment contribution considered at this level of analysis.

The governing relation for average shear stress is τ_avg = V / A, valid as a section-averaged quantity consistent with the shear force resultant.

The two stress components act on the same cross-sectional plane: σ_avg acts normal to the plane (axial direction) and τ_avg acts tangential to the plane (parallel to width b).
