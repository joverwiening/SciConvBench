The rod is a straight, prismatic, circular-cross-section bar made of AISI 1020 steel.
The bar has length L = 1500 mm and diameter d = 16 mm.
Compute the cross-sectional area as A = π d² / 4 = π (16)² / 4 ≈ 201.06 mm².
Use elastic modulus E = 200 000 MPa (200 GPa).
Neglect the self-weight and mass of the bar (massless rod assumption).
The only applied load is an impact load caused by a weight W = 800 N dropped from a height h = 150 mm onto the top end of the rod.
The bottom end of the rod is fixed (built-in support); the weight strikes the top end axially.
Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behavior throughout.
Assume the dropped weight and the rod top collar remain in contact after impact (no rebound separation during the loading phase).
Assume the impact is perfectly plastic at the moment of contact (the weight does not bounce; all kinetic energy is transferred to elastic strain energy in the rod).
Assume no energy is lost to heat, sound, or plastic deformation.

Governing relations:

Compute the static deflection of the rod under the weight W treated as a static axial load.
δ_st = W L / (A E)
δ_st = (800 × 1500) / (201.06 × 200 000)
δ_st ≈ 0.02984 mm

Compute the impact factor (dynamic magnification factor) using energy conservation.
The total potential energy available equals W (h + δ_dyn), which is set equal to the elastic strain energy (1/2) k δ_dyn², where k = AE/L is the axial stiffness of the rod.
This yields the quadratic: (1/2) k δ_dyn² − W δ_dyn − W h = 0.
The impact factor is n = 1 + sqrt(1 + 2h / δ_st).
n = 1 + sqrt(1 + 2 × 150 / 0.02984)
n = 1 + sqrt(1 + 10053.6)
n ≈ 1 + 100.27 ≈ 101.27

Compute the maximum dynamic deflection.
δ_dyn = n × δ_st = 101.27 × 0.02984 ≈ 3.022 mm

Compute the maximum dynamic axial force in the rod.
P_dyn = n × W = 101.27 × 800 ≈ 81 016 N ≈ 81.0 kN

Compute the maximum dynamic axial (normal) stress in the rod.
σ_dyn = P_dyn / A = 81 016 / 201.06 ≈ 403 MPa

Check against the yield strength of AISI 1020 steel (σ_y ≈ 210 MPa for hot-rolled, 380 MPa for cold-drawn).
The computed dynamic stress of approximately 403 MPa exceeds the typical yield strength of AISI 1020 steel.
State explicitly in the solution that the linear-elastic result is therefore non-conservative and that yielding will occur; the small-deformation linear-elastic model gives an upper-bound stress estimate only.
Report the axial stiffness of the rod as k = AE/L = (201.06 × 200 000) / 1500 ≈ 26 808 N/mm.
Report all intermediate values (δ_st, n, δ_dyn, P_dyn, σ_dyn) clearly in the final solution.
State all assumptions listed above explicitly in the solution write-up.
