The shaft is a solid circular cross-section subjected to pure torque only; no transverse shear force is applied.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior throughout.

Assume the shaft is homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic (constant cross-section along its length).

Assume Saint-Venant's torsion theory applies (plane sections remain plane, warping is unrestrained or neglected for circular sections).

Given values are: diameter d = 30 mm, radius c = d/2 = 15 mm, length L = 1000 mm, applied torque T = 250000 N·mm, shear modulus G = 76923.1 MPa.

Compute the polar moment of inertia J for a solid circular cross-section using J = (pi * d^4) / 32.

Substituting: J = (pi * (30)^4) / 32 = (pi * 810000) / 32 = 79521.6 mm^4 (approximately).

The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface (r = c) and is governed by the torsion formula: tau_max = T * c / J.

Substituting: tau_max = 250000 * 15 / 79521.6 = 47.17 MPa (approximately).

The shear stress at any interior radius r is: tau(r) = T * r / J, varying linearly from zero at the centroidal axis to tau_max at the outer surface.

The angle of twist over the full length L is governed by: phi = T * L / (G * J).

Substituting: phi = 250000 * 1000 / (76923.1 * 79521.6) = 250000000 / 6116923077 = 0.04087 radians (approximately).

Converting to degrees: phi = 0.04087 * (180 / pi) = 2.342 degrees (approximately).

The shear stress distribution is linear in r and acts on cross-sectional planes tangentially.

No bending stress, no axial stress, and no transverse shear stress are present under pure torque loading.
