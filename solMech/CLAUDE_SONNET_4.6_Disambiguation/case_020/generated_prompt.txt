The shaft is a solid circular cross-section, linear-elastic, small-deformation, under torsion only.
The shaft is fixed (zero rotation) at x = 0.
The shaft has two torque application points: T1 = 200000 N·mm applied at x = 1200 mm (free end), and T2 = -250000 N·mm applied at x = 800 mm (intermediate point).
The total length of the shaft is L = 1200 mm.
Assume the shaft has uniform circular cross-section with diameter d and shear modulus G throughout (treat as symbolic unless numerical values are provided).
Assume no distributed torque along the shaft length.
Assume the shaft is statically determinate from the fixed end reaction.

Governing relations and sign convention:
Use the convention that internal torque T(x) is positive when it acts in the positive x-direction on the positive face (right-hand rule about x-axis).
The fixed wall at x = 0 provides a reaction torque R.

Determine the reaction torque at the fixed end by global moment equilibrium about the shaft axis:
R + T2 + T1 = 0
R + (-250000) + 200000 = 0
R = 50000 N·mm

Divide the shaft into two segments based on the torque application points.

Segment 1: 0 <= x < 800 mm
Cut the shaft in this segment and apply equilibrium. The internal torque is:
T_internal = -R = -50000 N·mm (the wall reaction is the only external torque to the left of any cut in this segment)
Using the sign convention, T(x) = 50000 N·mm (the internal torque resists the wall reaction).
State explicitly: T(x) = 50000 N·mm for 0 <= x < 800 mm.

Segment 2: 800 mm <= x < 1200 mm
A cut in this segment has T2 = -250000 N·mm applied at x = 800 mm to the left of the cut in addition to R.
T(x) = R + T2 = 50000 + (-250000) = -200000 N·mm for 800 mm <= x < 1200 mm.
The magnitude is 200000 N·mm, and the negative sign indicates the torque acts opposite to the assumed positive direction.

Check at the free end (x = 1200 mm):
T(1200) + T1 = -200000 + 200000 = 0. Equilibrium is satisfied.

The polar moment of inertia for a solid circular shaft of diameter d is:
J = pi * d^4 / 32

The shear stress at radius r in a cross-section carrying internal torque T is:
tau(r) = T * r / J

The maximum shear stress at any cross-section occurs at the outer surface (r = d/2):
tau_max = T * (d/2) / J = 16 * T / (pi * d^3)

For Segment 1, tau_max_1 = 16 * 50000 / (pi * d^3) = 800000 / (pi * d^3) N/mm^2.
For Segment 2, tau_max_2 = 16 * 200000 / (pi * d^3) = 3200000 / (pi * d^3) N/mm^2.
The critical (maximum) shear stress occurs in Segment 2 with magnitude tau_max = 3200000 / (pi * d^3) N/mm^2.

The angle of twist over a segment of length L_seg carrying uniform internal torque T_seg is:
phi = T_seg * L_seg / (G * J)

The angle of twist is measured relative to the fixed end (phi(0) = 0).

Angle of twist at x = 800 mm (end of Segment 1):
phi(800) = T_seg1 * 800 / (G * J) = 50000 * 800 / (G * J) = 40000000 / (G * J) rad.

Angle of twist at x = 1200 mm (free end):
phi(1200) = phi(800) + T_seg2 * 400 / (G * J)
phi(1200) = 40000000 / (G * J) + (-200000) * 400 / (G * J)
phi(1200) = 40000000 / (G * J) - 80000000 / (G * J)
phi(1200) = -40000000 / (G * J) rad.

The negative sign indicates the free end rotates in the direction opposite to the positive convention.

Substitute J = pi * d^4 / 32 to get explicit expressions in terms of d and G:
phi(800) = 40000000 * 32 / (G * pi * d^4) = 1280000000 / (G * pi * d^4) rad.
phi(1200) = -40000000 * 32 / (G * pi * d^4) = -1280000000 / (G * pi * d^4) rad.
