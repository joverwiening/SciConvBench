The beam has a rectangular cross-section with width b = 50 mm and height h = 100 mm.
The applied bending moment is M = 3,000,000 N·mm, acting as a sagging (positive) moment.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
Assume the beam is homogeneous, isotropic, and prismatic along its length.
Assume the cross-section is symmetric about both the horizontal and vertical centroidal axes.
The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section, located at h/2 = 50 mm from either outer fiber.
Define y as the distance measured from the neutral axis, positive upward, ranging from -50 mm to +50 mm.

Compute the second moment of area (moment of inertia) about the neutral axis:
I = (b * h^3) / 12 = (50 * 100^3) / 12 = 4,166,666.67 mm^4.

The governing relation for bending stress at any fiber location y is the Euler-Bernoulli flexure formula:
sigma(y) = M * y / I.

For a sagging moment (positive M convention), the top fiber is in compression and the bottom fiber is in tension.

Evaluate bending stress at the top fiber: y = +50 mm.
sigma_top = 3,000,000 * 50 / 4,166,666.67 = +36 N/mm^2 (tensile by formula sign, but physically compressive under sagging; apply sign convention: sagging causes compression at top, so sigma_top = -36 MPa).

Evaluate bending stress at the bottom fiber: y = -50 mm.
sigma_bottom = 3,000,000 * (-50) / 4,166,666.67 = -36 N/mm^2 (by formula), which under sagging convention is +36 MPa (tensile).

Evaluate bending stress at the neutral axis: y = 0 mm.
sigma_neutral = 0 N/mm^2.

For an arbitrary fiber at distance y from the neutral axis, the bending stress is:
sigma(y) = M * y / I = 3,000,000 * y / 4,166,666.67 = 0.72 * y N/mm^2, where y is in mm.

The stress distribution is linear across the cross-section height, varying from -36 MPa at the top fiber to +36 MPa at the bottom fiber under sagging bending.
The maximum absolute bending stress (occurring at the outermost fibers) is 36 MPa.
The section modulus is S = I / (h/2) = 4,166,666.67 / 50 = 83,333.33 mm^3, and the maximum bending stress can also be computed as sigma_max = M / S = 3,000,000 / 83,333.33 = 36 MPa.
