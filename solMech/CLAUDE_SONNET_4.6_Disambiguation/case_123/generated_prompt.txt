The rod is made of AISI 1020 steel, prismatic, with a circular cross-section of diameter d = 20 mm.

Use E = 200,000 MPa, α = 2×10⁻⁵ /°C, and ΔT = +50 °C (uniform temperature increase).

Work exclusively in N, mm, and MPa units throughout.

The cross-sectional area is A = π × (20)² / 4 = 100π mm² ≈ 314.159 mm².

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic, isotropic solid mechanics.

Assume the rod is prismatic and uniform along its entire length L (L remains symbolic as it cancels in the stress result).

Assume the rod is fully axially restrained at both ends, meaning the total axial deformation is constrained to zero (δ_total = 0).

Assume there is no bending, torsion, lateral load, or buckling; the problem is purely uniaxial.

Assume steady-state thermal conditions so the temperature change ΔT is spatially uniform and time-independent.

The free thermal strain (unrestrained) is ε_th = α × ΔT = (2×10⁻⁵) × 50 = 1×10⁻³ (dimensionless, tensile in sense).

With full axial restraint, the total strain must be zero: ε_total = ε_mechanical + ε_th = 0.

Therefore the mechanical strain is ε_mech = −ε_th = −1×10⁻³ (compressive).

The thermal stress is σ = E × ε_mech = 200,000 × (−1×10⁻³) = −200 MPa (compressive).

The reaction force at each support is F = σ × A = −200 × 100π = −20,000π N ≈ −62,832 N (compressive, i.e., the supports push inward on the rod).

The net axial deformation of the rod is δ = 0 mm by the full-restraint condition.

The free (unrestrained) elongation that would have occurred is δ_free = ε_th × L = 1×10⁻³ × L mm, which is fully suppressed by the supports.

No shear stress, shear strain, or lateral stress components arise under this uniaxial, fully restrained, uniform thermal loading assumption.
