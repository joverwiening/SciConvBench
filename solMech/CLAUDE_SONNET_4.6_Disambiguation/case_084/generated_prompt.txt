The beam is a built-up beam with a flange plate connected to a web at one interface using equally spaced fasteners.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.
Assume the beam material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the cross-section remains plane after bending (Euler-Bernoulli beam theory).
Assume the shear force V is constant over the region of interest (or that the spacing is determined at the cross-section where V is maximum).
Assume each fastener acts in single shear and carries load independently.
Assume fasteners are equally spaced at spacing s along the beam axis.

Given quantities are: V = 25,000 N, I = 18,000,000 mm⁴, Q = 45,000 mm³ at the flange-web interface, and allowable shear force per fastener F = 1,800 N.

Step 1: Compute the shear flow q at the flange-web interface using the shear flow formula.
The governing relation is q = VQ / I.
Substitute values: q = (25,000 × 45,000) / 18,000,000.
Compute q = 1,125,000,000 / 18,000,000 = 62.5 N/mm.

Step 2: Determine the maximum allowable fastener spacing s.
The governing relation relating fastener spacing to shear flow is F = q × s, which gives s = F / q.
This relation states that the shear force carried by one fastener over a length s equals the shear flow times that length.
Substitute values: s = 1,800 / 62.5 = 28.8 mm.

Step 3: State the result.
The shear flow at the flange-web interface is q = 62.5 N/mm.
The maximum allowable fastener spacing is s = 28.8 mm.
Fasteners must be placed at intervals no greater than 28.8 mm to ensure each fastener does not exceed its allowable shear force of 1,800 N.
In practice, round down to the nearest whole number or standard spacing, so use s ≤ 28 mm.
