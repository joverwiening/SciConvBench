The shaft is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 25 mm.

The applied axial tensile force is P = 15000 N (tensile, acting along the shaft axis).

The applied torque is T = 400000 N·mm.

No material properties are required; only stress quantities and principal stress/strain measures derived from stresses are to be computed.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.

Assume the shaft is prismatic, homogeneous, and isotropic.

Assume the cross-section is far from any stress concentration, end effects, or load application points, so Saint-Venant's principle applies and the stress state is uniform over the cross-section.

Compute the cross-sectional area A = π·d²/4.

Compute the polar moment of inertia J = π·d⁴/32.

Compute the axial (normal) stress σ_x = P / A, which acts uniformly over the entire cross-section.

Compute the shear stress due to torsion τ = T·c / J, where c = d/2 is the outer radius; this shear stress is maximum at the outer surface.

The critical point is at the outer surface of the shaft where both σ_x and τ are simultaneously present.

At the critical point, the stress state is: normal stress σ_x (axial), zero transverse normal stress σ_y = 0, and shear stress τ_xy = τ (torsional).

Compute the principal stresses using σ_1,2 = (σ_x/2) ± √((σ_x/2)² + τ²).

Compute the maximum in-plane shear stress τ_max = √((σ_x/2)² + τ²).

Compute the principal angle θ_p = (1/2)·arctan(2τ / σ_x), measured from the axial direction to the principal stress direction.

Compute the von Mises equivalent stress σ_vm = √(σ_x² + 3·τ²), applicable for combined normal and shear stress under plane stress.

State all intermediate computed values: A, J, c, σ_x, τ, then the principal stresses σ_1 and σ_2, τ_max, θ_p, and σ_vm.

Since no E or ν are provided, do not compute strains or deflections.

Present the governing relations clearly alongside each numerical result.
