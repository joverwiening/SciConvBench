The beam is a cantilever of length L = 1500 mm, fixed at one end and free at the other.
A downward point load P = 500 N is applied at the free end.
The beam material is linear-elastic with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa (N/mm²).
The second moment of area of the cross-section is I = 2500000 mm⁴.
Assume small deformations throughout.
Assume the beam is prismatic (uniform cross-section along its entire length).
Assume the beam is straight and initially unstressed.
Neglect shear deformation; only bending strain energy is considered (Euler-Bernoulli beam assumption).
Neglect self-weight; the only load is the applied point load P.

Objective: Determine the vertical deflection at the free end using Castigliano's Second Theorem.

Governing Relations:

Castigliano's Second Theorem states that the deflection at the point of load application in the direction of the load equals the partial derivative of the total strain energy U with respect to that load.
Write this as: delta = dU/dP.

The total strain energy stored in the beam due to bending is:
U = integral from 0 to L of [M(x)² / (2EI)] dx.

Establish a coordinate x measured from the fixed end toward the free end.
The bending moment at position x due to the end load P is:
M(x) = P * (L - x).

The partial derivative of M with respect to P is:
dM/dP = (L - x).

Apply Castigliano's theorem by differentiating under the integral sign:
delta = integral from 0 to L of [M(x) / (EI)] * [dM/dP] dx.

Substitute M(x) and dM/dP:
delta = integral from 0 to L of [P * (L - x)² / (EI)] dx.

Evaluate the integral:
integral from 0 to L of (L - x)² dx = L³ / 3.

The closed-form result for the free-end deflection is:
delta = P * L³ / (3 * E * I).

Substitute numerical values:
delta = (500 * 1500³) / (3 * 200000 * 2500000).

Compute the numerator: 500 * 1500³ = 500 * 3375000000 = 1687500000000 N·mm³.
Compute the denominator: 3 * 200000 * 2500000 = 1500000000000 N·mm².

delta = 1687500000000 / 1500000000000 = 1.125 mm.

The vertical deflection at the free end of the cantilever is delta = 1.125 mm, acting downward in the direction of the applied load.
