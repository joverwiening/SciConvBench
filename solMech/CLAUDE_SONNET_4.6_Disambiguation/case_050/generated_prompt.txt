The shaft is a hollow circular cross-section with outer diameter do = 40 mm (outer radius ro = 20 mm) and inner diameter di = 30 mm (inner radius ri = 15 mm), and length L = 1000 mm.
The applied loading is a pure torque T = 300,000 N·mm acting about the longitudinal axis of the shaft.
There is no transverse shear force, bending moment, or axial force applied.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic material behavior throughout.
Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic.
Assume the shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section along its entire length).
Assume one end of the shaft is fixed and the torque is applied at the free end (cantilever configuration for angle of twist calculation).
Assume a material of structural steel with shear modulus G = 80,000 N/mm² unless a different material is specified; state this as an assumption in the solution.
Assume Saint-Venant's principle applies and that end effects are neglected away from the point of load application.

Compute the polar second moment of area J using J = π(do⁴ - di⁴)/32.
Substitute do = 40 mm and di = 30 mm: J = π(40⁴ - 30⁴)/32.
Evaluate 40⁴ = 2,560,000 mm⁴ and 30⁴ = 810,000 mm⁴, giving do⁴ - di⁴ = 1,750,000 mm⁴.
Compute J = π × 1,750,000 / 32 = 171,806 mm⁴ (approximately).

Compute the maximum shear stress using the torsion formula τ_max = T × ro / J.
Substitute T = 300,000 N·mm and ro = 20 mm: τ_max = 300,000 × 20 / 171,806.
Report τ_max ≈ 34.9 N/mm² (MPa), occurring at the outer surface of the shaft.

Compute the minimum shear stress at the inner surface using τ_min = T × ri / J.
Substitute ri = 15 mm: τ_min = 300,000 × 15 / 171,806.
Report τ_min ≈ 26.2 N/mm² (MPa), occurring at the inner surface of the shaft.

The shear stress distribution is linear through the wall thickness, varying from τ_min at r = ri to τ_max at r = ro.
There is no normal stress on any cross-section since loading is pure torsion with no axial or bending component.

Compute the angle of twist using φ = T × L / (G × J).
Substitute T = 300,000 N·mm, L = 1000 mm, G = 80,000 N/mm², J = 171,806 mm⁴.
Compute φ = 300,000 × 1000 / (80,000 × 171,806) = 300,000,000 / 13,744,480,000.
Report φ ≈ 0.02183 radians, which is approximately 1.25 degrees.

The principal stresses on the outer surface element are σ₁ = +τ_max and σ₂ = −τ_max, oriented at 45 degrees to the shaft axis, as is standard for pure shear.
The maximum shear stress on the outer surface equals τ_max ≈ 34.9 MPa, consistent with the torsion formula result.
