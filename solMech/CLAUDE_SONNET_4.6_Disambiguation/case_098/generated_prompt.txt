The shaft is a solid circular shaft with two segments in series under pure torsion, fixed at end A, with torque T applied at end C.

Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior throughout.

Assume the material is homogeneous and isotropic with shear modulus G = 77,519.4 MPa.

Assume the cross-sections remain plane and circular (Saint-Venant torsion theory applies).

Segment AB has length L1 = 400 mm and diameter d1 = 30 mm.

Segment BC has length L2 = 700 mm and diameter d2 = 30 mm.

Both segments have the same diameter, so the shaft is geometrically uniform, but treat AB and BC as two distinct segments in series for bookkeeping purposes.

The torque T = 600,000 N·mm is applied at end C.

End A is fully fixed (zero rotation).

Since the shaft is statically determinate (fixed at one end, free torque applied at the other), the internal torque in every cross-section equals T = 600,000 N·mm throughout both segments.

Compute the polar second moment of area for each segment using J = π d^4 / 32.

For d = 30 mm: J = π (30)^4 / 32 = π (810,000) / 32 = 79,521.6 mm^4 (use this value for both segments).

Compute the maximum shear stress in each segment using τ_max = T c / J, where c = d/2 = 15 mm.

τ_max (AB) = 600,000 × 15 / 79,521.6 = 113.2 MPa.

τ_max (BC) = 600,000 × 15 / 79,521.6 = 113.2 MPa.

Because both segments are identical, the maximum shear stress is uniform along the entire shaft at 113.2 MPa.

Compute the angle of twist for each segment using φ = T L / (G J).

φ_AB = 600,000 × 400 / (77,519.4 × 79,521.6) = 240,000,000 / 6,167,700,000 = 0.03891 rad.

φ_BC = 600,000 × 700 / (77,519.4 × 79,521.6) = 420,000,000 / 6,167,700,000 = 0.06809 rad.

Compute the total angle of twist at C relative to the fixed end A as φ_total = φ_AB + φ_BC.

φ_total = 0.03891 + 0.06809 = 0.10700 rad.

Convert to degrees if required: 0.10700 rad × (180/π) = 6.13 degrees.

All units are consistent in N, mm, and MPa throughout.
