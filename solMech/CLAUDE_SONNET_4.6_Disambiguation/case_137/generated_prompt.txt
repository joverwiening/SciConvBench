The beam is a simply supported beam of span length L.
The beam has two simple supports, one at each end (x = 0 and x = L).
The single external load is a concentrated vertical force P acting downward at the midspan point x = L/2.
There is no distributed load, no axial load, and no applied moment anywhere on the beam.
The beam material is linear-elastic and the deformation is small (small-displacement theory applies).
The beam cross-section is prismatic (constant along the length) unless otherwise specified; treat it as such.
The beam is modeled as a 1D Euler-Bernoulli beam, meaning plane sections remain plane and shear deformation is neglected.
Axial (tension) effects are absent because no horizontal force is applied; the axial internal force N = 0 everywhere.

Determine the support reactions first.
By symmetry and vertical equilibrium, each support reaction is R = P/2 acting upward.
Moment equilibrium is automatically satisfied by symmetry.

Define x as the coordinate measured from the left support toward the right support, 0 <= x <= L.
Use the sign convention that internal shear force V is positive when the left face of a cut section carries an upward force, and internal bending moment M is positive when it causes sagging (tension on the bottom fiber).

For the left half of the beam, 0 <= x < L/2:
The internal shear force is V(x) = +P/2 (constant, positive).
The internal bending moment is M(x) = (P/2) * x (linearly increasing from 0 at x = 0).

For the right half of the beam, L/2 < x <= L:
The internal shear force is V(x) = -P/2 (constant, negative, due to the applied load P at midspan).
The internal bending moment is M(x) = (P/2) * (L - x) (linearly decreasing from the peak back to 0 at x = L).

At x = L/2 the shear force has a jump discontinuity of magnitude P, from +P/2 to -P/2.
At x = L/2 the bending moment reaches its maximum value M_max = P*L/4.
At both supports (x = 0 and x = L) the bending moment is zero, consistent with simple support conditions.
The bending moment diagram is triangular, peaking at midspan.
The shear force diagram is piecewise constant, with equal and opposite values on each half.

The governing differential relations used are dV/dx = -q(x) and dM/dx = V(x), where q(x) is the distributed load intensity (zero here except for the point load handled via the jump condition).
The normal (bending) stress at any cross-section is sigma(y) = -M(x) * y / I, where y is measured from the neutral axis and I is the second moment of area of the cross-section.
The maximum normal stress occurs at the extreme fibers (y = +/- c) of the midspan cross-section: sigma_max = M_max * c / I = P*L*c / (4*I).
The transverse shear stress at any cross-section is tau = V(x) * Q / (I * b), where Q is the first moment of area of the portion of the cross-section above (or below) the point of interest, and b is the width at that point.
The maximum shear stress on any cross-section occurs at the neutral axis.

Additional assumptions: the beam is straight, the load P is applied along the vertical plane of symmetry so no torsion or lateral bending occurs, and self-weight is neglected.
