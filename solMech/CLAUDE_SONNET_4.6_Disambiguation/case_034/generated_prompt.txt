The beam is an Euler-Bernoulli beam under small-deformation, linear-elastic assumptions.

The beam is simply supported at both ends with span L = 3000 mm.

A single point load P = 4000 N acts vertically downward at midspan (x = L/2).

The material and section properties are E = 69000 MPa and I = 3,500,000 mm⁴.

Compute EI = 69000 × 3,500,000 = 2.415 × 10¹¹ N·mm².

Assume the beam is prismatic (EI is constant along the full span).

Assume the beam self-weight is neglected; the only load is the point load P.

Assume no axial force, no torsion, and no out-of-plane effects are present.

Assume shear deformation is neglected (Euler-Bernoulli hypothesis: plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis).

The governing differential equation is EI·(d⁴w/dx⁴) = q(x), where w is the transverse deflection and q(x) is the distributed load intensity (zero except at the point load location).

For a simply supported beam with a midspan point load, use the standard closed-form solution derived from this governing equation with boundary conditions w(0) = 0, w(L) = 0, w''(0) = 0, w''(L) = 0.

The midspan deflection formula is δ_max = PL³ / (48·EI).

Substitute values: δ_max = (4000 × 3000³) / (48 × 2.415 × 10¹¹).

Compute numerator: 4000 × 27,000,000,000 = 1.08 × 10¹⁴ N·mm³.

Compute denominator: 48 × 2.415 × 10¹¹ = 1.1592 × 10¹³ N·mm².

Compute δ_max = 1.08 × 10¹⁴ / 1.1592 × 10¹³ = 9.317 mm (downward).

The support slope formula is θ = PL² / (16·EI), giving the magnitude of the rotation at each support (equal by symmetry).

Substitute values: θ = (4000 × 3000²) / (16 × 2.415 × 10¹¹).

Compute numerator: 4000 × 9,000,000 = 3.6 × 10¹⁰ N·mm².

Compute denominator: 16 × 2.415 × 10¹¹ = 3.864 × 10¹² N·mm².

Compute θ = 3.6 × 10¹⁰ / 3.864 × 10¹² = 0.009317 radians.

The left support slope is positive (beam rotates clockwise when viewed from the left) and the right support slope is equal in magnitude but opposite in sign by symmetry.

The shear force in the beam is constant within each half-span and equals the reaction force R = P/2 = 2000 N.

The shear force is +2000 N for 0 < x < L/2 and −2000 N for L/2 < x < L (using the sign convention that upward shear on the left face is positive).

The bending moment varies linearly from zero at each support to a maximum at midspan of M_max = PL/4 = 4000 × 3000 / 4 = 3,000,000 N·mm = 3.0 kN·m.

Summary of results: midspan deflection δ_max = 9.317 mm downward, support rotation θ = 0.009317 radians (0.5338 degrees), maximum bending moment M_max = 3.0 × 10⁶ N·mm, and maximum shear force V_max = 2000 N.
