Model an Euler-Bernoulli cantilever beam with a single downward point load at the free end.

The beam is fixed (clamped) at x = 0 and free at x = L.
The beam length is L = 800 mm = 0.8 m.
The applied load is a downward point load P = 2500 N acting at x = L (the free end).
There is no applied moment anywhere on the beam.

Assume small deformations throughout (linear-elastic regime).
Assume the Euler-Bernoulli beam hypothesis: plane sections remain plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis; shear deformation is neglected.
Assume the beam is prismatic: cross-section and material properties are constant along the length.
Assume the material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic with Young's modulus E and second moment of area I (EI = flexural rigidity, to be specified or left symbolic).
Assume the beam lies along the x-axis with the neutral axis coinciding with the centroidal axis.
Neglect self-weight of the beam unless otherwise stated.
Neglect axial loads; only transverse bending response is considered.

Governing differential equation: EI * d4v/dx4 = q(x), where v(x) is the transverse deflection and q(x) = 0 (no distributed load).
The bending moment relation is: M(x) = EI * d2v/dx2.
The shear force relation is: V(x) = EI * d3v/dx3.

Boundary conditions at the fixed end (x = 0): v(0) = 0 (no deflection) and dv/dx(0) = 0 (no slope/rotation).
Boundary conditions at the free end (x = L): M(L) = EI * d2v/dx2 at x=L = 0 (no applied moment) and V(L) = EI * d3v/dx3 at x=L = -P (applied shear from point load, using sign convention where downward load is negative).

Integrate EI * d4v/dx4 = 0 four times applying the four boundary conditions to obtain v(x).
The resulting deflection profile is: v(x) = (P / (6EI)) * (3Lx2 - x3).
The maximum deflection occurs at the free end x = L: v_max = PL3 / (3EI).
Substitute P = 2500 N and L = 0.8 m to get v_max = (2500 * 0.512) / (3EI) = 1280 / (3EI) meters, where EI is in N·m2.

The bending moment distribution is: M(x) = P(L - x), varying linearly from M(0) = PL at the fixed end to M(L) = 0 at the free end.
The maximum bending moment is at the fixed end: M_max = P * L = 2500 * 0.8 = 2000 N·m.
The shear force is constant along the beam: V(x) = P = 2500 N throughout.

The maximum bending stress at any cross-section is: sigma_max = M(x) * c / I, where c is the distance from the neutral axis to the outermost fiber.
The maximum bending stress in the beam occurs at the fixed end: sigma_max = M_max * c / I = 2000c / I, where c and I depend on the cross-section geometry (not yet specified).
