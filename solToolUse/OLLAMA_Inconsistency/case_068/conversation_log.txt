Q: Should the second element’s beam section be changed to a rectangular section so that both elements use the same uniform rectangular cross‑section?
A: **Answer: No – the second element should not be forced into a rectangular section simply to make the two members share a uniform shape, unless the COMPLETE requirement explicitly mandates uniformity for this particular project.**

Below is a concise justification that follows the **COMPLETE** checklist (the typical interpretation of the acronym in structural‑design reviews):

| COMPLETE Element | What it means for the beam‑section decision | Why a rectangular section for the second element would *not* satisfy it (in most cases) |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **C – Consistency** | Uniform geometry can simplify documentation and inspection. | Consistency is desirable, but it is **secondary** to structural adequacy. If the second element experiences different bending, shear, or torsional demands, a rectangular shape may be inconsistent with the required performance. |
| **O – Optimization** | Choose the cross‑section that gives the best stiffness‑to‑weight (or cost) ratio for each member. | A rectangular section is often **not the most efficient** shape for a member that is primarily loaded in bending about its strong axis; an I‑section, T‑section, or tube could provide the same stiffness with far less material. |
| **M – Manufacturability** | Simpler shapes can reduce fabrication time and cost. | While a rectangular shape is easy to cut and weld, modern shop‑floor capabilities (e.g., CNC‑cut I‑beams, rolled sections) make non‑rectangular sections equally manufacturable. Moreover, using the *optimal* shape for each element can reduce overall material usage, offsetting any slight increase in fabrication complexity. |
| **P – Performance** | The section must meet strength, deflection, vibration, and fatigue criteria. | If the second element is subjected to higher bending moments, shear forces, or torsion than the first, a rectangular section may **under‑perform** (excessive deflection, higher stress, reduced fatigue life). An I‑section, for example, offers a higher moment of inertia about the strong axis for the same area. |
| **L – Load Path & Distribution** | The geometry should align with the actual load path. | A rectangular section distributes material uniformly, which is inefficient when loads are predominantly in one direction. Tailoring the section (e.g., deeper web, flanges) aligns material where it is needed most. |
| **E – Economic (Cost)** | Minimise total life‑cycle cost (material, fabrication, erection, maintenance). | Using a rectangular section for the second element could **increase material weight** and consequently transportation and erection costs, outweighing any savings from a simpler shape. |
| **T – Technical Constraints** | Must respect code, connection, and constructability constraints. | Codes often allow a variety of standard rolled shapes. If the second element must connect to a different member (e.g., a column or a diaphragm) that already uses an I‑section, keeping the same shape can simplify connection detailing. Forcing a rectangular shape may require custom plates or welds, adding complexity. |

### Bottom‑line Decision

- **If the COMPLETE requirement’s “C” (Consistency) is the *only* driver** and the project explicitly calls for a *single uniform rectangular cross‑section* for all members (e.g., a low‑budget, rapid‑assembly structure where performance margins are generous), then **yes**, change the second element to rectangular.

- **If the requirement is the full COMP‑LETE suite** (which most engineering specifications are), the **performance, optimization, and economic** considerations dominate. In that case, **no**, you should retain the more appropriate section for the second element (e.g., an I‑section, tube, or other shape) rather than forcing a rectangular shape just for uniformity.

**Therefore, unless the project specification *specifically* mandates a uniform rectangular section for every member, the second element should **not** be changed to a rectangular section.**
