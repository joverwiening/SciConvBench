Q: What is the exact mathematical expression for the vertical traction on the hypotenuse when parameterized by the local arclength coordinate along that edge?
A: The complete requirement does **not** prescribe a local‑arclength formulation; it states that the sinusoidal traction must be evaluated with the global \(x\) coordinate.  

If you wish to express the same traction in terms of the arclength \(s\) measured along the hypotenuse \(x+2y=1\) (from the point \((1,0)\) to \((0,0.5)\)), a reasonable assumption is to map the global \(x\) coordinate onto \(s\) linearly:

\[
\begin{aligned}
L &= \sqrt{(1-0)^2+(0-0.5)^2}= \sqrt{1.25}= \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2},\\[4pt]
s &\in[0,L],\\[4pt]
x(s) &= 1-\frac{s}{L},\\[4pt]
y(s) &= \frac{s}{L}\,\frac{1}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The vertical ( \(y\) ) traction then becomes  

\[
t_y(s)= -5\times10^{3}\,\sin\!\bigl(\pi\,x(s)\bigr)
      = -5\times10^{3}\,\sin\!\Bigl[\pi\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{L}\Bigr)\Bigr],
\qquad 0\le s\le L .
\]

Thus the traction vector on the hypotenuse is  

\[
\mathbf{t}(s)=\bigl(0,\;t_y(s)\bigr)
            =\Bigl(0,\;-5\times10^{3}\,\sin\!\bigl[\pi(1-s/L)\bigr]\Bigr).
\]
