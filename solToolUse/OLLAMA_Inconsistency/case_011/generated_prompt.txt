Use the legacy FEniCS (dolfin) Python interface.  
Create a 2‑D rectangular domain of size 1 m × 0.5 m with a circular hole of radius 0.1 m centered at (0.5 m, 0.25 m).  
Generate a structured mesh with 64 elements along each edge of the outer rectangle (refine around the hole if needed).  
Define a vector‑valued continuous Lagrange function space (P1) for the displacement field **u**.  
Employ plane‑stress constitutive relations with unit thickness:  
\( C = \frac{E}{1-\nu^{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \nu & 0 \\ \nu & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1-\nu}{2} \end{bmatrix} \).  
Set material parameters: Young’s modulus \(E = 70\text{e}9\) Pa, Poisson’s ratio \(\nu = 0.33\), density \(\rho = 2700\) kg/m³, gravitational acceleration \(g = 9.81\) m/s².  
Apply body force **f** = (0, ‑ρ g) to account for gravity.  
Impose a pressure load of magnitude \(p = 1\text{e}5\) Pa on the hole boundary using the traction \( \mathbf{t} = +p\,\mathbf{n} \) where **n** is the outward unit normal of the solid (so the pressure acts inward on the cavity).  
Enforce a clamped (zero displacement) condition on the left edge \(x = 0\).  
Formulate the weak form of the small‑strain linear elasticity problem and solve the resulting linear system with a direct solver (e.g., PETSc LU).  
Compute the Cauchy stress tensor \(\boldsymbol{\sigma} = C:\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})\) and evaluate the von Mises stress \( \sigma_{vm} = \sqrt{\sigma_{xx}^2 - \sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} + \sigma_{yy}^2 + 3\sigma_{xy}^2} \).  
Save the displacement field **u**, the full in‑plane stress tensor components, and the scalar von Mises field to XDMF files (with accompanying HDF5 data) named `displacement.xdmf`, `stress.xdmf`, and `von_mises.xdmf`.  
Also write VTK files (`displacement.vtk`, `stress.vtk`, `von_mises.vtk`) for easy visualization in Paraview.
