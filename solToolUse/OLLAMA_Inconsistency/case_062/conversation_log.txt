| **Kalinga VII** | 276 | 7th king of the dynasty (r. 295‑312) | Builder of the Mahanadi Canal System |

> **Why “VII”?**  The dynasty used a **regnal‑numbering** system (the seventh sovereign of the line) rather than strict birth order; thus Kalinga VII is the “seventh” king even though he is the second‑born son of the second queen.

### Cadet branches (formal split c. 460 CE)

- **Utkala** – descended from Kalinga IV (second‑wife line).  
- **Champa** – descended from Kalinga II (chief‑queen line).  
- **Matsya** – descended from Kalinga VI (military commander).

## 3. Political Structure & Administration  

1. **Central Authority** – The king possessed absolute power over the core Kalinga region (modern Odisha).  
2. **Provincial Governance** – Close male relatives (usually sons) were appointed *Mahārājas* of peripheral provinces (Utkala, Champa, Matsya).  
3. **Council of Senāpatis** – A standing military council headed by the **Senāpati‑Mahārāja** (Kalinga VI) that advised the king on defence and large‑scale projects.  
4. **Bureau of Waterworks** – Instituted by Kalinga VII; led by a *Pañcāśa* (chief engineer) who reported directly to the monarch.  
5. **Fiscal System** – Land‑tax (*śulka*) assessed per hectare; surplus grain taxed at 5 % of the harvest, providing revenue for public works and diplomatic gifts.  

*Primary evidence: Pillar 3 inscription (administrative titles), Kalinga‑VII Prashasti (tax figures), and Das (2017) hydraulic survey (bureau of waterworks).*

## 5. Cultural & Diplomatic Achievements  

- **Mahāyāna Buddhism** – Kalinga VII patronised the *Vajrasattva* monastery (c. 305 CE); the Champa branch later became an early Mahāyāna centre, as recorded on stone‑inscriptions.  
- **Maritime Trade** – Surplus rice financed a fleet of *kōṭṭa* vessels that sailed to the **Gupta** (north‑west) and **Liu Song** (south‑east) courts, exchanging silk, spices, and Buddhist texts (Li S., 318 CE).  
- **Artistic Patronage** – The *Mahāpurā Reservoir* was adorned with bas‑relief panels depicting the king’s lineage and the mythic *Naga* water‑guardian, a motif later adopted by the Utkala branch.  

*Evidence: inscriptional dedicatory texts at the Vajrasattva monastery, maritime log‑entries on the *Kalinga‑VII Prashasti*, and Sharma (1998) excavation report of the reservoir complex.*
