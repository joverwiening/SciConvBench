Use legacy FEniCS (dolfin) to solve a 2D small-strain linear elasticity problem on a right triangular domain.
Model the domain with vertices (0,0), (1,0), (0,0.5).
Generate a mesh with a target resolution corresponding to 64 elements.
Use a vector-valued continuous Galerkin displacement space of first order (CG1) on the mesh.
Assume plane stress with unit thickness.
Use isotropic linear elasticity with E = 70e9 Pa and ν = 0.33.
Use the reduced plane-stress constitutive law, i.e., λ_ps = E*ν/(1-ν^2) and μ = E/(2*(1+ν)).
Define strain as ε(u) = sym(grad(u)).
Define Cauchy stress as σ(u) = 2μ ε(u) + λ_ps tr(ε(u)) I in 2D.
Apply body force due to gravity with density ρ = 2700 kg/m^3 and g = 9.81 m/s^2 as f = (0, -ρ g).
Apply a Neumann traction on the boundary x = 0 corresponding to σ_xx = 4000 Pa acting in the +x direction, i.e., t = (4000, 0) on x = 0.
Apply a clamped (Dirichlet) boundary condition u = (0,0) on the sloping edge x + 2y = 1.
Leave all other boundaries traction-free unless explicitly loaded.
Formulate the variational problem: find u in V such that ∫_Ω σ(u):ε(v) dx = ∫_Ω f·v dx + ∫_{x=0} t·v ds for all v in V with the Dirichlet condition enforced.
Assemble the linear system and solve using a direct linear solver available in dolfin (e.g., LU).
Compute and output the displacement field u over the domain.
Compute the in-plane Cauchy stress tensor field σ(u) over the domain.
Compute the von Mises stress from the in-plane stress components as σ_vm = sqrt(σ_xx^2 - σ_xx σ_yy + σ_yy^2 + 3 σ_xy^2).
Project von Mises stress to a scalar CG1 (or DG0) space for visualization.
Provide plots or saved output files for displacement magnitude and von Mises stress distributions.
