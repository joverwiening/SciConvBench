The simulation solves a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem in legacy FEniCS (dolfin).

The domain is a stepped polygon with vertices (0,0)-(0.8,0)-(0.8,0.2)-(0.5,0.2)-(0.5,0.5)-(0.3,0.5)-(0.3,0.2)-(0,0.2).

The mesh contains 72 triangular elements.

The material properties are Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, density ρ = 2700 kg/m³, and gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².

The constitutive law is the reduced plane-stress elasticity tensor with unit thickness.

The boundary conditions are: fixed displacement (Dirichlet u = (0,0)) on the bottom edge where y = 0.

The loading consists of two parts: body force due to gravity acting in the negative y-direction with magnitude ρ*g, and a surface traction on the top boundary segment where 0.3 ≤ x ≤ 0.5 and y = 0.5. The traction is purely vertical in the negative y-direction with magnitude σ_yy(x) = -8000*x Pa, varying linearly with x. The traction is zero on all other boundary segments.

The variational formulation uses a vector-valued Lagrange finite element space of degree 1 for displacement. The bilinear form integrates the plane-stress stiffness tensor contracted with the symmetric strain tensors. The linear form integrates the body force and the surface traction.

The linear system is solved with a direct solver (MUMPS or default LU).

The output quantities are the displacement field and the von Mises equivalent stress computed from the in-plane Cauchy stress components. The von Mises stress is calculated as sqrt(σ_xx² + σ_yy² - σ_xx*σ_yy + 3*σ_xy²) for plane stress.
