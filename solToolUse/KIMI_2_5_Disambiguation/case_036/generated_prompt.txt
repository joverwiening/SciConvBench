The simulation solves a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem in legacy FEniCS (dolfin).

The domain is a parallelogram with vertices at (0,0), (1,0), (1.2,0.3), and (0.2,0.3).

The mesh contains 72 triangular elements.

The right inclined edge connecting (1,0) to (1.2,0.3) is fixed with Dirichlet boundary conditions on both displacement components.

The left inclined edge connecting (0,0) to (0.2,0.3) carries a horizontal traction of 6000 Pa in the positive x-direction.

Body forces include gravity acting in the negative y-direction with magnitude 9.81 m/s^2 and material density 2700 kg/m^3.

Material properties are Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33.

The variational formulation uses a vector-valued displacement space of continuous piecewise linear Lagrange elements (CG1).

The plane-stress constitutive law reduces the 3D stiffness to 2D using the substitution λ = 2μν/(1-ν) where μ = E/(2(1+ν)).

The bilinear form integrates stress and strain over the domain with unit thickness.

The linear form includes contributions from both the traction boundary integral and the body force.

A direct linear solver (MUMPS or default LU) solves the symmetric positive-definite system.

Post-processing computes the in-plane Cauchy stress tensor from the displacement gradient.

The von Mises equivalent stress is computed from the in-plane stress components as sqrt(σ_xx^2 + σ_yy^2 - σ_xx*σ_yy + 3*σ_xy^2).

The solution outputs displacement magnitude and von Mises stress distributions for visualization.
