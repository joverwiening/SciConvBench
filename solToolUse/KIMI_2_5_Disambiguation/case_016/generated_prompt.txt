The simulation solves a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem on a cross-shaped domain using legacy FEniCS (dolfin).

The domain consists of a horizontal bar spanning x from -1 to 1 with width 0.6 in y, and a vertical bar spanning y from -1 to 1 with width 0.6 in x, forming a plus sign centered at the origin. Four circular holes of radius 0.15 are cut in the four arms, centered at (0.5, 0.5), (0.5, -0.5), (-0.5, 0.5), and (-0.5, -0.5).

The mesh is generated with a target resolution of 100 elements across the characteristic domain size, using Gmsh or CGAL-based boolean operations to construct the cross with holes.

The function space is VectorFunctionSpace(mesh, "Lagrange", 2) for second-order vector Lagrange elements.

Material properties are Young's modulus E = 210000 and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.3. The plane-stress constitutive law is implemented with the modified Lamé parameters: lambda_ = E * nu / (1 + nu) / (1 - nu) and mu = E / 2 / (1 + nu), or equivalently the stress-strain relation sigma = 2*mu*epsilon + lambda_*tr(epsilon)*I with the plane-stress reduction applied.

The strain-displacement relation is epsilon(u) = 0.5*(grad(u) + grad(u).T). The stress tensor is computed from strain using the plane-stress Hooke's law.

Boundary conditions: Dirichlet boundary condition u = (0, 0) on the left edge where x = -1. Neumann boundary condition: downward traction t = (0, -p) applied on the right edge where x = 1, with p = 1.0 (unit traction magnitude).

The variational problem is: find u in V such that inner(sigma(u), epsilon(v))*dx == dot(t, v)*ds for all v in V, where the integral over ds applies only to the right boundary.

The problem is solved using a direct linear solver (default MUMPS or LU solver in dolfin).

Post-processing computes the von Mises stress as sqrt(3/2 * s:s) where s = sigma - 1/3*tr(sigma)*I is the deviatoric stress, or equivalently for 2D plane stress as sqrt(sigma_xx^2 - sigma_xx*sigma_yy + sigma_yy^2 + 3*sigma_xy^2).

Output includes the displacement field u and the von Mises stress field, saved to XDMF or VTK format for visualization.
