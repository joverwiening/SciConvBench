Use FEniCS legacy (dolfin) to solve a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem on a perforated rectangular plate.

The plate geometry is a rectangle with corners at (0, 0) and (1, 0.5), containing a circular hole of radius 0.1 m centered at (0.5, 0.25).

The mesh is generated with 64 elements along each edge of the rectangular domain, with refinement around the hole.

The material properties are Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, and density ρ = 2700 kg/m^3.

The displacement function space is a vector-valued Lagrange space of degree 1 (P1) on the 2D mesh.

The plane-stress constitutive law is implemented with the reduced stiffness tensor: σ = (E/(1-ν^2)) * [[1, ν, 0], [ν, 1, 0], [0, 0, (1-ν)/2]] * ε, where ε is the Voigt-form strain vector [ε_xx, ε_yy, 2*ε_xy].

The variational form is: find u in V such that inner(σ(u), ε(v))*dx == L for all v in V, where L includes body forces and traction terms.

The left boundary at x = 0 is fixed with Dirichlet boundary condition u = (0, 0).

Gravity loading is applied as a body force b = (0, -ρ*g) with g = 9.81 m/s^2.

A torque M = 800 Nm is applied around a small arc boundary. The arc is centered at (1, 0.25) with radius r = 0.02 m and angular span of 30 degrees (π/6 radians), oriented such that the arc faces leftward toward the plate interior.

The torque is converted to tangential traction using t_θ = M / (2 * π * r) distributed over the arc surface. For the finite arc, the traction magnitude is scaled by (2*π) / (arc_angle) to maintain total torque equivalence, giving t_θ = M / (r * arc_angle * thickness) with thickness = 1.0 m.

The traction is applied as a Neumann boundary condition on the arc boundary, with components t_x = -t_θ * sin(θ) and t_y = t_θ * cos(θ) where θ is the angle from the arc center.

The linear system is solved using a direct solver (LU factorization via MUMPS or default PETSc LU).

Post-processing computes the von Mises equivalent stress from the in-plane Cauchy stress components: σ_vm = sqrt(σ_xx^2 + σ_yy^2 - σ_xx*σ_yy + 3*σ_xy^2).

The solution outputs include displacement magnitude, stress components (σ_xx, σ_yy, σ_xy), and von Mises stress for visualization and analysis.
