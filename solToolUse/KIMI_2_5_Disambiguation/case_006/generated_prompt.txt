The simulation solves a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem using legacy FEniCS (dolfin).

The domain is a stepped polygon with 8 vertices defined as follows: (0.0, 0.0), (0.3, 0.0), (0.3, 0.2), (0.5, 0.2), (0.5, 0.0), (0.8, 0.0), (0.8, 0.4), (0.0, 0.4). The bottom edge lies at y=0. The vertical stems are located at x=0.3 and x=0.5.

The material properties are: Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, density ρ = 2700 kg/m^3, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s^2.

The constitutive law uses plane-stress reduction with unit thickness. The Lamé parameters are computed as λ = E*ν/((1+ν)*(1-ν)) and μ = E/(2*(1+ν)). The stress-strain relationship for plane stress yields σ = λ*tr(ε)*I + 2*μ*ε with the appropriate reduction for σ_zz = 0.

The mesh contains 72 triangular elements generated from the polygonal domain.

Boundary conditions are mixed. The bottom edge (y=0) is fully clamped: displacement u = (0, 0). The stem regions are constrained: the left stem at x=0.3 for 0 ≤ y ≤ 0.2 has zero horizontal displacement u_x = 0, and the right stem at x=0.5 for 0 ≤ y ≤ 0.2 has zero horizontal displacement u_x = 0.

The loading consists of two parts. A horizontal traction of magnitude 5000 Pa is applied on the top edge segment where 0.3 ≤ x ≤ 0.5, acting in the positive x-direction. Body forces from gravity act in the negative y-direction with magnitude ρ*g.

The variational formulation uses a vector-valued displacement space V = VectorFunctionSpace(mesh, 'P', 1). The trial and test functions are u, v ∈ V. The strain is ε(u) = 0.5*(grad(u) + grad(u).T). The stress is σ(u) = λ*tr(ε(u))*Identity(2) + 2*μ*ε(u). The bilinear form is a(u,v) = inner(σ(u), ε(v))*dx. The linear form is L(v) = dot(f, v)*dx + dot(t, v)*ds, where f = (0, -ρ*g) is the body force and t is the traction vector.

The problem is solved using a direct linear solver via solve(a == L, u, bc) where bc enforces the Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Post-processing computes the von Mises stress from the in-plane Cauchy stress. The stress tensor is projected to a tensor function space. The von Mises equivalent stress is computed as σ_vm = sqrt(σ_11^2 + σ_22^2 - σ_11*σ_22 + 3*σ_12^2) for plane stress.

The output includes the displacement field u(x,y) and the von Mises stress distribution over the domain.
