The simulation solves a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem using FEniCS legacy (dolfin).

The domain is a parallelogram with vertices at (0,0), (1,0), (1.2,0.3), and (0.2,0.3).

The material properties are Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, density ρ = 2700 kg/m^3, and gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s^2.

The mesh consists of 72 triangular elements.

The left sloped edge connects (0,0) to (0.2,0.3) and is fixed with zero displacement.

The right sloped edge connects (1,0) to (1.2,0.3) and is fixed with zero displacement.

The bottom edge connects (0,0) to (1,0) and is traction-free.

The top edge connects (0.2,0.3) to (1.2,0.3) and has an inward normal traction of magnitude 5000 Pa applied in the negative y-direction.

Body forces include gravity acting in the negative y-direction with magnitude ρ*g.

The variational formulation uses a vector-valued displacement function space with Lagrange elements of degree 1.

The plane-stress constitutive law relates stress to strain using the reduced stiffness tensor for plane stress conditions with unit thickness.

The bilinear form integrates the stress-strain product over the domain.

The linear form integrates the body force contribution and the traction contribution over the top edge.

A direct linear solver (MUMPS or default LU) solves the resulting linear system.

Post-processing computes the Cauchy stress tensor from the displacement gradient.

Post-processing computes the von Mises equivalent stress from the in-plane stress components.

The output includes the displacement field and the von Mises stress distribution.
