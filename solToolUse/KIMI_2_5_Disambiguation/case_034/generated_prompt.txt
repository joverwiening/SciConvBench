The L-shaped structure is defined as the unit square [0,1]×[0,1] with the lower-right quadrant [0.5,1]×[0,0.5] removed.

Material properties are: Young's modulus E = 70×10⁹ Pa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, density ρ = 2700 kg/m³, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².

The computational domain uses plane-stress conditions with unit thickness. The constitutive law uses the reduced plane-stress stiffness tensor.

The mesh is generated with 64 elements per edge on the outer boundary, with appropriate resolution on the inner re-entrant corner.

The left boundary at x=0 is fully clamped (zero displacement in both x and y directions).

Body forces due to gravity act in the negative y-direction with magnitude ρg.

A vertically varying traction is applied on the outer right edge segment at x=1, y∈[0.5,1]. The traction vector is (0, σ_yy(y)) where σ_yy(y) = -2.65×10⁴ × y Pa. This represents a compressive traction increasing linearly from -1.325×10⁴ Pa at y=0.5 to -2.65×10⁴ Pa at y=1. The inner vertical edge at x=0.5, y∈[0,0.5] remains traction-free. All other boundaries are traction-free.

The variational formulation uses a vector-valued Lagrange finite element space of degree 1 for displacement. The bilinear form implements the plane-stress elasticity operator. The linear form integrates body forces over the domain and the prescribed traction over the appropriate boundary segment.

A direct linear solver (MUMPS or default LU) solves the resulting symmetric positive-definite system.

Post-processing computes the stress tensor field from the displacement gradient using the plane-stress constitutive relation. The von Mises equivalent stress is computed from the in-plane Cauchy stress components as √(σ_xx² + σ_yy² - σ_xxσ_yy + 3σ_xy²).

Output includes the displacement magnitude field, the von Mises stress field, and verification of boundary condition satisfaction.
