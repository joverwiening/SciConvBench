## 1.  What a pressure on a shell really means  

* In a **shell** element the pressure is **normal to the shell midsurface**.  
* The direction of the normal is taken from the **material orientation** that you assign to the layer (OR1, OR2 … in your file).  
* Therefore you do **not** have to give a vector (x‑y‑z) for the pressure – you only give the **magnitude** (positive = acting in the direction of the shell normal, negative = opposite).

## 3.  Create a *surface* that uses the **top layer** of those elements  

```abaqus
*SURFACE, TYPE=ELEMENT, NAME=TopSurf
Etop, S1
```

* `S1` = **first layer** of the composite lay‑up (the “top” layer).  
* If you wanted the **bottom** layer you would use `S4` (the fourth layer in your 4‑ply definition).

---

## 4.  Apply the pressure to that surface  

Now the pressure load is a **single line** – you do **not** have to list every element individually.

```abaqus
*STEP, NAME=PressStep, NLGEOM=YES
*STATIC
*DLOAD
TopSurf, P, 1.0e-3
*END STEP
```

* `NLGEOM=YES` is recommended for pressure loads because the pressure magnitude depends on the current area of the surface.  
* The magnitude `1.0e-3` is the pressure in the **current units** of your model (e.g. N/mm² if you are using mm‑N‑Pa).  
* The sign of the pressure follows the **shell normal** (positive = acting *outward* from the midsurface). If you need the opposite direction, just change the sign.

```abaqus
** -------------------------------------------------
*SURFACE, TYPE=ELEMENT, NAME=TopSurf
```
```abaqus
Etop, S1                ! S1 = outermost ply of the composite lay‑up

** -------------------------------------------------
** 3) Load step – uniform pressure on the top surface
** -------------------------------------------------
*STEP, NAME=PressStep, NLGEOM=YES
*STATIC
*DLOAD
```
```abaqus
TopSurf, P, 1.0e-3     ! <-- change 1.0e-3 to the pressure you need (units = stress)
*END STEP
```

### What you must edit inside the three blocks  

| Block | What to edit | Example |
|-------|--------------|---------|
| `*ELSET, ELSET=Etop` | Replace the numbers with **all element IDs whose outer face (S1) is to be pressurised**. You may list them individually, as a range (`first, last, increment`) or a mixture. | `1, 529, 1` → `1, 1000, 1` if elements 1‑1000 belong to the top surface. |
| `*DLOAD` line | Replace `1.0e-3` with the actual pressure magnitude you want (same units as the material stiffness, e.g. MPa). | `TopSurf, P, 2.5e-2` for 0.025 MPa. |

If you already have a step after the one that contains your original loads, you may paste the three blocks **after that step**; they will simply run as an additional step.
