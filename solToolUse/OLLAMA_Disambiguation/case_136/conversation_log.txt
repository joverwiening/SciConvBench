| Variable | Meaning (per contact point) | Units |
|----------|----------------------------|-------|
| **CDIS** | **Contact displacement** – the normal gap (or over‑closure) between the slave point and the master surface.  Positive values mean the two surfaces are separated; negative values mean they have penetrated (over‑closure). | length |

So, in short:

* **CDIS** – normal separation (gap) between the two contacting surfaces.  
* **CSTRE** – normal contact pressure (contact stress) acting across the

A: **Answer – What the two columns *CDIS* and *CSTRE* in an Abaqus *CONTACT FILE* represent**

When you request a *CONTACT FILE* in Abaqus (e.g.  

``` 
*CONTACT FILE, FREQUENCY=10
CDIS, CSTRE
```  

) Abaqus writes, for **every contact point** that belongs to the contact
pairs defined in the model, the values of the two **contact‑output
variables** that you have listed after the keyword.  

| Variable | Full name (as used in the Abaqus‑Analysis Reference Manual) | Physical meaning (per contact point) | Sign convention | Typical units |
|----------|------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **CDIS** | **Contact DISplacement** | The **normal gap** (or over‑closure) between the slave node and the master surface. It is the distance measured along the contact normal. | **Positive** → the two surfaces are separated (gap). <br>**Negative** → the slave point has penetrated the master surface (over‑closure). | Length (e.g. mm, m, in) |
| **CSTRE**| **Contact STRESS** (normal contact pressure) | The **normal contact pressure** (scalar) transmitted across the interface. It is the normal component of the traction vector acting on the slave surface. | **Positive** → compressive pressure (contact is “closed”). <br>**Zero** → no contact pressure (gap > 0). <br>**Negative** values are not normally produced; if they appear they indicate a tensile traction (rare, usually a modelling error). | Force / area (e.g. MPa, N/mm², psi) |

---

### How to read the file

The file is written at the frequency you specified (every 10 increments in the
example). For each output step you will see a block that looks like

```
STEP 1, INCREMENT 10
   NODE   PAIR   CDIS      CSTRE
   1234   1      0.00012   2.35E+03
   5678   1     -0.00003   1.78E+03
   ...
```

* **NODE / PAIR** – identifies the slave node (or integration point) and the
contact pair to which it belongs.  
* **CDIS** – the normal gap at that point (length).  
* **CSTRE** – the normal contact pressure at that point (stress).

If you need a **total contact force** you must integrate the pressure over the
contact area, i.e.

\[
F_{\text{contact}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \; C_{\text{STRE},i}\; A_i
\]

where \(A_i\) is the area (or length in 2‑D) associated with the i‑th contact
point. Abaqus does **not** write this total force automatically; you have to
post‑process it (e.g. with a Python script or a user subroutine).

---

### Common follow‑up questions

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| *What if I need the tangential (shear) contact stress?* | Request a different output variable, e.g. **CSTRS** (shear stress) or **CSTRN** (normal component of the traction vector). |
| *Can I get the contact force directly?* | Yes – use the *CONTACT OUTPUT* option `*CONTACT OUTPUT, VARIABLE=FORCE` (or request `*ELEMENT OUTPUT, CONTACT STRESS`) and then sum the forces, or write a *FIELD OUTPUT* request for `CFORCE`. |
| *Is CDIS the same as the “penetration” reported in the *Contact* dialog?* | Yes – a negative CDIS value corresponds to penetration (over‑closure). |
| *Do the values include any scaling (e.g. engineering vs. true stress)?* | No scaling is applied; CSTRE is the **Cauchy (true) normal pressure** as computed by the contact algorithm. |
| *What about units?* | Abaqus uses the units you defined for the model. If you model in SI, CDIS will be in meters (or mm if you chose mm) and CSTRE in Pascals (or MPa, N/mm², etc.). |

---

### Bottom line

- **CDIS** = *normal gap* (or over‑closure) between the contacting surfaces.  
- **CSTRE** = *normal contact pressure* (contact stress) acting across the interface.

These two columns give you, point‑by‑point, how far the surfaces are apart (or
how much they have interpenetrated) and how much compressive pressure is
being transmitted at each contact location. By post‑processing them you can
obtain the overall contact behaviour (total force, load‑distribution, etc.).

   - If you see a value of **0.000 mm**, the slave point is exactly on the master surface (contact is closed).

### How to read the two columns in the contact file  

1. **CDIS (column 1)** – tells you *how far* the two faces are from each other at that particular contact point.  
   * **Positive value** → a gap exists; the larger the number, the larger the separation.  
   * **Negative value** → the slave point has penetrated the master surface; the magnitude gives the amount of inter‑penetration (often a few microns in a well‑converged contact).  

2. **CSTRE (column 2)** – tells you *what pressure* the contact algorithm is transmitting across the interface at that point.  
   * **Positive value** → compressive pressure (the usual “contact pressure”).  
   * **Negative value** → tensile pressure (only appears if the contact definition permits separation and the algorithm detects a pulling‑off situation).

### From point‑wise data to a total contact force  

If you need the **resultant contact force** on a given surface:

1. **Obtain the contact area associated with each point**.  
   * In Abaqus the contact output file also contains a third column **CAREA** (contact area) when you request it, or you can compute the area from the element geometry (e.g., integration‑point weight × surface Jacobian).  

2. **Multiply pressure by area** for each point:  

\[
\mathbf{F}_i = \text{CSTRE}_i \; \times \; \text{CAREA}_i \; \mathbf{n}_i
\]

   where \(\mathbf{n}_i\) is the unit normal vector (pointing from slave to master).  

3. **Sum over all points** belonging to the contact pair:  

\[
\mathbf{F}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{F}_i
\]

   The magnitude of \(\mathbf{F}_{\text{total}}\) is the total normal contact force; the vector sum also gives any net shear component if you have also requested the tangential contact tractions (e.g., **CTANG**).

- **Check the unit system** of your model (e.g., mm‑kg‑s → CDIS in mm, CSTRE in N/mm²).  
- **Verify the sign convention** in the Abaqus manual: “positive normal pressure = compressive”.  
- **Make sure the contact pair is defined as “master–slave”** (or “surface‑to‑surface”) – CDIS is always measured from the *slave* toward the *master* along the master‑surface normal.  
- **If you see negative CDIS values**, the contact algorithm is allowing a small amount of penetration (typical for penalty or augmented Lagrange methods).  
- **If you need the total force**, request **CSTRE** *and* **CAREA** (or compute the area yourself) and perform the summation described above.

`CSTRE` is a **pressure** (force per unit area).  
To obtain a force you must multiply it by the **area that belongs to the same contact point**.  
That area is available as the output variable **CAREA** (contact area per point).  

\[
\boxed{F_{\text{total}} \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\text{CSTRE}_i \times \text{CAREA}_i\bigr)}

* `CAREA_i` – contact area associated with point *i* (same length‑units² as used in the model).  

If you only need an **average pressure** over the interface, divide the summed force by the summed contact area:

\[

| **5. Check signs** | Positive sum = net compressive force.  A negative contribution indicates a tensile segment (rare). |
| **6. (Optional) Average pressure** | `sum(CSTRE*CAREA) / sum(CAREA)`. |
| **7. Verify** | Compare the total force with reaction forces at the supporting boundaries to ensure equilibrium. |

* **CDIS** – normal gap (positive) or penetration (negative) measured along the contact normal.  
* **CSTRE** – normal contact pressure (compressive = positive, tensile = negative).  
* Both are **per‑contact‑point** quantities.

## 4. From pressure to a **resultant normal contact force**

`CSTRE` is a **pressure** (force / area).
