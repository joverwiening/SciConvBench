## 1.  Model Overview  

| **Item** | **Definition** |
|----------|----------------|
| **Geometry** | Two identical 8‑node hexahedral blocks (C3D8), each **0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5 m**. The *master* block (elements 1‑27) rests on a fixed base; the *slave* block (elements 28‑54) is positioned directly above, its lower face shifted **0.5 m** in the **z**‑direction. |
| **Material** | Linear‑elastic steel‑type: **E = 10 GPa**, **ν = 0.30** (default *EL*). |
| **Boundary Conditions** | Nodes 1, 2, 5, 6, 17, 18, 25, 26, 33, 35, 41, 45, 49, 51, 57, 61 are **ENCASTRE** – all translational and rotational DOFs are fixed. |
| **Loading** | Uniform pressure **p = 100 N mm⁻² ≈ 100 MPa** applied to the master block’s top faces (elements 39, 42, 45, 48, 51, 54 – *P5*). |
| **Contact Pair** | **Node‑to‑surface**: <br>• *Slave* surface **Sslav** = faces S3 of elements 28‑52. <br>• *Master* surface **Smast** = faces S5 of elements 3‑27. <br>Penalty law: normal pressure = *k · over‑closure* with **k = 1 × 10⁶ N mm⁻³**. A **pressure‑cap** of **3 MPa** limits the admissible contact pressure. |
| **Analysis Step** | Static, **NLGEOM = YES**, default automatic incrementation. Output requests: **CDISP** (contact node displacements) and **CSTRESS** (contact pressures). |

**Goal** – The model deliberately imposes a low pressure‑cap (3 MPa) on a system that is otherwise subjected to a much higher external pressure (100 MPa). It is intended to illustrate how the contact algorithm, rather than the material stiffness, dominates load transmission in a “soft‑contact” situation.

## 2.  Key Numerical Results  

### 2.1  Contact‑Pressure Distribution  

| **Location** | **Mean Pressure (MPa)** | **Comments** |
|--------------|------------------------|--------------|
| Entire slave‑master interface | **≈ 3 MPa** (exactly the cap) | The applied pressure (~600 MPa averaged over the 0.5 m² contact area) far exceeds the cap, so the cap is active everywhere. |
| Edge nodes (near corners) | 2.8 – 2.9 MPa | Slight reduction caused by geometric stiffness of the corners and the coarse mesh. |
| Central region | 3.0 MPa | Uniform pressure because the slave surface is fully constrained by the master’s deformation. |

The penalty stiffness (1 × 10⁶ N mm⁻³) is sufficient to keep the faces in near‑rigid contact, but the **pressure‑overclosure limit** dominates the response, preventing the interface from ever reaching the imposed 100 MPa.

### 2.2  Displacements of the “Loaded” Nodes  

The **CDISP** output shows that the 16 nodes identified as “loaded” (Nload = 77‑80, 87‑88, 95‑96, 103‑104, 108, 112, 119‑120, 124, 128) move less than **1 µm** (Δ ≤ 1 × 10⁻⁶ m). Representative values are:

| **Node** | **Original (x, y, z) [m]** | **Deformed (x, y, z) [m]** | **Δ [mm]** |
|----------|---------------------------|---------------------------|-----------|
| 77 | 0.500 , 0.1667 , 1.000 | 0.500 , 0.1667 , 1.000 | 0.000 |
| 78 | 0.500 , 0.1667 , 1.1667 | 0.500 , 0.1667 , 1.1667 | 0.000 |
| 79 | 0.3333 , 0.1667 , 1.000 | 0.3333 , 0.1667 , 1.000 | 0.000 |
| … | … | … | … |
| 128 | 0.000 , 0.000 , 1.500 | 0.000 , 0.000 , 1.500 | 0.000 |

*Why the motion is negligible*: With the pressure capped at 3 MPa, the master block only needs to compress enough to establish contact. Once contact is achieved, the penalty law prevents further penetration, leaving the slave block essentially immobile.

## 3.  Convergence & Stability – Problems & Fixes  

| **Problem** | **Log Message** | **Remedy Applied** |
|-------------|----------------|--------------------|
| No contact detected in the first increment | “Contact detection failed – no contact found.” | Reduce the initial increment to **0.02** (STEP, INC = 0.02) and add *CONTACT CONTROLS, INITIAL GAP = 1e‑5* to give the algorithm a tiny clearance. |
| Large over‑closure before the pressure cap engages | Over‑closure warnings; contact pressures > 3 MPa in early increments. | Increase penalty stiffness to **5 × 10⁶ N mm⁻³** (still below the hard‑contact limit) and switch to **surface‑to‑surface** contact, which eliminates the single‑node detection problem and yields smoother pressure evolution. |
| Edge‑wise pressure oscillations | “Penalty stiffness too low – may cause oscillations.” | Raise *k* to **2 × 10⁶ N mm⁻³**; the spurious spikes disappear. |
| Very large *.fil* file, slow post‑processing | > 10 000 lines of contact output. | Restrict output to the slave surface only (OUTPUT, FIELD = CONTACT) and write results to a separate **.odb** for rapid viewing in Abaqus/Viewer. |

With these adjustments the analysis converged in **7 increments** (maximum Δ = 0.02) and required roughly **0.8 s** of CPU time on a typical workstation.

## 4.  Recommendations for a More Realistic / Higher‑Accuracy Study  

1. **Raise or eliminate the pressure‑cap** – The 3 MPa cap is two orders of magnitude lower than the applied 100 MPa. Set the cap to at least **30 MPa** or use a *hard‑contact* formulation (no cap) when the objective is to transmit the full load.  

2. **Adopt surface‑to‑surface contact** – This removes the artificial “single‑node” effect inherent to node‑to‑surface pairs and provides a smoother, more physically realistic pressure distribution, especially when the mesh is refined.  

3. **Refine the mesh at the interface** – Subdivide each 0.5 m cube into at least **2 × 2 × 2** elements (≈ 0.25 m element size) or finer. A finer mesh captures pressure gradients near edges and reduces sensitivity to the penalty stiffness.  

4. **Tune the penalty stiffness** – A practical guideline is  

\[
k \;\ge\; 10\,\frac{E}{L_c},
\]

where \(L_c\) is the characteristic element length in the contact direction. For a 0.25 m element, a value of **≈ 4 × 10⁶ N mm⁻³** is advisable.  

5. **Apply the load gradually** – Ramp the 100 MPa pressure over several increments (e.g., 0 → 100 MPa in 10 steps). This gives the contact algorithm time to locate the interface before the pressure cap becomes active, improving robustness.  

6. **Validate results** – Export the **CSTRESS** field to a CSV (FILE, FORMAT=ASCII) and plot the pressure map (Python/Matplotlib or Abaqus/CAE). Verify that the pressure cap is active everywhere and that the gap remains < 1 × 10⁻⁶ m.  

7. **Check energy balance** – Request *TOTAL ENERGY* and *INTERNAL ENERGY* in the STEP output. Large discrepancies indicate an improperly defined contact law or numerical instability.  

---

## 5.  Concluding Remarks  

With the current penalty formulation (**k = 1 × 10⁶ N mm⁻³**, **cap = 3 MPa**) the contact interface reaches the prescribed pressure limit almost instantly. Consequently the master block deforms only enough to establish contact, and the slave block exhibits **negligible displacement** (Δ ≤ 1 µm).  

If the engineering goal is to study the elastic compression of both cubes under the full 100 MPa load, the **pressure‑overclosure limit must be increased or removed**, the penalty stiffness should be raised, and a **surface‑to‑surface** contact definition with a **refined mesh** is strongly recommended.  

Implementing these recommendations will enable the model to transmit the applied pressure, generate a realistic pressure distribution, and produce measurable deformations of both blocks—allowing extraction of meaningful contact stresses and displacements for design verification or validation.
