Use FEniCS legacy (dolfin) for all implementation.
Solve a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem using a vector-valued displacement finite element space.
Use a direct linear solver (e.g., LU) to solve the assembled linear system.
Material properties are: Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio nu = 0.33, density rho = 2700 kg/m^3, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s^2.
The geometry is a 1.0 m x 0.5 m rectangle with a circular hole of radius 0.1 m centered at (0.5, 0.25).
Mesh the domain using approximately 64 elements per edge using a Gmsh or CGAL-based mesh or a FEniCS-compatible meshing approach with equivalent resolution.
Use the plane-stress constitutive law with unit thickness.
The plane-stress stiffness tensor uses lambda_ps = (E * nu) / (1 - nu**2) and mu = E / (2 * (1 + nu)).
The stress tensor is sigma = lambda_ps * tr(eps) * I + 2 * mu * eps, where eps is the symmetric gradient of displacement.
Apply a Dirichlet boundary condition fixing displacement to zero on the entire left edge (x = 0).
Apply a body force f = (0, -rho * g) throughout the domain volume.
Apply a Neumann traction on a small loaded arc of radius r = 0.02 m centered at (1.0, 0.25) on the right boundary region.
The torque is M = 800 Nm applied over this arc.
Convert the torque to a tangential traction magnitude t_theta = M / (2 * pi * r).
Apply this traction as the fixed Cartesian vector T = (M / (2 * pi * r), 0) on the loaded arc boundary segment.
Identify the loaded arc as the portion of the boundary where the distance from point (1.0, 0.25) is less than or equal to r = 0.02 m.
Leave the circular hole boundary traction-free (no Neumann condition applied there).
Leave all other boundaries (top, bottom, right excluding the loaded arc) traction-free.
Define the variational problem: find u in V such that for all v in V, integral of sigma(u):eps(v) dx = integral of f dot v dx + integral over loaded arc of T dot v ds.
Use a continuous Lagrange vector element of degree 2 (P2) for the displacement space, or degree 1 (P1) if degree 2 causes memory issues, but prefer P2.
After solving, compute the in-plane Cauchy stress components sigma_xx, sigma_yy, sigma_xy by projecting or interpolating the stress tensor onto a suitable scalar or tensor function space.
Compute the von Mises stress as: von_mises = sqrt(sigma_xx**2 - sigma_xx*sigma_yy + sigma_yy**2 + 3*sigma_xy**2).
Output the displacement field u and the von Mises stress field to files in XDMF or PVD format for visualization.
Print the maximum displacement magnitude and the maximum von Mises stress value to the console.
