Use FEniCS legacy (dolfin) for the entire implementation.
Solve a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem using the dolfin finite element library.
Use a vector-valued displacement function space (VectorFunctionSpace) with a direct linear solver.
Material properties: Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, density ρ = 2700 kg/m³, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s².
Geometry: a rectangular plate of width 1 m and height 0.5 m with a circular hole of radius 0.1 m centered at (0.5, 0.25).
Mesh the domain using approximately 64 elements per edge, with the circular hole explicitly removed from the mesh.
Use the reduced plane-stress constitutive law with unit thickness.
Define the plane-stress stiffness tensor using the plane-stress relations: λ_eff = E·ν/((1+ν)(1-ν)), μ = E/(2(1+ν)).
Apply a Dirichlet boundary condition fixing all displacement components to zero on the left edge (x = 0).
Apply body force due to gravity: f = (0, -ρ·g) throughout the domain.
Apply a sinusoidal normal traction on the right edge (x = 1): t = (0, -6000·sin(4π·x[1])) Pa, where x[1] is the vertical coordinate y.
Apply a constant shear traction on the top edge (y = 0.5): t = (1000, 0) Pa.
Leave the hole boundary traction-free (no Neumann condition applied there).
Apply no traction on the bottom edge unless otherwise specified.
Assemble the weak form with the standard linear elasticity bilinear form and the load linear form including body forces and surface tractions on the right and top edges only.
Solve the resulting linear system using a direct solver.
After solving, compute the full Cauchy stress tensor from the in-plane displacement field using the plane-stress constitutive law.
Compute the von Mises stress from the in-plane stress components as: σ_vm = sqrt(σ_xx² - σ_xx·σ_yy + σ_yy² + 3·σ_xy²).
Output or save the displacement field, stress components, and von Mises stress field for post-processing.
