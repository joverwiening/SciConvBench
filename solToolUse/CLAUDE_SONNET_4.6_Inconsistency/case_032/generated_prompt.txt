Use FEniCS legacy (dolfin) for all implementation.

The problem is 2D plane-stress linear elasticity solved with a vector-valued displacement finite element space and a direct linear solver.

The domain is a right triangle with vertices at (0,0), (1,0), and (0,0.5).

Generate a triangular mesh of the domain using mshr or equivalent with approximately 64 elements.

Material properties are: Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio nu = 0.33, density rho = 2700 kg/m^3, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s^2.

Use the plane-stress reduced constitutive law throughout.

Assume unit thickness in the out-of-plane direction.

The body force vector is f = (0, -rho*g) applied over the entire domain.

Identify three boundary segments: the bottom edge from (0,0) to (1,0) where y=0, the vertical edge from (0,0) to (0,0.5) where x=0, and the sloping edge from (1,0) to (0,0.5) where x+2y=1.

Apply a Dirichlet boundary condition of zero displacement (u = (0,0)) on the sloping edge x+2y=1, which is the clamped boundary.

Apply a shear traction on the vertical edge x=0 interpreted as a traction tangent to that edge.

The vertical edge x=0 has outward normal n=(-1,0) and tangent direction t=(0,1).

The shear traction on x=0 is T = 4000 * t = (0, 4000) Pa, meaning a traction vector of (0, 4000) N/m^2 applied on that edge.

Leave the bottom edge x=0 to y=0 (from (0,0) to (1,0)) as a free (natural) boundary with no applied traction.

Define the variational formulation using the standard weak form: integral over domain of sigma:epsilon(v) dx = integral over domain of f dot v dx + integral over x=0 edge of T dot v ds.

Use the plane-stress constitutive relation: sigma = E/(1-nu^2) * ((1-nu)*epsilon + nu*tr(epsilon)*I) where I is the 2x2 identity, and epsilon = sym(grad(u)).

Use a continuous Lagrange vector element of degree 2 (P2) for the displacement space, or degree 1 (P1) if degree 2 causes issues, but prefer P2.

After solving, compute the full in-plane Cauchy stress tensor field sigma from the displacement solution.
