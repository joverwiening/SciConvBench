The simulation will be performed using the FEniCS legacy library (dolfin).
The physical model is small-strain linear elasticity.
The problem is a 2D plane-stress analysis.
The geometry is a rectangular plate with dimensions 1.0m in width (x-direction) and 0.5m in height (y-direction).
A circular hole with a radius of 0.1m is centered at the point (0.5, 0.25).
The material properties are: Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, and density ρ = 2700 kg/m^3.
The entire left boundary of the plate (x=0) is fixed with zero displacement.
A body force due to gravity is applied, with g = 9.81 m/s^2 acting in the negative y-direction.
A traction boundary condition is applied to a segment of the right boundary (x=1) to represent a pure moment of 800 Nm about the point (1, 0.25).
This traction is applied as a force couple over a vertical line segment of length 0.04m, centered at y=0.25.
A traction of +2.0e6 Pa in the positive y-direction is applied on the segment from y=0.25 to y=0.27 at x=1.
A traction of -2.0e6 Pa in the negative y-direction is applied on the segment from y=0.23 to y=0.25 at x=1.
The domain will be meshed with a resolution of approximately 64 elements per edge of the rectangular plate.
The simulation will solve for the vector-valued displacement field using a direct linear solver.
After solving for displacement, the in-plane Cauchy stress tensor will be computed.
The von Mises stress will be calculated from the Cauchy stress tensor.
The simulation assumes a unit thickness of 1.0m for all calculations.
