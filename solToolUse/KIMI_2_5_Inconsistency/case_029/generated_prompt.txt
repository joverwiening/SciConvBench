The simulation solves a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem using FEniCS legacy (dolfin).

The domain is a 1.0m by 0.5m rectangle with a circular hole of radius 0.1m centered at (0.5, 0.25).

The mesh consists of 72 triangular elements.

The vector-valued displacement field uses Lagrange elements of polynomial degree 1.

Material properties are: Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33, density ρ = 2700 kg/m³, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s² acting in the negative y-direction.

Plane stress conditions apply with unit thickness, using the reduced constitutive law where σ_zz = 0 and the out-of-plane strain is ε_zz = -ν/(1-ν) * (ε_xx + ε_yy).

Boundary conditions fix all displacements on the left edge (x = 0) and bottom edge (y = 0).

The top edge (y = 0.5) carries a normal traction of +1000 Pa directed inward (negative y-direction).

The bottom edge (y = 0) carries a normal traction of -1000 Pa directed outward (negative y-direction), interpreted as an additional applied load separate from the fixed boundary condition.

The hole boundary remains traction-free.

Body forces include gravity acting as (0, -ρ*g) in the y-direction.

The variational problem uses the standard linear elasticity bilinear form with the plane-stress stiffness tensor and linear form incorporating body forces and boundary tractions.

A direct linear solver (MUMPS or default LU) solves the resulting linear system.

Post-processing computes the Cauchy stress tensor from the displacement gradient using the plane-stress constitutive relation.

The von Mises equivalent stress computes as sqrt(σ_xx² - σ_xx*σ_yy + σ_yy² + 3*σ_xy²) from the in-plane stress components.

Output includes the displacement field and von Mises stress distribution for visualization.
