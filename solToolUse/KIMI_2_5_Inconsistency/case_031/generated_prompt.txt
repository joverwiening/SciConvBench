The simulation solves a 2D plane-stress linear elasticity problem using FEniCS legacy (dolfin).

The domain is a parallelogram with vertices at (0,0), (1,0), (1.2,0.3), and (0.2,0.3).

The left inclined edge runs from (0,0) to (0.2,0.3) along the line y=1.5x and is fully fixed (clamped) with Dirichlet boundary conditions u=0 on this boundary.

The material properties are Young's modulus E=70e9 Pa, Poisson's ratio ν=0.33, density ρ=2700 kg/m^3, and gravitational acceleration g=9.81 m/s^2 acting in the negative y-direction.

The body force due to gravity is applied as a distributed load with components (0, -ρ*g) over the entire domain.

A concentrated horizontal point load of -5 kN (negative x-direction) is applied at the specific point (1.1, 0.15) using PointSource on the x-component of displacement.

The plane-stress constitutive law is used with the reduced stiffness matrix for 2D plane stress conditions.

The mesh consists of 72 triangular elements.

The variational problem uses a vector-valued Lagrange finite element space of degree 1 for displacement.

The bilinear form implements the standard linear elasticity weak form with the plane-stress stress-strain relationship.

The linear form includes contributions from the gravity body force and the concentrated point load applied via PointSource.

A direct linear solver (MUMPS or default LU) is used to solve the resulting linear system.

Post-processing computes the Cauchy stress tensor from the displacement gradient using the plane-stress constitutive law.

The von Mises equivalent stress is computed from the in-plane stress components as sqrt(σ_xx^2 + σ_yy^2 - σ_xx*σ_yy + 3*σ_xy^2).

The simulation outputs the displacement field and von Mises stress distribution for visualization.
