The simulation solves small-strain linear elasticity in a 2D plane-stress right triangular domain using FEniCS legacy (dolfin).

The domain is defined by vertices (0,0), (1,0), and (0,0.5) with unit thickness.

The mesh consists of 64 triangular elements.

Material properties are Young's modulus E = 70e9 Pa and Poisson's ratio ν = 0.33.

The constitutive law uses the reduced plane-stress formulation with the elasticity tensor C_ijkl = E/(1-ν²) * [ν δ_ij δ_kl + (1-ν)/2 (δ_ik δ_jl + δ_il δ_jk)] for the in-plane components.

Boundary conditions fix u_x = 0 on the left edge where x = 0 and u_y = 0 on the bottom edge where y = 0.

The vertical load of -5 kN in the y-direction is applied at the centroid (0.5, 0.25) and regularized by distributing it as a uniform traction over the hypotenuse edge connecting (1,0) to (0,0.5).

The variational problem uses a vector-valued Lagrange finite element space of degree 1 for displacement.

The bilinear form is a(u,v) = ∫_Ω σ(u) : ε(v) dx where σ = C : ε and ε is the symmetric gradient of displacement.

The linear form is L(v) = ∫_Γ_hypotenuse t · v ds with traction magnitude chosen to yield equivalent total vertical force of -5 kN.

The system is solved with a direct linear solver (MUMPS or default LU).

Post-processing computes the Cauchy stress tensor from the displacement gradient.

Von Mises stress is computed as σ_vm = sqrt(σ_xx² - σ_xx σ_yy + σ_yy² + 3 σ_xy²) from the in-plane stress components.

Output includes the displacement field and von Mises stress distribution for visualization.
