The computational domain is a cube with corners at (0,0,0) and (1,1,1).
An obstacle is defined as a rectangular prism with corners at (0.375, 0.375, 0) and (0.625, 0.625, 0.25).
The mesh is a uniform Cartesian grid of 32x32x32 cells.
The simulation models two incompressible, isothermal, immiscible fluids using the Volume of Fluid method.
The first fluid is water with a density of 1000 kg/m^3 and a kinematic viscosity of 1.0e-6 m^2/s.
The second fluid is air with a density of 1 kg/m^3 and a kinematic viscosity of 1.48e-5 m^2/s.
The surface tension between water and air is 0.07 N/m.
A constant gravitational acceleration of (0, 0, -9.81) m/s^2 is applied.
The simulation runs for a total time of 0.1 seconds.
The time step is fixed at 0.001 seconds.
The top boundary at z=1 is an open atmosphere patch.
The remaining five external boundaries are walls.
The surfaces of the internal obstacle are walls.
The velocity boundary condition on all wall faces is no-slip, fixed at (0,0,0) m/s.
The pressure boundary condition on all wall faces is zero-gradient.
The phase fraction boundary condition on all wall faces is zero-gradient.
The pressure boundary condition on the atmosphere patch is a total pressure of 0 Pa.
The velocity boundary condition on the atmosphere patch allows fluid to exit and enter based on the pressure field.
The phase fraction boundary condition on the atmosphere patch is zero-gradient for outflow and a fixed value of 0 (air) for inflow.
At time t=0, the velocity field is initialized to (0,0,0) m/s everywhere.
At time t=0, the pressure field is initialized to be in hydrostatic equilibrium.
At time t=0, the phase fraction for water is 1 inside the region from (0,0,0) to (0.6, 0.1875, 0.75).
At time t=0, the phase fraction for water is 0 everywhere else.
