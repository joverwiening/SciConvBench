Use the Paris law da/dN = C (ΔK)^m with ΔK = Y·Δσ·√(πa).  
Convert the crack lengths to meters: a_i = 0.002 m, a_f = 0.006 m.  
Compute the integral N = ∫_{a_i}^{a_f} da / [C (Y·Δσ·√π·a^{1/2})^m] = [a^{1‑m/2} / (1‑m/2)]_{a_i}^{a_f} / [C (Y·Δσ·√π)^m].  
Insert the values C = 2.8 × 10⁻¹¹, m = 3.1, Y = 1.12, Δσ = 180 MPa.  
Calculate (Y·Δσ·√π) = 1.12 × 180 × √π ≈ 357 MPa·√m.  
Raise to the power m: (357)^{3.1} ≈ 8.2 × 10⁷.  
Denominator = C·(Y·Δσ·√π)^m ≈ 2.8 × 10⁻¹¹ × 8.2 × 10⁷ ≈ 2.3 × 10⁻³.  
Exponent term: 1‑m/2 = –0.55.  
Compute a_f^{‑0.55} ≈ 16.7, a_i^{‑0.55} ≈ 30.5.  
Difference divided by (1‑m/2): (16.7 – 30.5)/‑0.55 ≈ 25.2.  
Finally, N = 25.2 / 2.3 × 10⁻³ ≈ 1.1 × 10⁴ cycles.  

Therefore, approximately 1.1 × 10⁴ fatigue cycles are required for the crack to grow from 2 mm to 6 mm.
