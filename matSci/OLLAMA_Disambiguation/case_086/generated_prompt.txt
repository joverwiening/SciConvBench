Determine the equilibrium potential of the Cu²⁺/Cu electrode at 25 °C using the Nernst equation.

Use the standard reduction potential for the Cu²⁺/Cu couple: E° = +0.337 V versus the standard hydrogen electrode.

Apply the Nernst equation for the half‑reaction Cu²⁺ + 2 e⁻ → Cu(s):
E = E° + (RT / nF) ln a(Cu²⁺)

At 25 °C, RT/F = 0.025693 V, and n = 2, so (RT / nF) = 0.025693 V / 2 = 0.0128465 V.

Insert the given Cu²⁺ activity (a = 0.010):
ln a = ln 0.010 = –4.605170.

Calculate the Nernst term:
(0.0128465 V) × (–4.605170) = –0.0592 V (rounded to four decimal places).

Add this to the standard potential:
E = 0.337 V + (–0.0592 V) = 0.2778 V.

Report the equilibrium potential as approximately +0.278 V versus the standard hydrogen electrode.
