The relative density of a sintered material is defined as the ratio of its measured (apparent) density to its theoretical (fully dense) density. Porosity is the complement of relative density: Porosity = 1 − (relative density) (expressed as a fraction, then multiplied by 100 % for percent).  

If the relative density is 1.08 (108 % of the theoretical density), the implied porosity would be 1 − 1.08 = −0.08, i.e., –8 % porosity, which is physically impossible because porosity cannot be negative.  

Conversely, a reported total porosity of 12 % corresponds to a relative density of 1 − 0.12 = 0.88 (88 % of the theoretical density).  

Therefore the two reported values (relative density = 1.08 and porosity = 12 %) are mutually inconsistent and cannot both be correct. One of them must be erroneous; the relative density should be 0.88 if the porosity is truly 12 %, or the porosity should be –8 % if the relative density is truly 1.08, which is not physically plausible.
