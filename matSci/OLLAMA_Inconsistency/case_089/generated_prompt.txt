Use the Nernst equation E = E° + (0.0592/2) log a(Cu²⁺) at 25 °C.  
When a(Cu²⁺) = 1, log a = 0, so the electrode potential equals the standard potential E°.  
When a(Cu²⁺) = 10⁻², log a = –2, giving a term (0.0592/2)(–2) = –0.0592 V.  
Thus E = E° – 0.0592 V, i.e., the reduction potential becomes 0.0592 V more negative (less positive).  
Therefore lowering the Cu²⁺ activity from 1 to 10⁻² makes the Cu²⁺/Cu reduction potential shift in the negative direction.
