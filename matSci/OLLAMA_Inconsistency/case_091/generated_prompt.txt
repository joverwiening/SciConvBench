The static water contact angle of 195° is far above the typical threshold for super‑hydrophobicity (≈150°).  
Such a high angle indicates that water droplets will bead up and roll off the surface with minimal adhesion.  
Therefore the polymer surface is considered extremely non‑wettable (super‑hydrophobic).  
The apparent contact angle exceeding 180° suggests that the measurement may involve a Cassie‑Baxter state with trapped air beneath the droplet, further reducing solid‑liquid contact.  
In practical terms, the surface would repel water, resist wetting, and could be used for water‑repellent applications.
