Assume the carburizing process is performed at a typical temperature of 900 °C (1173 K).  
Use a diffusion coefficient for carbon in steel at this temperature of D = 1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ m²/s.  
Assume the initial carbon concentration in the foil is C₀ = 0.20 wt % (uniform throughout the foil).  
Assume the surface carbon concentration imposed by the carburizing atmosphere is C_s = 0.80 wt %.  
Assume the target carbon concentration to be reached at a depth of 0.8 mm is C_target = 0.60 wt %.  

Treat the foil as a semi‑infinite solid and use the error‑function solution for diffusion from a constant‑surface concentration:  

C(x,t) = C_s – (C_s – C₀) erf[ x / (2 √(D t)) ]  

Set x = 0.8 mm = 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ m and C(x,t) = C_target, then solve for t:  

erf[ x / (2 √(D t)) ] = (C_s – C_target) / (C_s – C₀)  

Compute the right‑hand side: (0.80 – 0.60) / (0.80 – 0.20) = 0.20 / 0.60 = 0.3333.  

Find the inverse error function: erf⁻¹(0.3333) ≈ 0.304.  

Thus:  

0.304 = x / (2 √(D t))  

Solve for t:  

t = [ x / (2 × 0.304) ]² / D  

t = [ (8.0 × 10⁻⁴ m) / 0.608 ]² / (1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ m²/s)  

t ≈ (1.316 × 10⁻³ m)² / 1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ m²/s  

t ≈ 1.73 × 10⁻⁶ m² / 1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ m²/s  

t ≈ 1.73 × 10⁵ s  

Convert to hours:  

t ≈ 1.73 × 10⁵ s / 3600 s h⁻¹ ≈ 48 hours.  

Therefore, under the stated reasonable assumptions, approximately 48 hours of carburizing at 900 °C are required for the carbon concentration at 0.8 mm depth to reach 0.60 wt %.
