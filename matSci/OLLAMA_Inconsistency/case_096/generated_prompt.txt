The specific surface area (SSA) of a spherical powder is inversely proportional to its mean particle diameter (d) according to SSA = 6 / (ρ · d), where ρ is the material density.  
Because both powders are made of the same material, ρ is identical and cancels when comparing the two powders.  
Therefore, the ratio of the specific surface areas equals the inverse ratio of their diameters:  

SSA_fine / SSA_coarse = d_coarse / d_fine = 5 μm / 0.5 μm = 10.  

Thus the finer powder (0.5 μm) has a specific surface area ten times larger than the coarser powder (5 μm). The correct trend is that the finer powder possesses the higher specific surface area.
