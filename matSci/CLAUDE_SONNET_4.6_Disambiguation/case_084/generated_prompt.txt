The task is to estimate the band gap energy of a semiconductor from its optical absorption edge wavelength.
The absorption edge wavelength is 620 nm.
Use the photon energy formula: E = hc / λ, where h is Planck's constant (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) and c is the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s).
Convert the wavelength from nanometers to meters: 620 nm = 620 × 10⁻⁹ m.
Calculate the photon energy in joules: E = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (620 × 10⁻⁹).
Convert the result from joules to electron volts by dividing by 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/eV.
The resulting photon energy at the absorption edge is the estimated band gap energy of the semiconductor.
Report the final band gap value in eV, rounded to two decimal places.
Use the simplified formula E (eV) = 1240 / λ (nm) as a cross-check, where λ = 620 nm gives E ≈ 2.00 eV.
State that the estimated band gap of the semiconductor is approximately 2.00 eV.
