Calculate the diffusion activation energy using the Arrhenius equation for two known diffusivity values.

Given values are:
D1 = 4.27×10^-15 m^2/s at T1 = 771°C = 1044 K
D2 = 1.13×10^-11 m^2/s at T2 = 1233°C = 1506 K
R = 8.314 J/mol·K

Use the Arrhenius relation: D = D0 × exp(-Qd / (R × T))

Divide D2 by D1 to eliminate D0:
D2/D1 = exp(-Qd/R × (1/T2 - 1/T1))

Take the natural log of both sides:
ln(D2/D1) = (-Qd / R) × (1/T2 - 1/T1)

Solve for Qd:
Qd = -R × ln(D2/D1) / (1/T2 - 1/T1)

Compute ln(D2/D1):
D2/D1 = (1.13×10^-11) / (4.27×10^-15) = 2647.5
ln(2647.5) = 7.881

Compute (1/T2 - 1/T1):
1/T2 = 1/1506 = 6.6401×10^-4 K^-1
1/T1 = 1/1044 = 9.5785×10^-4 K^-1
1/T2 - 1/T1 = 6.6401×10^-4 - 9.5785×10^-4 = -2.9384×10^-4 K^-1

Compute Qd:
Qd = -(8.314) × (7.881) / (-2.9384×10^-4)
Qd = (8.314 × 7.881) / (2.9384×10^-4)
Qd = 65.52 / 2.9384×10^-4
Qd = 223,000 J/mol

Convert to kJ/mol:
Qd ≈ 223 kJ/mol

The diffusion activation energy is approximately 223 kJ/mol.
