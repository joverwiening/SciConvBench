Use the rubber elasticity theory to estimate the Young's modulus of the elastomer.
The crosslink density is ν_e = 4.5×10^-5 mol/cm^3, which must be converted to SI units: ν_e = 4.5×10^-5 mol/cm^3 × 10^6 cm^3/m^3 = 45 mol/m^3.
The temperature is T = 298 K.
Use the universal gas constant R = 8.314 J/(mol·K).
Apply the shear modulus formula from rubber elasticity theory: G = ν_e × R × T.
Calculate G = 45 mol/m^3 × 8.314 J/(mol·K) × 298 K.
G = 45 × 8.314 × 298 = 111,458 Pa ≈ 1.115 × 10^5 Pa (approximately 111.5 kPa).
Apply the relationship between Young's modulus and shear modulus for an incompressible rubber: E = 3G.
Calculate E = 3 × 1.115 × 10^5 Pa = 3.345 × 10^5 Pa ≈ 334.5 kPa.
Report the final estimated Young's modulus as E ≈ 3.35 × 10^5 Pa (approximately 335 kPa).
Assume the elastomer is incompressible (Poisson's ratio ν = 0.5), which is the standard assumption for elastomers in rubber elasticity theory.
Assume ideal network behavior with no chain entanglements or defects beyond what is captured by the crosslink density.
