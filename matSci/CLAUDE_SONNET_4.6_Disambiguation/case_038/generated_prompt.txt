Calculate the steady-state heat transfer rate through a composite wall made of two layers arranged in series.

Layer 1 has a thickness of 16.79 mm (0.01679 m) and a thermal conductivity of 24.68 W/m·K.
Layer 2 has a thickness of 8.73 mm (0.00873 m) and a thermal conductivity of 94.15 W/m·K.
The wall area is 0.2423 m².
The temperature on the outer surface of Layer 1 is 134.0°C and the temperature on the outer surface of Layer 2 is -26.8°C.
The heat transfer is steady-state and one-dimensional (no heat generation).
Use the thermal resistance network approach for layers in series.

Compute the thermal resistance of Layer 1 as R1 = thickness1 / (k1 × Area) = 0.01679 / (24.68 × 0.2423).
Compute the thermal resistance of Layer 2 as R2 = thickness2 / (k2 × Area) = 0.00873 / (94.15 × 0.2423).
Compute the total resistance as R_total = R1 + R2.
Compute the heat transfer rate as Q = (T1 - T2) / R_total = (134.0 - (-26.8)) / R_total.

Performing the calculations:
R1 = 0.01679 / (24.68 × 0.2423) = 0.01679 / 5.980364 = 0.0028076 K/W.
R2 = 0.00873 / (94.15 × 0.2423) = 0.00873 / 22.822445 = 0.0003825 K/W.
R_total = 0.0028076 + 0.0003825 = 0.0031901 K/W.
Temperature difference = 134.0 - (-26.8) = 160.8°C (equivalent to 160.8 K).
Q = 160.8 / 0.0031901 = 50,405 W approximately.

The steady heat-transfer rate through the composite wall is approximately 50,405 W.
