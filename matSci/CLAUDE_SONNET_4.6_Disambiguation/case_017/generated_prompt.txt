The task is to calculate the critical flaw size for a brittle glass using Griffith fracture theory.

Given values are: applied tensile stress σ = 529.7 MPa = 529.7 × 10^6 Pa, Young's modulus E = 240.3 GPa = 240.3 × 10^9 Pa, surface energy γ_s = 2.576 J/m^2, and π = 3.14159.

Use the Griffith critical flaw size formula: a_c = (2 × E × γ_s) / (π × σ^2).

Compute σ^2 = (529.7 × 10^6)^2 = 2.80581 × 10^17 Pa^2.

Compute the numerator: 2 × E × γ_s = 2 × 240.3 × 10^9 × 2.576 = 1.238985 × 10^12.

Compute the denominator: π × σ^2 = 3.14159 × 2.80581 × 10^17 = 8.81488 × 10^17.

Compute a_c = 1.238985 × 10^12 / 8.81488 × 10^17 = 1.4055 × 10^-6 m.

Convert to micrometers: a_c ≈ 1.4055 μm.

The critical flaw size (half-crack length) is approximately 1.41 μm.
