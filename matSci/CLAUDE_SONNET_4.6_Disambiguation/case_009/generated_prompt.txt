A composite wall consists of two layers arranged in series (one-dimensional steady-state heat conduction).
Layer 1 has a thickness of 9.74 mm (0.00974 m) and a thermal conductivity of 12.31 W/m·K.
Layer 2 has a thickness of 13.27 mm (0.01327 m) and a thermal conductivity of 107.49 W/m·K.
The cross-sectional area of the wall is 0.0725 m².
The outer surface of Layer 1 is maintained at 198.1°C and the outer surface of Layer 2 is maintained at -25.4°C.
Calculate the steady-state heat-transfer rate in watts, neglecting contact resistance between the layers.

Compute the thermal resistance of each layer using R = thickness / (thermal conductivity × area).
R1 = 0.00974 / (12.31 × 0.0725) = 0.00974 / 0.892975 = 0.010909 K/W.
R2 = 0.01327 / (107.49 × 0.0725) = 0.01327 / 7.793025 = 0.001703 K/W.
Total resistance R_total = R1 + R2 = 0.010909 + 0.001703 = 0.012612 K/W.
Temperature difference ΔT = 198.1 - (-25.4) = 223.5°C (equivalent to 223.5 K difference).
Heat-transfer rate Q = ΔT / R_total = 223.5 / 0.012612 = 17,722 W (approximately 17,722 W).
