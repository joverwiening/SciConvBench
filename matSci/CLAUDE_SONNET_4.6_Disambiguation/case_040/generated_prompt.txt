The task is to compute the electrical resistivity of a wire given its physical dimensions and resistance.

Use the formula: resistivity (ρ) = R × A / L, where R is resistance, A is the cross-sectional area, and L is the length.
The wire has a length L = 3.365 m.
The wire has a circular cross-section with diameter d = 2.029 mm = 0.002029 m.
Compute the cross-sectional area as A = π × (d/2)² = π × (0.0010145)².
A = π × 1.02921025 × 10⁻⁶ = 3.23334 × 10⁻⁶ m².
The wire has a resistance R = 0.432 Ω.
Compute resistivity as ρ = (0.432 × 3.23334 × 10⁻⁶) / 3.365.
Numerator = 0.432 × 3.23334 × 10⁻⁶ = 1.39680 × 10⁻⁶.
ρ = 1.39680 × 10⁻⁶ / 3.365 = 4.1507 × 10⁻⁷ Ω·m.
The electrical resistivity of the wire is approximately 4.15 × 10⁻⁷ Ω·m.
