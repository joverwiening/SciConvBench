Compute the steady 1-D heat-transfer rate through a two-layer plane wall with layers in series.
Use layer 1 thickness L1 = 10.91 mm and thermal conductivity k1 = 4.69 W/m·K.
Use layer 2 thickness L2 = 8.09 mm and thermal conductivity k2 = 6.75 W/m·K.
Use wall area A = 0.0782 m².
Use boundary surface temperatures T_hot = 191.8 °C and T_cold = 21.2 °C.
Assume perfect thermal contact between layers, constant properties, no internal heat generation, and negligible contact resistance.
Convert thicknesses to meters: L1 = 0.01091 m and L2 = 0.00809 m.
Compute total thermal resistance R_total = L1/(k1·A) + L2/(k2·A).
Compute temperature difference ΔT = T_hot − T_cold = 170.6 K.
Compute heat-transfer rate q = ΔT / R_total.
Numerical result: R_total = 0.03856 K/W.
Numerical result: q = 4424 W (approximately 4.42×10^3 W), flowing from the 191.8 °C side to the 21.2 °C side.
