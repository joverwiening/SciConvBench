Use the reduction half-reaction Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu(s) at 25 °C.
Write the Nernst equation as E = E° − (0.0592/2) log Q.
Use Q = a(Cu)/a(Cu2+) = 1/a(Cu2+) because a(Cu(s)) = 1.
Substitute to get E = E° − (0.0592/2) log(1/a(Cu2+)) = E° + (0.0592/2) log a(Cu2+).
Lowering a(Cu2+) decreases log a(Cu2+), so E decreases (becomes less positive/more negative).
Numerically, changing a(Cu2+) from 1 to 10−2 changes E by ΔE = (0.0592/2) log(10−2) = (0.0296)(−2) = −0.0592 V.
Therefore, lowering a(Cu2+) from 1 to 10−2 makes the reduction potential 59.2 mV less positive, not more positive.
