Q: Using the Young–Dupré equation, should W_A be defined as the work required to separate the liquid from the solid (positive) or as the free-energy change upon adhesion (negative)?
A: Define \(W_A\) in the Young–Dupré relation as the **work of adhesion**, i.e., the **work required to separate** the liquid from the solid. It is **positive**:

\[
W_A=\gamma_{LV}(1+\cos\theta) > 0
\]

So a negative value (e.g., \(-10\ \text{mJ/m}^2\)) would have the wrong sign under this convention.
