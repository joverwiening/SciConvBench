Assess miscibility of a binary polymer blend using DSC glass transition data.
Assume the neat (pure) polymer glass transition temperatures are approximately 315 K and 375 K.
Use the criterion that a fully miscible polymer blend exhibits a single Tg between the two neat-polymer Tg values (often predictable by Fox or Gordon–Taylor behavior).
Interpret the observation of two distinct Tg values at 315 K and 375 K as evidence of two amorphous phases whose compositions are close to the respective pure components.
Conclude that the blend is not fully miscible under the DSC measurement conditions and is phase-separated or only partially miscible.
State that “fully miscible” is inconsistent with the DSC result unless the system has special circumstances (e.g., extremely slow interdiffusion, incomplete mixing, or measurement artifacts), but the default assessment is immiscibility/phase separation.
Recommend confirming with complementary methods (e.g., DMA for Tg breadth/number, microscopy for phase morphology, scattering for phase separation, and checking thermal history/annealing) if needed.
