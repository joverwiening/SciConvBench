The task is to identify and correct a misclassified point defect in an ionic crystal.

The defect described consists of one cation vacancy paired with one nearby cation interstitial.
This defect type is a Frenkel defect, not a Schottky defect.
A Frenkel defect occurs when a cation leaves its normal lattice site, creating a vacancy, and occupies a nearby interstitial position.
The Frenkel defect involves a vacancy-interstitial pair of the same ion species.
The crystal remains electrically neutral because no ions are added or removed, only displaced.
Frenkel defects are common in ionic crystals where cations are significantly smaller than anions, such as AgCl and ZnS.
A Schottky defect, by contrast, involves the removal of one cation and one anion from the crystal interior to the surface, creating a paired cation vacancy and anion vacancy with no interstitial involved.
The original statement incorrectly labels the described defect as a Schottky defect.
The correct defect type for one cation vacancy plus one nearby cation interstitial is a Frenkel defect.
