Q: The statement contains an error. A defect consisting of one cation vacancy paired with one nearby cation interstitial is actually a **Frenkel defect**, not a Schottky defect.

Let me clarify the distinction:

**Frenkel Defect:**
- A cation (or anion) leaves its lattice site (creating a vacancy) and moves to an interstitial position nearby.
- It involves a **vacancy + interstitial pair** of the **same ion**.
- The crystal maintains electrical neutrality locally.
- Common in crystals where cations are much smaller than anions (e.g., AgCl, ZnS).

**Schottky Defect:**
- Involves the **removal of a pair of ions** (one cation + one anion) from the interior to the surface of the crystal.
- It creates **paired vacancies** (one cation vacancy + one anion vacancy).
- No interstitials are involved.
- Common in crystals with similar-sized ions (e.g., NaCl, KCl).

---

**Correct Identification:**

The defect described — **one cation vacancy + one nearby cation interstitial** — is a **Frenkel defect**.

The original statement incorrectly labels it as a Schottky defect.
A: Yes, that's correct. The defect described is a **Frenkel defect**, not a Schottky defect as incorrectly stated in the incomplete version.
