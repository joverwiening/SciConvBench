Calculate the energy of a photon with wavelength 240 nm using E = hc/λ.
Use h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, and convert the result to electron volts using 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
Compare the photon energy to the work function φ = 4.8 eV.
If the photon energy is greater than φ, conclude that photoelectric emission occurs.
If the photon energy is less than or equal to φ, conclude that emission does not occur.
If emission occurs, calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons using KE_max = E_photon − φ.
If KE_max is greater than zero, state that the emitted electrons have nonzero kinetic energy and report the value in eV.
If KE_max equals zero, state that emission just barely occurs with electrons having zero kinetic energy.
Report all intermediate values clearly: photon energy in eV, work function in eV, and KE_max in eV.
