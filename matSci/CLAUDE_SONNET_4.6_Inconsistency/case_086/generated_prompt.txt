The material is niobium (Nb) with a critical temperature T_c of 9.2 K.
The measurement temperature is 4.2 K.
Since 4.2 K is below T_c of 9.2 K, the material is in the superconducting state.
In this state, the electrical resistance of the material is exactly zero.
The material also exhibits the Meissner effect, expelling magnetic fields from its interior, provided the applied field is below the critical magnetic field.
The expected physical state at 4.2 K is the zero-resistance superconducting state.
The original claim of "zero-resistance superconducting state" is physically correct when the measurement temperature is 4.2 K, not 12 K.
The earlier description using 12 K was contradictory because 12 K exceeds T_c, which would place the material in the normal (resistive) metallic state.
