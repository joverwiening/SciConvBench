The Young–Dupré equation for work of adhesion is W_A = γ_LV (1 + cos θ).
Substitute the given values: γ_LV = 72.8 mN/m and θ = 30°.
cos 30° = 0.866.
Compute 1 + cos 30° = 1.866.
Compute W_A = 72.8 × 1.866 = 135.8 mJ/m².
The work of adhesion represents the energy per unit area required to reversibly separate the liquid from the solid, creating two new interfaces.
For any contact angle θ < 90°, cos θ is positive, so (1 + cos θ) > 1, and W_A is strictly positive.
For θ = 30°, which is well below 90°, the liquid wets the surface favorably, meaning adhesion is strong and W_A must be large and positive.
A negative value of W_A would imply the system gains energy upon separation, which would mean the liquid spontaneously dewets — contradicting the given contact angle of 30°.
Therefore, the reported value of W_A = -10 mJ/m² has the wrong sign and is also wrong in magnitude.
The correct value of W_A is +135.8 mJ/m².
The correct sign of W_A is positive.
