This is a lever rule calculation at the eutectic temperature for a hypereutectic alloy. Here is the full specification:

The alloy is a binary system with overall composition C0 = 14.36 wt% B.
The alloy is hypereutectic, meaning C0 lies between C_αe and C_e on the phase diagram.
At the eutectic temperature (just above it), two phases coexist: alpha phase and liquid.
The alpha phase has composition C_αe = 4.63 wt% B.
The liquid phase has composition C_e = 40.31 wt% B (the eutectic liquid composition).
Apply the lever rule to find the weight fraction of liquid just above the eutectic temperature.
The lever rule formula for the weight fraction of liquid is: W_L = (C0 - C_αe) / (C_e - C_αe).
Substitute the values: W_L = (14.36 - 4.63) / (40.31 - 4.63).
Compute the numerator: 14.36 - 4.63 = 9.73.
Compute the denominator: 40.31 - 4.63 = 35.68.
Compute the weight fraction: W_L = 9.73 / 35.68 = 0.2727, approximately 0.273 or 27.3%.
This liquid fraction (27.3 wt%) is the portion of the alloy that is liquid just above the eutectic temperature.
On cooling through the eutectic temperature, this liquid transforms entirely into eutectic microconstituent.
Therefore, the weight fraction of eutectic microconstituent in the final solid is approximately 0.273 (27.3%).
The remaining fraction, approximately 1 - 0.273 = 0.727 (72.7%), is the proeutectic alpha phase that formed above the eutectic temperature.
