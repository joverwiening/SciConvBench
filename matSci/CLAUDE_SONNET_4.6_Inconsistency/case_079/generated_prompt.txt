Assume the semiconductor is silicon at room temperature (300 K).

Use the standard intrinsic carrier concentration for silicon at 300 K: n_i = 1.0×10^10 cm^-3 = 1.0×10^16 m^-3.

The donor concentration is fully ionized at N_D = 1.0×10^22 m^-3.

Since N_D >> n_i (by six orders of magnitude), the semiconductor is strongly extrinsic n-type.

The majority carrier concentration (electrons) is dominated by the ionized donors, so n ≈ N_D = 1.0×10^22 m^-3.

The minority carrier concentration (holes) is determined by the mass-action law: p = n_i^2 / n.

Substitute values: p = (1.0×10^16)^2 / (1.0×10^22) = 1.0×10^32 / 1.0×10^22 = 1.0×10^10 m^-3.

Qualitatively, the electron concentration is enormously larger than the hole concentration (n >> p).

The electron concentration is approximately equal to the donor concentration, confirming strong n-type behavior.

The hole concentration is suppressed far below the intrinsic level due to the high donor doping, consistent with the mass-action law.

The semiconductor is unambiguously n-type: electrons are the majority carriers and holes are the minority carriers.
