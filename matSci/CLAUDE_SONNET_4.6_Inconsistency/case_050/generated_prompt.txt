The task is a conceptual assessment question in materials science regarding substitutional solid solubility and the Hume-Rothery size factor rule.

The two metals differ in atomic radius by 8%, not 25%.

The Hume-Rothery size factor rule states that for substitutional solid solubility to be favorable, the difference in atomic radii between the solute and solvent must be less than or equal to 15%.

Since 8% is less than 15%, the size factor criterion is satisfied.

Based on the size factor alone, complete substitutional solid solubility is expected and the prediction is valid.

The assessment conclusion is that the expectation of complete substitutional solid solubility is correct when the atomic radius difference is 8%, as it comfortably falls within the 15% size factor limit prescribed by the Hume-Rothery rules.

Note that size factor is only one of the Hume-Rothery rules; complete solid solubility in practice also requires consideration of crystal structure compatibility, electronegativity similarity, and valence electron compatibility, but on the basis of size factor alone the expectation is fully justified.
