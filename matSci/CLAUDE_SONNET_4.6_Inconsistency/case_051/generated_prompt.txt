The problem involves carburization of a 5.0 mm thick steel plate treated as a semi-infinite solid, carburized from one face.

Use a carburizing temperature of 927°C (1700°F), which is a standard industrial gas carburizing temperature.

Use a surface carbon concentration Cs of 1.20 wt% C, representing a typical carburizing atmosphere potential.

Use an initial carbon concentration C0 of 0.20 wt% C, representing a typical low-carbon steel substrate.

Use a target carbon concentration Cx of 0.80 wt% C at a depth x of 0.8 mm (0.0008 m).

Calculate the diffusivity of carbon in austenite (FCC iron) using the Arrhenius equation: D = D0 * exp(-Qd / (R * T)).

Use D0 = 2.3 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s and Qd = 148,000 J/mol for carbon diffusion in austenite (values from Callister's Materials Science and Engineering).

Use the universal gas constant R = 8.314 J/(mol·K).

Convert the temperature to Kelvin: T = 927 + 273 = 1200 K.

Calculate D at 1200 K using the above parameters.

Apply the semi-infinite solid diffusion solution: (Cx - C0) / (Cs - C0) = 1 - erf(x / (2 * sqrt(D * t))).

Substitute the known values to compute the argument of the error function: erf(z) = 1 - (Cx - C0)/(Cs - C0) = 1 - (0.80 - 0.20)/(1.20 - 0.20) = 1 - 0.60 = 0.40.

So erf(z) = 0.60, where z = x / (2 * sqrt(D * t)).

Find z such that erf(z) = 0.60 using standard error function tables or interpolation; this gives z ≈ 0.5951.

Solve for t: t = (x / (2 * z))² / D, where x = 0.0008 m and z ≈ 0.5951.

Report the required carburizing time t in seconds and convert to hours for practical interpretation.
