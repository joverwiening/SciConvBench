The task is to determine the correct trend in specific surface area for two spherical powders of the same material with mean diameters 0.5 μm and 5 μm, and to evaluate the claim that the finer powder has the lower specific surface area.

Use the standard specific surface area formula for spherical particles: S = 6 / (ρ · d), where ρ is the material density and d is the mean particle diameter.

Since both powders are made of the same material, ρ is identical for both and cancels out when comparing the two.

Specific surface area is therefore inversely proportional to particle diameter: S ∝ 1/d.

For the finer powder with d = 0.5 μm, the specific surface area is S₁ = 6 / (ρ · 0.5×10⁻⁶).

For the coarser powder with d = 5 μm, the specific surface area is S₂ = 6 / (ρ · 5×10⁻⁶).

Calculate the ratio S₁/S₂ = (5×10⁻⁶) / (0.5×10⁻⁶) = 10.

The finer powder (0.5 μm) has a specific surface area 10 times greater than the coarser powder (5 μm).

The correct trend is: smaller diameter → higher specific surface area.

The stated claim that the finer powder has the lower specific surface area is incorrect.

The finer powder (0.5 μm) has the higher specific surface area, and the coarser powder (5 μm) has the lower specific surface area.
