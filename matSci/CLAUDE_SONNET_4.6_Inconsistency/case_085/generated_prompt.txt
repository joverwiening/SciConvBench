The system obeys the Curie law for paramagnetism, given by χ = C/T, where χ is the magnetic susceptibility, C is the material-specific Curie constant, and T is the absolute temperature in Kelvin.
The relationship between χ and T is inverse: as T increases, χ decreases.
Evaluate the susceptibility at T = 300 K: χ₁ = C/300.
Evaluate the susceptibility at T = 600 K: χ₂ = C/600.
Compare the two values: χ₁ = C/300 is twice as large as χ₂ = C/600.
Therefore, when temperature rises from 300 K to 600 K, the susceptibility decreases by a factor of 2.
The correct trend is that susceptibility decreases as temperature increases for a paramagnet obeying χ = C/T.
The originally stated claim that susceptibility increases as temperature rises from 300 K to 600 K is physically incorrect and contradicts the Curie law.
