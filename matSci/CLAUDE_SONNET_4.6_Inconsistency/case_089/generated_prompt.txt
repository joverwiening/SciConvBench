The Cu²⁺/Cu electrode follows the Nernst equation: E = E° + (0.0592/2) × log(a_(Cu²⁺)) at 25°C.

The standard reduction potential E° for Cu²⁺/Cu is +0.337 V, which applies when a_(Cu²⁺) = 1.

When a_(Cu²⁺) = 1, log(1) = 0, so E = E° = +0.337 V.

When a_(Cu²⁺) is lowered to 10⁻², log(10⁻²) = -2.

Substituting into the Nernst equation gives E = 0.337 + (0.0592/2) × (-2) = 0.337 - 0.0592 = +0.2778 V.

The electrode potential decreases from +0.337 V to approximately +0.278 V when a_(Cu²⁺) drops from 1 to 10⁻².

The correct trend is that lowering a_(Cu²⁺) makes the reduction potential more negative (lower), not more positive.

This is physically consistent with Le Chatelier's principle: reducing the concentration of the oxidized species (Cu²⁺) disfavors the reduction half-reaction Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, thereby decreasing the driving force for reduction and lowering the electrode potential.

The original stated claim that lowering a_(Cu²⁺) makes the reduction potential more positive is incorrect.
