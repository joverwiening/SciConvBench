The reaction is FePO₄ + Li⁺ + e⁻ ↔ LiFePO₄.
In FePO₄, iron exists as Fe³⁺.
In LiFePO₄, iron exists as Fe²⁺.
Per formula unit of the reaction, exactly one iron atom undergoes a change in oxidation state, from Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺.
This change represents a gain of exactly one electron per iron atom.
There is only one iron atom per formula unit in both FePO₄ and LiFePO₄.
Therefore, the correct number of electrons transferred per formula unit is n = 1.
The use of n = 2 in the theoretical specific capacity calculation is incorrect for this reaction.
The theoretical specific capacity should be calculated using n = 1, the formula Q = nF/M, where F is Faraday's constant (96485 C/mol) and M is the molar mass of the active material (FePO₄ or LiFePO₄ depending on the reference electrode material chosen).
Using the molar mass of LiFePO₄ (approximately 157.76 g/mol) and n = 1, the theoretical specific capacity is approximately 169.9 mAh/g.
Using the molar mass of FePO₄ (approximately 150.82 g/mol) and n = 1, the theoretical specific capacity is approximately 177.9 mAh/g.
The correct electron count for this reaction is n = 1 electron per formula unit.
