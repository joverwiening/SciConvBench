A Zn/Cu galvanic cell consists of a zinc electrode in a zinc ion solution and a copper electrode in a copper ion solution.
Standard reduction potentials are: Zn²⁺/Zn = −0.76 V and Cu²⁺/Cu = +0.34 V.
The electrode with the lower (more negative) standard reduction potential undergoes oxidation.
Zinc has the lower standard reduction potential (−0.76 V), so zinc is oxidized.
The electrode where oxidation occurs is the anode, therefore Zn is the anode.
Copper has the higher standard reduction potential (+0.34 V), so copper ion is reduced.
The electrode where reduction occurs is the cathode, therefore Cu is the cathode.
The original assignment given (Cu as anode, Zn as cathode) is incorrect and reversed.
The correct assignment is: Zn is the anode and Cu is the cathode.
The cell notation is written as: Zn | Zn²⁺ || Cu²⁺ | Cu.
The standard cell potential is calculated as E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = +0.34 V − (−0.76 V) = +1.10 V.
The positive cell potential confirms this is a spontaneous galvanic cell.
