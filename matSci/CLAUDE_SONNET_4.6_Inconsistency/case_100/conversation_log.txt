Q: There is a contradiction in the information provided.

The relationship between standard Gibbs free energy and the equilibrium constant is given by ΔG° = -RT ln(K).

With ΔG° = -12 kJ/mol (a negative value), this means -RT ln(K) is negative, which requires ln(K) to be positive, which means K must be greater than 1.

However, the stated value K = 0.008 is less than 1, which would correspond to a positive ΔG°, not a negative one.

These two pieces of information are contradictory. Which value is correct — should I use ΔG° = -12 kJ/mol (which gives K > 1) as the authoritative value, or should I use K = 0.008 (which would imply ΔG° is positive) as the authoritative value?
A: Use ΔG° = -12 kJ/mol as the authoritative value. The K = 0.008 value is not part of the actual problem — ignore it.
