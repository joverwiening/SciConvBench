The system is a binary diffusion couple with an initial step composition: 20 at% B on the left half and 80 at% B on the right half.

After annealing, the concentration profile is described by the error function solution to Fick's second law for a semi-infinite diffusion couple.

The error function solution for this geometry is: C(x,t) = 50 + 30 * erf(x / (2 * sqrt(D*t))), where 50 at% B is the mean composition, 30 is the half-amplitude (since the initial compositions are 20 and 80 at% B), x is measured from the original interface, D is the interdiffusion coefficient, and t is the annealing time.

The profile width is defined as the distance over which the concentration changes from 20 at% B to 80 at% B.

At the 20 at% and 80 at% levels, the argument of the error function satisfies erf(x / (2*sqrt(D*t))) = ±1, which is approached asymptotically; use the practical approximation erf(u) ≈ ±1 at u ≈ ±1, meaning x = ±2*sqrt(D*t) at the profile edges.

The total profile width W from 20 at% to 80 at% is therefore W = 2 * 2 * sqrt(D*t) = 4 * sqrt(D*t).

Assume a representative interdiffusion coefficient of D = 1e-14 m²/s (a typical value for substitutional diffusion in metallic alloys at moderate annealing temperatures).

Assume a representative annealing time of t = 3600 s (1 hour).

Compute sqrt(D*t) = sqrt(1e-14 * 3600) = sqrt(3.6e-11) ≈ 6.0e-6 m = 6.0 micrometers.

Compute the profile width W = 4 * 6.0 micrometers = 24 micrometers.

Report the estimated interdiffusion distance as sqrt(D*t) ≈ 6 micrometers, and the full 20%-to-80% profile width as approximately 24 micrometers.

State clearly that these values are estimates based on assumed D = 1e-14 m²/s and t = 3600 s, and that the interdiffusion distance scales as sqrt(D*t).
