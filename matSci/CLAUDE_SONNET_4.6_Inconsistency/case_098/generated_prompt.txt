The defect pair consisting of one cation vacancy and one anion vacancy is identified as a Schottky defect.
A Schottky defect involves the simultaneous removal of one cation and one anion from their respective lattice sites.
The two vacancies are created together to maintain overall charge neutrality in the crystal.
The removed ions migrate to the surface of the crystal lattice.
A Frenkel defect is a distinct and separate defect type where a single ion leaves its lattice site and moves to an interstitial position, creating a vacancy-interstitial pair involving only one ion species.
The correct identification of the described defect pair is therefore a Schottky defect, not a Frenkel defect.
