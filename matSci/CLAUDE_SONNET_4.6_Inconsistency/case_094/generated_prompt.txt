The task is to determine whether the mass ordering in an Archimedes density measurement is physically consistent.

The three measured values are: dry mass = 12.4 g, saturated mass = 13.1 g, immersed mass = 8.6 g.

Check the physical consistency of the ordering as follows.

The dry mass is the mass of the ceramic sample with no water in its pores, measured in air.
The saturated mass is the mass of the ceramic sample with all open pores filled with water, measured in air.
The immersed mass is the mass of the saturated ceramic sample measured while fully submerged in water, reflecting the net effect of buoyancy.

For physical consistency, the following two conditions must both hold.
First, the saturated mass must be greater than the dry mass, because water has been absorbed into the open pores, adding mass.
Second, the immersed mass must be less than the saturated mass, because the buoyant force of the surrounding water reduces the apparent weight.

Evaluate condition one: saturated mass (13.1 g) > dry mass (12.4 g). This is true. The difference of 0.7 g represents the mass of water absorbed into the open pores.

Evaluate condition two: immersed mass (8.6 g) < saturated mass (13.1 g). This is true. The difference of 4.5 g represents the buoyant force, equal to the mass of water displaced by the total volume of the sample.

Also verify that the immersed mass is positive and less than the dry mass is not required, but note that immersed mass (8.6 g) is less than dry mass (12.4 g), which is physically reasonable since buoyancy acts on the entire outer volume of the sample.

Conclude that the mass ordering dry mass < saturated mass, and immersed mass < saturated mass (and immersed mass < dry mass) is fully physically consistent with the Archimedes method for a porous ceramic.
