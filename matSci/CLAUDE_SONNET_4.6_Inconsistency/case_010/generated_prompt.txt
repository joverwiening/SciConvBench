The composite wall consists of two layers arranged in series (heat flows through Layer 1 then Layer 2).
Layer 1 has a thickness of 9.74 mm (0.00974 m) and a thermal conductivity of 12.31 W/m·K.
Layer 2 has a thickness of 13.27 mm (0.01327 m) and a thermal conductivity of 107.49 W/m·K.
The wall cross-sectional area is 0.0725 m².
The outer surface of Layer 1 is maintained at 198.1°C and the outer surface of Layer 2 is maintained at -25.4°C.
The steady-state heat transfer rate is calculated using the total thermal resistance of the two layers in series.
Calculate the thermal resistance of each layer as R = thickness / (thermal conductivity × area).
R1 = 0.00974 / (12.31 × 0.0725).
R2 = 0.01327 / (107.49 × 0.0725).
Total resistance R_total = R1 + R2.
Calculate the heat transfer rate as Q = (198.1 - (-25.4)) / R_total.
Contact resistance between the two layers is neglected.
Report the final answer in Watts.
