The diffusion coefficient follows Arrhenius behavior: D = D0 × exp(-Q/RT), where Q is the activation energy.

Take the ratio of the two diffusion coefficients to eliminate D0:

D2/D1 = exp[-Q/R × (1/T2 - 1/T1)]

Take the natural logarithm:

ln(D2/D1) = -Q/R × (1/T2 - 1/T1)

Solve for Q:

Q = -R × ln(D2/D1) / (1/T2 - 1/T1)

Substitute the given values (temperatures in Kelvin: T1 = 866 K, T2 = 1438 K):

Q = -8.314 × ln(1.86×10^-12 / 4.76×10^-18) / (1/1438 - 1/866)

Calculate the ratio: D2/D1 = 1.86×10^-12 / 4.76×10^-18 = 3.9076×10^5

Calculate the logarithm: ln(3.9076×10^5) = 12.875

Calculate the temperature term: 1/1438 - 1/866 = 6.954×10^-4 - 1.155×10^-3 = -4.596×10^-4 K^-1

Calculate Q: Q = -8.314 × 12.875 / (-4.596×10^-4) = 233,000 J/mol = 233 kJ/mol

The activation energy for diffusion is 233 kJ/mol.
