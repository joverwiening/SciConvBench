The problem requires calculation of carrier concentration in a semiconductor using the Hall effect.

The Hall voltage equation for a rectangular sample is: VH = IB/(net), where n is carrier concentration, e is electron charge, and t is thickness.

Rearranging for carrier concentration: n = IB/(VHet)

The given parameters are:
Current I equals 15 milliamperes.
Magnetic field B equals 0.80 Tesla.
Hall voltage VH equals 24 millivolts.

The thickness t of the semiconductor sample is not provided in the problem statement.

To solve for carrier concentration, the thickness dimension perpendicular to both current and magnetic field directions must be known.

Without the thickness value, the carrier concentration cannot be determined numerically.

The final answer should express n in terms of t, or state that t is required for a numerical solution.

If assuming a standard thin film thickness of 1 micrometer as a typical value, the calculation would proceed, but this assumption should be noted.

The carrier concentration n equals (15 times 10 to the negative 3 amperes) times (0.80 tesla) divided by (24 times 10 to the negative 3 volts times 1.602 times 10 to the negative 19 coulombs times t).

This simplifies to n equals 3.12 times 10 to the 21 divided by t, where t is in meters.

For t equals 1 micrometer, n equals 3.12 times 10 to the 27 per cubic meter, or equivalently 3.12 times 10 to the 21 per cubic centimeter.
