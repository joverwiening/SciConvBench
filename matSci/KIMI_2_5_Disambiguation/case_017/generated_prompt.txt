The critical flaw size shall be calculated using Griffith's fracture criterion for brittle materials.

The critical stress intensity relationship for Griffith theory is given by: σ_f = sqrt(2*E*γ_s / (π*a_c)), where σ_f is the fracture stress, E is Young's modulus, γ_s is the surface energy, and a_c is the critical flaw size (half-crack length for an internal crack, or full crack length for a surface crack).

Rearranging to solve for critical flaw size: a_c = 2*E*γ_s / (π*σ_f^2).

Substitute the given values: E = 240.3 × 10^9 Pa, γ_s = 2.576 J/m^2, σ_f = 529.7 × 10^6 Pa.

Calculate a_c = 2 × (240.3 × 10^9) × 2.576 / (π × (529.7 × 10^6)^2).

Compute numerator: 2 × 240.3 × 10^9 × 2.576 = 1.238 × 10^12 N/m.

Compute denominator: π × (529.7 × 10^6)^2 = π × 2.806 × 10^17 = 8.815 × 10^17 N^2/m^4.

Calculate a_c = 1.238 × 10^12 / 8.815 × 10^17 = 1.404 × 10^-6 m.

Convert to micrometers: a_c = 1.404 μm.

The critical flaw size is 1.40 μm (or 2.81 μm if interpreted as full crack length 2a_c for an internal crack, though standard convention uses half-crack length a_c for surface cracks).
