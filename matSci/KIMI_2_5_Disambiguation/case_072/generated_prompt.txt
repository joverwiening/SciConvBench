Assume the carburizing process is conducted at 927°C (1200 K), which is a standard industrial temperature for gas carburizing of steel.

Use Fick's second law for non-steady state diffusion with the error function solution for a semi-infinite solid with constant surface concentration.

The governing equation is: (Cx - C0)/(Cs - C0) = 1 - erf(x/(2√(Dt)))

Where Cx = 0.40 wt% C (target concentration at depth x), C0 = 0.20 wt% C (initial concentration), Cs = 0.95 wt% C (surface concentration), x = 0.80 mm = 0.80 × 10^-3 m, and D = 1.6 × 10^-11 m^2/s.

Calculate the dimensionless concentration ratio: (0.40 - 0.20)/(0.95 - 0.20) = 0.20/0.75 = 0.2667.

Set up the equation: 0.2667 = 1 - erf(z), where z = x/(2√(Dt)).

Solve for the error function argument: erf(z) = 1 - 0.2667 = 0.7333.

Determine z from error function tables or inverse calculation: z ≈ 0.785.

Substitute known values: 0.785 = (0.80 × 10^-3)/(2√(1.6 × 10^-11 × t)).

Solve for the square root term: √(1.6 × 10^-11 × t) = (0.80 × 10^-3)/(2 × 0.785) = 5.096 × 10^-4 m.

Square both sides: 1.6 × 10^-11 × t = (5.096 × 10^-4)^2 = 2.597 × 10^-7 m^2.

Calculate the time: t = (2.597 × 10^-7)/(1.6 × 10^-11) = 16231 seconds.

Convert to hours: t = 16231/3600 = 4.51 hours.

The estimated time required for the carbon content to reach 0.40 wt% C at a depth of 0.80 mm is approximately 4.5 hours (or 16200 seconds).
