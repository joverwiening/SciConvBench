The semiconductor is n-type.
The donor concentration is N_D = 1.0 x 10^22 m^-3.
The donors are fully ionized.
The semiconductor material is assumed to be Silicon (Si).
The assessment is performed at room temperature (300 K).
The intrinsic carrier concentration for Si at 300 K is n_i = 1.0 x 10^16 m^-3.
The equilibrium electron concentration (n) is approximately equal to the donor concentration, so n ≈ 1.0 x 10^22 m^-3.
The equilibrium hole concentration (p) is the minority carrier concentration, calculated using the mass action law p = n_i^2 / n.
The equilibrium hole concentration is p ≈ 1.0 x 10^10 m^-3.
The electron concentration is many orders of magnitude greater than the hole concentration (n >> p), which is characteristic of an n-type material.
The electron concentration is significantly greater than the intrinsic carrier concentration (n >> n_i), which is characteristic of an extrinsic semiconductor.
