Q: What is the expected relationship between the lattice parameter a and the interplanar spacing d_(100) for a cubic crystal, assuming a simple cubic, body-centered cubic (BCC), or face-centered cubic (FCC) structure?
A: For a cubic crystal, the interplanar spacing \( d_{(hkl)} \) is given by:

\[
d_{(hkl)} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}}
\]

For the (100) planes, \( h=1, k=0, l=0 \), so:

\[
d_{(100)} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{1^2 + 0^2 + 0^2}} = \frac{a}{1} = a
\]

Thus, for any cubic structure—simple cubic, BCC, or FCC—the expected relationship is \( d_{(100)} = a \).

Given the complete requirement, \( a = 0.360 \) nm and \( d_{(100)} = 0.360 \) nm, the measured spacing matches the theoretical value for a cubic structure. Therefore, the structure is consistent with a simple cubic lattice. BCC and FCC structures do not have (100) planes with \( d_{(100)} = a \) due to atomic positions causing diffraction rules, but the geometric spacing formula still holds; however, (100) reflections are forbidden in BCC and FCC due to structure factor rules.

So, the observation of a (100) spacing equal to \( a \) supports a simple cubic structure.
