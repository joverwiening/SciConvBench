A paramagnet obeys the Curie law, given by χ = C/T, where χ is the magnetic susceptibility, C is the Curie constant, and T is the absolute temperature.  
The susceptibility χ is inversely proportional to the temperature T.  
As temperature increases from 300 K to 600 K, the value of T doubles.  
Since χ ∝ 1/T, doubling the temperature results in halving the susceptibility.  
Therefore, the susceptibility decreases as temperature rises from 300 K to 600 K.  
The correct trend is that susceptibility decreases with increasing temperature.
