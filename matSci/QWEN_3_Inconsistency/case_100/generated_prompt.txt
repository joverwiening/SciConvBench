Use the equation ΔG° = -RT ln K to relate the standard Gibbs free energy change to the equilibrium constant.  
Convert ΔG° to joules: -12 kJ/mol = -12,000 J/mol.  
Use R = 8.314 J/mol·K and T = 298 K.  
Calculate ln K = -ΔG° / (RT) = 12,000 / (8.314 × 298).  
Compute the value of ln K.  
Exponentiate to find K = e^(ln K).  
Compare the calculated K to 1.  
Since ΔG° is negative, K should be greater than 1.  
The given K value of 0.008 is less than 1, which contradicts the negative ΔG°.  
Therefore, the stated equilibrium constant K = 0.008 is incorrect for ΔG° = -12 kJ/mol at 298 K.
