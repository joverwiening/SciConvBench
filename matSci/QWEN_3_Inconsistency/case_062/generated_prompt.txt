The thermal-shock parameter is defined as R ∝ σ_f k / (E α).  
R is a measure of thermal-shock resistance, so higher R indicates better resistance.  
Fracture strength σ_f is in the numerator, so increasing σ_f improves resistance.  
Thermal conductivity k is in the numerator, so higher k enhances resistance by promoting uniform temperature distribution.  
Young’s modulus E is in the denominator, so lower E improves resistance by reducing thermal stress.  
The coefficient of thermal expansion α is in the denominator, so increasing α decreases R, worsening thermal-shock resistance.  
Therefore, increasing α does not improve thermal-shock resistance; it degrades it.  
The initial conclusion that increasing α improves resistance is incorrect.
