Assume the semiconductor is at room temperature (300 K).  

  
The semiconductor is n-type with a donor concentration of 1.0×10^22 m^-3.  
The donors are fully ionized.  
The system is at thermal equilibrium.  
The temperature is 300 K.  
The given electron concentration is 1.0×10^18 m^-3.  
The given hole concentration is 8.0×10^21 m^-3.  
The product of electron and hole concentrations is 8.0×10^39 m^-6.  
This product exceeds the square of the intrinsic carrier concentration for typical semiconductors by many orders of magnitude.  
The reported carrier concentrations violate the mass action law (n·p = ni²) for semiconductors in thermal equilibrium.  
Therefore, the given carrier concentrations are not physically consistent with equilibrium conditions.  
The hole concentration is higher than the donor concentration in an n-type material, which is not possible under full ionization and equilibrium.  
The reported values indicate either a fundamental error in measurement or a misrepresentation of the system state.
