A Frenkel defect is a point defect in which an ion, typically a cation, leaves its regular lattice site and occupies an interstitial site. 
This creates a vacancy at the original lattice site and an interstitial defect at the new location. 
Both defects involve the same type of ion—either a cation or an anion—but in most cases, it is the smaller, more mobile cation that moves. 
A Frenkel defect does not involve the simultaneous creation of a cation vacancy and an anion vacancy. 
The defect pair described in the query, consisting of one cation vacancy and one anion vacancy, is not a Frenkel defect. 
That pair is characteristic of a Schottky defect in an ionic compound.
