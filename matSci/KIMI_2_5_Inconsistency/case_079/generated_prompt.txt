The semiconductor is n-type with fully ionized donor concentration N_D = 1.0×10^22 m^-3.

Assume the semiconductor is non-degenerate and at room temperature where the intrinsic carrier concentration n_i is much smaller than N_D.

The charge neutrality condition requires n = N_D + p.

For an n-type semiconductor with N_D >> n_i, the majority carrier concentration is approximately equal to the donor concentration: n ≈ N_D = 1.0×10^22 m^-3.

The minority carrier concentration is determined by the mass action law: p = n_i^2 / n.

Assume a typical value for silicon at room temperature where n_i ≈ 1.0×10^16 m^-3, giving p ≈ (1.0×10^16)^2 / (1.0×10^22) = 1.0×10^10 m^-3.

The equilibrium carrier concentrations are therefore n ≈ 1.0×10^22 m^-3 and p ≈ 1.0×10^10 m^-3, with electrons as majority carriers and holes as minority carriers.

The product n × p = n_i^2 is satisfied, confirming thermal equilibrium.

The original listed values of n = 1.0×10^18 m^-3 and p = 8.0×10^21 m^-3 are physically inconsistent with the stated n-type doping and charge neutrality.
