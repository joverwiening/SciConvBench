The user wants to calculate the activation energy for diffusion using Arrhenius behavior.

The Arrhenius equation for diffusion is given as D = D_0 exp(-Q_d/RT), though the user wrote D = D_0 exp(Q_d/RT) which appears to be a typo (the standard form has negative sign in the exponent). I will use the standard physically correct form: D = D_0 exp(-Q_d/RT).

Given data:
- D1 = 4.76 × 10^-18 m²/s at T1 = 866 K
- D2 = 1.86 × 10^-12 m²/s at T2 = 1438 K
- R = 8.314 J/mol·K

To find Q_d, take the ratio of the two diffusion coefficients:
D2/D1 = [D_0 exp(-Q_d/RT2)] / [D_0 exp(-Q_d/RT1)] = exp[-Q_d/R × (1/T2 - 1/T1)]

Taking the natural logarithm:
ln(D2/D1) = -Q_d/R × (1/T2 - 1/T1) = Q_d/R × (1/T1 - 1/T2)

Solving for Q_d:
Q_d = R × ln(D2/D1) / (1/T1 - 1/T2)

Calculate the values:
- ln(D2/D1) = ln(1.86 × 10^-12 / 4.76 × 10^-18) = ln(3.9076 × 10^5) = 12.873
- 1/T1 = 1/866 = 1.1547 × 10^-3 K^-1
- 1/T2 = 1/1438 = 6.9541 × 10^-4 K^-1
- 1/T1 - 1/T2 = 4.5929 × 10^-4 K^-1

Q_d = 8.314 × 12.873 / (4.5929 × 10^-4) J/mol
Q_d = 107.03 / (4.5929 × 10^-4) J/mol
Q_d = 233,000 J/mol = 233 kJ/mol

The activation energy for diffusion is 233 kJ/mol.
