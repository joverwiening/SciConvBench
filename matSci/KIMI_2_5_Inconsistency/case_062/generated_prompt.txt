The thermal-shock parameter R is proportional to σ_f k/(Eα), where σ_f is fracture strength, k is thermal conductivity, E is Young's modulus, and α is the coefficient of thermal expansion.

The conclusion that increasing α improves thermal-shock resistance is incorrect.

Analyze the proportionality: R ∝ 1/α. This means R is inversely proportional to α. Therefore, increasing α actually decreases R, which worsens thermal-shock resistance, not improves it.

Explain the physical reasoning: Thermal shock occurs when rapid temperature changes create thermal gradients and thus thermal stresses. The thermal stress σ_thermal is proportional to EαΔT. A higher α means larger dimensional change for a given temperature change, producing higher thermal stresses for the same temperature gradient. This makes the material more susceptible to cracking under thermal shock.

The correct qualitative relationship is: materials with lower α have better thermal-shock resistance because they generate lower thermal stresses for the same temperature change.
