The task is to calculate the activation energy for diffusion using Arrhenius behavior.

The Arrhenius equation for diffusion coefficient is D = D_0 * exp(-Q_d / RT), where D is the diffusion coefficient, D_0 is the pre-exponential factor, Q_d is the activation energy, R is the gas constant, and T is absolute temperature.

Take the natural logarithm of the Arrhenius equation to get ln(D) = ln(D_0) - Q_d / RT.

Set up two equations for the two given conditions:
ln(D_1) = ln(D_0) - Q_d / (R * T_1)
ln(D_2) = ln(D_0) - Q_d / (R * T_2)

Subtract the second equation from the first to eliminate ln(D_0):
ln(D_1) - ln(D_2) = -Q_d / (R * T_1) + Q_d / (R * T_2)
ln(D_1 / D_2) = (Q_d / R) * (1/T_2 - 1/T_1)

Solve for Q_d:
Q_d = R * ln(D_1 / D_2) / (1/T_2 - 1/T_1)

Substitute the given values:
D_1 = 1.0 × 10^-12 m^2/s at T_1 = 800 K
D_2 = 2.0 × 10^-15 m^2/s at T_2 = 1100 K
R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)

Calculate:
D_1 / D_2 = (1.0 × 10^-12) / (2.0 × 10^-15) = 500
ln(500) = 6.215

1/T_2 - 1/T_1 = 1/1100 - 1/800 = 0.000909 - 0.001250 = -0.000341 K^-1

Q_d = 8.314 * 6.215 / (-0.000341) = 51.67 / (-0.000341) = -151,500 J/mol

The negative sign indicates the temperature dependence direction. Rechecking: At 800 K, D = 1.0×10^-12. At 1100 K, D = 2.0×10^-15. This is physically impossible for normal thermally activated diffusion (D should increase with T).
