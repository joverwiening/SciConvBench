The degree of crystallinity shall be calculated using the two-phase model (rule of mixtures) based on mass fractions.

The mass fraction crystallinity (w_c) is defined by the relationship: 1/ρ = w_c/ρ_c + (1-w_c)/ρ_a, where ρ is the measured density, ρ_c is the crystalline density, and ρ_a is the amorphous density.

Rearranging to solve for w_c yields: w_c = (ρ_c/ρ) × (ρ - ρ_a)/(ρ_c - ρ_a).

Substituting the given values: ρ = 1.42 g/cm³, ρ_a = 1.05 g/cm³, ρ_c = 1.30 g/cm³.

Calculate (ρ - ρ_a) = 1.42 - 1.05 = 0.37 g/cm³.

Calculate (ρ_c - ρ_a) = 1.30 - 1.05 = 0.25 g/cm³.

Calculate the ratio (ρ - ρ_a)/(ρ_c - ρ_a) = 0.37/0.25 = 1.48.

Calculate ρ_c/ρ = 1.30/1.42 = 0.9155.

Multiply: w_c = 0.9155 × 1.48 = 1.355, or 135.5%.

The result exceeds 100%, which is physically impossible for mass fraction crystallinity.

This indicates an inconsistency in the input data: the measured density (1.42 g/cm³) exceeds both the amorphous density (1.05 g/cm³) and the crystalline density (1.30 g/cm³).

For a valid two-phase model, the measured density of a semicrystalline polymer must lie between ρ_a and ρ_c.

Report that the given data contains an error: the measured density cannot exceed the crystalline phase density if the crystalline phase is the densest component.

If forced to proceed with calculation, note that volume fraction crystallinity (φ_c) would similarly fail: φ_c = (ρ - ρ_a)/(ρ_c - ρ_a) = 0.37/0.25 = 1.48, or 148%, also impossible.

Conclusion: The degree of crystallinity cannot be estimated from these inconsistent values. Verify the measured density value; if it should be 1.20 g/cm³ instead of 1.42 g/cm³, then w_c = (1.30/1.20) × (1.20-1.05)/(1.30-1.05) = 1.083 × 0.15/0.25 = 0.65 or 65%.
