The specific surface area per unit mass (SSA_m) is defined as total surface area divided by total mass.

For spherical particles of diameter d and material density ρ:
- Volume of one particle: V = (π/6)d³
- Surface area of one particle: A = πd²
- Mass of one particle: m = ρV = ρ(π/6)d³

The specific surface area per unit mass for a single particle is:
SSA_m = A/m = πd² / [ρ(π/6)d³] = 6/(ρd)

Since SSA_m is inversely proportional to particle diameter d, smaller particles have higher specific surface area per unit mass.

Given:
- d₁ = 0.5 μm (finer powder)
- d₂ = 5 μm (coarser powder)

The ratio is: SSA_m,₁ / SSA_m,₂ = d₂/d₁ = 5/0.5 = 10

Therefore, the finer powder (0.5 μm) should have 10 times higher specific surface area per unit mass than the coarser powder (5 μm).

The statement that "the finer powder has the lower specific surface area" is physically incorrect for SSA_m. The correct trend is that the finer powder has the higher specific surface area per unit mass.
