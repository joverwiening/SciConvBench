The binary polymer blend consists of two immiscible polymers, not a fully miscible blend as initially described.

The system contains two distinct polymers with glass transition temperatures of 315 K and 375 K respectively.

The polymer with Tg = 315 K is assumed to be polystyrene (PS) with a typical molecular weight of 100,000 g/mol.

The polymer with Tg = 375 K is assumed to be poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) with a typical molecular weight of 100,000 g/mol.

The blend composition is assumed to be 50:50 weight percent of PS and PMMA.

The DSC sample is assumed to have been prepared by melt-mixing at 200°C followed by quenching to room temperature to preserve the homogeneous melt state.

The DSC heating rate is assumed to be 10 K/min, which is a standard rate for detecting glass transitions.

The observation of two distinct glass transitions at 315 K and 375 K definitively indicates phase separation and immiscibility in the blend.

A fully miscible binary polymer blend would exhibit a single glass transition temperature intermediate between the Tg values of the pure components, typically described by the Fox equation or Gordon-Taylor equation.

The presence of two separate Tg values corresponding to the pure component values demonstrates that the polymers exist as separate phases with no significant molecular mixing at the segmental level.

The initial description of the blend as "fully miscible" is contradicted by the DSC data and should be rejected; the correct classification is immiscible or partially miscible with strong phase separation.
