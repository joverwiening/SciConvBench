The problem requires calculation of resolved shear stress using the Schmid factor.

The Schmid factor is defined as the product of the cosines of two angles: the angle φ between the loading direction and the normal to the slip plane, and the angle λ between the loading direction and the slip direction.

The Schmid factor m is given by: m = cos(φ) × cos(λ)

The resolved shear stress τ is related to the applied tensile stress σ by: τ = σ × m

Given values:
- φ = 30°
- λ = 45°
- σ = 100 MPa
- Stated maximum Schmid factor = 0.5

Verification of the Schmid factor:
- cos(30°) = √3/2 ≈ 0.866
- cos(45°) = √2/2 ≈ 0.707
- m = 0.866 × 0.707 ≈ 0.612

Note: The calculated Schmid factor of approximately 0.612 differs from the stated value of 0.5. However, the problem explicitly states to use the maximum Schmid factor of 0.5.

Calculation of resolved shear stress:
- τ = σ × m = 100 MPa × 0.5 = 50 MPa

The resolved shear stress at σ = 100 MPa is 50 MPa.
