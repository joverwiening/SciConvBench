The Zn/Cu galvanic cell operates under standard conditions with the following electrochemical principles.

Zinc has a standard reduction potential of -0.76 V.

Copper has a standard reduction potential of +0.34 V.

The species with the more negative reduction potential undergoes oxidation and serves as the anode.

The species with the more positive reduction potential undergoes reduction and serves as the cathode.

Zinc is the anode where oxidation occurs: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻.

Copper is the cathode where reduction occurs: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu.

The cell potential E°cell is calculated as 0.34 V - (-0.76 V) = 1.10 V.

The original statement identifying Cu as the anode and Zn as the cathode is incorrect and must be reversed.
