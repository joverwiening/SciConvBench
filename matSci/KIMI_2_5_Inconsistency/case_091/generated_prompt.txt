A static water contact angle of 195° is physically impossible for a smooth surface. The theoretical maximum contact angle for water on any smooth surface is 180°, which represents a perfectly non-wetting surface where the droplet forms a perfect sphere touching the surface at a single point. Reported contact angles exceeding 180° typically indicate measurement artifacts, surface roughness effects (Cassie-Baxter state), or errors in the measurement technique. Assume the reported value contains a typographical error and the intended value is 95° or 105°. For a smooth polymer surface with a contact angle of approximately 95-105°, classify the surface as hydrophobic. Water will bead up on the surface with limited spreading. The surface exhibits poor wettability by water, which correlates with low surface energy typical of hydrocarbon-based polymers such as polyethylene or polypropylene.
