The intrinsic carrier concentration for silicon at 300 K is n_i = 1.0×10^16 m^-3.

The value n = p = 1.0×10^20 m^-3 is incorrect for intrinsic silicon at 300 K and appears to be erroneous or misattributed.

The correct intrinsic carrier concentration is determined by the mass action law: n × p = n_i^2.

For intrinsic silicon, charge neutrality requires n = p = n_i.

Therefore, at 300 K, the correct values are n = p = n_i = 1.0×10^16 m^-3.

The value 1.0×10^20 m^-3 is physically inconsistent with intrinsic silicon at 300 K, as it would require either heavy doping or a temperature far above 300 K.
