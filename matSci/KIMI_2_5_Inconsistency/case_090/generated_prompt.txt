The oxidation state of copper in Cu^2+ is +2, which means each copper ion requires 2 electrons to be reduced to metallic copper (Cu^0).

The correct electron number for Faraday's law calculation is n = 2 electrons per Cu atom.

The original calculation using n = 1 is incorrect and will yield a deposited mass that is twice the actual value.

To compute the correct deposited mass:
Use Faraday's law: m = (M × I × t) / (n × F)
Where M = 63.55 g/mol (molar mass of copper), I = 2.0 A, t = 1800 s (30 min), n = 2, and F = 96485 C/mol.

The correct deposited mass is approximately 1.18 grams of copper.
