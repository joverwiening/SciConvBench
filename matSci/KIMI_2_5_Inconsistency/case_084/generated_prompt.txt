The reported relative permittivity ε_r = 0.82 for a ceramic capacitor at room temperature is physically inconsistent for a passive dielectric.

All passive dielectric materials must have ε_r ≥ 1.

The relative permittivity is defined as ε_r = ε/ε_0, where ε is the material's permittivity and ε_0 is the vacuum permittivity.

Vacuum represents the minimum possible permittivity, with ε_r = 1 exactly.

Any material medium polarizes in response to an electric field, which always increases the effective permittivity relative to vacuum, giving ε_r > 1.

Values of ε_r < 1 would require the material to reduce the electric field compared to vacuum, which violates fundamental electromagnetic principles for passive materials.

Typical ceramic capacitors use dielectrics with ε_r ranging from approximately 10 to 10,000 depending on the ceramic composition (e.g., BaTiO3-based ceramics).

The value ε_r = 0.82 likely represents a typographical error, possibly meaning ε_r = 820 or another value in the hundreds or thousands range common for ceramic dielectrics.
