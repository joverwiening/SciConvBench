I apologize, but there's a fundamental error in the initial statement about the anode and cathode. In a Zn/Cu galvanic cell:

Zn is actually the ANODE (oxidation occurs here)
Cu is the CATHODE (reduction occurs here)

This is because:
- Zn has a more negative standard reduction potential
- Zn will undergo oxidation (lose electrons)
- Cu will undergo reduction (gain electrons)

Would you like me to elaborate on the electrochemical principles behind this cell's configuration?


The Zn/Cu galvanic cell has the following characteristics:
Anode: Zinc (Zn)
Cathode: Copper (Cu)
The cell operates with spontaneous electron flow from Zn to Cu.
Zinc undergoes oxidation at the anode.
Copper undergoes reduction at the cathode.
Standard conditions assume 1 M concentration of metal ions, 25°C temperature, and 1 atm pressure.
