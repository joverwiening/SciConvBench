I'll choose sodium chloride (NaCl) as the reference ionic crystal, which is a classic example for studying point defects.


The Frenkel defect is identified in a sodium chloride (NaCl) ionic crystal.
The defect consists of one cation vacancy (Na+ vacancy) and one interstitial cation (Na+ ion in an interstitial position).
This type of point defect represents a local displacement of an ion from its regular lattice site to an interstitial site within the crystal structure.
The Frenkel defect occurs without changing the overall stoichiometry of the crystal.
The analysis assumes standard temperature and pressure conditions for the ionic crystal.
