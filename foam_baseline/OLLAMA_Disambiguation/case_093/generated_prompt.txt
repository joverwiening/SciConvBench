Simulate a hot plume rising from a small heat source on the floor of a large, open room.  
The room dimensions are 2 m width, 4 m height, and 1 m depth (unit depth for a 2‑D case).  
The heat source is a fixed‑temperature surface at 400 K, located centrally on the floor with a length of 0.2 m along the floor and a depth of 1 m; its thickness is effectively zero.  
The ambient air is initialized at 300 K.  
Floor patch “heatSource”: velocity noSlip, temperature fixedValue 400 K, turbulence kinetic energy k using kqRWallFunction, turbulence dissipation epsilon using epsilonWallFunction, turbulent viscosity nut using nutkWallFunction.  
Floor patch “floorSides”: velocity noSlip, temperature zeroGradient, k using kqRWallFunction, epsilon using epsilonWallFunction, nut using nutkWallFunction.  
Side walls (vertical walls): velocity slip, temperature zeroGradient, k zeroGradient, epsilon zeroGradient, pressure‑related field p_rgh zeroGradient.  
Top (open boundary): p_rgh totalPressure 0, velocity pressureInletOutletVelocity, temperature inletOutlet with inletValue 300 K and outletValue zeroGradient, k inletOutlet with inletValue 1e‑5 and outletValue zeroGradient, epsilon inletOutlet with inletValue 1e‑5 and outletValue zeroGradient.  
Front and back patches (empty for 2‑D): all fields empty (zeroGradient in the out‑of‑plane direction).  
Use the RANS k‑epsilon turbulence model (set simulationType RAS and RASModel kEpsilon in constant/momentumTransport).  
Run a transient simulation for a total of 100 s with an initial time step of 0.01 s, adjustable Δt to maintain a maximum Courant number of 0.8, and write results every 5 s.  
Create a graded blockMesh with approximately 100 cells in the x‑direction (graded toward the centre of the floor) and approximately 200 cells in the y‑direction (graded toward the bottom near the heat source).  
Apply the gravity vector (0  -9.81  0) m s⁻².  
Define thermophysical properties of air (e.g., using standard air values: density ≈1.225 kg m⁻³ at 300 K, dynamic viscosity ≈1.8e‑5 Pa·s, thermal conductivity ≈0.026 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, specific heat cp ≈1005 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, ideal gas model).
