Conduct a compressible flow simulation through an angled porous duct.
Use the porousSimpleFoam solver with the SIMPLE algorithm.
The simulation is a steady-state case.
The domain is a 45-degree angled rectangular duct with a total length of 350mm and a width of 50mm.
The duct consists of a 150mm inlet section, a 100mm porous section, and a 100mm outlet section.
The mesh will be generated using the provided blockMeshDict content.
The mesh has 15 cells in the inlet section, 20 cells in the porous section, and 20 cells in the outlet section.
The mesh has 20 cells in both the y and z directions, for a total grid of 55x20x20 cells.
The inlet and outlet boundaries are of type patch.
The porosityWall surfaces are of type wall with no-slip conditions.
The remaining surfaces are default walls.
The simulation runs from time 0 to 1000.
The timestep is 1.
Results are written every 100 timesteps.
The fluid for the simulation is air.
The GAMG solver is used for pressure with a tolerance of 1e-08 and a relative tolerance of 0.05.
The smoothSolver with a GaussSeidel smoother is used for velocity and turbulence quantities.
The tolerance for velocity is 1e-06.
The tolerance for the k-epsilon turbulence model quantities is 1e-07.
The relaxation factor for pressure is 0.3.
The relaxation factor for velocity is 0.7.
The relaxation factor for turbulence quantities is 0.9.
Bounded Gauss upwind schemes are used for convective terms.
Gauss linear corrected schemes are used for diffusive terms.
The Darcy coefficient (d) for the porous section is 1e9.
The Forchheimer coefficient (f) for the porous section is 1000.
The porous resistance is applied isotropically.
The inlet velocity magnitude is 10 m/s.
The static pressure at the outlet is 101325 Pa.
The temperature at the inlet is 300 K.
The turbulent kinetic energy (k) at the inlet is 0.375 m^2/s^2.
The turbulent dissipation rate (epsilon) at the inlet is 10.7 m^2/s^3.
