Use the interFoam solver to simulate the mixing of water and sludge in a 2‑D rectangular tank.  
Set the tank width to 1 meter and the tank height to 1 meter.  
Define two phases in the transportProperties dictionary: water and sludge.  
Configure the water phase with a Newtonian transport model, kinematic viscosity ν = 1e‑6 m²/s, and density ρ = 1000 kg/m³.  
Configure the sludge phase with a Bingham plastic transport model, yield stress τ₀ = 15 Pa, plastic viscosity μ₀ = 0.1 Pa·s, and density ρ = 1500 kg/m³.  
Set the surface tension coefficient sigma to 0.07 N/m.  
Create the transportProperties file reflecting the above phase properties and the sigma value.  
Initialize the volume fraction field (alpha) to represent the initial distribution of water and sludge in the tank.  
Apply appropriate boundary conditions for velocity, pressure, and volume fraction on the tank walls and any inlet/outlet boundaries.  
Select suitable time‑step size, solver tolerances, and numerical schemes to handle the non‑Newtonian Bingham plastic behavior.  
Run the simulation until the mixing process reaches the desired end time or convergence criteria.
