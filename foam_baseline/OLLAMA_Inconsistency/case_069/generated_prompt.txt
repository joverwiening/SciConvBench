Simulate the flow in a cyclone separator using simpleFoam with the k‑epsilon turbulence model.  
The cyclone geometry has an overall diameter of 0.3 m, a tangential inlet diameter of 0.05 m, and a total height of 0.5 m.  
Apply a uniform tangential inlet velocity of 10 m s⁻¹ at the inlet.  
Set the turbulence intensity at the inlet to 5 % and specify a turbulent kinetic energy k of approximately 0.38 m² s⁻² (acceptable range 0.3–0.5 m² s⁻²).  
Specify the turbulent dissipation rate ε at the inlet as approximately 15 m² s⁻³ (acceptable range 10–20 m² s⁻³).  
Use zeroGradient boundary conditions for the pressure field p on the inlet, top outlet (vortex finder), bottom dustOutlet, and all walls.  
The top outlet pressure is atmospheric (0 gauge).  
Apply no‑slip wall boundary conditions for velocity on all solid surfaces and standard k‑epsilon wall functions for turbulence quantities.  
Generate a computational mesh that adequately resolves the inlet region, the swirling core, and the wall boundary layers.  
Run simpleFoam until the residuals for continuity, momentum, k, and ε fall below 1 × 10⁻⁵ and verify mass‑flow balance between inlet and outlets.
