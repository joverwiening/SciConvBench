Set up the classic 2D dam-break problem in OpenFOAM using twoPhaseEulerFoam.
Assume OpenFOAM-v2206 (ESI) as the distribution, or the closest compatible version that supports twoPhaseEulerFoam.
The overall domain is a 2D rectangular box 4 m wide by 2 m tall, extruded one cell (0.1 m) in the z-direction.
The water column is 1 m wide and 1 m tall, positioned at the left side of the domain (x from 0 to 1, y from 0 to 1).
Use a blockMesh mesh with a resolution of 40 cells in x and 20 cells in y, and 1 cell in z.
Set the z-direction patches (front and back) to type empty in the boundary conditions.
In constant/polyMesh/blockMeshDict, define the block and boundary patches accordingly.
In system/controlDict, set application to twoPhaseEulerFoam, startTime to 0, endTime to 1, deltaT to 0.001, and writeInterval to 0.05.
Use adjustableRunTime for writeControl and set maxCo to 0.5 with adjustTimeStep yes.
Define two phases: water and air, with water density 1000 kg/m³ and air density 1 kg/m³.
Set the kinematic viscosity of water to 1e-6 m²/s and air to 1.48e-5 m²/s.
In constant/phaseProperties, define the two phases as water and air.
Use setFields to initialize alpha.water to 1 inside the water column region (x: 0 to 1, y: 0 to 1) and 0 elsewhere.
Provide the system/setFieldsDict with a defaultFieldValues of alpha.water = 0 and a boxToCell region setting alpha.water = 1 for the water column.
Set initial velocity U to (0 0 0) everywhere and pressure p_rgh to 0.
Apply gravity g = (0 -9.81 0) in constant/g.
Use standard wall boundary conditions (no-slip for U, zeroGradient for alpha.water and p_rgh) on the bottom and side walls, and an inletOutlet or zeroGradient condition on the top patch.
Use the MULES-based interface compression scheme for alpha.water transport.
Provide all required OpenFOAM case directory files: constant/, system/, and 0/ directories with all necessary fields and dictionaries.
