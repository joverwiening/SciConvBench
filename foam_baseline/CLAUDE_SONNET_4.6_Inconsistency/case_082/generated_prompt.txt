Configure a simulation for the separation of three immiscible fluids (oil, water, and gas) in a horizontal gravity separator using the VOF method.
The solver specified in controlDict is interFoam.
The phases are defined in transportProperties with their respective densities and viscosities.
Assume reasonable fluid properties: water density 1000 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 1e-3 Pa·s, oil density 800 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 5e-3 Pa·s, gas density 1.2 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 1.8e-5 Pa·s.
The domain is a horizontal box with reasonable best-practice dimensions: length 1.0 m, height 0.3 m, width 0.1 m.
Use a structured mesh appropriate for the domain, with a reasonable cell count such as 100 × 30 × 1 cells (2D or quasi-2D simulation).
The initial state is a stratified mixture set using setFieldsDict.
Assume reasonable initial layer thicknesses: gas occupies the top third (height 0.2 m to 0.3 m), oil occupies the middle third (height 0.1 m to 0.2 m), and water occupies the bottom third (height 0.0 m to 0.1 m).
The alpha.water field represents the volume fraction of water.
Define alpha.oil as a separate phase field for the oil volume fraction.
Gas volume fraction is implicitly 1 - alpha.water - alpha.oil.
Gravity is set to (0 -9.81 0) in the g file.
Use a simulation end time of 10 seconds with a maximum time step of 0.005 seconds and adjustable time stepping with a maximum Courant number of 0.5.
Write output results every 0.5 seconds.
Use standard VOF interface compression settings with interfaceCompression coefficient of 1 in fvSolution.
Apply standard boundary conditions: no-slip walls on top, bottom, front, and back; inlet/outlet or wall conditions on left and right ends as appropriate for a closed separator.
Use the MULES scheme for phase fraction equations and PIMPLE algorithm for pressure-velocity coupling.
