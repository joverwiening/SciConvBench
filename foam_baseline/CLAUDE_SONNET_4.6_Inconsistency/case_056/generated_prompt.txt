Set up a 3D dam break simulation in OpenFOAM (assume OpenFOAM v9 or a recent ESI release such as v2206 as a reasonable modern choice).
The domain is a rectangular tank of dimensions 1m (x) x 0.6m (y) x 0.4m (z).
Use the twoPhaseEulerFoam solver for this transient three-phase simulation.
The three phases are water, oil, and air, defined in transportProperties (or phaseProperties as appropriate for the solver).
Gravity acts in the -y direction, defined in the constant/g file.
Initially, a column of water occupies the region x: 0 to 0.2m, y: 0 to 0.2m, z: 0 to 0.6m (one corner of the tank).
A layer of light oil sits on top of the water column, occupying x: 0 to 0.2m, y: 0.2m to 0.25m, z: 0 to 0.6m.
The remainder of the domain is filled with air.
Use the setFieldsDict utility to initialize the phase fraction fields alpha.water, alpha.oil, and alpha.air in their respective regions.
Ensure alpha.water + alpha.oil + alpha.air = 1 everywhere.
All wall boundaries (bottom, sides, and ends) use no-slip velocity conditions and zeroGradient for pressure.
The top boundary is an atmospheric inlet/outlet (use totalPressure for pressure and pressureInletOutletVelocity for velocity).
Provide the full case directory structure including: constant/polyMesh/blockMeshDict, constant/transportProperties (or phaseProperties), constant/g, system/controlDict, system/fvSchemes, system/fvSolution, system/setFieldsDict, and all required 0/ boundary condition files for alpha.water, alpha.oil, alpha.air, U, and p_rgh.
Use reasonable physical properties: water density ~1000 kg/m³, oil density ~800 kg/m³, air density ~1 kg/m³, with appropriate viscosities.
Use a blockMesh with a reasonable uniform mesh resolution (e.g., 50x30x20 cells).
Set a simulation end time of 1 second with a maximum Courant number of 0.5 and adjustable time stepping.
