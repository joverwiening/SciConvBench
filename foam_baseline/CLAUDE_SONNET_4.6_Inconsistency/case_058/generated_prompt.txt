Model the injection of a high-pressure, compressible gas into a tank of water using OpenFOAM.
Use the interFoam solver for two-phase flow simulation.
Define the gas phase using the perfectGas equation of state.
Define the water phase as incompressible.
The simulation is transient, targeting observation of gas jet penetration and expansion in water.
The gas enters at Mach 0.8.
Compressibility effects of the gas are significant due to high velocity and pressure differences.
Assume a 2D axisymmetric rectangular domain as the tank geometry based on best practices, with dimensions 0.5 m in length and 0.2 m in width/radius.
Place the gas inlet at the left boundary center, with an inlet nozzle diameter of 0.01 m.
Assume inlet gas pressure of 500,000 Pa (5 bar absolute) and inlet temperature of 300 K.
Derive the inlet velocity corresponding to Mach 0.8 using the perfectGas properties for air (gamma = 1.4, R = 287 J/kg/K), giving approximately 278 m/s.
Initialize the tank as fully filled with water at rest, at atmospheric pressure (101,325 Pa) and 300 K.
Use air as the gas phase with density computed from the perfectGas EOS, dynamic viscosity of 1.8e-5 Pa·s.
Use water as the incompressible liquid phase with density 1000 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 1e-3 Pa·s.
Set surface tension between gas and water to 0.07 N/m.
Use a maximum Courant number of 0.5 to control the time step.
Set the initial time step to 1e-6 s.
Run the simulation to an end time of 0.05 s.
Use a structured mesh with refinement near the inlet and the gas-water interface.
Apply a fixed velocity inlet boundary condition for the gas inlet.
Apply a zero-gradient or atmospheric pressure outlet at the top or right boundary of the tank.
Apply no-slip wall boundary conditions on all remaining tank walls.
Use the MULES scheme for interface capturing (alpha field).
Output results at every 0.001 s for post-processing of gas volume fraction, pressure, and velocity fields.
