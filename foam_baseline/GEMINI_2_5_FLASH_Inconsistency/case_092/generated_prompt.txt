Simulate the compressible, supersonic flow of air over a 10-degree wedge to form an oblique shock.
The freestream Mach number is 2.0.
Treat air as a perfect gas.
The simulation uses a 2D mesh extruded one cell in z with `frontAndBack` set to `empty`.
The wedge surface has a length of 1.0 meter.
The overall computational domain has a length of 4.0 meters in the x-direction.
The overall computational domain has a height of 2.5 meters in the y-direction.
The mesh has 150 cells along the wedge surface.
The mesh has 75 cells along the inlet boundary.
The cell grading ratio along the wedge surface is 8, with refinement towards the wedge.
The cell grading ratio along the inlet boundary is 1 (uniform).
The freestream/inlet conditions are `U = (686 0 0) m/s`, `T = 293 K`, and `p = 101325 Pa`.
In `constant/thermophysicalProperties`, configure a perfect-gas model using `hePsiThermo`, `equationOfState perfectGas`, and `transport sutherland`.
The inlet boundary conditions are fixed values for `U`, `p`, and `T`.
The outlet boundary conditions are `waveTransmissive` for `p` and `T` and `zeroGradient` for `U`.
The wedge surface and top boundary conditions are slip walls.
The `frontAndBack` boundary condition is `empty`.
The OpenFOAM solver application is `rhoPimpleFoam`.
In `system/controlDict`, the `application` entry is set to `rhoPimpleFoam`.
