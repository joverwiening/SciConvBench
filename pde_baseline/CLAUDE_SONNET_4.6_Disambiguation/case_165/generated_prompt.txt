Solve the 1D Compressible Navier–Stokes PDE (no heat conduction) numerically in Python.

The unknown fields are rho(x,t), v(x,t), p(x,t).

Use a cell-centered finite volume grid with Nx = 800 cells on the domain x in [-1, 1].
dx = 2.0 / 800; cell centers are at x_i = -1 + (i + 0.5)*dx for i = 0..799.
Time domain is t in [0, 0.2].

Parameters: Gamma = 1.6666666666666667, eta = 1e-08, zeta = 1e-08.

The PDE is in conservative form with m = rho*v and total energy E = p/(Gamma-1) + 0.5*rho*v^2.
The viscous stress is sigma = (zeta + 4/3*eta) * v_x.
The equations are:
- rho_t + (m)_x = 0
- m_t + (m**2/rho + p - sigma)_x = 0
- E_t + ((E+p)*v - v*sigma)_x = 0

Initial condition is a Riemann (shock-tube) problem with discontinuity at x=0.
For x < 0: rho_L = 0.445, v_L = 0.698, p_L = 3.528.
For x >= 0: rho_R = 0.5, v_R = 0.0, p_R = 0.571.

Boundary conditions are transmissive/outflow (zero-gradient) at both x=-1 and x=1.

Use an appropriate numerical scheme (e.g., HLLC or similar Riemann-solver-based finite volume method) with a CFL-based adaptive time step.

Compute rho(x,T), v(x,T), p(x,T) at T=0.2 on the cell centers.

Save a file named "solution_pdebench_cns1d_sod6_eta1em8_outflow.npz" containing arrays x (1D, length 800), rho (1D), v (1D), p (1D), and scalars Gamma, eta, zeta, T, dx, CFL.

Save a 3-panel (or 3 separate) line plot of rho, v, p vs x at T=0.2 as "solution_pdebench_cns1d_sod6_eta1em8_outflow.png".

Print the min and max of rho and p (positivity check) and the mean of rho over the domain (mass conservation check).
