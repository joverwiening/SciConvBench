Model a 2D rectangular furnace with a height of 2 meters and a width of 0.5 meters.  
Inject pulverized coal particles into the furnace with a mass flow rate of 0.1 kg/s.  
Use a Rosin-Rammler particle size distribution with a mean diameter of 100 microns for the coal particles.  
Define the coal as high-volatile bituminous coal using standard ultimate and proximate analysis typical for this type: approximately 68% carbon, 5% hydrogen, 15% oxygen, 1.5% nitrogen, 1.5% sulfur, 9% moisture, and 10% ash by mass; volatile matter content is approximately 35–40% on a dry, ash-free basis.  
Set the primary air temperature to 400 K.  
Inject coal particles with a velocity of 20 m/s in the positive y-direction (U = (0, 20, 0) m/s).  
Treat the furnace walls as having a fixed temperature of 1200 K.  
Model radiation using the P1 approximation with the weighted sum of gray gases model (WSGG) for gas radiation properties.  
Assume the furnace walls are gray-diffuse with an emissivity of 0.8, consistent with typical refractory wall materials, in the absence of a specified value.  
Set the particle emissivity to 0.9.  
Simulate the combustion process including devolatilization, volatile combustion, and char oxidation.  
Include convective and radiative heat transfer between particles, gas, and walls.
