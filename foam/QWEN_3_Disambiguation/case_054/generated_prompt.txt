Use a perfect gas with a ratio of specific heats γ = 1.4.  
Set the initial static pressure of the supersonic flow to 101325 Pa and the static temperature to 300 K.  
Define the computational domain as 1.0 m long and 0.5 m high.  
Place the 10-degree wedge starting at x = 0.2 m on the bottom wall, extending to the outlet with a length of approximately 0.8 m along the bottom.  
Use the density-based finite volume solver `rhoCentralFoam` with a central-upwind scheme for inviscid flows.  
Generate a uniform structured mesh using `blockMeshDict` with 200 cells in the x-direction and 100 cells in the y-direction.  
Initialize the flow with a Mach number of 2.0 upstream of the wedge.  
Apply supersonic inflow conditions at the inlet and supersonic outflow conditions at the outlet.  
Set adiabatic wall boundary conditions for the wedge and bottom wall.  
Set symmetry or free-slip conditions on the top boundary of the domain.  
Run the simulation until a steady-state oblique shock wave is clearly established.
