The solid porous block is 0.1 m long, 0.05 m high, and 0.01 m thick, centered within the fluid domain.  
The solid porous block is made of aluminum foam with an effective thermal conductivity of 10 W/m·K.  
The overall fluid domain is 0.5 m long, 0.1 m high, and 0.01 m thick, with the inlet on the left end and the outlet on the right end.  
The fluid is air, modeled as a compressible perfect gas with a specific heat capacity of 1005 J/kg·K and a molecular weight of 28.9 g/mol.  
The inlet velocity of the air is 1 m/s in the x-direction and 0 m/s in the y- and z-directions.  
The inlet temperature of the air is 400 K.  
The initial temperature of the solid porous block is 300 K.  
The simulation models conjugate heat transfer between the solid porous block and the surrounding fluid flow.  
The simulation accounts for conduction within the solid and convection in the fluid.  
The fluid flow is governed by the Navier-Stokes equations for a compressible fluid.  
Heat transfer in the solid is governed by the transient heat conduction equation.  
Thermal coupling at the solid-fluid interface is two-way and enforces temperature and heat flux continuity.  
The simulation runs until steady-state thermal conditions are achieved or for a specified duration if transient results are required.
