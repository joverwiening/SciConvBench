Simulate a thermal plume rising from a hot strip on the floor of a large, open room using a turbulent RANS model.  
The hot strip has a temperature of 400 K and is located on the floor centered at x = [0.9, 1.1] m with a width of 0.2 m in the x-direction and a thickness of 0.01 m in the z-direction.  
The ambient temperature of the room is 300 K.  
The room is 2.0 m long in the y-direction and has a z-thickness of 0.01 m, simulating a quasi-2D configuration.  
The top boundary (y = 2.0 m) is an outlet with boundary conditions: U as pressureInletOutletVelocity, p_rgh as totalPressure with p0 = 0, T as inletOutlet with inletValue 300 K, and k/omega as inletOutlet.  
The side walls (x = 0 and x = 2.0 m) have no-slip velocity (U), fixed temperature at 300 K (T), and fixedFluxPressure for p_rgh.  
The simulation uses a multi-block structured mesh with approximately (160 160 1) cells in the x, y, and z directions.  
The mesh is refined near the floor, particularly around the hot strip region (x = [0.9, 1.1]), and employs non-uniform grading in the y-direction near y = 0 to resolve the thermal boundary layer and plume development.  
The turbulence model used is RAS kOmegaSST.  
The simulation must resolve turbulent buoyant flow initiated by the heated strip, capturing the rise and spread of the thermal plume in the room.
