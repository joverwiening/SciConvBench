Set up a 2‑D transient simulation of vortex shedding behind a circular cylinder of diameter 1 m at Reynolds number 100 using the pimpleFoam solver.  
Create a rectangular channel domain that is 30 m long (5 m upstream of the cylinder and 25 m downstream) and 10 m high.  
Place the cylinder with its centre offset 0.2 m upward from the channel centreline (i.e., 0.2 m from the mid‑height).  
Use a uniform inlet velocity U = (1 0 0) m/s, fluid density ρ = 1 kg/m³, and kinematic viscosity ν = 0.01 m²/s.  
Apply the following boundary conditions:  
- inlet: fixedValue U = (1 0 0) m/s, zeroGradient p.  
- outlet: zeroGradient U, fixedValue p = 0.  
- topAndBottom: slip (zeroGradient U, zeroGradient p).  
- cylinder: noSlip (fixedValue U = (0 0 0) m/s).  
- frontAndBack: empty (2‑D simulation).  
Generate a structured mesh with sufficient refinement near the cylinder (at least 30 cells across the cylinder diameter) and gradual coarsening toward the far field.  
Set the simulation time from t = 0 s to t = 200 s with a constant time step Δt = 0.1 s.  
Configure pimpleFoam with appropriate relaxation factors and a maximum of 2 inner correctors per time step to ensure numerical stability at Re = 100.  
Enable the calculation of lift and drag forces on the cylinder and write the force coefficients to file every 10 time steps.  
Post‑process the time‑series of the lift coefficient to determine the dominant shedding frequency using a Fast Fourier Transform.  
Compute the Strouhal number as St = f · D / U, where f is the shedding frequency, D = 1 m, and U = 1 m/s.  
Visualize the velocity field and vorticity to confirm the formation of a von Kármán vortex street downstream of the cylinder.
