Simulate the steady‑state, compressible flow of natural gas through a horizontal circular pipe that contains a porous baffle using OpenFOAM.

- Use the solver **porousSimpleFoam** for a compressible, steady‑state simulation.
- Model the gas as a perfect gas with molecular weight 16.04 kg kmol⁻¹ and specific heat ratio γ = 1.4. Define these in the **thermophysicalProperties** dictionary.
- Set the kinematic viscosity **nu** in **transportProperties** to 1.5e‑5 m² s⁻¹ (typical value for natural gas at ambient temperature).
- Pipe geometry: length **L = 10 m**, inner diameter **D = 0.20 m**. The pipe wall is a no‑slip, impermeable boundary.
- Inlet (patch “inlet”): specify a total (stagnation) pressure **p₀ = 2 × 10⁵ Pa** and a temperature of 300 K. Use a **totalPressureInletVelocity** condition to impose the total pressure.
- Outlet (patch “outlet”): specify a static pressure **p = 1 × 10⁵ Pa** with a **fixedValue** condition.
- Porous baffle: a cylindrical porous zone of length **0.5 m** centered at **x = 5 m** (mid‑pipe). Define the zone in **fvOptions** using the **porousDarcyForchheimer** model.
  - Darcy coefficient **D = 1.0e5 m⁻¹**.
  - Forchheimer coefficient **F = 1.0e3 m⁻¹**.
  - Apply the porous model to the cellSet that corresponds to the baffle region.
- Mesh: generate a hexahedral mesh with at least 200 cells along the pipe length and at least 30 cells across the diameter, refined near the porous zone to capture pressure drop accurately.
- Set the compressible turbulence model to **k‑epsilon** (or laminar if Reynolds number is low). Use default coefficients unless otherwise required.
- Convergence criteria: residuals for all equations below 1e‑5 and monitor the mass flow rate at inlet and outlet until they differ by less than 0.1 %.
- Post‑processing: extract pressure distribution, Mach number, and mass flow rate. Visualize the pressure drop across the porous baffle.
