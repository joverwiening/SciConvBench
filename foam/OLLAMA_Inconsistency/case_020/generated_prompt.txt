Set up a laminar counter-flow diffusion flame using the reactingFoam solver in a 2-D opposed-jet configuration.  
Define the computational domain with x ranging from 0 to 0.02 m, y from –0.01 to 0.01 m, and z from –0.01 to 0.01 m.  
Create a mesh with 100 cells in the x‑direction, 40 cells in the y‑direction, and a single cell in the z‑direction.  
Assign boundary names: the plane at x = 0 is “fuel”, the plane at x = 0.02 m is “air”, the planes at y = ±0.01 m are “outlet”, and the front and back faces (z = ±0.01 m) are empty.  
At the fuel inlet, specify a pure methane (CH₄) inlet at approximately 293 K with a normal velocity of 0.1 m s⁻¹ directed into the domain.  
At the air inlet, specify an oxidizer mixture of air at approximately 293 K with mass fractions Y_O₂ = 0.23 and the remainder N₂, and a normal velocity of –0.1 m s⁻¹ (directed into the domain).  
Initialize the entire domain at time = 0 s with temperature T = 2000 K and uniform pressure of 101325 Pa.  
Use compressible finite‑rate chemistry with the hePsiThermo thermodynamic model, coefficientWilkeMultiComponentMixture mixing, Sutherland transport properties, and JANAF thermodynamic data.  
Set the fluid viscosity to zero everywhere to represent an inviscid flame.  
Select laminar momentum transport and a laminar combustion model.  
Configure chemistry integration with EulerImplicit type and the Rosenbrock43 ODE solver.  
Run the simulation with a time step of 1 × 10⁻⁴ s, advancing to an end time of 0.5 s, and write results every 0.05 s.
