Model the injection of a high‑pressure, compressible gas into a cubic water tank using the `interFoam` solver.  
Assume the water tank dimensions are 1 m × 1 m × 1 m (width, depth, height).  
Assume the gas inlet is a circular nozzle 10 mm in diameter and 20 mm long, aligned horizontally at the mid‑height of the tank.  
Set the gas inlet Mach number to 0.8.  
Assume the gas stagnation (total) pressure at the inlet is 5 bar (500 kPa).  
Assume the gas stagnation temperature at the inlet is 300 K and use the `perfectGas` equation of state for the gas phase.  
Treat the water phase as incompressible with constant density (1000 kg m⁻³).  
Run the simulation as a transient case, recording flow fields for at least 0.1 s of physical time with a time step that satisfies a Courant number ≤ 0.5.  
Configure the mesh to resolve the nozzle and the near‑field jet region with cell sizes of roughly 0.5 mm, coarsening to 5 mm away from the jet.  
Apply a pressure outlet boundary condition on the tank walls opposite the nozzle and slip walls on the remaining tank surfaces.  
Use appropriate turbulence modeling (e.g., k‑omega SST) for the gas phase.  
Post‑process the results to visualize gas volume fraction, pressure distribution, and jet penetration depth over time.
