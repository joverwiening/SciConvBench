Set up a 3D dam break simulation with three phases: water, air, and light oil.
The domain is a rectangular tank of size 1m x 0.6m x 0.4m.
Initially, a column of water (0.2m x 0.6m x 0.2m) is positioned in one corner.
A layer of oil (0.2m x 0.6m x 0.05m) sits on top of the water column.
The rest of the domain is filled with air.
Use the twoPhaseEulerFoam solver for this transient simulation.
Gravity acts in the -y direction with magnitude 9.81 m/s².
The phases are defined in transportProperties as air, water, and oil.
Air properties: density 1.225 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity 1.8e-5 Pa·s.
Water properties: density 1000 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity 1e-3 Pa·s.
Oil properties: density 800 kg/m³, dynamic viscosity 5e-3 Pa·s.
Use the setFieldsDict utility to initialize the phase fractions alpha.air, alpha.water, and alpha.oil in their respective regions.
All walls have no-slip boundary conditions.
The top boundary is an atmospheric inlet/outlet with zero gradient pressure and appropriate phase fraction conditions.
The water column is located at one corner of the domain starting from x=0, z=0.
The oil layer extends from y=0.2m to y=0.25m above the water column.
Air fills all remaining space in the domain.
