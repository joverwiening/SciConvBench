Model the injection of a high-pressure, compressible gas into a tank of water using the interFoam solver for two-phase flows.
The gas enters at Mach 0.8 with significant compressibility effects.
Define the gas phase using the perfectGas equation of state.
Define the water phase as incompressible.
The simulation is transient to observe gas jet penetration and expansion in water.
Set the gas inlet pressure to 10 bar (1 MPa) to achieve Mach 0.8 injection velocity.
Set the initial water tank pressure to 1 bar (atmospheric pressure).
Use a rectangular water tank with dimensions 1.0 m length × 0.5 m width × 0.8 m height.
Set the gas injection nozzle diameter to 10 mm (0.01 m).
Run the transient simulation for 0.1 seconds total time to capture initial jet penetration and bubble formation dynamics.
Use appropriate mesh refinement near the injection nozzle to resolve high-velocity gradients.
Apply no-slip boundary conditions on tank walls.
Set atmospheric pressure outlet at the top of the tank to allow gas escape.
Initialize the tank as fully filled with water at rest.
Monitor gas volume fraction, pressure fields, and velocity fields throughout the simulation.
