The simulation models a steady-state, buoyancy-driven flow in a vertical pipe.
The buoyantSimpleFoam solver is used.
The pipe has a length of 2 meters and a diameter of 0.1 meters.
The pipe is aligned with the Z-axis.
The fluid is air, treated as a perfect gas.
The gravity vector is set to (0, 0, -9.81).
The k-epsilon turbulence model is used.
The inlet is located at the bottom of the pipe.
The inlet velocity boundary condition is pressureInletOutletVelocity.
The inlet temperature is a fixed value of 300 Kelvin.
The inlet pressure (p_rgh) boundary condition is fixedFluxPressure.
The inlet turbulent kinetic energy (k) is a fixed value of 3.75e-5 m^2/s^2.
The inlet turbulent dissipation rate (epsilon) is a fixed value of 5.7e-5 m^2/s^3.
The outlet is located at the top of the pipe.
The outlet velocity boundary condition is zeroGradient.
The outlet temperature boundary condition is zeroGradient.
The outlet pressure (p_rgh) is a fixed value of 0 Pa.
The outlet turbulent kinetic energy (k) boundary condition is zeroGradient.
The outlet turbulent dissipation rate (epsilon) boundary condition is zeroGradient.
The pipe wall has a no-slip boundary condition for velocity.
The pipe wall is maintained at a constant fixed temperature of 350 Kelvin.
The wall pressure (p_rgh) boundary condition is fixedFluxPressure.
The wall boundary condition for turbulent kinetic energy (k) is kqRWallFunction.
The wall boundary condition for turbulent dissipation rate (epsilon) is epsilonWallFunction.
