The simulation is a steady-state compressible flow analysis through an angled porous duct.
The solver used is porousSimpleFoam with the SIMPLE algorithm.
The fluid is air, modeled as a perfect gas with a molar weight of 28.96 g/mol and a specific heat ratio of 1.4.
The domain is a 45-degree angled rectangular duct.
The duct has a total length of 350mm and a width of 50mm.
The duct consists of a 150mm inlet section, a 100mm porous section, and a 100mm outlet section.
The mesh has 15 cells in the inlet section, 20 cells in the porous section, and 20 cells in the outlet section.
The mesh has 20 cells in the y-direction and 20 cells in the z-direction.
The total grid size is 55x20x20 cells.
The provided blockMeshDict file will be used to generate the mesh.
The simulation runs from time 0 to 1000.
The time step is 1.
Results are written every 100 time steps.
The turbulence model is k-epsilon.
The inlet boundary condition is a uniform velocity of 10 m/s.
The inlet temperature is 300 K.
The inlet turbulence kinetic energy (k) is 0.375 m^2/s^2.
The inlet turbulence dissipation rate (epsilon) is 11 m^2/s^3.
The outlet boundary condition is a static pressure of 101325 Pa.
All wall surfaces have a no-slip velocity condition.
All wall surfaces are adiabatic.
The porous section is defined with a Darcy coefficient (d) of 1e8 1/m^2.
The porous section is defined with a Forchheimer coefficient (f) of 1e6 1/m.
The pressure field (p) is solved using the GAMG solver with a tolerance of 1e-08 and a relative tolerance of 0.05.
Velocity (U) and turbulence (k, epsilon) fields are solved using the smoothSolver with a GaussSeidel smoother.
The tolerance for the velocity solver is 1e-06.
The tolerance for the turbulence solvers is 1e-07.
Relaxation factor for pressure is 0.3.
Relaxation factor for velocity is 0.7.
Relaxation factors for turbulence quantities are 0.9.
Convective terms use the bounded Gauss upwind scheme.
Diffusive terms use the Gauss linear corrected scheme.
