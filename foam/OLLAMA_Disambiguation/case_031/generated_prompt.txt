The simulation domain is a two‑dimensional square cavity 1 m wide (x‑direction) and 0.25 m high (y‑direction).  
The left vertical wall (x = 0) is a fixed temperature hot wall at 310 K.  
The right vertical wall (x = 1 m) is a fixed temperature cold wall at 290 K.  
The top (y = 0.25 m) and bottom (y = 0 m) walls are thermally insulated (zero heat flux) and have a no‑slip velocity condition.  

The fluid is an air‑like incompressible fluid with the following constant properties:  
Density ρ₀ = 1000 kg m⁻³.  
Dynamic viscosity μ = 1 × 10⁻³ Pa·s (corresponding to kinematic viscosity ν = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m² s⁻¹).  
Specific heat capacity cₚ = 1000 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ (chosen to satisfy the Prandtl number requirement).  
Thermal conductivity k = 1.43 × 10⁻⁴ W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, calculated from Pr = ν cₚ/k = 7.0.  

The buoyancy model uses the Boussinesq approximation with reference temperature T₀ = 300 K and thermal expansion coefficient β = 1/T₀ = 3.33 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹.  

The solver is buoyantBoussinesqSimpleFoam for a steady‑state solution.  
Mesh: structured orthogonal grid with at least 100 cells in the x‑direction and 25 cells in the y‑direction (Δx = 0.01 m, Δy = 0.01 m) to resolve the thermal boundary layers.  

Boundary conditions:  
- Hot left wall: fixedValue temperature 310 K, fixedValue velocity (0 0).  
- Cold right wall: fixedValue temperature 290 K, fixedValue velocity (0 0).  
- Top and bottom walls: zeroGradient temperature, fixedValue velocity (0 0).  

Pressure reference point: set pressure to zero at the centre of the cavity (0.5 m, 0.125 m).  

Solution controls:  
- Relaxation factors: 0.3 for pressure, 0.7 for velocity, 0.7 for temperature.  
- Linear solver: smoothSolver for all fields with GAMG preconditioner, tolerance 1e‑6, maxIter 1000.  
- Convergence criteria: residuals for p, U, and T below 1e‑6 and the net heat flux through the hot wall changing by less than 0.1 % between successive iterations.  

Post‑processing: output velocity, pressure, and temperature fields in VTK format every 100 iterations; compute Nusselt number distribution on the hot wall and report the average Nusselt number for the converged solution.
