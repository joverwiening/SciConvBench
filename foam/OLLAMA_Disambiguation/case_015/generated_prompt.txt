The computational domain is a rectangular box spanning x = 0 m to 0.02 m, y = –0.01 m to 0.01 m, and z = –0.01 m to 0.01 m.  
The left boundary at x = 0 is named “fuel” and the right boundary at x = 0.02 m is named “air”.  
The top and bottom boundaries (y = ±0.01 m) are named “outlet”.  
The front and back faces (z = ±0.01 m) are treated as empty (symmetry).  

At the fuel inlet, inject methane (CH₄) with a mass fraction of 0.95, temperature 293 K, and a normal velocity of 0.1 m s⁻¹ directed into the domain.  
At the air inlet, supply oxidizer as air with mass fractions Y_O₂ = 0.23 and Y_N₂ = 0.77, temperature 293 K, and a normal velocity of –0.1 m s⁻¹ directed into the domain.  

Initialize the entire domain at time 0 s with a uniform temperature of 2000 K and zero velocity.  

Run the simulation with the reactingFoam solver for a total physical time of 0.5 s using a constant time step of 1 × 10⁻⁴ s.  

Use compressible finite‑rate chemistry with the following models:  
- Thermodynamic model: hePsiThermo with JANAF thermodynamic data.  
- Transport model: Sutherland transport with coefficientWilkeMultiComponentMixture mixing.  
- Chemistry integration: EulerImplicit type with the Rosenbrock43 ODE solver.  

Apply a laminar combustion model and laminar momentum transport.  

All specified boundary conditions, initial conditions, solver settings, and physical models are to be implemented exactly as described.
