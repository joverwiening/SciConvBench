Use OpenFOAM version 2112 or any later stable release that includes the buoyantPimpleFoam solver and the k‑omega SST turbulence model.  
Set up the case by copying the standard buoyantCavity tutorial directory and retaining the same geometry and mesh.  
Select air as the working fluid and use its standard thermophysical properties (ideal gas, Pr≈0.71, ν≈1.5e‑5 m²/s, β≈1/T).  
Configure the solver to use the k‑omega SST turbulence model by setting “RASModel kOmegaSST” in the turbulenceProperties file.  
Specify a temperature difference of 10 K between the hot and cold vertical walls (e.g., hot wall temperature 310 K, cold wall temperature 300 K) to achieve a Rayleigh number of approximately 1 × 10⁶ for air.  
Apply fixed temperature (Dirichlet) boundary conditions on the hot and cold walls and adiabatic (zeroGradient) conditions on the remaining walls.  
Use the PIMPLE algorithm settings from the original tutorial, adjusting the under‑relaxation factors if necessary for turbulent convergence.  
Run the case with buoyantPimpleFoam, monitor residuals, and ensure that the turbulent kinetic energy and specific dissipation rate fields develop as expected.  
Post‑process the results to visualize temperature, velocity, and turbulence fields, confirming that the flow exhibits turbulent buoyancy‑driven behavior.
