Simulate incompressible flow over a periodic‑hill geometry with the following specifications:

Hill height H = 0.028 m.  
Streamwise wavelength Lₓ = 9 H = 0.252 m (single hill period).  
Spanwise domain width L_z = 4.5 H = 0.126 m.  
Wall‑normal domain height L_y = 3.036 H ≈ 0.085 m (top wall at y = L_y).  

Bulk inlet velocity U_b = 1.0 m s⁻¹, giving Reynolds number based on hill height Re_H = 10 595.  
Kinematic viscosity ν = 2.64 × 10⁻⁶ m² s⁻¹ (computed from U_b, H, and Re_H).  

Boundary conditions: periodic in the streamwise (x) and spanwise (z) directions, no‑slip wall at the hill surface (y = 0), and a symmetry/free‑slip condition at the top wall (y = L_y).  

Sub‑grid‑scale model: Wall‑Adapting Local Eddy‑viscosity (WALE) model (Smagorinsky may be used as an alternative).  

Near‑wall resolution: target y⁺ ≈ 1–2 on the hill surface. With ν and U_b this yields a first cell height Δy₁ ≈ 4 × 10⁻⁶ m (≈ 4 µm).  

Grid resolution:  
- Streamwise direction: 120 cells per hill wavelength (≈ 2.1 mm spacing over Lₓ).  
- Spanwise direction: 80 cells across L_z (≈ 1.6 mm spacing).  
- Wall‑normal direction: geometric stretching from Δy₁ at the wall to the top wall, using ~120 cells total to resolve the boundary layer and outer flow.  

Time stepping: choose Δt to keep the Courant‑Friedrichs‑Lewy (CFL) number ≤ 0.5 based on the maximum velocity and the smallest cell size.  

Numerical scheme: second‑order accurate spatial discretization (central differencing for convective terms), second‑order implicit time integration, and pressure–velocity coupling via a fractional‑step or PISO algorithm.  

Simulation procedure: run the LES until a statistically stationary state is reached, then collect time‑averaged flow statistics for at least ten flow‑through times to ensure convergence of mean and turbulent quantities.
