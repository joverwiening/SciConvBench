Use a perfect‑gas fluid representing air with constant specific heat ratio γ = 1.4 and a constant transport model.  
Set the upstream static pressure to 101 325 Pa and the upstream static temperature to 300 K, giving a Mach 2 supersonic inflow with velocity U = (694.5 0 0) m s⁻¹.  
Model the flow as a two‑dimensional planar inviscid (Euler) simulation.  
Define the computational domain as a rectangle 1.0 m long in the flow direction (x) and 0.5 m high in the normal direction (y).  
Place the wedge tip at (x = 0.2 m, y = 0).  
Define the bottom wall of the wedge as a solid wall coincident with the lower boundary from x = 0.2 m to the outlet (x = 1.0 m).  
Define the upper surface of the wedge as a straight line inclined 10° to the horizontal, described by y = tan(10°)·(x − 0.2) for 0.2 m ≤ x ≤ 1.0 m, giving a wedge height of approximately 0.112 m at the outlet.  
Apply a slip (zero‑normal‑velocity, zero‑shear‑stress) boundary condition at the top far‑field boundary (y = 0.5 m).  
Apply a supersonic‑inflow (fixedValue) boundary condition at the left inlet (x = 0) with U = (694.5 0 0) m s⁻¹, p = 101 325 Pa, and T = 300 K.  
Apply a zeroGradient boundary condition for all flow variables at the right outlet (x = 1.0 m).  
Apply a slip boundary condition at the bottom wedge wall (including the lower wall from x = 0.2 m to the outlet).
