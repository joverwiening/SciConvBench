Model compressible, steady-state, Reynolds‑averaged Navier‑Stokes (RANS) flow of air through a rectangular duct with a porous filter.  
Set inlet boundary condition: supersonic Mach 2 uniform flow, total temperature 300 K, total pressure 101 kPa, turbulence intensity 5 % and turbulent length scale 0.01 m.  
Set outlet boundary condition: static pressure equal to ambient (101 kPa) with subsonic outflow.  
Define duct geometry: height H = 0.10 m, depth W = 0.01 m, total length at least 0.5 m to allow development of shock and recovery.  
Place porous filter from x = 0.20 m to x = 0.25 m (thickness 0.05 m) across the full cross‑section.  
Assume filter porosity (void fraction) = 0.80 and isotropic permeability calculated from empirical correlations for fibrous media (e.g., K ≈ 1 × 10⁻⁸ m²).  
Use the k‑ω SST turbulence model with compressibility corrections.  
Employ a density‑based solver with second‑order upwind spatial discretization and a shock‑capturing scheme (e.g., TVD or MUSCL).  
Generate a structured mesh refined near the filter and expected shock location: at least 200 cells along the flow direction, and at least 50 cells across height and depth, with local refinement to achieve y⁺ ≈ 30 in the near‑wall region.  
Enable energy equation to capture temperature changes across the shock.  
Run until residuals drop below 1 × 10⁻⁶ and monitor mass flow, pressure ratio, and shock position for convergence.  
Post‑process results: pressure, Mach number, temperature fields, shock location, and pressure drop across the porous filter.
