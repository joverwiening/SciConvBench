Create a rectangular computational domain spanning x = 0 m to 0.02 m, y = –0.01 m to 0.01 m, and z = –0.01 m to 0.01 m with a single cell in the z‑direction.  
Generate a mesh with 100 uniform cells in the x‑direction, 40 uniform cells in the y‑direction, and 1 cell in the z‑direction.  
Name the left (x = 0) boundary “fuel”, the right (x = 0.02 m) boundary “air”, the top (y = 0.01 m) and bottom (y = –0.01 m) boundaries “outlet”, and set the front and back faces as empty.  
Apply a uniform inlet velocity of 0.1 m s⁻¹ at the fuel boundary (positive x‑direction) and –0.1 m s⁻¹ at the air boundary (negative x‑direction).  
Set the fuel inlet temperature to 293 K and specify a mass fraction of methane Y_CH4 = 1.0 (pure CH₄) at the fuel inlet.  
Set the air inlet temperature to 293 K with mass fractions Y_O2 = 0.23 and Y_N2 = 0.77.  
Use the reactingFoam solver with compressible finite‑rate chemistry, hePsiThermo thermodynamic model, coefficientWilkeMultiComponentMixture transport model, Sutherland viscosity, and JANAF thermodynamic data.  
Select the chemistry model EulerImplicit and the ODE solver Rosenbrock43.  
Configure both the combustion model and momentum transport as laminar.  
Set the reference pressure to 101 325 Pa (standard atmospheric pressure).  
Initialize all fields to the ambient pressure of 101 325 Pa and temperature of 293 K.  
Run the simulation with a constant time step of 1 × 10⁻⁴ s, a total simulated time of 0.5 s, and write results every 0.05 s.
