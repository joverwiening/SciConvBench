Simulate turbulent channel flow in a rectangular domain 1 m long (x‑direction), 0.1 m high (y‑direction), and 0.2 m wide (z‑direction).  
Use an incompressible fluid with density ρ = 1 kg m⁻³ and kinematic viscosity ν = 1 × 10⁻⁵ m² s⁻¹.  
Drive the flow with a constant pressure gradient of –500 Pa m⁻¹ applied in the streamwise (x) direction, which yields a bulk Reynolds number of approximately 10 000 and ensures fully turbulent conditions.  
Apply no‑slip velocity boundary conditions on the top and bottom walls (y = 0 and y = 0.1 m).  
Apply periodic boundary conditions in the spanwise (z) direction.  
Specify a uniform inlet velocity profile consistent with the imposed pressure gradient and a zero‑gradient (Neumann) condition for all variables at the outlet.  
Employ the standard k‑ε turbulence model with default wall functions.  
Generate a structured hexahedral mesh with at least 40 cells across the channel height, refined near the walls to maintain y⁺ ≈ 30, and at least 100 cells in the streamwise direction and 20 cells in the spanwise direction.  
Run a steady‑state simulation using the SIMPLE algorithm for pressure‑velocity coupling.  
Use second‑order upwind discretization for momentum, turbulence kinetic energy (k), and turbulence dissipation rate (ε).  
Set convergence criteria of 1 × 10⁻⁶ for residuals of continuity, momentum, k, and ε, and monitor the bulk velocity at the channel centerline until it changes by less than 0.1 % between iterations.  
Post‑process to obtain the mean velocity profile, turbulent kinetic energy distribution, and wall shear stress along the channel height.
