Ignite the mixture instantaneously at one closed end of the tube.  
Assume adiabatic, no‑slip wall conditions and no heat loss to the surroundings.  
Use a detailed hydrogen‑air chemical kinetic mechanism (e.g., the Li et al. mechanism) to model combustion.  
Discretize the tube axially with a uniform grid spacing fine enough to resolve the flame front (e.g., ≤1 mm).  
Employ an appropriate compressible flow solver with second‑order accuracy in space and time, using a time step that satisfies the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition for stability.  
Record the flame front position, local temperature, pressure, and species concentrations as functions of time until the flame reaches the opposite end and the system reaches a quasi‑steady post‑combustion state.
