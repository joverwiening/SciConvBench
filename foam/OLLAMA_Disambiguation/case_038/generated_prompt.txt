Simulate natural convection of air in a rectangular porous block that is 0.10 m wide (x‑direction), 0.10 m high (y‑direction), and 0.01 m deep (z‑direction).  
Set the bottom wall (y = 0) temperature to 310 K (hotWall) and the top wall (y = 0.10 m) temperature to 290 K (coldWall).  
Treat all side walls (x = 0, x = 0.10 m, z = 0, z = 0.01 m) as adiabatic by applying a zeroGradient condition for temperature and a no‑slip condition for velocity.  
Apply no‑slip velocity boundary conditions on the bottom and top walls as well.  
Use a porous zone that fills the entire block volume. Represent the porous resistance with a Darcy‑Forchheimer model:  
- Darcy (linear) resistance coefficient d = (1e7 1e7 1e7) m⁻¹ in the x, y, and z directions.  
- Forchheimer (quadratic) coefficient f = (0 0 0).  
Assume a porosity of 0.40 for the porous medium.  
Model the fluid as air with constant properties evaluated at the reference temperature 300 K:  
- Density ρ = 1.177 kg m⁻³  
- Dynamic viscosity μ = 1.85 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s  
- Thermal conductivity k = 0.026 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹  
- Specific heat at constant pressure cp = 1005 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹  
Include buoyancy using the Boussinesq approximation with gravity vector g = (0 ‑9.81 0) m s⁻².  
Run a steady‑state simulation using the buoyantSimpleFoam solver (or an equivalent steady natural‑convection solver).  
Set pressure reference (zeroGradient) on all walls and fix the pressure at a single internal cell to zero to close the pressure field.  
Use a structured mesh with at least 20 cells in each spatial direction (≥20 × 20 × 20 cells) to resolve the thermal boundary layers and porous resistance.  
Converge the solution until residuals for continuity, momentum, energy, and turbulence (if any) fall below 1 × 10⁻⁶ and the volume‑averaged temperature and velocity fields change by less than 0.1 % between successive iterations.
