Set up a counter‑flow diffusion flame simulation using OpenFOAM’s reactingFoam solver.  
Create a cylindrical domain with a vertical height of 0.02 m and a horizontal width (diameter) of 0.04 m.  
Place a circular fuel inlet nozzle at the top of the domain and an identical circular air inlet nozzle at the bottom; both nozzles have a diameter of 0.03 m (radius 0.015 m).  
Define the fuel inlet patch as “fuelInlet” with a uniform velocity vector U = (0 ‑0.5 0) m s⁻¹ (downward) and a mass‑fraction field Y.CH₄ = 1.0 (pure methane).  
Define the oxidizer inlet patch as “oxidizerInlet” with a uniform velocity vector U = (0 0.5 0) m s⁻¹ (upward) and mass‑fraction fields Y.O₂ = 0.233 and Y.N₂ = 0.767.  
Apply no‑slip wall boundary conditions on the cylindrical side walls.  
Use the GRI‑Mech 3.0 detailed chemical reaction mechanism for methane combustion.  
Configure reactingFoam to solve the compressible reacting flow, enable species transport, energy equation, and appropriate turbulence model (e.g., k‑ε or LES) as needed.  
Generate a structured or hexahedral mesh that resolves the nozzle diameters and the 0.02 m gap with sufficient refinement near the inlets to capture flame structure.  
Set initial conditions of ambient temperature (≈300 K) and pressure (1 atm) throughout the domain.  
Run the simulation until a steady‑state flame structure is achieved, monitoring temperature, species concentrations, and flame position.
