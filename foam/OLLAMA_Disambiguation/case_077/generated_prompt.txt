Simulate a two‑dimensional bubble column using an Euler‑Euler multiphase approach.  
The water column is 1.0 m tall with a square cross‑section of 0.2 m × 0.2 m (cross‑sectional area = 0.04 m²).  
Air is injected through the entire bottom face with a superficial gas velocity of 0.2 m s⁻¹, giving a volumetric gas flow rate of 0.008 m³ s⁻¹.  

Geometry and Mesh  
- Create a 2‑D planar domain 1.0 m (height) by 0.2 m (width).  
- Generate a structured mesh with a uniform cell size of 0.005 m (≈200 cells vertically and 40 cells horizontally) to resolve bubble dynamics while keeping the cell‑based Courant number below 0.5.  

Physical Models  
- Multiphase model: Euler‑Euler two‑fluid model.  
- Turbulence model: k‑ε realizable (standard for gas‑liquid columns).  
- Interphase momentum exchange: Schiller‑Naumann drag law.  
- Phase properties (ambient temperature, 20 °C):  
  * Water: density = 998 kg m⁻³, dynamic viscosity = 1.0 × 10⁻³ Pa s.  
  * Air: density = 1.204 kg m⁻³, dynamic viscosity = 1.81 × 10⁻⁵ Pa s.  
- Surface tension between water and air: 0.072 N m⁻¹.  

Initial Conditions  
- Liquid phase (water) fills the entire domain, velocity = 0 m s⁻¹, pressure = 101325 Pa.  
- Gas phase (air) volume fraction = 0 everywhere initially.  

Boundary Conditions  
- Bottom inlet (entire width): fixed superficial gas velocity of 0.2 m s⁻¹, zero liquid velocity, fixed gas volume fraction corresponding to the inlet flow rate (0.008 m³ s⁻¹).  
- Side walls: no‑slip for both phases, zero gradient for pressure.  
- Top outlet: pressure outlet at 101325 Pa, zero gradient for velocity and volume fraction.  

Numerical Settings  
- Solver: transient pressure‑velocity coupling (PISO).  
- Time stepping: initial Δt = 1 × 10⁻³ s, `adjustTimeStep yes`, `maxCo 0.5`.  
- Total physical simulation time: 20 s (endTime = 20).  
- Discretization: second‑order upwind for momentum and volume fraction, central differencing for diffusion terms.  

Output  
- Write fields (pressure, velocity of both phases, gas volume fraction) every 0.1 s.  
- Enable probe points at mid‑height and near the inlet to monitor gas hold‑up and superficial velocities.  

Post‑Processing  
- Generate time‑resolved plots of gas volume fraction distribution, bubble plume development, and overall gas hold‑up.  
- Compute average gas holdup and compare with empirical correlations for validation.
