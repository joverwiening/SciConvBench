Set up a laminar counter-flow diffusion flame using the reactingFoam solver in a 2‑D opposed‑jet configuration.  
Define a rectangular computational domain with x ranging from 0 m to 0.02 m, y ranging from –0.01 m to 0.01 m, and z ranging from –0.01 m to 0.01 m.  
Create a mesh with 100 cells in the x‑direction, 40 cells in the y‑direction, and a single cell in the z‑direction.  
Assign boundary names: the plane at x = 0 m is “fuel”, the plane at x = 0.02 m is “air”, the top (y = 0.01 m) and bottom (y = –0.01 m) planes are “outlet”, and the front and back faces (z = ±0.01 m) are empty.  
At the fuel inlet, specify a methane mass fraction Y_CH4 = 0.055, temperature 293 K, and a normal velocity of 0.1 m s⁻¹ directed into the domain.  
At the air inlet, specify an oxidizer mixture with mass fraction Y_O2 = 0.23, the remainder being N₂, temperature 293 K, and a normal velocity of –0.1 m s⁻¹ (directed into the domain).  
Initialize the entire domain at time = 0 s with a uniform temperature of 2000 K and zero velocity.  
Use compressible finite‑rate chemistry with the hePsiThermo thermodynamic model, coefficientWilkeMultiComponentMixture for species diffusion, Sutherland transport properties, and JANAF thermodynamic data.  
Select the EulerImplicit chemistry integration scheme with the Rosenbrock43 ODE solver.  
Configure the combustion model and momentum transport as laminar.  
Run the simulation with the reactingFoam solver.
