Use a rectangular domain with length 10 m, height 2 m, and width 2 m to represent the horizontal gravity separator.  
Apply a structured mesh with a base resolution of 0.05 m in all directions, resulting in 200×40×40 cells.  
Use mesh grading with a expansion ratio of 2 in the vertical (y) direction to refine the mesh near the bottom wall for better resolution of water and oil layer interactions.  
The mesh is generated using blockMesh with six vertices defining the rectangular prism.  
The inlet is located at the left face (x = 0) and the outlet at the right face (x = 10).  
The top, bottom, front, and back faces are set as walls.  
The initial stratified mixture consists of gas in the upper third, oil in the middle third, and water in the lower third of the domain.  
The `setFieldsDict` defines three box-shaped regions to initialize the volume fractions:  
  - Water: from (0 0 0) to (10 0.667 2)  
  - Oil: from (0 0.667 0) to (10 1.333 2)  
  - Gas: from (0 1.333 0) to (10 2 2)  
The `transportProperties` dictionary defines the three phases:  
  - Water: density = 1000 kg/m³, kinematic viscosity = 1e-6 m²/s  
  - Oil: density = 800 kg/m³, kinematic viscosity = 5e-5 m²/s  
  - Gas: density = 1.2 kg/m³, kinematic viscosity = 1.5e-5 m²/s  
The `interFoam` solver is specified in `controlDict` with a gravitational acceleration vector of (0 -9.81 0) m/s².  
The simulation runs for 100 seconds with a maximum Courant number of 0.5.  
Time step is adaptive based on the Courant number.  
The initial time step is 0.001 s.  
Volume fraction fields are initialized: `alpha.water` for water, `alpha.oil` as a secondary phase, and gas is the background phase.  
Phase separation is driven by gravity with no inlet or outlet flow (closed system).  
Boundary conditions:  
  - Walls: no-slip for velocity, zero gradient for pressure, and fixed value of 0 for all volume fractions (except at inlet/outlet)  
  - Inlet: velocity specified as zero, zero gradient for pressure, and fixed volume fractions matching initial layers  
  - Outlet: zero gradient for velocity, fixed pressure at 0, and zero gradient for volume fractions  
Pressure-velocity coupling is handled via PIMPLE algorithm with 2 correctors.  
Turbulence is modeled as laminar for all phases.  
Data is written every 1 second for post-processing of phase interfaces.  
The VOF method is used with interface compression via the `cAlpha` coefficient set to 1.  
Surface tension is neglected.  
The simulation assumes incompressible, isothermal flow.
