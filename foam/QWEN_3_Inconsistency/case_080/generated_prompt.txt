Set up a cylindrical column with a diameter of 0.1 m and a height of 1 m.
Define three phases: air (gas), water (liquid), and sand (solid particles) in the phaseProperties file as (air water sand).
Treat air as a perfect gas, water as an incompressible liquid, and sand as a constant density solid with a particle diameter of 500 microns.
Use the twoPhaseEulerFoam solver for the simulation.
Set gravity to act in the negative y-direction with a value of (0 -9.81 0).
Configure the simulation to be transient with a total runtime of 10 seconds.
Initialize the domain with a bed of sand at the bottom 0.2 m of the column, where the volume fraction of sand (alpha.sand) is 0.6 and the volume fraction of water (alpha.water) is 0.4.
Set the initial volume fraction of air (alpha.air) to 0 throughout the domain.
Fill the region above the sand bed with water, setting alpha.water to 1 in that region.
Apply an air inlet boundary condition at the bottom of the column with a velocity of 0.5 m/s.
Use the Gidaspow drag model for interphase drag interactions between all phase pairs.
Apply appropriate boundary conditions for pressure and phase fractions at the top outlet to allow phase escape.
Ensure numerical stability by selecting suitable time step sizes and convergence criteria.
