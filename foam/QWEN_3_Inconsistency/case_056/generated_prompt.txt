Use the following standard physical properties for each phase:

Density of air: 1.225 kg/m³  
Density of water: 998 kg/m³  
Density of oil: 850 kg/m³  
Dynamic viscosity of air: 1.78e-5 Pa·s  
Dynamic viscosity of water: 1.0e-3 Pa·s  
Dynamic viscosity of oil: 3.0e-3 Pa·s  

  
Set up a 3D dam break simulation with three phases: water, air, and a light oil.  
The domain is a rectangular tank of size 1m x 0.6m x 0.4m.  
Initially, a column of water (0.2m x 0.6m x 0.2m) is located in one corner of the tank.  
A layer of oil (0.2m x 0.6m x 0.05m) is positioned directly on top of the water column.  
The remaining volume of the tank is filled with air.  
Use the `twoPhaseEulerFoam` solver for this transient simulation.  
Gravity acts in the negative y-direction with a magnitude of 9.81 m/s².  
The phases are defined in `transportProperties` as `air`, `water`, and `oil`.  
The density of air is 1.225 kg/m³.  
The density of water is 998 kg/m³.  
The density of oil is 850 kg/m³.  
The dynamic viscosity of air is 1.78e-5 Pa·s.  
The dynamic viscosity of water is 1.0e-3 Pa·s.  
The dynamic viscosity of oil is 3.0e-3 Pa·s.  
Use the `setFieldsDict` utility to initialize the phase fractions.  
In the water column region, set alpha.water = 1, alpha.oil = 0, alpha.air = 0.  
In the oil layer region, set alpha.oil = 1, alpha.water = 0, alpha.air = 0.  
In the remaining domain, set alpha.air = 1, alpha.water = 0, alpha.oil = 0.  
All walls have no-slip boundary conditions.  
The top boundary is an atmospheric inlet/outlet, allowing air to escape or enter.  
The simulation is transient and should capture the evolution of the multiphase flow over time.
