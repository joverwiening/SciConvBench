The simulation models the transient injection of a high-pressure, compressible gas into a tank of water using the interFoam solver.

The gas phase is defined using the perfectGas equation of state with a specific heat ratio of 1.4 and a molecular weight of 28.97 g/mol, representing air.

The water phase is modeled as incompressible with a constant density of 1000 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity of 1e-3 Pa·s.

The gas enters the domain at Mach 0.8 through a circular inlet with a diameter of 5 mm.

The absolute pressure at the injection inlet is assumed to be 5 atm (506625 Pa), which provides sufficient pressure ratio to drive the high-velocity jet while remaining physically reasonable for industrial gas injection applications.

The temperature at the injection inlet is assumed to be 300 K (27°C), representing ambient conditions.

The tank dimensions are 0.5 m in length, 0.3 m in width, and 0.3 m in height, with the water initially filling the tank to a depth of 0.2 m.

The gas inlet is positioned at the center of the tank bottom, oriented vertically upward.

The simulation uses a maximum Courant number of 0.5 with adaptive time stepping.

The mesh employs local refinement near the inlet and gas-water interface with a base cell size of 5 mm and minimum cell size of 0.625 mm.

Turbulence is modeled using the k-omega SST model with compressibility corrections enabled for the gas phase.

Surface tension between gas and water is set to 0.072 N/m.

Gravity acts in the negative z-direction at 9.81 m/s².

The simulation runs for a total physical time of 0.1 seconds with data output every 0.001 seconds.

The primary output quantities include gas volume fraction distribution, velocity magnitude, pressure field, and gas jet penetration depth over time.
