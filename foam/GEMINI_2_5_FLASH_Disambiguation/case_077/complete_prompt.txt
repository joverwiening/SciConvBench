Simulate an air-water bubble column using an Euler-Euler approach (dispersed gas in continuous liquid).

Solver: twoPhaseEulerFoam

Geometry: 3D rectangular column, 0.2 m x 1.0 m x 0.2 m.
Mesh: blockMesh 40 x 200 x 40.

Phases and models (constant/phaseProperties etc):
- phase1: water (continuous)
- phase2: air (dispersed)
Use standard closures: drag SchillerNaumann; turbulence in liquid (RAS kEpsilon or mixture turbulence); bubble diameter model (constant d=2 mm) unless otherwise specified.

Gravity: g (0 -9.81 0)

Initial conditions:
Euler-Euler does not represent a sharp free surface. Initialize the entire column as mostly liquid with a small gas holdup everywhere:
- alpha.air = 1e-6 in the whole domain
- alpha.water = 1 - alpha.air
- U.water = (0 0 0); U.air = (0 0 0)
- p_rgh = 0; T (if solved) uniform

Boundary conditions:
- gasInlet patch (small area on the bottom): inject gas upward
  - U.air fixedValue (0 0.2 0) (upwards)
  - alpha.air fixedValue 0.05 (or specified gas volume fraction at the sparger)
  - U.water fixedValue (0 0 0) (or pressureInletOutletVelocity)
- bottom wall outside sparger: noSlip for U.water; slip or zeroGradient for U.air; alpha gradients zero
- top outlet: open to atmosphere
  - p_rgh totalPressure p0 0
  - U.water and U.air pressureInletOutletVelocity
  - alpha.air inletOutlet (inletValue 0; value 0)
- side walls: noSlip for U.water; slip for U.air; alpha zeroGradient

Time controls: endTime 20; deltaT 1e-3; adjustTimeStep yes; maxCo 0.5; writeInterval 0.2.
