Simulate a turbulent thermal plume rising from a hot strip on the floor of a large, open room.

The hot strip has a temperature of 400 K.

The ambient temperature of the room is 300 K.

The hot strip has a width of 0.2 m located at x in [0.9, 1.1] m and a length of 0.01 m representing the thickness of the 2D domain in z.

The room has a height of 2.0 m in the y-direction.

The room has a width of 2.0 m in the x-direction.

Use the Sutherland viscosity model for air with Cp = 1005 J/(kg·K) and molecular weight = 28.9 g/mol.

Set the reference viscosity muRef = 1.716e-5 Pa·s at reference temperature Tref = 273.15 K.

Use a Prandtl number of 0.71 for air.

Use a Sutherland constant S = 110.4 K for air.

The simulation must resolve turbulent flow structures using appropriate turbulence modeling or direct numerical simulation with sufficient grid resolution.

Apply no-slip boundary conditions on all walls.

Apply fixed temperature boundary condition of 400 K on the hot strip surface.

Apply fixed temperature boundary condition of 300 K on all other wall surfaces.

Use a buoyancy-driven flow solver with the Boussinesq approximation or fully compressible formulation.

Initialize the domain with quiescent flow at 300 K.

Run the simulation until statistically steady state is achieved for time-averaged quantities.
