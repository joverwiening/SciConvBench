Simulate transient two-phase flow through a porous dam using the interFoam solver with Darcy-Forchheimer porous media treatment.

The computational domain spans 5 m (x) × 2 m (y) × 0.1 m (z), representing a 2D slice with finite thickness.

The porous dam occupies a rectangular block from x = 2 m to x = 3 m (1 m thickness), spanning the full height of 2 m and full z-thickness of 0.1 m.

The Darcy-Forchheimer coefficients for the porous region are d = (1e6 1e6 0) m⁻² for viscous resistance and f = (100 100 0) m⁻¹ for inertial resistance.

The porosity is assumed to be 0.4, representing a typical value for granular porous materials.

The initial condition consists of a water column at height 1.5 m in the region x = 0 to x = 1 m, with the remainder of the domain initially dry (air).

The water is released instantaneously at t = 0, and the transient evolution is computed for 10 seconds total simulation time.

The initial time step is 0.001 s with adjustable time step enabled and a maximum Courant number limit of 0.5.

The fluid pair is water and air with the following properties: water density 1000 kg/m³ and kinematic viscosity 1×10⁻⁶ m²/s; air density 1 kg/m³ and kinematic viscosity 1.48×10⁻⁵ m²/s.

The surface tension between water and air is 0.07 N/m.

The contact angle for water on the porous dam material is assumed to be 45 degrees, representing a hydrophilic wetting condition typical for natural granular materials like sand or soil.
