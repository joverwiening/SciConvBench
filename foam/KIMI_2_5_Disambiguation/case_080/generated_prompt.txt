The simulation models a fluidized bed reactor using an Euler-Euler two-fluid approach with the following specifications:

The reactor geometry is a rectangular domain with dimensions 0.1 m in width, 0.1 m in depth, and 0.5 m in height.

The gas phase enters through the bottom boundary with a uniform superficial velocity of 0.2 m/s directed vertically upward.

The gas phase has a density of 1.2 kg/m^3 and a kinematic viscosity of 1.5e-5 m^2/s.

The solid phase consists of spherical particles with a density of 2500 kg/m^3 and a diameter of 200 microns.

The initial condition consists of a packed bed of solid particles occupying the bottom 0.2 m of the reactor height with a solid volume fraction of 0.55, corresponding to a void fraction of 0.45.

The kinetic theory of granular flow closure is applied with a particle-particle restitution coefficient of 0.9.

The particle-wall restitution coefficient is set to 0.9 for all wall boundaries.

The top boundary is specified as a pressure outlet with atmospheric pressure.

The side and bottom walls are modeled as no-slip for the gas phase and partial slip for the solid phase using appropriate wall boundary conditions for granular flow.

The simulation tracks the transient expansion of the bed and the formation of bubbles as the gas velocity exceeds the minimum fluidization velocity.
