The simulation is an incompressible lid driven cavity flow using the pisoFoam solver.

The computational domain uses a grid of 20 cells in the x-direction, 20 cells in the y-direction, and 1 cell in the z-direction to enforce 2D flow characteristics.

The top wall is named 'movingWall' and moves in the x-direction with a uniform velocity of 1 m/s.

The remaining walls are named 'fixedWalls' and have a no-slip boundary condition with velocity equal to zero at the wall.

The front and back faces are designated as 'empty' boundary type to indicate 2D simulation.

The simulation runs from time 0 to 10 seconds with a fixed time step of 0.005 seconds.

Results are written to disk every 100 time steps, corresponding to every 0.5 seconds of simulation time.

The kinematic viscosity nu is set as constant with a value of 1e-05 m^2/s.

The turbulence model is k-epsilon with the following initial and boundary conditions:

The turbulent kinetic energy k is initialized uniformly to 1.5e-4 m^2/s^2 throughout the domain.

The k field uses kqRWallFunction on all walls, with front and back faces left empty.

The turbulent dissipation rate epsilon is initialized uniformly to 1.4e-4 m^2/s^3 throughout the domain.

The epsilon field uses epsilonWallFunction on all walls, with front and back faces left empty.

The turbulent viscosity nut is initialized uniformly to 0 m^2/s throughout the domain.

The nut field uses nutkWallFunction on all walls, with front and back faces left empty.

The velocity field U is initialized to zero uniformly throughout the domain at time zero.

The pressure field p uses zeroGradient boundary condition on all walls.

The divergence schemes are specified as follows:

The momentum convection term div(phi,U) uses Gauss limitedLinearV with a coefficient of 1.

The turbulent kinetic energy convection term div(phi,k) uses Gauss limitedLinear with a coefficient of 1.

The turbulent dissipation convection term div(phi,epsilon) uses Gauss limitedLinear with a coefficient of 1.

The viscous term div((nuEff*dev2(T(grad(U))))) uses Gauss linear.

The gradient scheme for velocity uses Gauss linear.

The Laplacian schemes use Gauss linear corrected.

The interpolation scheme for velocity uses linear.
