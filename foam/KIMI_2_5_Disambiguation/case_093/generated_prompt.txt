Simulate a turbulent hot plume rising from a small heat source on the floor of a large, open room.

Use a 2D computational domain with dimensions 2m width by 4m height.

Place the heat source as a central 0.2m section on the floor.

Set the heat output of the source to 500 watts, representing a typical small heat source such as a hot plate or small burner.

Set the ambient air temperature to 27°C (300K).

Run the simulation for a total duration of 100 seconds.

Use a turbulence model appropriate for buoyancy-driven flows, such as the k-epsilon model with buoyancy modifications or Large Eddy Simulation (LES) if computational resources permit.

Apply no-slip boundary conditions on all walls.

Apply zero-gradient pressure boundary conditions at the open top boundary to allow entrainment and outflow.

Initialize the entire domain with quiescent air at the ambient temperature.

Output velocity, temperature, and turbulence kinetic energy fields at regular intervals for visualization and analysis.
