Simulate buoyancy-driven heat transfer in a 2D channel using the buoyantPimpleFoam solver.
The geometry is a 2D channel (one cell thick in the z-direction with empty boundary conditions on the front and back faces).
Gravity is set to (0, -9.81, 0) m/s² in the constant/g file, acting in the negative y-direction.
The working fluid is air, initialized at a uniform temperature of 300 K throughout the domain.
Initialize pressure and velocity to zero (or ambient) throughout the domain.
In 0/T, set the bottomWall boundary condition to type fixedValue with a uniform value of 400 K.
In 0/T, set the topWall boundary condition to type zeroGradient (adiabatic, zero heat flux).
In 0/T, set the left and right walls (or inlet/outlet, depending on channel orientation) to appropriate boundary conditions consistent with a closed or periodic channel as required by the geometry.
Use thermophysical properties for air defined in constant/thermophysicalProperties, including appropriate values for Cp, thermal conductivity, and dynamic viscosity.
Use the Boussinesq approximation or the compressible formulation native to buoyantPimpleFoam to couple temperature and buoyancy-driven flow.
Set up fvSolution and fvSchemes files appropriate for buoyantPimpleFoam, using PIMPLE algorithm settings with at least 2 outer corrector loops.
Run the simulation until a statistically steady or fully developed natural convection state is reached.
Monitor temperature and velocity fields to confirm buoyancy-driven circulation has developed between the heated bottom wall and the adiabatic top wall.
