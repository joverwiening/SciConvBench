Simulate a single spherical particle settling in a quiescent fluid column using the DPMFoam solver in OpenFOAM.
The domain is a vertical cylinder with a height of 1 meter along the Y-axis and a diameter of 0.2 meters.
The cylinder axis is aligned with the Y-axis, centered at x=0, z=0.
The fluid is liquid water with a kinematic viscosity of 1e-6 m^2/s and a density of 1000 kg/m^3.
A single spherical particle is introduced at position (0, 0.9, 0) at the start of the simulation.
The particle has a diameter of 1mm (0.001 m) and a density of 2500 kg/m^3.
The particle is initialized with zero velocity.
The simulation uses the DPMFoam solver to track the particle's Lagrangian trajectory as it settles under gravity.
In the constant/g dictionary, specify gravity as `g (0 -9.81 0)` so that gravity acts in the negative Y direction.
The fluid velocity field is initialized to zero everywhere (quiescent fluid).
The pressure field is initialized to zero (or hydrostatic if required by the solver).
The cylindrical side wall patch uses a noSlip boundary condition for fluid velocity.
The top patch (at Y = 1m) uses a wall boundary condition with noSlip for fluid velocity.
The bottom patch (at Y = 0m) uses a wall boundary condition with noSlip for fluid velocity.
All wall patches use a zeroGradient boundary condition for pressure.
The particle boundary condition at all walls is set to rebound or stick as appropriate for a settling simulation; use rebound (reflect) unless the particle reaches the bottom wall, where it should stop (escape or stick).
Use the sphereDrag model for particle drag force calculation.
Enable gravity force on the particle using the sphereNonUniformDrag or standard gravity model available in DPMFoam.
The simulation runs until the particle reaches the bottom of the cylinder or for a physical time sufficient to observe complete settling (at minimum 5 seconds).
Write particle position, velocity, and fluid field data at regular time intervals of 0.01 seconds.
The mesh should be a structured or blockMesh-generated cylindrical mesh with sufficient resolution to capture the flow around the particle (recommend at least 20 cells across the diameter).
Use a time step small enough to satisfy Courant number stability, with a maximum Courant number of 0.5.
