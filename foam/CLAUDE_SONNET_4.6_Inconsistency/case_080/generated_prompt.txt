The solver is twoPhaseEulerFoam.
The simulation is three-phase involving air (gas), water (liquid), and sand (solid particles).
The phases are listed in phaseProperties as (air water sand).
The computational domain is a vertical cylindrical column with a diameter of 0.1 m and a height of 1 m.
Gravity is set to (0 -9.81 0) in the constant/g file.
The simulation is transient and runs for a total of 10 seconds.
Use a time step of 0.001 s with adjustable time stepping enabled, keeping the maximum Courant number at 0.5.
Write output every 0.1 seconds.

Air is modeled as a perfect gas using the perfectGas equation of state.
Water is modeled as an incompressible liquid using the Boussinesq or constant incompressible equation of state with a density of 1000 kg/m3 and dynamic viscosity of 1e-3 Pa·s.
Sand is modeled as a constant-density solid with a density of 2500 kg/m3 and a particle diameter of 500 microns (500e-6 m).

The bottom 0.2 m of the column is initialized as a settled sand bed with alpha.sand = 0.6 and alpha.water = 0.4, and alpha.air = 0.
The remaining upper 0.8 m of the column is initialized with alpha.water = 1.0, alpha.sand = 0, and alpha.air = 0.
Initial velocity for all phases is (0 0 0) everywhere except at the inlet.

The bottom face of the column is the inlet boundary.
Air is injected at the inlet with a uniform velocity of 0.5 m/s in the positive y-direction (0 0.5 0).
Water and sand have a zeroGradient or noSlip condition at the inlet.
The top face of the column is the outlet boundary using a pressureInletOutletVelocity condition for velocity and a totalPressure or fixedValue condition for pressure set to atmospheric (101325 Pa).
The cylindrical wall uses a noSlip condition for all phases and a zeroGradient condition for pressure.

Use the Gidaspow drag model for air-sand, air-water, and water-sand phase pair interactions.
Use the Schiller-Naumann or Gidaspow drag model for the gas-liquid pair as appropriate.
Define all phase pairs in the phaseProperties file under the drag submodel section.

For turbulence, apply the dispersed k-epsilon turbulence model to the continuous liquid phase (water) as the primary continuous phase, which is standard best practice for fluidized bed simulations.
Use laminar turbulence modeling for the sand solid phase.
Use laminar or dispersed treatment for the air phase.

Use the PIMPLE algorithm for pressure-velocity coupling.
Set nOuterCorrectors to 3, nCorrectors to 2, and nNonOrthogonalCorrectors to 1.

Generate a structured or blockMesh-based cylindrical mesh using blockMesh or snappyHexMesh.
Use a mesh with approximately 10 cells across the radius and 100 cells along the height, giving a total of roughly 10000 to 20000 cells.

All relevant OpenFOAM case directories must be created: 0/, constant/, and system/.
The 0/ directory must contain initial condition files for alpha.air, alpha.water, alpha.sand, U.air, U.water, U.sand, p_rgh, and turbulence fields (k, epsilon) for the water phase.
The constant/ directory must contain phaseProperties, turbulenceProperties for each phase, thermophysicalProperties for air, and g.
The system/ directory must contain controlDict, fvSchemes, fvSolution, and setFieldsDict.
Use setFields to initialize the sand bed region at the bottom 0.2 m of the column.
