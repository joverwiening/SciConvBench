Simulate natural convection in a 2D square cavity (the de Vahl Davis benchmark case) using OpenFOAM.
Use the solver buoyantBoussinesqPimpleFoam.
The cavity is a 2D square domain filled with air.
The left wall is the hot wall with a fixed temperature of 300 K.
The right wall is the cold wall with a fixed temperature of 290 K.
The top and bottom walls are adiabatic; apply a zeroGradient boundary condition for temperature on these walls (overriding the user's fixedValue=295 request, as adiabatic walls require zero heat flux, not a fixed temperature).
Gravity is defined as g = (0, -9.81, 0) m/s², acting in the -y direction.
Use the Boussinesq approximation for buoyancy.
The reference temperature for the Boussinesq approximation is 295 K (the mean of hot and cold wall temperatures).
All four walls use a noSlip boundary condition for velocity.
The front and back faces (empty direction in 2D) use the empty boundary condition for all fields.
Set up the thermophysical properties for air with appropriate density, specific heat, thermal conductivity, and kinematic viscosity for the temperature range 290–300 K.
Use a structured, uniform mesh for the square cavity.
Initialize the velocity field to zero and the temperature field to 295 K throughout the domain.
Configure PIMPLE loop settings in fvSolution with appropriate tolerances for p, U, and T.
Set the time step and end time in controlDict to allow the simulation to reach a statistically steady natural convection state.
Output the fields U, p, T, and p_rgh at regular write intervals.
