The simulation uses simpleFoam to model steady-state, incompressible, turbulent swirling flow in a cyclone separator.

The geometry has four boundary patches: inlet (tangential entry), outlet (top vortex finder), dustOutlet (bottom), and walls (all solid surfaces).

The turbulence model is k-epsilon, using standard wall functions.

The fluid is air at room temperature with a kinematic viscosity of 1.5e-05 m²/s and a density of 1.225 kg/m³.

The inlet velocity is assumed to be 15 m/s, applied as a fixedValue condition on the U field at the inlet patch, with the direction tangential to the cyclone body as defined by the mesh geometry.

The pressure field p boundary conditions are as follows.
inlet: zeroGradient.
outlet: fixedValue uniform 0.
dustOutlet: zeroGradient.
walls: zeroGradient.

The velocity field U boundary conditions are as follows.
inlet: fixedValue with a magnitude of 15 m/s directed tangentially (vector components depend on mesh orientation).
outlet: pressureInletOutletVelocity with inletValue uniform (0 0 0).
dustOutlet: pressureInletOutletVelocity with inletValue uniform (0 0 0).
walls: noSlip.

The turbulent kinetic energy k boundary conditions are as follows.
inlet: fixedValue uniform 0.0338, calculated assuming 5% turbulence intensity (k = 1.5 * (U * I)^2 = 1.5 * (15 * 0.05)^2).
outlet: zeroGradient.
dustOutlet: zeroGradient.
walls: kqRWallFunction with the same value as the inlet (0.0338).

The turbulent dissipation rate epsilon boundary conditions are as follows.
inlet: fixedValue uniform 0.0648, calculated assuming a turbulent length scale of 0.007 m (approximately 7% of a typical inlet hydraulic diameter of 0.1 m), using epsilon = Cmu^0.75 * k^1.5 / l, where Cmu = 0.09.
outlet: zeroGradient.
dustOutlet: zeroGradient.
walls: epsilonWallFunction with the same value as the inlet (0.0648).

The internal fields are initialized as follows.
p: uniform 0.
U: uniform (0 0 0).
k: uniform 0.0338.
epsilon: uniform 0.0648.

The solver is simpleFoam, which solves the steady-state RANS equations for incompressible flow.

Standard simpleFoam numerical schemes are used (Gauss linear for gradients, bounded Gauss linearUpwind for divergence terms, Gauss linear corrected for Laplacian terms).

The solution controls use SIMPLE with typical relaxation factors (0.7 for U, 0.3 for p, 0.7 for k and epsilon).

Convergence residual targets are 1e-05 for p and 1e-06 for U, k, and epsilon.
