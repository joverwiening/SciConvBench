The simulation is a transient two-phase Euler–Euler gas–solid riser simulation using twoPhaseEulerFoam.
The two phases are air (continuous) and catalyst (dispersed).

Corrected inlet boundary conditions for volume fractions:
In 0/alpha.catalyst, the inlet patch is type fixedValue with value uniform 0.01.
In 0/alpha.air, the inlet patch is type fixedValue with value uniform 0.99.

Riser geometry assumes a typical pilot-scale circulating fluidized bed riser with a height of 10 m and an internal diameter of 0.2 m.
The mesh is a three-dimensional cylindrical domain meshed with blockMesh, using approximately 20 cells across the diameter and 200 cells along the height, with modest refinement near the walls.

The catalyst particles are typical FCC catalyst with a diameter of 75 micrometers and a density of 1500 kg/m3.
Air properties are standard conditions with a density of 1.225 kg/m3 and a kinematic viscosity of 1.48e-5 m2/s.

The gas inlet velocity U.air is set to a fixedValue of (0 0 5), representing 5 m/s upward, which is above minimum fluidization for FCC catalyst.
The catalyst inlet velocity U.catalyst is set to a fixedValue of (0 0 3), representing 3 m/s upward, slightly lower than the gas velocity to reflect typical slip.

At the outlet (top), use pressureInletOutletVelocity for both U.air and U.catalyst, and fixedValue uniform 1e5 (1 atm) for pressure p.
At the walls, use no-slip (fixedValue uniform (0 0 0)) for U.air and a Johnson-Jackson partial-slip condition for U.catalyst.
For alpha.catalyst at the walls, use a zeroGradient condition.
For alpha.catalyst at the outlet, use inletOutlet with inletValue uniform 0.
For alpha.air at the outlet, use inletOutlet with inletValue uniform 1.

The drag model is Gidaspow, which blends Wen-Yu and Ergun correlations and is the standard choice for dense gas-solid flows in risers.

Granular kinetic theory settings for the catalyst phase:
The granular viscosity model is Gidaspow.
The granular conductivity model is Gidaspow.
The granular pressure model is Lun.
The frictional stress model is Johnson-Jackson with a friction angle of 25 degrees.
The radial distribution function is Lun.
The coefficient of restitution between particles is 0.9.
The maximum packing fraction alphaMax is 0.63.

Turbulence modeling uses a k-epsilon model applied to the continuous (air) phase.
The dispersed (catalyst) phase turbulence is modeled as laminar, relying on the kinetic theory of granular flow for its effective viscosity.

The simulation end time is 20 seconds to allow the flow to develop and capture transient riser behavior.
Time stepping uses adaptive time stepping controlled by a maximum Courant number of 0.5.
The initial deltaT is 1e-4 seconds.
The maximum deltaT is 1e-3 seconds.

The gravity vector is (0 0 -9.81), acting downward opposite to the flow direction.

The PIMPLE algorithm is used for pressure-velocity coupling with 2 outer correctors and 1 non-orthogonal corrector.

Write output data every 0.1 seconds for post-processing.
