The simulation models the injection of high-pressure compressible air into a tank of water using the interFoam solver framework in OpenFOAM.

The geometry is a 2D axisymmetric domain represented as a wedge. The tank is 0.1 m in diameter (radial direction) and 0.3 m in height (axial direction). The gas inlet is a circular orifice of 2 mm diameter centered at the bottom of the tank. The wedge angle is 5 degrees, using a single cell in the azimuthal direction, consistent with OpenFOAM axisymmetric conventions.

The gas phase is air, defined using the perfectGas equation of state. The molecular weight of air is 28.97 g/mol. The specific heat at constant pressure (Cp) for air is 1005 J/(kg·K). The dynamic viscosity of air is 1.8e-5 Pa·s.

The water phase is treated as incompressible with a constant density of 998.2 kg/m³. The specific heat at constant pressure (Cp) for water is 4182 J/(kg·K). The dynamic viscosity of water is 1.0e-3 Pa·s. The surface tension between air and water is 0.072 N/m.

The gas inlet conditions correspond to Mach 0.8 air flow. The stagnation temperature is 300 K. The stagnation pressure is 5 bar (500000 Pa). Using isentropic relations for Mach 0.8 with gamma = 1.4, the static temperature at the inlet is approximately 262.7 K, the static pressure is approximately 3.28 bar (328000 Pa), and the inlet velocity is approximately 259.6 m/s directed axially into the tank.

The initial conditions inside the tank are as follows. The tank is initially filled entirely with water. The initial pressure in the tank is 101325 Pa (atmospheric). The initial temperature in the tank is 300 K. The initial velocity everywhere in the tank is zero. The volume fraction (alpha.water) is initialized to 1 throughout the domain.

The boundary conditions are as follows. The inlet is a fixed velocity of 259.6 m/s in the axial direction, with fixed value alpha.water = 0, fixed value temperature of 262.7 K, and a zero-gradient pressure condition. The top of the tank is an open outlet with a total pressure condition set to atmospheric (101325 Pa), an inlet-outlet condition on velocity, and an inlet-outlet condition on alpha.water with an inflow value of 0 (air). The side wall is a no-slip wall with a zero-gradient condition on pressure, alpha.water, and temperature. The bottom wall (excluding the inlet patch) is a no-slip wall with zero-gradient conditions on pressure, alpha.water, and temperature. The front and back wedge planes use the wedge boundary condition type.

The turbulence model is k-omega SST (RANS). The inlet turbulent kinetic energy k is estimated as 1.5 * (0.05 * 259.6)^2 = 253.1 m²/s². The inlet specific dissipation rate omega is estimated using a turbulent viscosity ratio of 10, giving omega = k / (nu_t) where nu_t = 10 * 1.8e-5 / rho_air_inlet, computed consistently. Wall functions (kqRWallFunction, omegaWallFunction, nutkWallFunction) are applied at all wall boundaries.

The solver is interFoam (or compressibleInterFoam if available, to properly handle the perfectGas equation of state for the gas phase with energy transport). The PIMPLE algorithm is used for pressure-velocity coupling with 3 outer correctors and 2 inner correctors (non-orthogonal correctors set to 1).

The time-step control is adaptive based on a maximum Courant number of 0.5 and a maximum interface Courant number of 0.25. The initial time step is 1.0e-7 s. The maximum allowable time step is 1.0e-5 s.

The total simulation duration is 5.0e-3 s (5 milliseconds), which is sufficient to observe the initial gas jet penetration and expansion dynamics in the water.

The output (write) interval is every 1.0e-4 s (0.1 ms), resulting in approximately 50 saved time steps for post-processing.

The mesh resolution is approximately 200 cells in the axial direction and 100 cells in the radial direction, with refinement near the inlet region (grading ratio of 4 toward the inlet) to resolve the high-velocity gas jet and the air-water interface. The total cell count is approximately 20000 cells.

The discretization schemes use Gauss linear for gradient terms, Gauss vanLeer for divergence terms involving alpha, Gauss linearUpwind for velocity divergence terms, and Gauss linear corrected for Laplacian terms. The time scheme is Euler (first-order implicit) for robustness given the strong compressibility and density ratio.
