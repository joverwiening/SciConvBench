Simulate a steady-state, two-phase (oil–water) flow in a horizontal pipe using twoPhaseEulerFoam.

The pipe is horizontal with an inner diameter of 0.1 m (100 mm).
The pipe length is 2.0 m, giving a length-to-diameter ratio of 20, sufficient for flow development.

Water is the continuous phase with density 998 kg/m3 and kinematic viscosity 1.0e-06 m2/s.
Oil is the dispersed phase with density 850 kg/m3 and kinematic viscosity 5.0e-06 m2/s (representative of a light crude oil).

The interphase drag model is SchillerNaumann, which is a standard and robust choice for dispersed liquid-liquid flows.
The dispersed oil droplet diameter is assumed constant at 1 mm (0.001 m).

The fixed mixture velocity at the inlet is 1.0 m/s, directed along the pipe axis.
The inlet water volume fraction alpha.water is fixed at 0.3 (uniform).
The inlet oil volume fraction alpha.oil is therefore 0.7.

The outlet boundary condition for alpha.water is zeroGradient.
The outlet boundary condition for pressure (p_rgh) is fixedValue uniform 0.
The outlet boundary condition for velocity (U) for both phases is zeroGradient or pressureInletOutletVelocity.
The inlet boundary condition for pressure (p_rgh) is fixedFluxPressure.

The pipe walls use noSlip for velocity of both phases.
The pipe walls use zeroGradient for alpha.water.
The pipe walls use fixedFluxPressure for p_rgh.

Turbulence is modeled using the k-epsilon model for each phase, as the bulk Reynolds number (Re approximately 100,000 based on mixture velocity and pipe diameter) indicates turbulent flow.
Appropriate turbulent inlet conditions are set: turbulent intensity of 5%, turbulent length scale of 7 mm (0.07 times the diameter), with corresponding k and epsilon values derived from these.
Wall functions (nutkWallFunction for nut, kqRWallFunction for k, epsilonWallFunction for epsilon) are used on the pipe walls for both phases.

The mesh is a structured O-grid (butterfly mesh) generated with blockMesh.
The mesh has approximately 20 cells across the pipe diameter and 200 cells along the pipe length.
The total cell count is approximately 60,000 to 80,000 cells.

The simulation uses the PIMPLE algorithm for pressure-velocity coupling.
Since steady-state behavior is sought, the simulation runs as a transient case until a statistically steady state is reached.
The time step is controlled by a maximum Courant number of 0.5.
The simulation end time is 5.0 seconds, which corresponds to approximately 2.5 flow-through times, sufficient to reach steady state.

The gravity vector is (0, 0, 0) since the pipe is horizontal, but the vertical component is set to (0, -9.81, 0) to capture any gravitational phase segregation across the pipe cross-section.

Write results every 0.5 seconds for post-processing.
