The simulation models a turbulent thermal plume in a room.
The computational domain is a cube with dimensions 2.0 m by 2.0 m by 2.0 m.
The Y-axis represents the vertical direction.
The fluid is air, and its properties are temperature-dependent.
The ideal gas law is used to model air density.
Gravity is active with a magnitude of 9.81 m/s^2 in the negative Y-direction.
The simulation uses the standard k-epsilon turbulence model.
The initial condition for the entire domain is quiescent air at a uniform temperature of 300 K.
The floor is located at Y=0.
A hot strip is located on the floor, defined by the area where X is between 0.9 m and 1.1 m, and Z is between 0.995 m and 1.005 m.
The hot strip has a constant surface temperature of 400 K.
The remainder of the floor at Y=0 is an adiabatic wall.
The four vertical walls at X=0, X=2, Z=0, and Z=2 are no-slip walls with a constant temperature of 300 K.
The ceiling at Y=2 is a pressure outlet boundary with a gauge pressure of zero.
