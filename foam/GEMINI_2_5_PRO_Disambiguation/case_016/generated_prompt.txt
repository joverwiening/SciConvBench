The simulation will be run using the reactingFoam solver.
The domain is a rectangular prism.
The domain spans from x=0 to 0.02 m, y=-0.01 to 0.01 m, and z=-0.01 to 0.01 m.
The mesh has 100 cells in the x-direction, 40 cells in the y-direction, and 1 cell in the z-direction.
The boundary at x=0 is named 'fuel'.
The boundary at x=0.02 is named 'air'.
The boundaries at y=-0.01 and y=0.01 are named 'outlet'.
The front and back boundaries are of type 'empty'.
At the 'fuel' boundary, velocity is a fixed value of (0.1 0 0) m/s.
At the 'fuel' boundary, temperature is a fixed value of 293 K.
At the 'fuel' boundary, the mass fraction of CH4 is 1.0 and other species are 0.
At the 'air' boundary, velocity is a fixed value of (-0.1 0 0) m/s.
At the 'air' boundary, temperature is a fixed value of 293 K.
At the 'air' boundary, the mass fraction of O2 is 0.23 and N2 is 0.77.
At the 'outlet' boundaries, a zeroGradient condition is applied to velocity, temperature, pressure, and all species.
The combustion and momentum transport models are laminar.
The simulation uses compressible finite-rate chemistry.
The thermodynamics model is hePsiThermo with JANAF format data.
The species mixing model is coefficientWilkeMultiComponentMixture.
The transport model is Sutherland.
A one-step global reaction mechanism for methane-air combustion will be used.
Thermodynamic and transport property files for CH4, O2, N2, CO2, and H2O must be provided.
The operating pressure is 101325 Pa.
The initial pressure field is uniform at 101325 Pa.
The initial temperature field is uniform at 293 K.
The initial velocity field is linearly interpolated along the x-axis between the fuel and air inlet values.
The initial species mass fraction fields are linearly interpolated along the x-axis between the fuel and air inlet values.
The chemistry type is EulerImplicit with the Rosenbrock43 ODE solver.
Standard second-order numerical schemes are used for discretization.
The simulation end time is 0.5 s.
The time step is 1e-4 s.
Results are written to file every 0.05 s.
Gravitational acceleration is neglected.
