The simulation is a 2D flow past a circular cylinder.
The domain is a rectangle.
The domain extends from x = -5 m to x = 5 m in the streamwise direction.
The domain extends from y = -1.5 m to y = 2.5 m in the cross-stream direction.
The front and back faces are of type `empty` to enforce a 2D simulation.
A cylinder with a diameter of 2 m is centered at the origin (x=0 m, y=0 m).
The inlet boundary is at x = -5 m.
The inlet velocity is a uniform value of (1 0 0) m/s.
The outlet boundary is at x = 5 m.
The outlet velocity condition is `zeroGradient`.
The outlet pressure condition is a fixed value of 0.
The top boundary is at y = 2.5 m and the bottom boundary is at y = -1.5 m.
The top and bottom boundaries are no-slip walls.
The pressure condition at the top and bottom boundaries is `zeroGradient`.
The cylinder surface is a no-slip wall.
The pressure condition at the cylinder surface is `zeroGradient`.
The initial velocity field is (0 0 0) m/s everywhere.
The initial pressure field is 0 everywhere.
The flow is simulated as laminar.
The fluid is a generalized Newtonian fluid using the `CrossPowerLaw` viscosity model.
The zero-shear-rate kinematic viscosity (`nu0`) is 0.01 m^2/s.
The infinite-shear-rate kinematic viscosity (`nuInf`) is 10 m^2/s.
The power-law time constant (`m`) is 0.4 s.
The power-law index (`n`) is 3.
The simulation end time is 0.5 s.
The time step is controlled by a maximum Courant number of 0.5.
The mesh contains approximately 40,000 cells, with refinement near the cylinder and in its wake.
The pressure-velocity coupling uses the PIMPLE algorithm.
The time derivative scheme is `Euler`.
The gradient scheme is `Gauss linear`.
The divergence scheme for the velocity convection term is `Gauss linearUpwindV grad(U)`.
The Laplacian scheme is `Gauss linear corrected`.
The interpolation scheme is `linear`.
The linear solver for pressure uses a Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) method with a tolerance of 1e-6.
The linear solver for velocity uses a Preconditioned Bi-conjugate Gradient (PBiCG) method with a tolerance of 1e-6.
