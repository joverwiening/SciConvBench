The simulation will model a three-phase fluidized bed in a cylindrical column using the `multiphaseEulerFoam` solver.
The column has a diameter of 0.1 m and a height of 1 m.
Gravity acts in the negative y-direction with a value of (0 -9.81 0) m/s^2.
The phases are air (gas), water (liquid), and sand (solid particles), defined in `phaseProperties` as `(air water sand)`.
Air is treated as a perfect gas with a reference density of 1.225 kg/m^3, a dynamic viscosity of 1.8e-5 Pa.s, a specific heat ratio of 1.4, and a molecular weight of 0.02896 kg/mol.
Water is treated as an incompressible liquid with a density of 1000 kg/m^3 and a dynamic viscosity of 0.001 Pa.s.
Sand is treated as a constant density solid with a density of 2650 kg/m^3 and a diameter of 500 microns.
The simulation will be transient, running for 10 seconds.
Initial conditions consist of a bed of sand settled at the bottom 0.2 m of the column, saturated with water, where `alpha.sand = 0.6` and `alpha.water = 0.4`.
The rest of the column (from 0.2 m to 1 m height) is initially filled with water, where `alpha.water = 1`.
Initial air phase fraction is 0 in all regions.
All phases are initialized with zero velocity.
Initial pressure is set to a hydrostatic distribution based on the initial phase fractions.
Air is injected from the bottom inlet at a velocity of 0.5 m/s.
For water and sand, the bottom inlet is a no-slip wall boundary condition.
The top of the cylindrical column is an outlet boundary condition for all phases, with atmospheric pressure (0 Pa gauge) and zero gradient for velocity and phase fractions.
The cylindrical wall of the column is a no-slip wall boundary condition for all phases.
Standard drag models, specifically Gidaspow, will be used for the interactions between the phases.
