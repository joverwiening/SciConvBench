Simulate the compressible, supersonic flow of air over a 10-degree wedge to form an oblique shock.
The freestream Mach number is 2.0.
Treat air as a perfect gas.
The simulation uses a 2D mesh extruded one cell in z with `frontAndBack` set to `empty`.
The characteristic length of the wedge (horizontal projection of the inclined surface) is 1 meter.
The computational domain extends from x=-2 meters to x=6 meters and from y=0 meters to y=2 meters.
The wedge starts at x=0 meters, y=0 meters.
The inclined wedge surface is defined by y = x * tan(10 degrees) for x in the range [0, 1] meters.
The freestream/inlet conditions are `U = (686 0 0) m/s`, `T = 293 K`, and `p = 101325 Pa`.
In `constant/thermophysicalProperties`, configure a perfect-gas model using `hePsiThermo`, `equationOfState perfectGas`, and `transport sutherland`.
The inlet boundary condition at x=-2 meters uses fixed values for `U`, `p`, and `T`.
The outlet boundary condition at x=6 meters uses `waveTransmissive` for `p` and `T`, and `zeroGradient` for `U`.
The wedge surface, the bottom boundary from x=-2 meters to x=0 meters, the bottom boundary from x=1 meter to x=6 meters, and the top boundary at y=2 meters are all slip walls.
The `frontAndBack` boundaries are set to `empty`.
In `system/controlDict`, set `application simpleFoam`.
