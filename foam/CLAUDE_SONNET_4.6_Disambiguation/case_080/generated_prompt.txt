The simulation domain is a 3D rectangular box with width 0.1 m, height 0.5 m, and depth 0.1 m.
Use OpenFOAM's twoPhaseEulerFoam solver for the Euler-Euler two-fluid model.
The gas phase is air with density 1.2 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 1.8e-5 Pa·s (derived from rho_g = 1.2 kg/m³ and nu_g = 1.5e-5 m²/s).
The solid phase consists of spherical particles with diameter 200 microns and density 2500 kg/m³.
Initialize the solid phase with a packed bed occupying the bottom 0.2 m of the domain, with a solid volume fraction of 0.55 in that region.
Set the solid volume fraction to 0 in the region above 0.2 m height at initialization.
Apply a uniform inlet superficial gas velocity of 0.2 m/s in the upward direction at the bottom boundary.
Use a pressure outlet boundary condition at the top of the domain.
Apply no-slip or partial-slip wall boundary conditions on all four lateral walls and the bottom wall for both phases.
Use the Schiller-Naumann drag model for gas-solid interphase momentum exchange.
Include the Kinetic Theory of Granular Flow (KTGF) to model solid phase stresses and granular temperature transport.
Set the particle-particle restitution coefficient to 0.9 in the KTGF closure.
Include a frictional stress model (e.g., Johnson-Jackson or Schaeffer) to handle solid phase stresses in dense packing regions where solid volume fraction approaches the maximum packing limit.
Set the maximum solid packing volume fraction to 0.63.
Run the simulation for a total physical time of 5 seconds.
Start with an initial time step of 1e-4 s, allowing adaptive time stepping up to a maximum of 5e-4 s.
Limit the maximum Courant number to 0.3 to control adaptive time stepping.
Write simulation output (field data) every 0.05 seconds of physical time.
Use a structured or block-structured mesh appropriate for the rectangular domain, with sufficient resolution to capture bubble formation and particle dynamics (recommend at least 10 cells across the width and depth, and proportionally finer in the height direction).
Monitor and output solid volume fraction, gas and solid velocity fields, granular temperature, and pressure fields at each write interval.
