Simulate supersonic flow over a forward-facing step using OpenFOAM's rhoPimpleFoam solver.

The computational domain is a 2D channel 0.5 m long and 0.1 m tall.
A forward-facing step of height 0.02 m is located at x = 0.1 m from the inlet, spanning the full bottom wall from x = 0.1 m to the outlet.
The step face is vertical and the step top surface is horizontal at y = 0.02 m.

Use blockMeshDict to generate a structured hexahedral mesh with approximately 20000 total cells.
Refine the mesh near the step corner (around x = 0.1 m, y = 0.02 m) and near the region where the shock impinges on the bottom wall downstream of the step.
The mesh is 2D (one cell thick in the z-direction with empty boundary conditions on front and back patches).

Use air as a perfect gas with gamma = 1.4 and Sutherland viscosity law.
Set the inlet static temperature to 293 K and inlet static pressure to 101325 Pa.
Compute the inlet velocity from Mach 3 using the relation U = M * sqrt(gamma * R * T), where R = 287 J/(kg·K).
Set inlet turbulence intensity to a low value (e.g., 1%) and compute k and omega accordingly for the k-omega SST model.

Apply the following boundary conditions at the inlet: fixedValue for U, T, p (static), k, and omega corresponding to Mach 3 supersonic inflow.
Apply the following boundary conditions at the outlet: waveTransmissive for p, zeroGradient for U, T, k, and omega.
Apply slip condition for all fields on the top wall (upperWall).
Apply noSlip for U on the bottom wall and step surfaces.
Apply zeroGradient for p on the bottom wall and step surfaces.
Apply fixedValue T = 293 K on the bottom wall and step surfaces (isothermal walls).
Apply kqRWallFunction for k, omegaWallFunction for omega, and nutkWallFunction for nut on the bottom wall and step surfaces.

Use the k-omega SST RAS turbulence model.
Set thermophysical properties using hePsiThermo with pureMixture and the perfectGas equation of state.
Use Sutherland transport model with appropriate coefficients for air (As = 1.458e-6, Ts = 110.4 K).

For numerical schemes, use Gauss linearUpwind or Gauss vanLeer for divergence terms involving U and energy to handle supersonic gradients robustly.
Use Gauss linear for gradient schemes and Gauss linear corrected for Laplacian schemes.
Use Euler or localEuler time scheme for transient advancement.

Set the time step using a maximum Courant number of 0.5 to maintain stability.
Run the simulation until the flow reaches a statistically steady shock structure, targeting at least 0.01 s of physical time or until residuals stabilize.
Write solution fields every 0.001 s.

Output fields include U, p, T, rho, Mach number, k, omega, and nut for post-processing.
Visualize the shock structure using pressure and Mach number contour plots in ParaView.
