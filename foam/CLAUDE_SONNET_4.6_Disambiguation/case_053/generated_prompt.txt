Model a de Laval (converging-diverging) nozzle with a normal shock in the diverging section.

The inlet total (stagnation) pressure is 5 bar (5.0e5 Pa).
The inlet total (stagnation) temperature is 600 K.
The outlet static back pressure is 3.5e5 Pa.
The working fluid is air, treated as a calorically perfect ideal gas with ratio of specific heats gamma = 1.4 and gas constant R = 287 J/(kg·K).
Design the nozzle throat area to achieve Mach 1 (choked flow) at the throat.
Choose a nozzle area ratio (exit area to throat area) of 2.0, which corresponds to a design exit Mach number of approximately 2.2 under fully isentropic supersonic flow.
The back pressure of 3.5e5 Pa is between the value that would place the shock at the throat and the value that would place it at the exit, so a normal shock will stand inside the diverging section at the Mach number consistent with this back pressure.
Compute the shock location by iterating on the diverging-section area ratio: find the cross-sectional area A_shock where the Rankine-Hugoniot normal shock relations, combined with isentropic subsonic deceleration from the shock to the exit, produce a static pressure equal to 3.5e5 Pa at the exit plane.
Use the following solution procedure: assume a Mach number M1 upstream of the shock, compute post-shock Mach number M2 and total pressure ratio P02/P01 using normal shock relations, then apply isentropic relations from M2 to the exit area ratio to find exit static pressure, and iterate M1 until exit static pressure equals 3.5e5 Pa.
Output the following results: throat Mach number, Mach number just upstream of the shock (M1), Mach number just downstream of the shock (M2), static pressure just upstream and just downstream of the shock, total pressure loss across the shock, and the exit plane Mach number and static pressure.
Verify that the computed exit static pressure matches the specified back pressure of 3.5e5 Pa within a tolerance of 1 Pa.
