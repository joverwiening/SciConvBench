Simulate a lid-driven cavity flow using OpenFOAM (or equivalent finite-volume CFD solver).
The cavity is a square domain with side length 0.1 m.
The top wall moves in the positive x-direction at a velocity of 1 m/s.
The left, right, and bottom walls are stationary no-slip walls.
Set the kinematic viscosity nu to 0.01 m²/s in the transport properties configuration.
The Reynolds number is Re = 10, computed as Re = (1 m/s × 0.1 m) / 0.01 m²/s.
Use an incompressible, laminar, steady-state (or time-marched to steady state) flow solver.
Apply no-slip boundary conditions (velocity = 0) on the bottom, left, and right walls.
Apply a moving wall boundary condition (velocity = 1 m/s in x, 0 in y) on the top wall.
Set the pressure boundary condition to zero-gradient on all walls, with a fixed reference pressure value of 0 Pa at one cell to remove the pressure singularity.
Use a structured, uniform mesh for the cavity interior.
The mesh should have at least 20 cells in each direction (20×20 minimum) for adequate resolution at Re = 10.
Initialize the internal field with zero velocity and zero pressure.
Run the simulation until the solution reaches a steady state, defined as residuals dropping below 1×10⁻⁵ for all solved quantities.
Output the final velocity field (U) and pressure field (p) for post-processing.
