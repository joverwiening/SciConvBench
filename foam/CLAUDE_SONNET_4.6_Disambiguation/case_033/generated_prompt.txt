The simulation is a 2D transient natural convection case of a hot vertical plate in a room of air, implemented in the latest stable OpenFOAM release.

Use the buoyantPimpleFoam solver, which is the transient compressible buoyant solver.

The 2D simulation domain is 2.0 m wide (x-direction) and 3.0 m tall (y-direction), with a depth of 0.01 m in the z-direction (one cell thick, front and back treated as empty patches).

The hot vertical plate is located on the left wall (x=0), spanning from y=1.0 m to y=2.0 m.

The plate temperature is fixed at 350 K as a Dirichlet boundary condition.

The initial and ambient air temperature is 300 K.

Use the kOmegaSST turbulence model with appropriate wall functions applied at all solid walls.

Generate the mesh using blockMesh with approximately 80 cells in the x-direction, 120 cells in the y-direction, and 1 cell in the z-direction.

Apply grading in the x-direction to cluster cells near the left wall (hotPlate region) to better resolve the thermal boundary layer, with a grading ratio of approximately 4 to 1 (finer near x=0).

The left wall boundary consists of three patches: a lower adiabatic wall from y=0 to y=1.0 m, the hotPlate from y=1.0 m to y=2.0 m, and an upper adiabatic wall from y=2.0 m to y=3.0 m.

Set the hotPlate patch temperature to fixedValue 350 K for the T field.

Set all other solid wall patches (lower left, upper left, right wall, top, bottom) to zeroGradient for temperature, representing adiabatic conditions.

Set the top and bottom boundaries as walls with no-slip velocity and zeroGradient temperature.

Set the right wall as a wall with no-slip velocity and zeroGradient temperature.

Apply the front and back patches as empty type to enforce the 2D condition.

Initialize the entire domain with T=300 K, p_rgh equal to the reference pressure, and U=(0,0,0).

Use air as the thermophysical fluid with the hePsiThermo thermodynamic model, pureMixture composition, and sutherland transport properties appropriate for air.

Set the reference pressure to 101325 Pa and reference temperature to 300 K.

Enable gravity as g=(0,-9.81,0) m/s^2 in the constant/g file.

Use PIMPLE algorithm with nOuterCorrectors=2, nCorrectors=2, and nNonOrthogonalCorrectors=1.

Set the initial time step to 0.01 s and use adjustable time stepping with a maximum Courant number of 0.5.

Run the simulation for a total physical time of 60 seconds to allow the thermal plume to develop and reach a statistically quasi-steady state.

Write output every 2 seconds of simulation time.

Apply kLowReWallFunction for k, omegaWallFunction for omega, and nutkWallFunction for nut at all solid wall patches.

Initialize k to 1e-6 m^2/s^2 and omega to 1.0 s^-1 throughout the domain.

Use linear interpolation schemes for divergence terms and Gauss linear corrected for Laplacian terms.

Monitor residuals for U, p_rgh, T, k, and omega each time step.

The primary output fields to visualize are T (temperature), U (velocity magnitude), and p_rgh to observe the thermal plume developing above the hot plate.
