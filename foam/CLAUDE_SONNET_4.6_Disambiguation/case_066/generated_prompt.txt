The simulation is steady-state, incompressible, and laminar flow through a 2D porous medium channel using OpenFOAM (simpleFoam or similar incompressible steady solver).

The domain is a 2D rectangle, 0.5 m long (x-direction) and 0.1 m high (y-direction).

The fluid is water with density 1000 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 1e-3 Pa·s (kinematic viscosity 1e-6 m²/s).

The mesh is a structured 2D block mesh covering the full domain from x=0 to x=0.5 and y=0 to y=0.1.

The domain is divided into three regions along x: a clear fluid inlet region from x=0 to x=0.2, a porous zone from x=0.2 to x=0.3, and a clear fluid outlet region from x=0.3 to x=0.5.

The porous zone from x=0.2 to x=0.3 uses the Darcy-Forchheimer model.

The Darcy-Forchheimer resistance coefficients for the porous zone are d = (1e8 1e8 1e10) and f = (0 0 0), applied in the x, y, and z directions respectively.

No separate porosity value is specified; the porous medium is treated as fully saturated with no porosity correction to velocity.

The inlet boundary condition is a fixed uniform velocity U = (1 0 0) m/s and zero-gradient pressure.

The outlet boundary condition is fixed pressure p = 0 Pa (uniform) and zero-gradient velocity.

The top and bottom walls use a no-slip condition for velocity (U = noSlip) and zero-gradient for pressure.

The front and back faces are set to empty boundary conditions to enforce the 2D simulation.

The simulation solves the steady-state incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with the Darcy-Forchheimer source term active only in the porous zone cells.

Use the SIMPLE algorithm for pressure-velocity coupling.

Apply standard relaxation factors, for example 0.7 for velocity and 0.3 for pressure.

Convergence criteria are residuals below 1e-6 for all solved quantities (U and p).

Output the pressure field p and velocity field U for post-processing.
