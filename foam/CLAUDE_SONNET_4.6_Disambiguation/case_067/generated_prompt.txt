Model turbulent flow through a fully porous pipe using OpenFOAM with the simpleFoam solver and the standard k-epsilon turbulence model.

The pipe has a length of 2 m and a diameter of 0.1 m (radius 0.05 m).

The fluid is air with kinematic viscosity nu = 1.5e-5 m^2/s as defined in constant/transportProperties. Assume air density rho = 1.225 kg/m^3 as a standard best-practice value.

The inlet boundary condition for velocity is fixedValue uniform (10 0 0) m/s.

The outlet boundary condition for pressure is fixedValue uniform 0 (gauge pressure).

The inlet turbulent kinetic energy is k = 0.375 m^2/s^2, calculated from k = 1.5*(U*I)^2 with U = 10 m/s and I = 0.05.

The inlet turbulent dissipation rate is epsilon = 0.23 m^2/s^3, calculated from epsilon = Cmu^0.75 * k^1.5 / L with Cmu = 0.09 and L = 0.0035 m.

Use zeroGradient for k and epsilon at the outlet.

Apply standard wall functions at the pipe wall: kqRWallFunction for k, epsilonWallFunction for epsilon, and nutkWallFunction for the turbulent viscosity nut.

The entire pipe domain is the porous zone.

Define the porous media using the DarcyForchheimer model in constant/fvOptions.

Set the Darcy coefficient d = (5e7 5e7 5e7) and the Forchheimer coefficient f = (0 0 0) in the fvOptions file.

Apply the porous zone to the entire mesh region (cellZone covering all cells).

Use standard simpleFoam RANS settings in fvSolution with SIMPLE algorithm, and set relaxation factors typical for turbulent flow (e.g., U = 0.7, p = 0.3, k = 0.5, epsilon = 0.5).

Use second-order discretisation schemes for divergence terms (e.g., Gauss linearUpwind grad(U)) and Gauss linear for gradient and Laplacian schemes in fvSchemes.

Run the simulation until convergence, monitoring residuals for U, p, k, and epsilon to drop below 1e-5.
