Model a single air bubble rising in a quiescent liquid column using a VOF (Volume of Fluid) solver in a 2D (quasi-2D) configuration with OpenFOAM or equivalent finite-volume VOF framework.

The domain is a rectangular column 0.05 m wide, 0.2 m tall, and 0.01 m deep.

Use a single computational cell in the depth (z) direction to enforce the 2D condition.

Apply empty boundary conditions on the front and back faces (z-direction) to enforce 2D treatment.

Apply no-slip wall boundary conditions on the left and right side walls (x-direction).

Apply a no-slip wall boundary condition at the bottom face (y = 0).

Apply an atmospheric pressure outlet boundary condition at the top face (y = 0.2 m).

Initialize the domain as fully filled with liquid (alpha.water = 1 everywhere).

Place a spherical air bubble of diameter 0.01 m centered at coordinates (0.025, 0.025, 0.005).

Set alpha.water = 0 inside the bubble region and alpha.water = 1 outside.

Set the liquid phase (water) density to 1000 kg/m³ and kinematic viscosity to 1e-6 m²/s.

Set the gas phase (air) density to 1.2 kg/m³ and kinematic viscosity to 1.5e-5 m²/s.

Set the surface tension coefficient between air and water to 0.072 N/m.

Apply gravitational acceleration of 9.81 m/s² in the negative y-direction.

Initialize the velocity field to zero everywhere (quiescent liquid).

Set the simulation end time to 2.0 s.

Set the initial time step deltaT to 1e-4 s.

Set the maximum Courant number (maxCo) to 0.4 and allow adaptive time-stepping to enforce this limit.

Use a MULES-stabilized VOF advection scheme for the phase fraction equation.

Use a pressure-velocity coupling algorithm appropriate for transient two-phase flow (e.g., PIMPLE or PISO loop).

Output the phase fraction field (alpha.water), velocity field (U), and pressure field (p_rgh) at regular intervals sufficient to capture bubble rise dynamics (e.g., every 0.05 s).

Track and record bubble centroid position and rise velocity over time for post-processing.
