Measure the length to be removed accurately using a ruler or measuring tape.  
Mark the cut line clearly with a pencil, chalk, or marker.  
Select the appropriate cutting tool for the material (e.g., handsaw for wood, hacksaw for metal, utility knife for thin plastics or cardboard).  
Inspect the tool to ensure it is sharp, in good condition, and properly installed.  
Secure the workpiece firmly using clamps, a vise, or a workbench to prevent movement.  
Wear appropriate personal protective equipment: safety glasses, hearing protection, gloves, and dust mask if needed.  
Position the tool so the cutting edge aligns with the marked line and the material is supported on both sides of the cut.  
Apply steady, even pressure and let the tool do the work; avoid forcing the cut, which can cause binding or damage.  
For power tools, set the correct speed and feed rate according to the material thickness and tool manufacturer’s recommendations.  
Pause periodically to clear debris and check alignment, adjusting as necessary to stay on the marked line.  
After the cut is complete, turn off and unplug power tools before removing the workpiece.  
Smooth the cut edge with sandpaper, a file, or a deburring tool to remove splinters or burrs.  
Inspect the final length to confirm it matches the desired measurement; make minor adjustments if needed.  
Clean the work area, store tools safely, and dispose of waste material according to local regulations.
