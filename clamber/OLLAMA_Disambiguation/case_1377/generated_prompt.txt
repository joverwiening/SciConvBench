Moles can cause extensive damage to lawns and gardens by creating a network of tunnels that uproot grass and plants.  
Their burrowing activity can disturb the root systems of crops, ornamental plants, and trees, leading to reduced growth or plant death.  
Mole tunnels can create uneven ground surfaces, posing a tripping hazard for people and animals.  
The tunnels may undermine the stability of sidewalks, driveways, and other hardscape features, potentially causing cracks or collapse.  
Moles can increase the risk of soil erosion by disrupting soil structure and exposing soil to wind and water runoff.  
In agricultural settings, mole activity can interfere with irrigation systems and damage underground utilities such as water, gas, and electrical lines.  
Moles may carry parasites such as fleas, ticks, and mites, which can be transmitted to pets or humans.  
Their presence can attract predators like hawks, owls, and snakes, which may increase the likelihood of encounters with these wildlife species.  
Mole infestations can lead to increased maintenance costs for homeowners, landscapers, and farmers who must repair damage and implement control measures.
