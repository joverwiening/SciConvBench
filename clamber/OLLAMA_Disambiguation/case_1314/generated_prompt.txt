Assume the racket is a standard tennis racket.  
Use a vibration dampener attached to the strings to absorb string vibrations.  
Select softer, low‑vibration strings (e.g., multifilament or natural gut) and keep string tension on the lower end of the recommended range.  
Install a cushioned overgrip or grip tape to reduce handle vibrations transmitted to the hand.  
Choose a racket frame made from vibration‑dampening materials such as graphite composites with built‑in dampening technology.  
Add a rubber or silicone sleeve to the racket throat to further isolate vibrations.  
Maintain proper swing technique: keep the swing smooth, avoid excessive wrist snap, and strike the ball near the racket’s sweet spot.  
If possible, play on surfaces that generate less acoustic feedback (e.g., carpet or cushioned hard courts).  
Periodically inspect the racket for loose components; tighten any bolts or screws to prevent rattling.  
Store the racket in a padded case to protect the frame and strings from damage that could increase noise.
