Create a list of eight common causes that necessitate restoring data from a backup.
The list is for a general technical audience.
Each cause must be accompanied by a brief, one-sentence explanation.
The first cause is Hardware Failure, where physical components like hard drives or servers malfunction.
The second cause is Human Error, such as the accidental deletion or modification of files.
The third cause is Software Corruption, resulting from bugs or errors in an application or operating system that damage data.
The fourth cause is Malicious Attacks, including ransomware that encrypts files or viruses that delete data.
The fifth cause is Natural Disasters, such as fires, floods, or earthquakes that destroy physical infrastructure.
The sixth cause is Power Outages, which can interrupt write processes and lead to data corruption.
The seventh cause is Theft, where the physical loss of equipment like laptops or servers results in data loss.
The eighth cause is Upgrade or Migration Failure, where a failed update process leaves data in an unusable or corrupted state.
