The simulation will model the process of charging by friction, also known as the triboelectric effect.
A rubber balloon and human hair are the two objects in the simulation.
Initially, both the balloon and the hair are electrically neutral, having an equal number of protons and electrons.
The process begins by rubbing the balloon against the hair.
During the rubbing process, electrons are transferred from the hair to the surface of the balloon.
This is because rubber has a higher electron affinity than human hair.
After the rubbing is complete, the balloon has an excess of electrons, resulting in a net negative charge.
The hair has a deficit of electrons, resulting in a net positive charge.
The total charge of the system (balloon plus hair) remains zero, demonstrating the conservation of charge.
