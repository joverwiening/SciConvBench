The process describes the general timeline and symptoms for opioid withdrawal for a non-medical audience.
Early withdrawal symptoms typically begin 6 to 12 hours after the last dose for short-acting opioids.
Early symptoms include muscle aches, agitation, anxiety, watery eyes, runny nose, sweating, and yawning.
Symptoms typically peak in severity around 72 hours after the last dose.
Peak symptoms include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramping, goosebumps, and dilated pupils.
The acute withdrawal phase generally lasts for about one week.
A post-acute withdrawal syndrome (PAWS) can persist for weeks or months.
PAWS symptoms may include mood swings, anxiety, irritability, sleep disturbances, and fatigue.
This timeline is a general guide and can vary based on the individual and the specific opioid used.
Professional medical supervision is strongly recommended for managing opioid withdrawal.
