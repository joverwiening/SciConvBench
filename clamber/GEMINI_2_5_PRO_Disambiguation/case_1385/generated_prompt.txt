To "try" someone is to conduct a formal trial to determine their guilt or innocence regarding a specific accusation.
The person on trial is accused of breaking a specific law or rule.
A prosecutor is responsible for presenting evidence to prove the accusation.
A defense counsel is responsible for representing the accused and challenging the prosecutor's evidence.
The judge presides over the trial, ensures rules of procedure are followed, and makes rulings on matters of law.
A jury of peers is responsible for listening to the evidence and determining the facts of the case.
The trial begins with opening statements from the prosecution and then the defense.
The prosecution presents its evidence and witnesses, which the defense can cross-examine.
The defense then presents its evidence and witnesses, which the prosecution can cross-examine.
The trial concludes with closing arguments from both sides.
The jury must find the defendant guilty "beyond a reasonable doubt" to convict.
The jury delivers a unanimous verdict of either "Guilty" or "Not Guilty".
If the verdict is "Guilty", the judge is responsible for determining the appropriate sentence or consequence.
