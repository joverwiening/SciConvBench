The analysis is for a simply supported beam under a uniformly distributed load.
The analysis assumes linear-elastic material behavior and small deformations.
The beam material is structural steel with a Young's modulus of E = 200000 MPa.
The beam has a length of L = 2500 mm.
The beam has a constant moment of inertia of I = 2000000 mm^4.
A uniformly distributed transverse load of w = 0.7 N/mm is applied over the full span of the beam.
The analysis is based on Euler-Bernoulli beam theory.
The self-weight of the beam is considered negligible.
The governing differential equation for the beam's deflection v(x) is EI * d^2v/dx^2 = M(x).
The boundary conditions are zero deflection at both ends (x=0, x=L) and zero moment at both ends.
The equation for the slope as a function of position x is θ(x) = (w / (24 * E * I)) * (L^3 - 6*L*x^2 + 4*x^3).
The equation for the deflection as a function of position x is v(x) = (w * x / (24 * E * I)) * (L^3 - 2*L*x^2 + x^3).
The maximum slope occurs at the supports (x=0 and x=L).
The value of the maximum slope is (w * L^3) / (24 * E * I).
The maximum deflection occurs at the mid-span (x=L/2).
The value of the maximum deflection is (5 * w * L^4) / (384 * E * I).
