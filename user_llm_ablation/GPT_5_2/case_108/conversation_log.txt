Q: Euler buckling is governed by the second moment of area (I = π*d^4/64), not the polar moment of inertia (J). Shall I use the physically correct second moment of area (I) for the calculation?
A: Use the physically correct second moment of area for buckling:  
\( I = \dfrac{\pi d^{4}}{64} \).  

Do **not** use the polar moment \( J \) in the Euler buckling calculation.
