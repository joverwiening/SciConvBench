Calculate the steady‑state heat‑transfer rate through a composite wall consisting of two layers in series. 
Layer 1 has a thickness of 16.79 mm (0.01679 m) and a thermal conductivity of 24.68 W/(m·K). 
Layer 2 has a thickness of 8.73 mm (0.00873 m) and a thermal conductivity of 94.15 W/(m·K). 
The wall area is 0.2423 m². 
The surface temperature on the hot side is 134.0 °C and on the cold side is –26.8 °C. 
Assume one‑dimensional steady‑state conduction with perfect thermal contact between the layers and no additional convection or contact resistance. 
Use the temperature difference in Celsius (or Kelvin) as ΔT = 134.0 °C – (–26.8 °C). 
Compute the total thermal resistance as the sum of the resistances of the two layers (R = L1/(k1·A) + L2/(k2·A)). 
Determine the heat‑transfer rate Q as Q = ΔT / R and report the result in watts.
