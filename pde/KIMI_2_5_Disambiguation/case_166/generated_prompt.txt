The task is to numerically solve the 1D compressible Navier-Stokes equations (without heat conduction) for a Sod shock tube problem with outflow boundary conditions and very small viscosity.

The PDE system uses conservative variables: density rho, momentum m = rho*v, and total energy E = p/(Gamma-1) + 0.5*rho*v^2, with viscous stress sigma = (zeta + 4/3*eta) * v_x where zeta = eta = 1e-08.

The spatial domain is x in [-1, 1] with transmissive/outflow (zero-gradient) boundary conditions at both ends. The temporal domain is t in [0, 0.2].

The initial condition is a Riemann problem with discontinuity at x=0: left state (rho_L=1.0, v_L=0.0, p_L=10.0) for x<0, and right state (rho_R=1.0, v_R=0.0, p_R=0.1) for x>=0.

The equation of state uses Gamma = 1.6666666666666667 (5/3), giving pressure p = (Gamma-1)*(E - 0.5*rho*v^2).

The numerical method should use a finite volume or finite difference approach on a uniform grid. For cell-centered output, use Nx cells with cell centers at x_i = -1 + (i+0.5)*dx for i=0..Nx-1, where dx = 2/Nx.

Time integration should use an explicit method with CFL condition. The viscous term requires careful treatment due to the small coefficient; use sufficient resolution to resolve the thin viscous layers.

The deliverables are: solution arrays saved to NPZ file with keys x, rho, v, p and scalars Gamma, eta, zeta, T, dx, CFL; a plot saved as PNG showing rho, v, p vs x; and printed diagnostics including min/max of rho and p for positivity, and mean of rho for mass conservation.
