Analyze a solid circular column under compressive load for yielding vs. Euler buckling, and perform a buckling/instability check.

Cross-sectional diameter is d = 25 mm.
Column length is L = 2000 mm.
Material is structural steel with Young's modulus E = 200000 MPa and yield strength Sy = 250 MPa.
Effective length factor is K = 0.7, giving an effective length Le = K·L = 0.7 × 2000 = 1400 mm.
Applied compressive load is P = 25000 N.
Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
Assume the column is perfectly straight and centrally loaded (no eccentricity).
Assume the cross-section is uniform along the full length.

Compute the cross-sectional area A = π·d²/4.
Compute the second moment of area I = π·d⁴/64.
Compute the radius of gyration r = sqrt(I/A).
Compute the slenderness ratio SR = Le/r.
Compute the critical slenderness ratio separating elastic buckling from yielding using SR_critical = sqrt(2·π²·E / Sy).
Compare SR to SR_critical to determine whether Euler (elastic) buckling or yielding governs.
Compute the Euler critical buckling load P_cr = π²·E·I / Le².
Compute the compressive (axial) stress σ = P/A and compare to Sy to check yielding.
Compute the factor of safety against Euler buckling as FS_buckling = P_cr / P.
Compute the factor of safety against yielding as FS_yield = Sy / σ.
State any additional assumptions made (e.g., no imperfections, no residual stresses, no inelastic buckling correction).
Present all governing relations clearly before substituting numerical values.
