Analyze a solid circular shaft under torsion using small-deformation, linear-elastic solid mechanics.
The shaft is a solid circular cross-section with diameter d = 40 mm.
The total length of the shaft is L = 1500 mm.
The shaft is fixed at x = 0 and free at x = L (cantilever configuration).
Two external torques are applied: T1 = 300,000 N·mm at x = 1500 mm (free end) and T2 = -150,000 N·mm at x = 750 mm.
The shear modulus is G = 25939.8 MPa (6061-T6 aluminum alloy).
Assume small deformations and linear-elastic material behavior throughout.
Assume the shaft is prismatic (uniform cross-section along its entire length).
Assume Saint-Venant torsion theory applies (plane sections remain plane, warping is unrestrained or neglected for circular sections).
Determine the internal torque distribution T(x) along the shaft using equilibrium, constructing the torque diagram by sectioning the shaft between load application points.
Determine the polar moment of inertia J = π d^4 / 32 for the solid circular cross-section.
Determine the maximum shear stress in each segment using τ_max = T·c / J, where c = d/2.
Determine the angle of twist in each segment using φ = T·L_segment / (G·J).
Determine the total angle of twist at the free end (x = 1500 mm) by summing contributions from each segment.
Present the governing relations clearly and state all additional assumptions made.
