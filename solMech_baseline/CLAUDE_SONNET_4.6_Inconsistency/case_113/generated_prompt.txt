Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.
Assume linear elasticity.
The shaft is a hollow circular cross-section made of structural steel.
Outer diameter do = 50 mm, inner diameter di = 44 mm, wall thickness t = 3 mm.
Shaft length L = 800 mm.
Applied torque T = 600000 N·mm, constant along the length.
Model the hollow section as a thin-walled closed tube and use the mean-area shear-flow relation (Bredt's formula) for shear stress and twist.
The mean radius for the thin-walled model is r_m = (do + di) / 4 = 23.5 mm.
The enclosed mean area is A_m = π × r_m² = π × (23.5)².
Compute shear stress as τ = T / (2 × A_m × t).
Compute the polar moment of inertia using J = π(do⁴ − di⁴) / 32.
Use shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa.
Compute the angle of twist as φ = T × L / (J × G), in radians.
Deliver two results: (i) maximum shear stress at the outer surface in MPa, and (ii) angle of twist over the length in radians.
