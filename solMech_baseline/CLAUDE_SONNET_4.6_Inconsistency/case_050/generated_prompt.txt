Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.
Assume linear elasticity.
The shaft is a hollow circular cross-section made of brass.
Outer diameter do = 40 mm, inner diameter di = 30 mm, wall thickness t = 5 mm.
Shaft length L = 1000 mm.
Applied constant torque T = 300000 N·mm.
Model the hollow shaft as a thin-walled closed tube using the mean-area shear-flow relation for both stress and twist.
The mean radius for the thin-walled model is r_m = (do + di) / 4 = (40 + 30) / 4 = 17.5 mm.
The enclosed mean area is A_m = π × r_m² = π × (17.5)².
Compute the shear flow q = T / (2 × A_m).
Compute the maximum shear stress as τ = q / t, where t = 5 mm.
For the angle of twist, use φ = (T × L × s_m) / (4 × A_m² × G × t), where s_m = 2 × π × r_m is the mean perimeter.
Also compute the polar moment of inertia using J = π(do⁴ − di⁴) / 32.
Use shear modulus G = 39179.1 MPa.
Report (i) the maximum shear stress at the outer surface in MPa, and (ii) the angle of twist over the full length in radians.
