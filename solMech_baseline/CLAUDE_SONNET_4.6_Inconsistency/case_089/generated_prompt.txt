Use N–mm–MPa units throughout.
The bar is prismatic with constant cross-sectional area A = 500 mm² along its full length.
The bar has four points: A (left end, fixed), B, C, and D (right end).
Segment lengths are AB = 700 mm, BC = 400 mm, and CD = 300 mm.
The material is structural steel with elastic modulus E = 200000 MPa.
Axial forces applied (positive direction is to the right): at B, +15000 N; at C, –12000 N; at D, +10000 N.
The left end A is fixed, so displacement u_A = 0.
The bar is statically determinate; use equilibrium (method of sections) to find the internal axial force in each segment.
Determine the internal axial force N in each of the three segments AB, BC, and CD using the method of sections.
Compute the normal stress σ in each segment as σ = N / A.
Compute the axial deformation of each segment as δ = N·L / (A·E).
Compute the displacement of point D relative to A as u_D = sum of deformations of all three segments.
The instruction to "treat the internal axial force as uniform along the full length and compute one stress level from the net applied load" refers to a simplified single-force interpretation; however, solve the problem correctly segment by segment using the method of sections, as the forces at B, C, and D each affect only the segments to their right (or left, depending on free-body direction).
Report internal axial force, normal stress, and cumulative displacement at each segment.
