Use N–mm–MPa units for all calculations.
Assume Euler–Bernoulli beam theory is applicable.
Compute the total deflection at midspan by superposing the effects of two separate load cases.
The beam is simply supported and has a span (L) of 3500 mm.
The beam's material has a Young's modulus (E) of 200000 MPa.
The beam's cross-section has a constant moment of inertia (I) of 3000000 mm^4.
The first load case is a uniformly distributed load (w) of 0.3 N/mm acting over the entire span.
Calculate the midspan deflection for the distributed load case using the standard formula for a uniformly loaded, simply supported beam.
The second load case is a downward point load (P) of 1200 N applied at midspan.
Calculate the midspan deflection for the point load case using the standard formula for a centrally loaded, simply supported beam.
The final result is the sum of the deflections from the two individual load cases.
