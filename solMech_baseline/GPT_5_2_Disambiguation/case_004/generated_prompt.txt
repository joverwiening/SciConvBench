Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic axial bar behavior.
Use a prismatic bar with constant cross-sectional area A = 300 mm².
Use AISI 1020 steel with Young’s modulus E = 200,000 MPa.
Model the bar along the axis with points A–B–C–D in order.
Use segment lengths AB = 500 mm, BC = 600 mm, CD = 400 mm (total length 1500 mm).
Adopt the global sign convention that positive applied axial load acts to the right.
Apply point loads: at B, +18,000 N (to the right); at C, −12,000 N (to the left); at D, +16,000 N (to the right); at A, no applied load.
Impose boundary condition u_A = 0 (A fixed), with the reaction at A developing as needed.
Specify that point D is free (no displacement constraint) and has no support reaction other than the applied load at D.
Use tension-positive sign convention for internal axial force and normal stress (N > 0 in tension, σ > 0 in tension).
Determine the internal axial force as constant values over each segment AB, BC, and CD.
Determine the normal stress as constant values over each segment AB, BC, and CD.
Determine the displacement at D relative to A, reporting u_D − u_A with u_A = 0.
State any additional assumptions needed and present the governing relations clearly.
