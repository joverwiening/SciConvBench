Assume a prismatic, small-deformation, linear-elastic axial bar with constant properties.
Use constant cross-sectional area A = 500 mm² along the entire bar.
Use constant Young’s modulus E = 200,000 MPa along the entire bar.
Define points A (left end), B, C, D (right end) with segment lengths AB = 700 mm, BC = 400 mm, CD = 300 mm, total AD = 1400 mm.
Apply point loads with positive direction to the right: at B, +15,000 N; at C, −12,000 N; at D, +10,000 N.
Impose boundary conditions: at A, fixed with prescribed displacement u_A = 0 and an unknown axial reaction; at D, free end with no support reaction other than the applied point load at D.
Neglect self-weight, distributed axial loads, and temperature effects.
Use sign convention that internal axial force N(x) is positive in tension and negative in compression, consistent with the external load sign convention.
Determine relevant response quantities for an axially loaded bar with multiple point loads, including piecewise internal axial force N(x), stress σ(x), strain ε(x), and the displacement of point D relative to A (u_D with u_A = 0).
Do not compute or report displacements at B or C.
Report final results in N–mm–MPa units (forces in N, lengths/displacements in mm, stresses in MPa).
Present governing relations and results piecewise over segments AB, BC, and CD using a coordinate x measured from A with appropriate intervals for each segment.
