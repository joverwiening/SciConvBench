Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic axial bar behavior.
Use a prismatic bar with constant cross-sectional area A = 200 mm².
Use Young’s modulus E = 200,000 MPa.
Use the axial geometry: AB = 700 mm, BC = 600 mm, CD = 500 mm, total AD = 1800 mm, with x measured from A.
Apply axial point loads with positive to the right: at B (x = 700 mm) +18,000 N, at C (x = 1300 mm) −8,000 N, at D (x = 1800 mm) +20,000 N.
Impose boundary conditions: A fixed with u_A = 0; D otherwise free (no support reaction or prescribed displacement) with only the applied point load at D.
Neglect temperature effects, initial strains, and body forces including self-weight.
Use sign convention: internal axial force N is positive in tension and negative in compression.
Determine the constant internal axial force N in each segment AB, BC, and CD.
Determine the corresponding constant normal stress σ in each segment AB, BC, and CD.
Determine the displacement of point D relative to A, u_D − u_A, with u_A = 0.
State any additional assumptions needed for axial bar theory and present the governing relations clearly (equilibrium for N by sections, σ = N/A, ε = σ/E, and axial deformation by integrating/summing ε over segment lengths).
