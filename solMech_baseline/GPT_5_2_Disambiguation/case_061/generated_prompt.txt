Assume small-deformation, linear-elastic behavior and a short column so buckling and second-order (P-Δ) effects are neglected.
Use a rectangular cross-section with width b = 60 mm and height h = 120 mm.
Apply a compressive axial load P = 60,000 N.
Apply the load with constant eccentricity e = 20 mm from the centroid along the width direction, producing bending about the centroidal z-axis.
At the section of interest, take the bending moment as M = P e and use superposition for normal stress: σ = P/A ± M c/I.
Compute and report normal stress at the two extreme width edges (near edge toward the load and far edge away from the load).
Determine and state whether any portion of the cross-section is in tension based on the computed extreme stresses.
Report stresses only; do not compute strains, curvature, or deflections and do not use E.
