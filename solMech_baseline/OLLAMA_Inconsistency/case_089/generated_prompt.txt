Use N–mm–MPa units throughout the calculation.  
Treat the bar as prismatic with constant cross‑sectional area A = 500 mm² and Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa.  
The bar is fixed at the left end A (u_A = 0).  
Apply the axial forces at the points: +15 000 N at B, –12 000 N at C, and +10 000 N at D (positive to the right).  
Assume the internal axial force is uniform along the entire length, equal to the net applied load obtained by summing the forces at B, C, and D.  
Compute the internal axial force in each segment AB, BC, and CD as that uniform value.  
Calculate the normal stress in each segment as the internal axial force divided by the cross‑sectional area, reporting tension as positive stress and compression as negative stress.  
Determine the axial displacement of point D relative to A using the uniform stress, the total length L = AB + BC + CD = 1 400 mm, and the relation ε = σ/E, u_D = ε·L, reporting displacement positive to the right (direction of tension).  
Report forces and stresses to the nearest whole number (equivalent to three significant figures) and report the displacement of point D to three decimal places.
