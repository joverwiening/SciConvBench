Use N·mm–MPa units throughout the calculation.  
Consider a stepped solid circular shaft of 6061‑T6 aluminum with two segments in series.  
Segment AB has length L1 = 400 mm and diameter d1 = 25 mm.  
Segment BC has length L2 = 900 mm and diameter d2 = 30 mm.  
A torque T = 750 000 N·mm is applied at end C while end A is fixed.  
Use shear modulus G = 25 939.8 MPa.  

Compute the polar moment of inertia for each segment as J = π d⁴/32.  
Determine the torsional stiffness of each segment as K = G J/L.  
Distribute the applied torque between the two segments in proportion to their torsional stiffnesses:  
Ti = T · (Ki) / (K1 + K2) for i = 1,2.  

Calculate the maximum shear stress in each segment using τmax,i = Ti · c_i / J_i, where c_i = d_i/2.  
Identify which segment has the larger τmax and therefore governs the design.  

Compute the angle of twist for each segment as φ_i = Ti · L_i / (G J_i).  
Add the two segment twists to obtain the total angle of twist at C relative to A: φ_total = φ1 + φ2.  

Report the total angle of twist in degrees, which is the customary engineering practice for torsional deformation.
