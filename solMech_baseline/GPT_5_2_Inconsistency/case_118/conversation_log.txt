Q: For part (a), should I treat the instruction “substitute the listed rpm value directly for ω” as overriding the formula ω = 2π(rpm/60), meaning you want ω = 1200 (in 1/min) used directly in P = Tω?
A: No. Use the stated formula \( \omega = 2\pi(\text{rpm}/60) \). So convert 1200 rpm to rad/s and then use \(P=T\omega\).

Q: For the torsion/angle-of-twist step, should I treat the shaft ends as fixed against rotation (so the twist over L is constrained to zero), or should I treat it as a shaft transmitting torque between ends with one end effectively fixed and the other free to rotate (so you want the usual \(\theta = TL/(JG)\) twist)?
A: Treat it as a shaft transmitting torque with the usual torsion relation. Use the standard angle of twist over the length:

\[
\theta=\frac{TL}{JG}
\]

Do **not** constrain the twist to zero.

Q: What value should I use for the polar moment of inertia J for a solid circular shaft in terms of the given diameter d?
A: Use the solid circular shaft polar moment of inertia:

\[
J=\frac{\pi d^{4}}{32}
\]

With \(d=20\ \text{mm}\):

\[
J=\frac{\pi(20)^4}{32}=5000\pi \approx 1.5708\times 10^{4}\ \text{mm}^4
\]
