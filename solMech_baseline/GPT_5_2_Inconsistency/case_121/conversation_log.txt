Q: When you say “treat the pure-bending normal stress as uniform over the section depth,” what constant stress distribution should I use (e.g., equal to the extreme-fiber stress from σ = My/I, or some other specified value)?
A: Don’t use a uniform stress distribution. Use the standard linear bending-stress relation:

\[
\sigma(y)= -\frac{M\,y}{I}
\]

(with \(y\) positive upward and \(M\) sagging).

=== 2026-04-12 21:14:02 ===
Q: What sign convention should I use for the stress output (i.e., should negative σ be reported as compression and positive σ as tension)?
A: Use the standard convention: **positive σ = tension**, **negative σ = compression**. With \(y\) positive upward and \(M\) sagging in \(\sigma(y) = -My/I\), the **top fiber (positive \(y\)) is in compression (negative σ)** and the **bottom fiber (negative \(y\)) is in tension (positive σ)**.

=== 2026-04-12 21:14:05 ===
Q: What is the exact y-coordinate of the top fiber relative to the neutral axis (e.g., +50 mm if h = 100 mm)?
A: Top fiber is at **y = +h/2 = +50 mm** relative to the neutral axis (since \(h = 100\) mm and \(y\) is positive upward).

=== 2026-04-12 21:14:07 ===
Q: What is the exact y-coordinate of the bottom fiber relative to the neutral axis?
A: Bottom fiber is at **\(y = -h/2 = -50\ \text{mm}\)** relative to the neutral axis (since \(h = 100\) mm and \(y\) is positive upward).
