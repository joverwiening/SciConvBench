Q: When you say “take the normal stress to be identical in the two segments,” do you want me to follow that instruction even though the segments have different cross-sectional areas?
A: No—do **not** force the stresses to be identical. Use the correct axial relation: the **same force** \(P\) passes through both segments, so the stresses are generally **different** because \(A_1 \neq A_2\). Compute \(\sigma_1 = P/A_1\) and \(\sigma_2 = P/A_2\).

Q: Do you want the total elongation computed as the sum of the two segment elongations, \(\delta=\delta_1+\delta_2\), using \(\delta_i = \dfrac{P L_i}{A_i E}\)?
A: Yes. Compute the total elongation as the sum of the two segment elongations:

\[
\delta=\delta_1+\delta_2,\qquad \delta_i=\frac{P\,L_i}{A_i\,E}
\]

using each segment’s own area \(A_i=\frac{\pi d_i^2}{4}\) and the same axial force \(P\) through both segments.
