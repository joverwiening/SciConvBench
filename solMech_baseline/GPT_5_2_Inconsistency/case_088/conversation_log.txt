Q: Do you want me to follow your instruction to treat the internal axial force as uniform along the full length using the net applied load, even though that conflicts with also asking for the internal axial force in each segment?
A: No—do **not** treat the internal axial force as uniform along the full length.

Follow the complete requirement: compute the **internal axial force separately in each segment (AB, BC, CD)** based on the applied point loads, then compute the corresponding **stress in each segment** and the **displacement of D relative to A**.

Q: What sign convention do you want for internal axial force (e.g., tension positive, compression negative)?
A: Use the standard convention: **internal axial force \(N\) is positive in tension and negative in compression** (tension causes elongation).

Q: To compute the displacement of D relative to A, should I assume the bar is in static equilibrium with an unknown reaction force at the fixed end A that balances the applied loads?
A: Yes. Assume static equilibrium: include an unknown axial reaction at the fixed end **A** so that the sum of axial forces on the whole bar is zero (with \(u_A=0\)). Use that reaction when determining the internal axial force in each segment and then compute \(u_D-u_A\).
