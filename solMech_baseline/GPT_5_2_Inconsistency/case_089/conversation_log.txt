Q: When you say “treat the internal axial force as uniform along the full length,” should I ignore the segment-by-segment force jumps at B and C and instead use a single constant internal force equal to the net applied load for all three segments?
A: No. Do **not** treat the internal axial force as uniform along the full length.

Use **segment-by-segment** internal axial forces, with **jumps at B and C** due to the applied point loads, and then compute stress per segment and the total displacement \(u_D-u_A\) by summing segment elongations/shortenings.

Q: What sign convention should I use for internal axial force \(N\) (e.g., \(N>0\) in tension)?
A: Use the standard convention: take internal axial force \(N>0\) as **tension** (segment in axial tension), and \(N<0\) as **compression**. External point loads are already defined as positive to the right.

Q: Do you want the displacement \(u_D-u_A\) reported with positive meaning “to the right” (consistent with your external load sign convention)?
A: Yes—report \(u_D-u_A\) with **positive displacement meaning to the right**, consistent with the external load sign convention.
