The bar is prismatic, homogeneous, isotropic, and initially unstressed.  
Small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior is assumed.  
The cross‑sectional area is A = 300 mm², the length is L = 800 mm, the Young’s modulus is E = 200 000 MPa, and the axial load applied suddenly is P = 8000 N.  
One end of the bar is fixed and the load is applied instantaneously at the other end, with no damping or external restraints other than the fixed support.  
The static axial stress is σ_static = P / A.  
The static axial strain is ε_static = σ_static / E.  
The static axial displacement is δ_static = P L / (A E).  
For a step (sudden) load, the maximum dynamic stress is σ_max = 2 · σ_static = 2 P / A, obtained by equating the work of the load to the strain energy stored in the bar.  
The corresponding maximum dynamic strain is ε_max = σ_max / E.  
The corresponding maximum dynamic displacement is δ_max = σ_max L / E = 2 δ_static.  
These governing relations provide the required response quantities for the suddenly applied axial load.
