Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for the rod.  
Assume the rod material is structural steel with Young’s modulus \(E = 200{,}000\ \text{MPa}\) and density \(\rho = 7850\ \text{kg/m}^3\).  
The rod length is \(L = 1200\ \text{mm}\) (1.2 m).  
The rod diameter is \(d = 30\ \text{mm}\) giving a cross‑sectional area \(A = 7.07\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2\).  
The rod’s self‑weight is neglected.  
The top end of the rod is fixed (zero axial displacement) and the bottom end is free where the impact load is applied.  
A weight of \(W = 500\ \text{N}\) (mass \(m = 51\ \text{kg}\)) is dropped from a height \(h = 200\ \text{mm}\) (0.2 m) onto the free end of the rod.  
The impact is assumed perfectly elastic with no energy loss.  
No additional static axial load is applied (axial static load = 0 N).  
Use one‑dimensional axial wave theory: impact velocity \(v = \sqrt{2 g h}\); longitudinal wave speed in the rod \(c = \sqrt{E/\rho}\); particle velocity in the rod after impact \(v_p = \frac{2 m v}{m + \rho A L}\) (but rod mass is neglected, so \(v_p \approx v\)).  
Peak axial stress in the rod is \(\sigma_{\max} = \rho c v\).  
Axial strain is \(\varepsilon = \sigma_{\max}/E\).  
Axial displacement at the impact point is \(\delta = \varepsilon L\).  
The governing relation for stress wave propagation is \(\partial \sigma/\partial t = \rho c^2 \partial \varepsilon/\partial x\) with appropriate boundary conditions (fixed top: \(\varepsilon=0\) at \(x=0\); free bottom: stress equals impact load).  
Present the calculated response quantities (impact velocity, wave speed, peak stress, strain, displacement) using the above relations.
