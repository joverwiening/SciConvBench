Assume small deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic material behavior. 
Use a circular shaft radius of 17.5 mm. 
Assume Young’s modulus E = 210 000 MPa for a typical steel. 
Assume Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30. 
Compute shear modulus G = E / [2(1+ν)]. 
Apply a bending moment M = 3 000 000 N·mm about the specified axis. 
Apply a torque T = 500 000 N·mm. 
No axial force is present. 
Use the second moment of area I = π r⁴ / 4 and the torsional constant J = π r⁴ / 2 for a solid circular shaft. 
Determine the normal bending stress distribution σ_b = −M y / I, where y is the distance from the neutral axis. 
Determine the torsional shear stress distribution τ_t = T r / J. 
Superpose σ_b and τ_t to obtain the combined stress state at any point in the cross‑section. 
Calculate the principal stresses σ₁, σ₂ using σ₁,₂ = (σ_b ± √(σ_b² + 4τ_t²))/2. 
Calculate the principal strains ε₁, ε₂ from Hooke’s law using E, ν, and the principal stresses. 
State additional assumptions: plane sections remain plane, stress‑free lateral surface, temperature effects neglected, and loading is static. 
Present all governing relations and derived expressions clearly.
