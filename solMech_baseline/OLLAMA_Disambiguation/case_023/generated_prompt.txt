Determine the cantilever beam deflection under a uniformly distributed transverse load using small‑deformation, linear‑elastic solid mechanics.  
Assume the beam material is isotropic, homogeneous, and follows Hooke’s law with Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa.  
Assume the beam cross‑section is constant along its length with moment of inertia I = 1 000 000 mm⁴.  
Assume plane sections remain plane (Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory) and shear deformation is neglected.  
Assume the load is static, uniformly distributed with intensity w = 1.0 N/mm acting downward over the entire length L = 2500 mm.  
Use the governing differential equation E I d⁴v/dx⁴ = w for a uniformly distributed load on a cantilever.  
Apply boundary conditions at the fixed end (x = 0): deflection v = 0 and slope dv/dx = 0; and at the free end (x = L): bending moment M = 0 and shear V = 0.  
The bending moment distribution is M(x) = ‑w (L ‑ x)² / 2.  
The shear force distribution is V(x) = ‑w (L ‑ x).  
The deflection curve is v(x) = [w x² (6L² ‑ 4L x + x²)] / (24 E I).  
The tip deflection (at x = L) is δ_max = w L⁴ / (8 E I).  
The tip slope (at x = L) is θ_max = w L³ / (6 E I).  
Report the numerical values of δ_max and θ_max using the given parameters.  
Present all governing relations, assumptions, and final expressions clearly.
