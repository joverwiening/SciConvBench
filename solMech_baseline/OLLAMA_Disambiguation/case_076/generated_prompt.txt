Consider a simply supported beam of length L = 4000 mm with supports at the left end A and right end B.  
The beam is subjected to a single vertical point load P = 12 000 N applied at a distance a = 2000 mm from support A.  
No axial (tension) load is applied (N = 0 N) and no external moment is applied (M_ext = 0 N·mm).  
Assume small deformations, linear‑elastic material behavior, constant flexural rigidity EI, and Euler‑Bernoulli beam theory.  
The internal shear force V(x) is defined piecewise: for 0 ≤ x < 2000 mm, V(x) = +12 000 N; for 2000 mm < x ≤ 4000 mm, V(x) = –12 000 N.  
The internal bending moment M(x) is defined piecewise: for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2000 mm, M(x) = 12 000 N·x; for 2000 mm ≤ x ≤ 4000 mm, M(x) = 12 000 N·(4000 mm – x).  
At the left support (x = 0) and right support (x = 4000 mm) the bending moment is zero.  
The maximum bending moment occurs at the load point (x = 2000 mm) and equals M_max = 12 000 N × 2000 mm = 24 000 000 N·mm (24 kN·m).  
The governing equilibrium relations are dV/dx = –q(x) and dM/dx = V(x), where q(x) = 0 everywhere except at the point load where it is represented by a Dirac delta of magnitude P.  
The curvature‑moment relation is M(x) = EI · d²w/dx², linking the internal moment to the beam deflection w(x).
