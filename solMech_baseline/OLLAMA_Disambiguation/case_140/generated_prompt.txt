The shaft has a diameter of 45 mm, giving a radius of 22.5 mm.  
Assume the shaft material is isotropic, homogeneous steel with Young’s modulus E ≈ 210 GPa, shear modulus G ≈ 80 GPa, and Poisson’s ratio ν ≈ 0.30.  
The applied bending moment is M = 2 000 000 N·mm.  
The applied torsional moment (torque) is T = 500 000 N·mm.  
No axial force is present; only combined bending and torsion are considered.  
Small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior is assumed.  
Use the standard circular‑section properties: second moment of area I = π d⁴/64 and torsional constant J = π d⁴/32.  
Compute the bending normal stress at the outer surface as σ = M·y/I, where y = radius = 22.5 mm.  
Compute the torsional shear stress at the outer surface as τ = T·c/J, where c = radius = 22.5 mm.  
Determine the principal normal stresses at the outer surface from σ₁,₂ = σ/2 ± √[(σ/2)² + τ²].  
Obtain the shear strain at the outer surface as γ = τ/G.  
Compute the principal strains from Hooke’s law for plane stress: ε₁,₂ = (σ₁,₂/E) − ν(σ₂,₁/E).  
State that all governing relations are those of linear elasticity for isotropic materials under combined bending and torsion.
