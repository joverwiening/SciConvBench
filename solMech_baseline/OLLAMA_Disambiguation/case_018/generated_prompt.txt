Determine the torsional response of a solid circular shaft of length 1500 mm and radius 20 mm made of 6061‑T6 aluminum, assuming small‑deformation, linear‑elastic, isotropic, homogeneous behavior.  
Assume a shear modulus G = 25 939.8 MPa and adopt typical values for the remaining elastic constants, namely Young’s modulus E ≈ 68 900 MPa and Poisson’s ratio ν ≈ 0.33.  
The shaft is clamped (fixed) at the end x = 0 and free of axial loads, bending, or thermal effects.  
A torque T₁ = +300 000 N·mm is applied at the free end x = 1500 mm, and a torque T₂ = ‑150 000 N·mm is applied at x = 750 mm (opposite sense to T₁).  
The reaction torque at the fixed support equals the algebraic sum of the applied torques: R = ‑(T₁ + T₂).  
Use Saint‑Venant torsion theory for a circular shaft; the polar moment of inertia is J = π r⁴ / 2.  
The internal torque at any cross‑section x is the algebraic sum of all external torques applied to the right of that section (including the reaction at x = 0).  
The torsional shear stress distribution is τ(r) = T(x) · r / J, varying linearly with radius and reaching its maximum at the outer surface (r = 20 mm).  
The angle of twist over any segment Δx is θ = ∫(T(x) / (G J)) dx; for constant torque over a segment, θ = T · Δx / (G J).  
Compute the twist per unit length (θ′) as θ′ = T(x) / (G J) for each segment defined by the torque application points (0–750 mm, 750–1500 mm).  
Report the following response quantities: reaction torque at the clamp, internal torque diagram along the shaft, maximum shear stress at the outer surface, shear stress distribution τ(r) for each segment, angle of twist at x = 750 mm and at the free end, and total twist of the shaft.  
State that superposition of the effects of T₁ and T₂ is valid because the material response is linear‑elastic.  
Neglect any shear deformation correction beyond Saint‑Venant theory, as the shaft is slender (L ≫ r).
