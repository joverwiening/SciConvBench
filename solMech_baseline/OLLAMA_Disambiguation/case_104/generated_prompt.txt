Determine the axial elongation of a vertical bar caused solely by its self‑weight.  
Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior with constant material properties throughout the bar.  
Assume the bar has a uniform cross‑sectional area of 300 mm² and length of 3000 mm.  
Assume the material density is 8900 kg/m³ and Young’s modulus is 69 000 MPa.  
Assume gravitational acceleration is 9.81 m/s².  
Assume the bar is rigidly fixed at its top end and free at its bottom end; no other axial loads are applied.  
Neglect temperature effects, shear deformation, and any geometric non‑linearity.  
Use the distributed axial load due to self‑weight: force per unit length = ρ A g.  
The axial stress at a distance x measured from the fixed top is σ(x) = ρ g (L − x).  
The axial strain is ε(x) = σ(x)/E.  
The total elongation is obtained by integrating the strain over the length: δ = ∫₀ᴸ ε(x) dx = ρ g L²/(2E).  
Present the governing relations clearly and compute the numerical value of the elongation using the given data.
