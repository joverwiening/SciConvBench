Assume a small‑deformation, linear‑elastic solid mechanics model.  
Assume the column material is steel with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa.  
The rectangular cross‑section has width b = 100 mm and height h = 100 mm.  
The axial load magnitude is P = 90 000 N applied with an eccentricity e = 15 mm from the centroid of the cross‑section.  
Treat the column as short so that Euler buckling effects are neglected.  
Compute the cross‑sectional area A = b·h.  
Compute the second moment of area about the centroidal axes I = (b·h³)/12 for bending about the width direction (the direction of eccentricity).  
Determine the axial stress σₐ = P/A.  
Determine the bending moment M = P·e caused by the eccentricity.  
Determine the bending stress distribution σ_b(y) = M·y/I, where y is the distance from the neutral axis.  
Superpose axial and bending stresses to obtain the total normal stress σ(y) = σₐ + σ_b(y).  
Identify the maximum and minimum normal stresses at the extreme fibers (y = ±h/2).  
State that shear stresses are zero for pure axial and bending loading in this configuration.  
Present the governing relations and the computed response quantities clearly.
