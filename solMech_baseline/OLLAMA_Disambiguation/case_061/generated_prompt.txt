Consider a short rectangular column with width b = 60 mm and height h = 120 mm.  
Assume the column is made of structural steel with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa (typical value) and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.3.  
The column is subjected to an axial load P = 60 000 N applied with an eccentricity e = 20 mm measured from the centroid of the rectangular cross‑section.  
Use small‑deformation, linear‑elastic solid mechanics.  
Assume plane sections remain plane, shear deformation is negligible, and end effects are ignored because the column is short.  
Compute the direct axial stress as σ_a = P / (b h).  
Compute the bending moment caused by eccentricity as M = P e.  
Determine the second moment of area of the rectangular section about the centroidal axis as I = b h³ / 12.  
Compute the bending stress at a distance y from the neutral axis as σ_b = M y / I, where y = h/2 for the extreme fibers.  
The total normal stress at the extreme fibers is σ = σ_a ± σ_b, with the plus sign for the fiber in tension due to bending and the minus sign for the fiber in compression.  
Present the governing relations: equilibrium of forces and moments, linear‑elastic stress‑strain law σ = E ε, and the section property definitions for area A = b h and moment of inertia I.  
State the resulting expressions for σ_a, σ_b, and σ explicitly using the given numerical values.
