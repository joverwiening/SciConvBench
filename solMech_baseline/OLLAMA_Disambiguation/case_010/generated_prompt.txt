Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for both bars. 
The two bars are arranged in parallel, have equal length L = 800 mm, and are perfectly bonded so that they experience the same axial strain. 
Bar 1 is made of structural steel with Young’s modulus E₁ = 200 000 MPa and cross‑sectional area A₁ = 150 mm². 
Bar 2 is made of cast iron with Young’s modulus E₂ = 110 000 MPa and cross‑sectional area A₂ = 200 mm². 
The composite bar is fixed at the bottom plate and a tensile axial load P = 30 000 N is applied upward on the top plate. 
Neglect temperature effects, shear deformations, and transverse strains. 
Use the axial stress relation σᵢ = Eᵢ ε for each bar, the axial force relation Fᵢ = σᵢ Aᵢ, the equilibrium condition P = F₁ + F₂, and the compatibility condition ε₁ = ε₂ = δ/L where δ is the axial displacement of the top plate. 
From these governing relations determine the equivalent axial stiffness of the composite, the axial strain ε, the axial displacement δ, and the individual stresses σ₁ and σ₂ in each bar.
