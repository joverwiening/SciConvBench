Consider a hollow circular shaft with outer radius 20 mm and inner radius 15 mm, length 1000 mm, made of an isotropic homogeneous linear‑elastic material with shear modulus G = 39179.1 MPa (Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio may be taken as E ≈ 100 GPa, ν ≈ 0.34 if needed).  
The shaft is subjected to a pure torque of magnitude 300 000 N·mm and no transverse shear force.  
Small‑deformation, linear‑elastic assumptions apply; plane sections remain plane and rotate as rigid cross‑sections.  
The polar moment of inertia of the hollow section is J = π (r_o⁴ − r_i⁴)/2.  
The torsional shear stress distribution is τ(r) = T·r / J.  
The maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface: τ_max = T·r_o / J.  
The angle of twist over the length L is θ = T·L / (G·J).  
The shear strain is γ(r) = τ(r) / G and the corresponding circumferential displacement at radius r is u_θ = γ(r)·L.  
No axial deformation or warping is considered; the shaft ends are free to rotate.
