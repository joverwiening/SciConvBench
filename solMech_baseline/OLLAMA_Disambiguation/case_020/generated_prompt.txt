Consider a solid circular steel shaft of length L = 1200 mm and radius r = 17.5 mm.  
Assume linear‑elastic, small‑deformation behavior with shear modulus G = 77519.4 MPa.  
The shaft is fixed (zero rotation) at the end x = 0.  
Apply a torque T₁ = +200 000 N·mm at the free end x = L and a torque T₂ = –250 000 N·mm at x = 800 mm.  
No transverse shear forces are present.  
Use the polar moment of inertia J = π r⁴ / 2 for a solid circular shaft.  
Determine the internal torque distribution by superposition: for 0 ≤ x < 800 mm, internal torque = T₁ + T₂; for 800 mm ≤ x ≤ L, internal torque = T₁.  
Compute the shear stress at the outer surface as τ(x) = T_int(x)·r / J.  
Obtain the angle of twist distribution by integrating dφ/dx = T_int(x) / (G J) from the fixed end, with the boundary condition φ(0) = 0.  
Report the maximum shear stress, the angle of twist at the free end, and the piecewise twist distribution along the shaft.  
State that axial loads, temperature effects, and material non‑linearity are neglected.
