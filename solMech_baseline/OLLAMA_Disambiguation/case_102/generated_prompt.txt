Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for the rod.  
Use Young’s modulus \(E = 200{,}000\ \text{MPa}\) and density \(\rho = 7850\ \text{kg/m}^3\) (typical for AISI 1020 steel).  
The rod length is \(L = 800\ \text{mm}\) and diameter \(d = 25\ \text{mm}\) giving cross‑sectional area \(A = \pi d^2/4\).  
A static axial load of \(W = 1500\ \text{N}\) is applied to the rod.  
A weight of mass \(m = 1500\ \text{N}/9.81\ \text{m/s}^2 \approx 153\ \text{kg}\) is dropped from a height \(h = 200\ \text{mm}\) onto the free end of the rod.  
The rod is fixed at its top end and free at the bottom during impact.  
Assume the impact is perfectly elastic with no energy loss and neglect the rod’s own weight.  
Apply energy conservation: \(W\,(h+\delta_{\max}) = \dfrac{P_{\max}^2 L}{2 A E}\), where \(\delta_{\max}\) is the maximum axial compression and \(P_{\max}\) the peak impact force.  
Use axial strain‑displacement relation \(\delta = P L/(A E)\) and stress relation \(\sigma = P/A\).  
Determine the response quantities: maximum impact force \(P_{\max}\), maximum axial displacement \(\delta_{\max}\), maximum stress \(\sigma_{\max}\), and corresponding strain \(\varepsilon_{\max}\).  
Present the governing equations clearly and state any additional assumptions required for the analysis.
