Determine the relevant response quantities for a solid circular steel column under axial compression, focusing on a comparison between material yielding and Euler buckling for the given column and load, and evaluate a buckling/instability check if relevant.  
Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic solid mechanics unless otherwise required.  
The column material is structural steel with Young’s modulus E = 200 000 MPa and yield strength Sy = 250 MPa.  
The column length is L = 2000 mm and the circular diameter is d = 25 mm.  
The applied axial compressive load is P = 25 000 N.  
The effective‑length factor is K = 0.7, corresponding to a fixed‑pinned end condition.  
Use the cross‑sectional area A = π d²/4 and the second moment of area I = π d⁴/64 in all calculations.  
Compute the slenderness ratio λ = (K L)/r, where r = √(I/A).  
Calculate the Euler critical load Pcr = π² E I/(K L)².  
Determine the axial load that would cause yielding, Py = Sy · A.  
Compare the applied load P to both Pcr and Py to assess whether yielding or buckling governs the response.  
If P exceeds Pcr, indicate that buckling instability is expected; if P exceeds Py but is below Pcr, indicate yielding is the controlling failure mode; otherwise, the column remains safe under the given load.  
Present the governing relations clearly and state any additional assumptions made (e.g., uniform material properties, no initial imperfections, temperature effects neglected).
