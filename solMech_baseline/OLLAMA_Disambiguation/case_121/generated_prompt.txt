Assume a small‑deformation, linear‑elastic rectangular column with width b = 120 mm, height h = 200 mm, length L = 500 mm, and Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa (structural steel).  
The column is subjected to an axial load P = 40 kN applied at an eccentricity e = 25 mm measured in the width direction, producing bending about the centroidal height axis (z‑axis).  
End conditions are assumed pinned‑pinned at both ends.  
Cross‑sectional area A = b · h.  
Moment of inertia about the height axis I_z = h · b³ / 12.  
Axial stress σ_a = P / A.  
Bending moment at any section x is M(x) = P · [ e + w(x) ], where w(x) is the lateral deflection.  
The differential equilibrium for a pinned‑pinned column is E I_z w''(x) + P w(x) = P e.  
The critical Euler load for the assumed end condition is P_cr = π² E I_z / L².  
The lateral deflection shape solution is w(x) = (P e / (P_cr – P)) · [1 – cos(π x / L)].  
Maximum lateral deflection occurs at mid‑span (x = L/2) and is w_max = P e / (P_cr – P).  
Maximum bending moment occurs at mid‑span and is M_max = P · (e + w_max).  
Bending stress at the extreme fiber (distance c = b/2 from the neutral axis) is σ_b = M_max · c / I_z.  
Combined normal stress at the extreme fiber is σ = σ_a ± σ_b (tension on the side of the eccentricity, compression on the opposite side).  
The required response quantities are: axial stress σ_a, maximum bending stress σ_b, combined stress distribution σ, maximum lateral deflection w_max, and the safety ratio P / P_cr.  
All calculations are to be performed using the assumed material properties and geometry under the small‑deformation, linear‑elastic assumption.
