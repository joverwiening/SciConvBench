Assume the shaft material is isotropic homogeneous steel with Young’s modulus E = 210 GPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30.  
The shaft is a solid circular rod with diameter d = 25 mm, giving a radius c = 12.5 mm.  
The applied torque is T = 400 000 N·mm and the axial tensile load is P = 15 000 N.  
Adopt small‑deformation linear‑elastic theory and neglect temperature, residual stresses, and bending effects.  
Compute the cross‑sectional area A = π c² and the polar moment of inertia J = π c⁴ / 2.  
Determine the uniform axial normal stress σₐ = P / A.  
Determine the torsional shear stress distribution τ(r) = T · r / J, with the maximum shear stress τ_max = T · c / J occurring at the outer surface.  
Combine the axial and torsional stresses to obtain the normal stress on any plane as σ = σₐ ± τ_max, depending on the orientation of the plane.  
Calculate the principal normal stresses using the stress transformation for combined axial and shear loading: σ₁ = σₐ + τ_max, σ₂ = σₐ − τ_max, and σ₃ ≈ 0 (radial stress is negligible).  
Obtain the principal strain values from Hooke’s law for isotropic material: ε₁ = [σ₁ − ν(σ₂ + σ₃)] / E, ε₂ = [σ₂ − ν(σ₁ + σ₃)] / E, ε₃ = [σ₃ − ν(σ₁ + σ₂)] / E.  
Present the governing relations clearly: σₐ = P/A, τ(r) = T r/J, J = πc⁴/2, A = πc², stress transformation equations for principal stresses, and Hooke’s law for strain.  
State that plane sections remain plane and that the analysis applies along the length of the shaft where the cross‑section is uniform.
