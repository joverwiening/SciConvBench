Assume small‑deformation, linear‑elastic behavior for a homogeneous isotropic steel shaft with Young’s modulus E = 200 GPa and Poisson’s ratio ν = 0.30.  
The shaft is circular with radius r = 12.5 mm and length L = 100 mm.  
The shaft is subjected to a torque T = 300 000 N·mm and an axial tensile load P = 25 000 N applied uniformly at the ends.  
Use the torsion formula τ(r) = T·r / J, where J = π r⁴ / 2, to obtain the shear stress distribution.  
Use the axial stress formula σ_z = P / A, where A = π r², to obtain the uniform normal stress.  
Combine the axial and torsional stresses to obtain the total stress state at any point: σ_z (axial) and τ_θz (shear).  
Compute the principal stresses σ₁, σ₂ using σ₁,₂ = (σ_z / 2) ± √[(σ_z / 2)² + τ_θz²].  
Compute the corresponding principal strains using Hooke’s law for plane stress: ε₁,₂ = (1/E)[σ₁,₂ – ν σ₂,₁].  
Report the maximum shear stress τ_max = √[(σ_z / 2)² + τ_θz²].  
Provide the values of axial stress, maximum shear stress, principal stresses, and principal strains at the outer surface (r = 12.5 mm).  
State that warping is neglected and that the shaft is short enough that end effects are ignored.
