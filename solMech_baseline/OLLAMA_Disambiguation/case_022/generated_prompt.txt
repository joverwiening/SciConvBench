Use small‑deformation, linear‑elastic theory for an isotropic material.  
The shaft consists of two cylindrical segments: segment AB has length 300 mm and radius 12.5 mm; segment BC has length 700 mm and radius 15 mm.  
A constant torque of 450 000 N·mm is applied at end C and is transmitted unchanged through both segments.  
The shear modulus of the material is G = 77 519.4 MPa; assume no transverse shear force is present.  
End A is fixed, i.e., no translation or rotation is allowed.  
Assume temperature effects are negligible and the material behaves isotropically.  
Compute the torsional shear stress in each segment using τ = T·r / J, where J = π r⁴ / 2, and evaluate τ at the outer surface (r = radius) for each segment.  
Compute the angle of twist for each segment using θ = T·L / (J·G).  
The total twist at end C is the sum of the twists of segments AB and BC.  
Report the maximum shear stress in each segment and the total twist of the shaft.
